# Volume 02 Outline — The Sable Market

Volume number: 2
Chapter range: 51–100
Civic period: **MR 413–414**, seed month, growth month, and the ninth month
Batch map: 0001 (51–60), 0002 (61–70), 0003 (71–80), 0004 (81–90), 0005 (91–100)

Central pressure: A rain-basin seed cooperative called **the Elmsworth Seed Share** — sixty-one smallholders who pledged their own labour and a third of their seed to keep a seed house open through the bad autumn of MR 411 — is one short of a harvest it cannot lose, and the **Sable Compact's Morrowfen house** will sell the Share's paper and its members' district-rate arrears as a single bundled instrument at a price smallholders can afford. The Trust's credit window for the season has closed. Every signature in the stack is permitted by a rule, every figure balances, and nothing anybody signs is a lie. The cost of the Share's sequence clause is a **counterweight named as a district**, and a district cannot be asked anything; when the low ground's rate is charged, the ratepayers in arrears **assign** their collection rights to the Compact at a discount, the Compact sells the paper, and the chain walks nine links out of Rainmarket and ends on one household's arrears in Tallow Ward in the ninth month, the same month the ward's own reserve carry comes due.

Starting state: The tower is trusted because it works, and Volume 01's one institutional change — an emergency appeals board with a public duty to record dissent — exists, has sat twice, and has never yet been asked to look at a **private** instrument. The ward has been saved for the season and is paying a levy whose wage comes out of a three-hundred-year-old carry with a district's name on it. Hesper Rann is in custody in Crown March and has offered four dates. Renata Vole is in the coastal returns and has conceded nothing. The Trust's sealed notice is nailed to the Tally Hall door and cannot be carried out by anybody, because the office it names has been vacant for three centuries. And a blank first page is sitting in the public register with a signature written out in full beneath it and a clerk's name with no office after it.

Starting relationships:

- **Marek ↔ Ovid Merrow:** has not met. Merrow is a courteous man who is right about a thing, and Marek has no answer to him.
- **Marek ↔ Neris Kade:** professional respect and one argument neither of them won, and she has stopped asking him questions in a corridor because a kindness can be taken back. **In this volume she is a Rainmarket examiner and he is a man with no office who keeps getting into rooms; the volume's argument about him is that usefulness is not standing.**
- **Marek ↔ Oren Pike:** Pike is a grain factor who has come to use Marek, and Marek is a man who lets people.
- **Marek ↔ Ondra Kelb:** Kelb is back at a desk with a left hand that does fine work and is not his, and he is the only clerk in Bell Stair who counts twice.
- **Marek ↔ Tamsin and Tomas:** unchanged and not improved. The school is shut, the north-road span is worked three seasons of eleven days, and the Crown March countersignature is still on a road between here and the coast.
- **Neris Kade ↔ the Share:** her own district's cooperative sealed a paper of this kind two years ago and she certified it, and the next generation of seed-keepers in that district is gone. She is not asked about it in the first half of this volume and does not offer it.

Starting power level: **Ledger Sight, and one spent founding standing hold that can never be used again.** Marek is a clerk of the Chamber with no office. He may read anything, certify nothing, and enter nothing.

Volume power change: **Boundary Hold, practised.** Marek already used a hold once, badly, in Chapter 8, and it injured a junior clerk. In this volume he uses it deliberately, twice, on instruments that are **lawful**, and he learns the thing the outline says: a hold can delay a settlement perfectly and cannot touch the market's legal consequences at all. The delay goes into his own yearline and stays there as a year scar. Nothing above Boundary Hold. The founding standing hold is spent and is not wished for.

Major locations:

- **The Tally Halls, Bell Stair** — the copy room, the recertification desk, the assignment desk, the posting board, the public register.
- **The Threshold, Stillstep Island** — the intake windows and the public complaint board, and the shuttered Trust credit window in the seed month.
- **Rainmarket** — seed, grain, river trade, weather gauges, warehouses. The Elmsworth seed house and low ground; **Ovid Merrow's house over a chandler's shop**; **Oren Pike's counting floor** at the second bell in the morning.
- **Tallow Ward** — four yards, the Gate tank, the board, and the second round of doors nobody has been to.
- **The copy room** — where the hand-copies are made, and where the volume's one panel is spent alone on a stair landing.

Major factions and forces in this volume: the **Sable Compact's Morrowfen house** (a licensed lender, not a criminal body, headed by Ovid Merrow and underwritten by Halvard Sunn, a Saltglass harbor notary), the **Chamber of Accounts**, the **Yearspire Trust** through one man — **Rustam Vey**, the settlement officer at the Rainmarket window, who applies rules correctly and is not a villain — the **Lantern Wardens** and Tallow Ward's own yard under Aldis Kern, the **seed trade** through Oren Pike, the chamber-clerks' **Benevolent Fund** and the emergency appeals board, and the ward. **No Choir, no military body, no Crown of Continuation, and no regional political crisis enters this volume.**

### New Volume 02 names (locked)

- **Ovid Merrow**, 47, head of the Sable Compact's Morrowfen house. Charismatic, patient, genuinely good at pricing risk, and never once writes down a thing that is not true. **He is refuted by nobody in this volume's first half.**
- **Oren Pike**, 51, Rainmarket grain factor, thirty years on the counting floor. Begins as Ovid's commercial opponent and ends the volume as the first person in the basin to put his own house's figures into a public register — and he is not forgiven and is not a good man.
- **Ilse Ferrow**, 35, the Morrowfen house's underwriter. She does the work, she sits on a barrel in a kitchen, she answers every question truthfully, and she is likable. She is the face of the offer.
- **Halvard Sunn**, 44, a Saltglass harbor notary resident in Morrowfen, whose counterseal is the thing that makes the paper tradeable to houses that do not buy Morrowfen paper, and who cannot be checked by anybody in Bell Stair.
- **Rustam Vey**, 50, Trust settlement officer at the Rainmarket credit window, nineteen years in the post. He closes the window on the sixth for a reason that is on paper and correct, and four households lose a fortnight they did not have.
- **The Elmsworth Seed Share** — a cooperative of sixty-one Rainmarket smallholders, incorporated at the Elmsworth seed house, with a germination bench, a seed keeper, and a roll on which a sixteen-year-old appears because her mother is a member.
- **Iren Orleb**, 61, smallholder, member since the Share was founded, whose assignment is **eight months older than the Compact's house in Morrowfen**.
- **Mirren Fisk**, 34, seed-house keeper, who runs the germination bench and will not speak to a man from Bell Stair.
- **Bessok Tan**, 29, second-generation member, who has the arithmetical mind and asks the question nobody else asks.
- **Ansgar Drel**, 78, joined the Share at sixty, the last of a line of seed-keepers, whose own kept-back seed is the Share's oldest stock.
- **Wenna Drel**, 16, on the Share's roll because her mother is, and who signs where her mother signs.

### New Volume 02 local terms (locked)

- **the Share** — the Elmsworth Seed Share, and its seed house.
- **the paper** — a Compact bundled instrument, sold as one line.
- **an assignment** — a ratepayer in arrears assigns the collection right in the arrears to whoever pays them at a discount. One line, dated, signed by the assignor. **There is no box for a rate on the form, and the form predates the practice.**
- **the fourth kind** — an entry in the account desk that is not a levy, a schedule, a posting or a return. Named in this volume: an assignment. **Hesper Rann offered four such dates in Volume 01 and there was no box for the kind; the kind is named here and the fourth date is still Grale's.**
- **counterweight** — the named future consequence that balances a sequence loan. It may be named as a district, and a district is not a person.
- **the chain** — the series of assignments along which a charged rate walks away from the people who caused it.
- **the register of risk** — the volume's institutional change. Any bundled instrument entered publicly must be entered as its parts, in the words of the people who stand behind them, with the counterweight named to the people who will bear it.
- **calendar lag** — the interval between a local contract date and its recognised Morrowfen Reckoning date. A Tide Reckoning mark that has not been translated sits in lag and cannot be silently folded into an MR year.

### Locked instruments in the Elmsworth file (Chapters 51–100)

- **The Share's seed instrument.** A cooperative loan sealed once for sixty-one members. All five parts present. Need: seed, roof, and a threshing share, in the bad autumn of MR 411. Pledge: next season's labour, eleven days each, and a third of the seed kept back after harvest. Scope: the seed house and the low ground. Witnesses: two, neither paid by the Share. Settlement: out of the harvest. **It is well made. Nobody in this volume finds a defect in it and nobody may.** Its **fourth clause recites a counterweight: the district named in the schedule bears the failure of the season favoured.** The district named in the schedule is the Rainmarket low ground, which is correct, and which is the whole of the trouble.
- **The assignment schedule.** Fifty-two lines out of sixty-one members, each one an assignment of the collection right in a rate arrears, each dated, each signed by the assignor, **with no rate box and no name box for the person the rate is charged to beyond the assignor.** Nine members have nothing to assign and did not sign. The form predates the practice.
- **The bundle, or the paper.** The Compact's single instrument: the Share's seed instrument plus the fifty-two assigned claims, sold as one line at **300** against a seed cost of **1,100** and a plough worth **40**. It carries a stated rate and an unstated counterweight, and it carries **Halvard Sunn's Tide Reckoning counterseal**, which is the thing that makes it acceptable to houses that will not buy Morrowfen paper.
- **The recertification.** The Share's instrument must be recertified before it can draw, and the recertification is a second clerk's eye. **Marek is given the list because he is the only clerk in Bell Stair with nothing to do**, which is the volume's standing joke and is not funny by Chapter 60.

### Locked figures (do not make them close)

**Sixty-one** members. **Fifty-two** assignments signed; **nine** have not signed because they have no arrears to assign. The Share borrowed **2,400** in the bad autumn. Assignments total **1,916** across fifty-two hands. The paper is sold at **300**. Seed cost **1,100**; a second-hand plough **40**. **Nine links in the chain.** The second link from the end is one Tallow Gate carrier's arrears. The last is a district. **The carry is 40,900, named against Tallow Ward, and is due in the ninth month of MR 413** — the same month the chain arrives. **Do not make the assigned total and the bundle total agree, and do not make the chain's nine links sum to anything in particular; the chain is a list of dates and names, not an account.**

Midpoint reversal (Chapters 55–56, with the setup beginning at 51): Marek enters this volume looking for a false-witnessed instrument, because that is what he found last time and because a false term is contestable. **The midpoint turns it around in two parts. There is no false term.** Every rate, every assignment, every figure and every date in the Compact's stack is permitted by a rule that predates the Compact, and Ondra Kelb — the boy whose hand was destroyed by a hold, who is now the only clerk in the building who counts twice — finds that the bundle balances to the penny and says so in a room, and is ashamed of how much he is relieved. **And the fourth kind is named.** Hesper Rann offered four dates on which he entered something in the account desk that was not a levy, a schedule, a posting or a return, and there was no box for the fourth kind; a character, out of arithmetic, in a room, names it in this volume: an assignment. The volume's question stops being *who wrote a lie* and becomes **who is standing behind a name, and who is not in the room when the name is written.** The reader's sympathy is forced to divide, exactly as in Volume 01, and this time there is no villain to divide it with — only Ovid Merrow, who is pleasant.

Escalation sequence, in five movements:

- **I. What the Share Signed (51–60).** The first demand on the reserve carry in three hundred years; a recertification list of twenty-two files; a seed house, a germination bench, and a woman who says usefulness is not standing; two clerks discovering what a counterweight clause is; Ovid Merrow's house and a question Marek cannot answer; a kitchen where nobody lies and a family signs anyway; the discovery that the whole stack balances; a grain factor who is right for a bad reason; a shower in the wrong place; a shuttered Trust window; and a hand copy in the open that Marek is not allowed to enter in anything.
- **II. The Fourth Kind (61–70).** The assignments are traced and the chain is found to be nine links long. Hesper names the fourth kind from a cell and cannot name the fourth date. The chain's second link is a Tallow Gate carrier. Aldis Kern and the ward's second round of doors: three hundred and seventy-seven of four hundred and six have never been asked one question by anybody, and the ward's method is a ward document and is not credited to Marek.
- **III. What a Counterweight Is (71–80).** The counterweight is named in a field. Neris Kade's own district's paper is produced, and she certified it. Marek practises the hold twice, on lawful instruments, and learns that a delay is not a defence, and pays for it in his own yearline.
- **IV. The Register (81–90).** The counter-move: a register in which a bundle may not be entered as one line. Tallow Ward's method gets a name and an owner, and the fight is over who pays for it and who may read it.
- **V. What the Paper Is Worth (91–100).** The ninth month. The carry and the chain arrive in the same week, in Tallow Ward. The board sits again and cannot order a refund. Grale proves the line and stops. The register's first morning, in forty-one hands.

Volume climax (Chapters 93–95): **The ninth month, in Tallow Ward, and the chain lands.** A district claim and the reserve carry are both charged against the ward in the same week, and the ward's own board — the emergency appeals board, whose duty to record dissent has been used against its author twice — is asked to choose between them, and **cannot**, and says so in a yard, in longhand, with the Chair writing it himself. **What breaks the Compact is not law and not a hold: it is a public register in which a bundle is entered as its parts, in the words of the people who stand behind them, and a queue of households who can tell the difference between a Trust seed loan and a Compact paper before they sign.** Ovid Merrow's monopoly is the right to be the only one in the basin who explains what a piece of paper is. He loses that and keeps the money, and he says the loss was fair, and the reader is left unable to say whether he is a good man or only a competently defeated one.

Concrete resolution (Chapters 96–100):

- **A public register of risk exists**, with hours on it, paid out of a levy the ward and the Share both agreed to, and its one rule: **a bundle may not be entered as one line.** It is entered as its parts, with the counterweight named to the people who will bear it and the number of people who bear it. **Ovid Merrow loses the Morrowfen assignment licence and the ability to sell a bundle as a single instrument. He does not lose his money, the Compact is not dissolved, and he is not prosecuted in this volume.**
- **Oren Pike is the first person in the basin to put his own house's figures into the register**, keeps his profit, and gives them for a reason he states without shame: a market in which nobody can read the paper is a market with one price. He is not forgiven and is not written as a good man.
- **Grale proves the line in the account desk, and stops**, on his own condition from a yard in the rains. The district account is then held by nobody, and the water office's second cut of the year has no officer to sign it — which is the volume's last institutional cost, and it is Grale's own doing.
- **Hesper Rann's four dates are closed at three and a half.** He names the kind in a cell, gives the fourth date to a room only once the room can be trusted with it, and never says he was wrong about the gate.
- **The chain ends in Tallow Ward and the ward is charged twice.** The carry comes due and the district claim comes with it, and **Aldis Kern refuses to choose between them in front of four hundred people and says why**, and the second cut order — which carries the name of the officer who enters it, and there is no officer — is the volume's sharpest small cruelty.
- **Neris Kade's own district's paper is entered in the register in her own handwriting and under her own name**, with a correction she publishes beside it. She is not forgiven by anybody in the volume, including herself, and she asks for no forgiveness.
- **Tomas Vale's Crown March countersignature comes back with a wrong date**, and he will not have it corrected by anybody but a clerk who will say so in a record. His three seasons of eleven days stand.
- **Marek's position at the end:** a clerk with no office who has been given, by other people and by no form, a standing duty to countersign the register. He is not appointed to it, he cannot enter anything in it, and the duty clerk lets him read it out, which is not the same as entering it, and she says so.

Next-volume question: **A Tide Reckoning mark on a Morrowfen paper cannot be checked by anybody in Morrowfen.** Halvard Sunn's counterseal is real and his hand appears on a second instrument he says he never sealed, and the same mark in the same hand turns up on a Red Uplands grain contract that has nothing to do with any of this. Why do counterfeit year-marks work in a different calendar? Volume 03's question — why are signatures being copied across unrelated contracts — is planted here and answered there.

Final chapter image: The register room on the first morning of the harvest month, and forty-one lines in forty-one hands, because a bundle may not be entered as one line and a hand is the only kind of entry there is. A man at the end of the counter reads a line out loud in the words the person behind it used, and there is a queue of people who have come to see whether their own line is right. In the lane outside, a man who cannot pay takes a Compact paper anyway, because a register is not a seed, and the man at the counter watches him do it and writes nothing down. And at the foot of the page there is a **line with nothing at the head of it**, which nineteen hands have declined to name and the yard has voted against filling, and which is left blank, because a heading is the one thing on a sheet that cannot be put in afterwards by anybody who was not there on the day.

## Chapter-type distribution (guide target, applied across the volume)

Roughly 20% investigation or discovery, 20% action or survival, 20% character and relationship work, 15% practice or controlled experiment, 15% political or social pressure, 10% aftermath. The volume's three deliberate practice chapters are **53** (two clerks and a fourth clause, with the batch's one Ledger Sight panel spent alone), **56** (Ondra Kelb's counting floor: the bundle balances, and the batch's midpoint is a controlled experiment that comes out wrong for everybody), and **87** (the first entry in the register, entered as its parts). **Chapter 60 is a fourth** and may be counted as practice rather than aftermath. **The volume runs one Ledger Sight panel and two Boundary Holds, and no panel may be used in front of a person who can be asked about it in a room.**

## Volume guardrails

- No answer to the missing year, the Last Convocation, the First Account's origin, or Edren Sable. The volume ends with a register and a blank column heading.
- **A-0 is not explained and is not explained toward.** The one permitted move is the one Ovid Merrow makes about a **page**: he has tried for nine years to buy the oldest page and cannot, because a line with nothing in it has nobody standing behind it, and he says that in about nine months there will be a public register with a blank line on it and a name under it that is not a clerk's, and he will buy that in a morning. **He may not say who A-0 is, may not have seen the page, and may not know what a signature is.**
- Nothing above Boundary Hold. The founding standing hold is spent and is not wished for. No Season Hand, no Returnpath, no market power.
- **There is no villain in this volume.** Ovid Merrow must be refuted by consequence and by people, never by a lie of his own, and a reader who liked him must still recognise him at the end. He loses a monopoly and keeps his money.
- **Grale's cut-order suspicion is not resolved** and is not connected to the counterweight clause, which is a current legal practice and not the origin of anything. The day he proves the line in the account desk he stops, and that is his own condition from Volume 01, not a discovery about sixty years ago.
- **The ward roll naming district four and the name Kallow is not mentioned.** It is in Marek's coat and it is owned by Volume 05.
- **Pella Rusk's unsigned surety cell on page three is not resolved and is not a surprise.** Marek's unanswered answer to her in a schoolroom is still unanswered and must not be paid off casually.
- **Marek's year scar is Volume 16's and its public testimony is MR 435's.** The two holds in this volume each cost him a ruled mark of a kind he cannot read and must not try to read. Do not let a second person decode a mark, and do not let Tamsin be asked anything.
- The romance does not advance past professional respect and one argument neither of them wins, until the volume's last movement, where the movement is **that she admits her own paper in her own hand** and that she asks for nothing.
- The resolution saves the season's seed and changes one practice. It does not make credit safe, it does not make a register a law, and it does not prevent a hungry man in a lane from signing.
