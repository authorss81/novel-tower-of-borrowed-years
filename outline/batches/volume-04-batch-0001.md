## 151 — Twenty-Two in Four Months

**Date:** the fourth of the sixth month, MR 415. **Place:** the tower's public witness counter, causeway side, from the second bell.
**Engine:** a counter, a book, a bundle of used forms tied with string that has to be untied and retied, and a woman who cannot find the thing she keeps everything in.
**Who:** **Perpetua Ohl, fifty-one, thirty years at that counter**, who keeps a book of other people's words. Marek, who is there because he reads other people's papers out loud and there is nobody else at that counter who will.
**What happens:** A challenge is free, anybody may bring one, and there is no form on which one may be refused. **Twenty-two have been brought since the first of the fourth month, which was about nine weeks ago, and there were two in the whole of MR 414.** Ohl reads the count out of her own book and will not say a rate. Nine of the twenty-two are the same case in different coats: a sheet that names a person as a witness, and a person who says they signed something else, years ago, about something else, at a counter, in type.
**Resistance:** Ohl is right that she is a counter keeper and not an investigator, right that she cannot refuse a challenge, and right that she has never once been asked whether the people in her book were free when they signed. **Marek wants a hand. There is no hand. Every one of the nine signed it themselves.**
**The change:** The bundle under the counter. Untwine the string, and the forms are all the same, in type, four lines, and **the fourth line takes a month and not a day, and the third line is printed and says *I will speak to any paper touching this line while I live*,** and **there is no line on the form that names the paper.** The string has to be retied and she reties it wrong on purpose the first time and then properly.
**Seeds:** the four lines; the month for a date; a line and not a paper; the count of twenty-two; the counter's book of other people's words; **nobody has ever thought to ask her either.**
**Do not:** invent a forger. **Do not name the year the print was stopped.** **Do not put Wenna Drel in this chapter.** **Do not thank Ohl.**

## 152 — The Ditch She Signed For

**Date:** the ninth of the sixth month, MR 415. **Place:** a boundary ditch between two seed yards in Rainmarket, at the third bell, in the rain.
**Engine:** a ditch, a wall that was never mended, a spade, and a woman who is not going to be told anything by anybody.
**Who:** **Odile Marn, thirty-one**, who signed an attestation in the growth month of MR 411 about this ditch. Two children. **Neris Kade, who is the season examiner for the district and is not a member of anything**, and who has been sent to the same ditch and has not been told why.
**What happens:** Odile Marn was told in the sixth month that she is a witness to a sheet about a district water account. She has never read the sheet. She signed about a ditch because a man from the water office said it would be doing the district a favour and because she wanted the wall mended, and the wall was mended in the growth month of MR 411 and the ditch is a ditch.
**Resistance:** She is not angry and she is not dramatic. She asks the correct question — *what am I to do* — and the answer is that there is no form on which a person may be entered as saying they do not consent. Kade and Marek both want to explain it to her and both get four lines and both are wrong in the same way, and she stops them by asking what they are going to do instead.
**The change:** She gives them the sheet, or the copy of it, without being asked, and says the reason: **she has been carrying it since the sixth month and there is nowhere in this kingdom to put a thing you do not want.** And she says the second thing that is the volume's question in a mouth with a spade in her hand: **nobody has ever asked her whether she was free.**
**Seeds:** the ditch; the wall; a line and not a paper; the fourth question at a counter in a different form.
**Do not:** make her a symbol. **Do not let either of them say they are sorry, and do not let either of them be thanked.** **No first names.**

## 153 — Two Names at the Head of It

**Date:** the thirteenth of the seventh month, MR 415. **Place:** a table in a first-floor room in Bell Stair, and then the copy room, and a form being cut.
**Engine:** a sheet of paper, a pencil, a straight-edge, and a column that gets a head — the first headed column in three volumes of this manuscript.
**Who:** Marek and **Neris Kade**, who have been put in one file by a regional records office and neither of them asked. **Sedge**, who cuts forms, and who **says nothing whatever about whether this one is right.**
**What happens:** A paper is going to need a field examiner and a man who reads papers out loud. They do the work and they cannot do it separately, and the first thing they build is the method: **a sheet ruled in two columns with two names at the head of them, and the rule that neither may write in the other's column.** The heads are the whole of the argument and Kade makes it.
**Resistance:** **The first honest disagreement about who should bear a risk, and it is about each other, and neither of them is wrong.** Marek does not want her name on anything. She says he keeps deciding who bears a risk and does not ask the person he is deciding for, and she is right, and he is right that a second name on a sheet is a thing a man in a good coat can produce at either of them in nine months, and the cost is said out loud and they do it anyway.
**The change:** **It is a method and not a refusal, and it is the first thing either of them has built.** And Sedge cuts it and does not say whether it is right.
**Seeds:** the paired field sheet; two names at the head of a column; the cost of a name on a sheet; Sedge's silence, which is Ch 192's.
**Do not:** **put Sedge's locked line in this chapter or in any chapter of this batch — it stands in five places in the manuscript and may not be put in a sixth mouth.** No warmth, no first names, nobody thanked, nothing built between them except paper.

## 154 — Five Lines

**Date:** the second of the eighth month, MR 415. **Place:** a print shop on Ropewalk in Bell Stair, from the third bell, with the door up and the forme unlocked.
**Engine:** a press, a forme, a compositor's stick, a drawer of proofs, and a sheet of type with one line on it that is not on the form any more.
**Who:** **Iveta Roslak, forty-three**, who has printed the lifelong attestation eleven times in nineteen years. **Orren Vaas, forty-one, a factor in Rainmarket**, who is in the shop for about nine lines and is not to be confused with Oren Pike and does not repeat what he said on a tail-board in the fifth month of MR 415.
**What happens:** Roslak keeps one of every form she has ever printed, which is what printers do. In the drawer, under eleven years of price lists, is **the proof of the first printing, and it has five lines.**
**Resistance:** **She is not hiding anything and there is nothing to catch.** She set the amended forme herself at twenty-four because it saved a half-sheet on every copy, and the fifth line — *the paper* — is the line that was cut, and the reason it was cut is that it was the line that was in type and not in ink and had always been in type. Nobody consulted anybody because there was nothing to consult about: it is a line, and a line is a length of metal, and a length of metal is money.
**The change:** The chapter's finding, in her mouth: **cutting that line did not make the form cheaper to read. It made every attestation ever signed since good against every paper on that line, for ever, and there is nobody in this kingdom to be angry with, because she was twenty-four.**
**Seeds:** the fifth line; *the paper*; nineteen years; a printer who keeps one of everything; the fact that a form is an economy before it is a law.
**Do not:** make her a villain or a penitent. **Do not let her say she wishes she had not.** **Do not explain the Saltglass Harbor Council and do not imply it.**

## 155 — The Price Is Not Money

**Date:** the sixth of the eighth month, MR 415, with the journey out on the fifth. **Place:** a night and a day up the river to Verrow, the records office, and the basket.
**Engine:** a basket, a case, a filing clerk opening eleven years of other people's shelves, and an entry sheet with a price on it that is not money.
**Who:** **Damir Pribis, forty-nine, eleven years opening the basket** — on the page in Volume 03 and used again, and **not Josip Malnar**. Marek, who has no right of entry and is admitted because a filing clerk can admit a person to a room he is standing in.
**What happens:** The record loan of the third month of MR 415. It is lawful, it is on a standing authority, and it stopped a district account being struck out. **Its price is entered as fourteen attestations and its scope names a line of district accounts, and the price is a class of people's willingness, and a class cannot consent, and that is the only defect in the instrument and it was not a mistake.**
**Resistance:** Pribis is right that a basket is a basket, right that he does not know what is in the cases above his own shelf, and right that he opened it because a man with no office asked him to and he is a filing clerk and people ask him. **He is also right that the entry is in his hand and that he cannot unwrite it, and he says so before Marek asks.**
**The change:** **The price is fourteen and the number in this basin is about nine and there is no form on which Marek may be told the number, and no office in the kingdom that will read a sheet of names and give a figure.** And on the way back down the river, in a cold room above a chandler's yard, Marek works out that nobody forged anything and everybody is affected, and that this is the first problem in three years he cannot answer with a document.
**Seeds:** fourteen; about nine; a class of people's willingness; the basket; the fifth month's rate that did not come.
**Do not:** invent an officer who did it for villainy. **Do not say the loan was unnecessary.** **Do not confuse Pribis with Malnar. No Ledger Sight panel in this chapter.**

## 156 — A Name on a Copy

**Date:** the nineteenth of the eighth month, MR 415, the second watch. **Place:** a roof, a lamp, a bundle of corded paper, and a street in this city that is not on any map a clerk in Bell Stair can look at.

**What happens:** Nim has been in this manuscript before, on a stair in the flood year, and she set a price then and it was not money: **the moment you find out anything at all about a name of mine, you tell me.** She has a **certified copy** — a true copy, taken on a press, of a page in a register in this city — and on it, in the fourth column, in a fair small hand, is the name **Ilen Rook.** She has had it for about four days. **She did not tell him on the day she got it and she says why and the reason is that she wanted to see whether he would ask her.**
**Resistance:** He does not ask her. He works it out, over about nine seconds, from a form, and she watches him do it. **There is no name from that street in any book he is allowed to open, and he is not told the name of the street and does not ask twice.**

**Seeds:** the certified copy; Ilen Rook; a fair small hand; a column with no head that is not a seventh; a street with no form on it and no name given; the price from Chapter 15 of Volume 01, still unpaid.

## 158 — A Fortnight on a Named Ground

**Date:** the twelfth of the ninth month, MR 415. **Place:** Rainmarket, a boundary ditch, a wall, and a form that goes in and comes out.
**Engine:** a trestle, a board of nine cut lines, a hand that slides four inches along it, and a drill that goes through a second sowing without being lifted.
**Who:** Odile Marn again, who has been told what she is a witness to and has not been asked what she needs. Marek, and a person he must ask.
**What happens:** The ninth month is the harvest month and a season works is in force in the district on a properly witnessed authority, and its scope names a line of holdings. Marek can point that effect at a named holding inside its own scope. **He asks Odile Marn what she needs before he points it at anything, and she says she does not want a winter's fuel and she wants a fortnight on the ground and the ditch cut, because the ditch is what her attestation is about and the ground is the only thing on it that has changed in four years.**
**Resistance:** The ledger's wording says *the line*. Her need says *a ditch, cut, this month*. **He points it at the ground and not at the line and it works**, and the cost is a season and not a person, and the woman's ground comes up for a fortnight and the wall is not mended and the ditch is not cut and both of those are still hers.
**The change:** **He asked, and it worked, and the whole of the volume's power change is in that sentence and it is nowhere near a revelation.** And the ditch is not cut, and she says so, and she is not grateful, and she says the reason: **a fortnight is not a ditch.**
**Seeds:** Season Hand, used once, in its own scope, on a named object; the cost is a season; the difference between the line and the need.
**Do not:** **do not name Season Hand, Ledger Sight, or any of the words.** **Do not make her grateful. Do not increase the effect. Do not cut the ditch.**

## 159 — A Stall That Is Not a Stall

**Date:** the twenty-first of the ninth month, MR 415. **Place:** the market on the low-ground road at the second bell, a shut pitch, and a lockable box under a counter.
**Engine:** a shut stall, a lease that needs a witness, a hand that has got worse over twenty-six years, and a piece of paper with a list on it and no head and no total.
**Who:** **Margit Sova, forty-one, twenty-six years in the trade**, one of the spent, who has been told twice and does not know it twice. Marek, who wants to write down what she says.
**What happens:** Her pitch cannot be held. **A lease is witnessed, a witness is spent, and there is no form on which a person may be re-witnessed**, so the lease has run out at the end of a season in which she could trade and cannot hold the ground, and the stall that shut four days running in the harvest month of MR 414 and is not a stall is a stall that cannot be licensed.
**Resistance:** **She is right about the law and she is right that nobody has ever asked her**, and she refuses to have what she says written down, and the reason she gives is that a list in a drawer is what she has been living inside for twenty-six years and she will not make one more. And Marek's hand goes to his coat and stops, because he wanted to write it down to be able to prove it later, and that is his whole flaw and she names it without ever having been told it.
**The change:** She gives him the thing she has instead, which is her own list, and it has a column and nothing at the head of it, and **it is not a seventh place, because it is the second of the six that already exist, and she says so and does not know she is saying anything remarkable.** And she says the sentence the volume closes its first movement on: **a man can be free when he signs a thing about a ditch and not free when somebody tells him what he is a witness to, and there is no form on either.**
**Seeds:** spent; a lease; a list; the sixth of the six; the fact that nobody has ever asked her.
**Do not:** **do not add a seventh bare column and do not count them out loud as seven.** Do not let him write anything down.

## 160 — The Counter-Signature

**Date:** the thirtieth of the ninth month, MR 415, from the sixth bell, and the year turns in the night. **Place:** the fourth of the four rooms behind the Threshold, eight feet by eleven, a table, a rail round three sides, a window with a wire in it.
**Engine:** a sheet, a pen, an ink that has to be shaken, and a clerk putting his name against a paper he has not read.
**Who:** **Nim Rook**, who has a copy with a name on it and needs it to be good in a room. **Neris Kade**, who is in the room and who is not asked to sign anything. Marek.
**What happens:** The copy is a true copy of a page that was never true, and it is not good in a room, and Nim needs it to be good in a room by the fourth of the next month, and the only authority in this basin that can make a copy hold good is a **counter-signature** by a clerk. **He can read a paper and he can counter-sign one and that is the whole of what he is qualified for in this city.**
**Resistance:** He tells her the cost as he understands it, which is that a chain that is false-witnessed walks to the living clerk who countersigned in good faith. **She chooses it. She is owed the choosing and she gets it. He is not redeemed and he is not forgiven and he is not going to be.**
**The change:** **The chain walks to him and what comes off him is his standing as a person whose word a room will take, and it is not a year and not a season and not a scar, and it is the first time in this manuscript that a cost has been paid in somebody's belief.** And **he did not tell her the moment**, which is the price she set in MR 412 and which he has now broken twice, and she says so, and the year turns in the night, and there is no bell for it and no office that keeps a calendar anybody in a lane has ever been shown.
**Seeds:** counter-signature; spent; the chain; the price from Chapter 15 of Volume 01; a copy that is true of a page that was never true.
