## 101 — The Weighbridge

**Date:** the fourth of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** the low-ground road, the weighbridge between the Gate and the north road, at night, about forty carts deep.
**Engine:** a scale, a queue, and a rate that is moving while the men in the queue are standing in it. Not a document, not a room.
**Who:** Petar Duss, the haulier, who is on the page from Ch 85 and who brought barley down with a mark on his paper that belongs to nobody he has met. Oren Pike, a factor, who is right about the mechanism for a bad reason and is not forgiven. Marek, who is there because he reads things out and there is nobody else to read them out.
**What happens:** The low ground's barley rate has fallen by about a third in nine days and nobody in Rainmarket can say why, and the reason is that grain is coming down off the Red Uplands early and in quantity. Forty carts at the bridge at night, weighed, and every one of them is carrying a paper, and about a third of the papers carry a mark in a calendar the weighbridge clerk cannot read. The clerk reads the rate aloud because that is what the bridge does; nobody has ever asked him to read anything else.
**Resistance:** Pike says the fall is ordinary and the basin is fed and that a man who reads marks all night is a man with nothing to do. He is right. Marek cannot produce a single figure that is wrong.
**The change:** Marek asks the haulier one question — not about the mark, about who told him the road was open — and the answer is a Crown March office, and the office is not in this basin. The chapter ends on a cart at the fourth bell and a rate that has fallen again since dusk.
**Seeds:** the mark in a foreign calendar; the Crown March offices; the fourth watch at the weighbridge; that a rate can fall because of a season rather than because of a market.
**Do not:** invent a false figure. **Do not name Halvard Sunn in this chapter.** **Do not give a price figure for barley** — the only number is *about a third in nine days*.

## 102 — The Road and the Four Terraces

**Date:** the eighth to the tenth of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** up the upland road, eleven switchbacks, a day; then the four terraces at dawn, a germination bench, and a seed store.
**Engine:** a bench, thirty-nine lots, nine of them sand, and a woman counting. Physical labour in bad light.
**Who:** Janko Vrabec, 58, keeper of the Hartlow cooperative's roll, who was told in the growth month of MR 413 that he is going to die this winter. Ruta Vrabec, 51, the counterweight named on the paper Neris Kade confessed to in Ch 97. Tomek, 19, her son, who is named once and enters nothing. Neris Kade, who is already up there and is not glad to see him.
**What happens:** Of thirty-nine lots sown on the second terrace, nine have failed, and the third terrace has stopped reporting because there is nobody left to report it. The sowing went in at the wrong time of the year and the terraces know it and cannot say how they know. Janko refuses to make the chapter about himself and says so at the seed store door.
**Resistance:** Marek arrives with Volume 02's finding — a class is not a person — and Kade tells him, in about nine words, that he has brought a good sentence to a bad morning. The terrace does not want a clerk on it.
**The change:** Marek does not say a class is not a person to the people it is about. He asks instead what the terrace was told, in the words the terrace used, and gets an answer, and cannot enter it anywhere.
**Seeds:** the class-named counterweight; the ninth line of the season works and a seed store with no keyholder; Janko's winter; the Hartlow room with the notice on its door.
**Do not:** resolve the Hartlow paper. **Do not have Kade warm.** **Do not have anybody ask Tomek anything.**

## 103 — Sixty-One Trays in the Dark

**Date:** the twelfth of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** the Elmsworth seed house shed, Rainmarket, at night, and the Gate shed in Tallow Ward in the afternoon.
**Engine:** sixty-one trays and a lamp that is not coming until the third month, and a bench moved into a room with a queue outside it.
**Who:** Hana Belic, 34, named for the first time, who draws the eleventh tray and has worked sixty-one trays twice a day in the dark since the sixth of the ninth month. Mirren Fisk, the keeper. Bessok Tan. Aldis Kern, from the step. Teodor Lask, the reader.
**What happens:** The Share moves the germination bench into the Gate shed, because the shed has a roof and a lamp in it and the seed shed has neither. The move costs the register its own working room on a queue morning, and the yard says so from the step, and the Share says the bench comes first.
**Resistance:** Kern will not let the register be a thing a cooperative hires. Fisk will not have her bench in a room with a queue outside it. Two correct positions and a shed eleven feet by nine.
**The change:** They share it, badly, on a rota, and **the rota has nobody's name at the head of it** — and the yard notices the shape of that and says nothing, because naming it would be naming a column.
**Seeds:** the lamp on the water office's list, the thirteenth line, supplied in the third month; the fifth line of the register's form, the hours, which is still empty; the yard's water still not coming down the Ropewalk standpipe on a queue morning.
**Do not:** pay the lamp thread off. **Do not add a line to the register's form.**

## 104 — Two Hundred and Eleven Sheets

**Date:** the fifteenth of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** a cellar under a chandler's yard in Bell Stair, reached down eleven steps.
**Engine:** a cellar, nine crates, and a widow who has kept the room since before the sheets were printed.
**Who:** Vesna Duss, 61, who holds the lease and has never read a form in her cellar. Sedge, who cut the register's form and is now shown the print run. Marek.
**What happens:** The Trust printed a run of seasonal advance forms in a year the office ledger calls *eleven hundred and something*, and about two hundred and eleven sheets are still in a cellar under a chandler's yard, and **nobody in the kingdom knows what was done with the rest.** Some went to season offices, which is lawful. Some went over the hills.
**Resistance:** Sedge wants to know whether the counterweight box is in the run. It is, in type, four hundred copies of it or more, and that is a stock form and not a crime. Vesna Duss wants to know who is going to pay for the water.
**The change:** Marek counts what is in the cellar and writes the number down, which is the first number he has written down in this volume, and finds one crate with a Crown March shipping mark on the lid in a hand that is not a clerk's — **and does not open it and does not say so, and the chapter says why.**
**Seeds:** the run; the cellar; the fourth box in type on a stock form; the mark on a lid.
**Do not:** connect the run to the founding page or to A-0. **Do not find a defect in the Trust's form.**

## 105 — The Man Who Came Down the River

**Date:** the nineteenth of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** a river stair and a granary barge, mid-morning.
**Engine:** a stair, a barge, a load, and an official counting other people's grain in order to be pleased with it.
**Who:** Sabin Rell, 54, Crown March granary official, twenty-nine years in the depots, on his first visit to Morrowfen. Marek, who was sent for because a man with no office is the only person who can be in a room without being on a list.
**What happens:** Rell has come because the basin is feeding three regions and the harvest is good and the granary board must certify the export. **He is right about all of it.** He is carrying an underwriting clause in a satchel and it is for the Red Uplands, and he is entirely comfortable with it, and the discomfort is the reader's and not his.
**Resistance:** Marek asks what the clause is for. Rell answers it plainly and does not conceal it and does not resent the question. He has been asked better questions by worse men.
**The change:** Rell says the four terraces are entered at the ten-year mean, and **the ten-year mean is not in the book he is carrying and is not in any book in Morrowfen.** He says it as a figure of speech. Marek writes it down and it is a second number and he does not know yet what it is for.
**Seeds:** Rell, the projection, the advance, the underwriting clause, the ten-year mean.

## 106 — Four Parts

**Date:** the twenty-first of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** two rooms on a lease above a chandler's shop, on the low-ground road.
**Engine:** a man at a table with four objects on it that are not marks, trying to describe a mark.
**Who:** Halvard Sunn, 44, Saltglass harbor notary, who has said out loud that he does not know what the marks are and has not known for nine years. Marek.
**What happens:** Marek asks him to say whether the mark on three instruments is his. **Sunn will not swear to it, and gives the reason in four parts — a month, a tide phase, a harbor seal, a translator — and the fourth part of the reason is the volume's hinge:** a mark that names a period instead of a day cannot be checked against a day by anybody who has not got a tide table, and there is one tide table in this basin and it is in front of him, and he is not going to swear to a thing that cannot be checked by a person who is not him.
**Resistance:** Marek wants a yes or a no and is refused in about nine sentences, correctly. Sunn will say what the mark is made of and what it is used for and nothing at all about whether it is his.
**The change:** Sunn says the one thing he will say, which is that a mark which names a month can be put on any day inside that month, and that this is a property of his calendar and not a trick, and that he has thought about it for nine years and has never been able to do anything about it. **The volume's question is asked out loud in this chapter in a plain sentence.**
**Seeds:** calendar lag; the translation; the fourth part; a notary with two rooms on a lease and a press that is not his.
**Do not:** explain the Saltglass Harbor Council. **Do not make Sunn a coward or a villain.** **No Ledger Sight panel in this chapter** — this is a practice chapter and the panel would wreck it.

## 107 — The Clause, Signed in Advance

**Date:** the twenty-fourth of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** a room over a chandler's shop with a satchel on the table.
**Engine:** a table, one sheet, a pen, and a man who is about to do his job correctly.
**Who:** Sabin Rell, Sabin's clerk Petrus Lenge if you want him early — **no: Lenge enters in Movement II and not in this batch.** Rell, Marek, and Ilse Ferrow, who is permitted to be present and says nothing for four pages.
**What happens:** The underwriting clause is one sheet. It names the four terraces, the season and the advance, and it assures a buyer that the district will deliver. It carries a seal and a Tide Reckoning mark **before anybody has written a word on it.** The counterweight on the sequence instrument it underwrites is named as a class on a form printed before the terraces had a bad year.
**Resistance:** There is no lie in the room. Every figure balances, the form is correct, and Rell is entitled to sign. **Marek's only available move is to ask what a projection is, and the answer is that a projection is what a book that has to balance in the year it is balancing contains instead of a number it does not have.**
**The change:** Rell signs at the fourth bell, correctly, and asks Marek, as a courtesy between two men who have been useful to each other, whether he intends to do anything. **And Marek says no, and means it, and it is the first time in this volume that he has a piece of information he is going to sit on.**
**Seeds:** the standing authority; the class counterweight; a courtesy that is also a warning.

## 108 — A Terrace at the Door

**Date:** the twenty-eighth of the tenth month, MR 413. **Place:** the Gate shed, Tallow Ward, fourth bell to seventh.
**Engine:** a room with a rule in it, and the first person in four months the rule was written for.
**Who:** Teodor Lask, nineteen, the reader. Sanne Droll, thirty-nine, the yard's second person, who is a subject and is not thanked. Neris Kade, who comes up the road with a terrace holder and is named at the door and gets a hand on nothing. Aldis Kern. Marek.
**What happens:** A woman from the second terrace comes down the road with an instrument in a cloth and wants it read, and it is an instrument **none of the people who brought it is the person behind**, and the counterweight on it is a class, so by the register's own first rule the line does not go in. It is the first time the rule has been asked to do this.
**Resistance:** The terrace has come a day to be told a thing they already know. Kade says the rule is right and is not a defence. Lask says it out loud, twice, at the ordinary speed.
**The change:** They go in as parts — the sum, the date, the receiving party — **and the fourth line, the counterweight, cannot be filled, and the reader writes nothing on the fourth line and does not explain what he is doing**, and the room reads the empty line aloud to about twenty people at the seventh bell. **The register's hole is now the size of a terrace.**
**Seeds:** the register cannot be left; the sixth line still empty; the levy trestle; the fifth line still empty.
**Do not:** fix the rule. **Do not make the register a remedy.** **Kade and Marek exchange at most four lines and it is not warm.**

## 109 — A Brother, a Slate, and a Kitchen

**Date:** the second of the second month, MR 414. **Place:** a kitchen on the north lane in the evening.
**Engine:** a kitchen, a table, a slate, and two brothers and a nine-year-old.
**Who:** Tomas Vale, thirty-four, on the span's off-season. Lio Vale, nine, with a slate. Marek, who has come to do something decent and does not do it.
**What happens:** Marek tries to keep the upland case off his brother, on the grounds that Tomas is a party to road work and has a wrong date on a Crown March countersignature and does not need a Crown March granary officer in his kitchen. **Tomas refuses, and refuses for the right reason, and says the sentence that the volume is built on:** that the whole of what has happened to their family is that men in rooms decided which of their losses was worth mentioning, and that his brother has been doing that for him for eleven years, including this morning, in a nicer coat.
**Resistance:** Lio asks the question. It is a question about why a man would go and see a person, and the answer is that it is nice, and the child is not satisfied and nobody helps her.
**The change:** Tomas goes up the road in the morning and asks for the work, and Marek goes with him because he is asked to, and not because he decided to.
**Seeds:** the case is Tomas's to carry; Lio's slate; the road work as a trade; the eleven years.

## 110 — A Cargo Going Down

**Date:** the fifth of the second month, MR 414. **Place:** the river stair, the second watch.
**Engine:** a boat leaving, a coil of paper on the deck, and the volume's question asked by a woman on a stair who wants to know about a boat.
**Who:** Petar Duss, loading. A woman on the stair who is nobody in particular and is the reader's ear. Marek, who is there because he was on the bridge on the fourth of the tenth month and it is the same job.
**What happens:** A barge goes down the river with a load of grain and a paper in a coil, and the paper carries a mark in a month nobody on the bank can name, and it is going to Saltglass and will not come back. Morrowfen is the best-fed it has been in nine years. The granary offices are content. **The woman on the stair asks a plain question — who is it that says a thing is all right — and gets a plain answer, and the plain answer is the volume's question and nobody in the chapter knows it is one.**
**Resistance:** Duss is not a villain, is under contract, has four children on the strength of a river, and does not care whose mark it is.
**The change:** Marek goes up the upland road in the morning with a sister's slate message and no authority whatsoever, and the volume's first movement closes on the fact that the harvest is abundant and the terraces are not and both of those are the same season.
**Seeds:** the paper leaving the basin; the question; the road in the morning.
