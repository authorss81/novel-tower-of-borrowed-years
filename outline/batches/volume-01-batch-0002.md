# Volume 01 Batch 0002 Outline — The Cost Has Two Names

Batch number: 0002
Chapter range: 11–20
Batch title: **The Cost Has Two Names**
Expected length: roughly 24,000–28,000 words across ten chapters. Ordinary chapters run 2,400–3,000 words. Chapters 11, 15, 19 and 20 may run longer, because they carry a cost, a night scene, a method, and a decision. Never pad to reach a number, and never cut a complete scene to hit one.

Batch goal: Make the reader pay attention to the fact that **two names live on the Tallow papers and only one of them is on the instrument the chain walks.** Show the ward's real gratitude and its real hatred at the same time, let the Trust certify the settlement as lawful anyway, let Hesper give the file a second and kinder false cause, put a boy with a hand through a benevolent fund's forms, make Marek learn what a hand-copy costs, let somebody offer to pay him, show the levy taking a school year off four children, give the volume its one method chapter, and end on the drafting of an aimed certification that will not be filed until Chapter 39.

Starting condition: MR 412, the **fifteenth of the rains, first light**. The peak was last night. Tallow Ward has water. Ondra Kelb is at the Morrowfen clinic with the years of his right hand gone. Marek Vale is a third-grade clerk who suspended a settlement without authority, told nobody, and then told everybody he could not stop himself from telling. The ward's copy of the levy is in a clerk's binding on the duty clerk's desk, in Kelb's longhand, with page three in Marek's hand and Kelb's name as copyist. The holding notice is still on the posting board with *no office of record* on the line. The hearing is **the fifteenth at the eighth bell, the Trust's third floor, Renata Vole presiding**, and the ward's copy and the file's copy are to be compared in the room.

Starting relationships: Marek and Kelb are strangers who spent a night together; Marek and Hesper are one confession past the point where either of them can pretend; Marek and Neris have met once, in a lane, and have had exactly one conversation; Marek has never been in Kelb's home; Pella Rusk has never spoken to Marek; Aldis Kern is not grateful.

Batch shape: **Beginning** (11–12) → **escalation** (13–14) → **midpoint** (15) → **escalation and choice** (16–18) → **climax** (19–20) → **aftermath and hook** (close of 20).

Batch midpoint: Chapter 15. Marek copies page three by hand a second time and discovers the batch's first hard limit: **a second document has no standing in a room.** The hand-copy is perfect, the figures are right, the hand is the levy office's, and it is worth nothing, because the Trust's founding order forbids any clerk to copy the sealed page and a document produced in breach of a standing order is a document nobody in the room may produce. The batch's opening belief — *I have the evidence now, and the evidence is the truth* — breaks against a procedure, not a person. Nim Rook becomes his runner and states a price that is not money.

Batch climax: Chapter 20. Hesper Rann asks Marek to witness a recount of the counter-desk totals, and writes the cause line so that the defect belongs to the hands that added the column. It is the first time in the volume that a man with one good lie chooses a person on purpose. Marek understands exactly what is being made, and is not yet able to stop it, and the reader is left holding a document that will not be filed for eighteen more chapters.

Aftermath: the tail of Chapter 20, the night of the twenty-first. The recount sits in a drawer in the supervisor's room. Hesper says something kind. The ward's arithmetic is in four hands now — the file's, the ward's, a fund's and an examiner's — and none of them agree. And the year scar, which has been arriving quietly since Chapter 11, has arrived.

**Locked instruments (carried from `outline/volume-01.md`, do not blur):** Hesper signed the ward settlement entry on the ninth day; **Kelb countersigned that entry the same afternoon in pencil**; **Marek signed the separate one-page clerk's certificate of posting on the eleventh day** and wrote *Yes* on the party query. **The chain follows the instrument, not the folder.** Marek's name is never where the collection lands. He surrenders the posting certificate in Chapter 44 and it is the Chamber's hold over him in Chapter 32.

**Locked levy roll:** eleven named households; the posting names the first four; **the Vale household is seventh of the eleven**. The posting names four *households*, not four yard lines, and the entry's four clauses and the posting's four pages are not the same four.

**Locked page three:** the labour-pledge schedule, eleven lines, four columns — household, days, surety, witness. **Eleven sureties named, all eleven witness cells blank.** It was never given to the ward, whose copy runs one, two, four, five. Marek's unofficial copy went into the **hearing bundle** as page three, as **Marek's** work with **Kelb's** name as copyist on the record, and into nothing else.

Required character changes:

- **Marek:** from a man who has paid to a man who has been paid for. Chapter 16 is the hinge. He begins the batch certain that the cost was extracted from him and that naming it in public is the only honest use of it. He ends it having discovered that his own private copy cannot be produced, that Hesper knows he is holding something, and that a certification is being drafted with a boy's name written into its cause line. He does not become a different man. He becomes a man with a second decision to make and no more certainty than the first time.
- **Ondra Kelb:** from the boy on the Chair step to a person with a household, a mother, a sister learning to write, and a claim with a form. Chapter 14 is his chapter and Marek is not in it for the first half. He is lucid, courteous, and not written to forgive anyone quickly. He is **not at the fifteenth hearing.**
- **Neris Kade:** from the woman in the corridor to a working professional with a method of her own. Chapter 19 is the first afternoon the two of them do work together, badly and then well, and it ends in an argument neither of them wins. No warmth. No physical affection. No instant trust.
- **Hesper Rann:** from a man who shaped a file to a man who is astonished that his arithmetic has a witness, and who defends the file by inventing a second culprit (a contractor's meter). He is human, tired, and useful to people. He becomes a monster in Chapter 20 and the chapter must not announce it.
- **Aldis Kern:** from a man who will not thank anyone to a man whose ward has to be in a room at the eighth bell with its bill and its gratitude in the same mouth.
- **Pella Rusk:** arrives in Chapter 18 with a roll and a reason to be angry that is hers alone, and is not yet told that her name is in a surety cell. Marek is asked and does not answer. That is the chapter's last beat and it belongs to him, not to her.
- **Tomas Vale:** refuses help again in Chapter 17, in public, in the voice of a man who has already decided what he is.

Required power changes:

- **No second hold.** The standing hold is spent. Marek cannot take it again and must not try, and no one may suggest that a second one is available.
- **Ledger Sight is optional in this batch and should be used zero times.** If a scene genuinely fails without it, the single licensed panel is in **Chapter 16**, when Hesper puts the ward file in front of Marek and the Sight confirms a column that is entirely correct. Sparse ink, plain language, no percentages, no verdicts, no predictions. Suggested line, which may be reworded but must keep the content: *The totals are the totals. The entry is not the totals.*
- **The limit this batch must teach:** a copy made in breach of a standing order is not evidence, it is a second document, and the difference is procedural, not moral. Chapter 15 is where this lands.
- **Marek's year scar** from the Chapter 8 hold surfaces **late, quietly, and not as a spectacle.** It is not a wound. It is a small administrative fact about his own body that he notices in Chapter 11 in somebody else's language and does not understand until Chapter 12. Sera told him it would not be dramatic and she is right. Nothing above Boundary Hold in this batch.

Required world reveals, all of them small and all of them institutional:

- **What a counter-signature is for.** A third-grade countersignature does not verify a document. It certifies that the document was *before the desk* and that the clerk who countersigned it looked at it. That is why Kelb is the chain's last name and Marek is not, and it is said once, in procedure, in Chapter 13.
- **The Counter-Clerks' Fund**, the mutual the counter-desk clerks keep, which pays a dead clerk's household and which is the only thing in the building that turns an injury into a claim. Named here: the **Benevolent Fund of the Counter-Clerks of the Chamber of Accounts**, and its secretary **Zorka Vendl**, who has kept it for nineteen years and whose entire profession is turning a person into a case number. New name, Volume 01, no further owner required.
- **Kelb's household:** his mother **Milena Kelb**, 43, who takes in washing on Sill Row's next street, and a sister of eleven learning to write at the ward school. The Fund will pay for the clinic and will not pay for the years.
- **The ward school is paid out of the district account**, and the district account is the first thing that goes when a ward is overdrawn, because a school year is the cheapest line in it and the easiest to call a saving. This is the mechanism behind Chapter 18 and it is arithmetic, not malice.
- **A levy takes a school year** in the only way it can: by drawing days at the ordinary wage out of an account the school is paid from. It does not take anybody's time at a desk. State it once, plainly, in Chapter 18.
- **Nobody holds the fund that pays for a held settlement's copy**, so the work is done for nothing by whoever is nearest. This is the whole of Nim Rook's grievance and the whole of Chapter 15's economics.

Panel budget: **zero is the target.** One panel is licensed, in Chapter 16 only. Chapters 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19 and 20 carry no panel. If a chapter ends up wanting a panel, cut the paragraph that wanted it.

- End the batch on a completed cost and an earned hook, not an interruption mid-action.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0011 — What the Years Cost

POV: Marek.

Location/time: The Morrowfen clinic and the lane behind it, the **fifteenth of the rains**, from first light to the eighth bell.

Immediate goal: Sit with what he did, and find out from a physician's mouth whether the hold can be undone, reduced, or paid back out of his own account in some way the forms recognise.

Resistance: He arrives with a question shaped like an administrative question and it keeps turning into a physical one. **Kelb's mother is in the waiting room and has been there since the small hours**, and she is not hostile, which is worse, and she has written down four facts in the order she wants them said. The clinic is full of frightened people from last night, and Sera Holt is running it on a bench and a lamp, and the answer to Marek's question is that there is no form.

Information revealed: In the body, not in a lecture. A hand that is warm, dry, perfectly still and can move a cup at sixty. That the years came out of the middle of a life and not off the end. That the tower's own vocabulary is *surety* and *pledge* and *settlement*, and none of those words has anything to say about a nineteen-year-old who countersigned a total. And, small and quiet and at the end: **Marek's own year scar begins to be visible** — not a wound, a mark that is not the shape of anything that happens to a man by accident, in the same place and at the same four-notch intervals as the mark on his forearm that Lio asked about in Chapter 5. He notices it in a physician's language before he notices it as his own.

Action or decision: He gives Milena Kelb the four facts in the order she wrote them, including his own name in the third position, because Kelb asked him for that order on a stair six hours ago and he is going to honour it with the only person who matters. He does **not** offer to pay, because she tells him what the Fund pays and what it does not, and it is not his to pay.

Emotional change: The batch opens on consequence rather than argument. He discovers that the cost he has been carrying as guilt is the *smallest* of the three costs in the room, and that the largest one belongs to a woman with a list.

Power or resource change: No gain. He spends the whole morning and gets nothing he can file. The one thing he takes away is a piece of vocabulary he does not yet understand: *the fine use of a hand.* And a scar he cannot explain.

Continuity fact: The scar is not a wound and not a disability. Kelb will be able to work; he will not be able to do the thing he trained for. The Fund pays the clinic. Nobody in this chapter knows about a second certification because none exists.

Ending type: cost, then a decision already taken by somebody else.

Next-chapter pull: The ward has been outside the clinic door since before light, and half of them are there to say thank you and the other half are there because a boy in a bed works at the counter they all queue at.

### Chapter 0012 — The Ward at Dawn

POV: Marek.

Location/time: Tallow Ward — the four yards, the school house on Sill Row, the queue outside the clinic lane — the **fifteenth**, the eighth bell to midday.

Immediate goal: Get the ward into the hearing room at the eighth bell with its roll, its copy and its grievance, and stop the queue at the clinic door from becoming a second emergency.

Resistance: **The ward is two things at once and will not be one thing.** Aldis Kern has a list of what the hold bought and a list of what the hold did not buy, and he reads the second list out loud in a yard because he has decided the ward should be owed the truth as well as the water. Three or four people in the queue are not there to thank anybody; they are there because the clerks have already been telling the ward that a junior clerk put a bad column on a water bill, and the ward has decided to believe it, because a boy is easier to be angry at than a building. **Nadja Ostrow** is one of the eleven named households and the only one of them who comes to the clinic door, and she comes for her own account, not for the ward's.

Information revealed: The hold bought six bells and no water. The two days bought in Chapter 7 are still running. The peak-day certification was never signed, so the ward's lawful requirement for last night is a quantity in no document, and the ward has to say out loud that it drank water it had no right to. A person in the queue uses the phrase *careless with the totals* and Marek has to decide what to do with it. **Marek's own scar is now plainly visible to somebody who reads year scars for a living** — Neris, passing through, one sentence, no sympathy, no warmth.

Action or decision: He gives the ward the second list, not the first. He tells the ward, in a yard, in front of Kern and four hundred people, that the water came off a valve a Warden broke seven days against a sealed order, and that the ward owes nobody for the hold and owes that man a debt the ward has not yet agreed to name. He does not defend the clerks. He does not blame the boy.

Emotional change: The chapter where gratitude and hatred are both real and he stops trying to make them one thing. He loses the ward's trust in a room and keeps the ward's water, which is the trade the whole volume is about.

Power or resource change: No gain, and one loss: the ward now knows a clerk was involved. That knowledge is what Chapter 20 needs, and he has given it away for nothing.

Continuity fact: Ostrow is one of the eleven and speaks for her own household. Rezek's fuel-yard household is among the first four. The Vale household is seventh and Marek does not say so in this chapter and nobody asks him here. The peak-day certification is still unsigned.

Ending type: decision, with a public cost.

Next-chapter pull: At the eighth bell the Trust's third floor will compare the ward's copy with the file's copy, and one of those copies has a page the other does not.

### Chapter 0013 — The Totals Are the Totals

POV: Marek.

Location/time: The Trust's third floor, Stillstep Island, the **fifteenth**, the eighth bell into the afternoon. Vole presiding.

Immediate goal: Get page three into the record of a proceeding, so that the hearing has the ward's own document and not only the file's.

Resistance: **The hearing is not a trial and everybody in the room knows it except the ward.** Vole treats an appeal as a scheduling problem and this proceeding as a comparison of two copies. The ward's copy runs one, two, four, five. The file's copy has all five and a sixth page three that no party was ever given notice of, eleven sureties, and eleven blank witness cells. Vole's ruling is that a schedule not disclosed to the parties is a schedule the parties cannot rely on, which makes it useless to the ward, and she is right on the procedure and it does the ward no good at all. **Kelb is not in the room.** And when the file's cause is read, it is not Hesper's reserve recital: it is an operator's failure — a Crown Terrace irrigation contractor's strainer and bypass, on an account the ward never drank from.

Information revealed: **What a countersignature is for**, said once in procedure and not argued: a third-grade countersignature certifies that the document was before the desk and that the clerk looked at it. It does not verify the document. That is the whole of why the chain reaches Kelb and not Marek, and it is said by an officer of the hearing, not by Marek. Also: the file acquires **a second, kinder, false cause**, and the ward's real answer — the meter, the strainer, the account — turns out to be *load-bearing for the lie*, because the file now needs the contractor to be at fault.

Action or decision: Marek does not argue the point that would win. He makes a smaller procedural move — he asks that the file's copy and the ward's copy both be entered as the same document, and that the difference between them be recorded as a difference, not resolved. Vole grants it because it costs her nothing and it is good practice. He is handed, in return, the finding he did not ask for: **the settlement is certified as lawful**, and the ward's lawful requirement for the peak stands at the standing schedule.

Emotional change: The first time he wins anything in this volume and understands what it cost. He got the difference onto a record, and the record now says the ward's lawful water was 160 hundreds of pints and the ward drank three times that.

Power or resource change: No power change. He acquires a fact that is worse for the ward than the fraud: the ward is **over its lawful schedule on the face of the record**, which is what will make the levy look deserved in every room that is not this one.

Continuity fact: Vole certifies the settlement lawful on the fifteenth. The file's stated cause is a contractor's maintenance failure on account 4-Crown. Kelb is absent and has given no account. Page three is entered in the proceeding as Marek's copy with Kelb's name as copyist and neither the ward nor the Trust has asked where it came from.

Ending type: victory that reads as a defeat.

Next-chapter pull: A boy is in a bed with a hand, and the only institution in the city that will do anything about it is a mutual of counter-clerks, and it will do it on a form.

### Chapter 0014 — A Number of the Boy's

POV: Marek, for the second half only.

Location/time: A laundry on the next street from Sill Row, the Sill Row school house, and the Counter-Clerks' Fund's one room over a chandler's shop, the **sixteenth**, from the second bell to dusk. **Kelb's chapter, not Marek's, for the first two-thirds.**

Immediate goal: Find out what actually happens to a counter-clerk whose name is the last name on a chain, and whether anybody in the building has to answer for it.

Resistance: The Fund pays out on a schedule and the schedule has no line for this. **Zorka Vendl** has kept the Fund for nineteen years and turns people into case numbers with a courtesy that is not unkind and is not warm, and she is not the villain of the scene; she is the instrument. Milena Kelb does not want money; she wants a record of what happened to her son written down in words that will still be true in a year, and the Fund's form has a box for *injury sustained in the course of duty* and no box for what actually happened. The junior clerks at the counter-desk want to know, in detail, whether this will happen to them, and Marek — who knows exactly, and is the only person in the room who knows exactly — finds that answering them is not available to him, because the answer is *yes, and I did it, and you will not believe me and I cannot prove it.*

Information revealed: The counter-desks have a mutual, a room, a secretary, a book, and a fund that has run a deficit every year since the flood years. Eleven other counter-clerks in the hall learn, in a week, what a countersignature is. The Fund will pay for the clinic and will not pay for the years, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole institution. Kelb's sister of eleven is at the ward school, and the ward school is the thing that Chapter 18 takes away.

Action or decision: Marek does the one thing available to him, which is to put the case on the Fund's book under the correct heading with the correct instrument named — *the ward settlement entry, countersigned the ninth day* — so that for the first time the paper has three clerks' names on it in one sentence instead of two. He does not offer money, does not offer help with the hand, and does not explain the mechanism, because nobody in the room is capable of receiving it and one of them is the Fund's secretary and one of them is a mother.

Emotional change: He is the reason a boy is on a list, and the list will still be here after he is forgiven or not. The chapter where the system is shown doing the one thing it knows how to do, competently, to the wrong thing.

Power or resource change: No gain. He acquires a debt of a kind he has no procedure for: eleven junior clerks now know his name in connection with the ward file, and one of them is going to be in the room in Chapter 20.

Continuity fact: **Zorka Vendl**, 61, Fund secretary, nineteen years. **Milena Kelb**, 43, laundress. Kelb's sister is eleven and at the ward school. The Fund's book now carries the ward settlement entry and Kelb's countersignature in the same line.

Ending type: cost, institutional and quiet.

Next-chapter pull: To get any of this into a room at all he has to write it out again, and this time by hand, and the only person awake at night who will carry it up a stair is a nineteen-year-old who wants paying.

### Chapter 0015 — The Second Document (Batch Midpoint)

POV: Marek.

Location/time: The Tally Halls copy room and the Tally Stair, the **sixteenth**, from the last of the light to the fourth bell of the night watch.

Immediate goal: Produce a clean, citable, hand-exact copy of page three that he can put in front of somebody tomorrow, and know where it has to live so it will still exist when the hearing needs it.

Resistance: A Tower hand-copy has to carry the four words at the foot that say what it is a copy of, and the Tower will not make a copy of a suspended settlement because a held settlement is not a state that was ever made, and a document produced in breach of a standing order of the Yearspire Trust is a document that **nobody in the room is permitted to produce.** He has done the work perfectly. It is worth nothing. The second document is a second document. And the labour of it is not free: a full page in the levy office's hand, at the pace a copy has to be written, takes two hours, and by the fourth bell his hand is going and there is a wet pain in the inside of his elbow that is not a headache and is not the nose.

Information revealed: **The copy is not the truth and it is not a lie; it is a third thing, and the building has a name for it.** A hand-copy of a sealed page that may not be copied is evidence nobody may give. The cost of the batch's first hoard, discovered at the moment he thought he had made it usable. And: the arithmetic of a night copy in this weather is that a nineteen-year-old girl carried the ward's levy up four storeys in the small hours for no pay at all, because there is no office that pays for the copy of a settlement that does not exist, and that is Nim Rook's entire subject.

Action or decision: He keeps the copy and he does not file it, because filing it would make it an exhibit and because producing it would break an order he is already breaking by having made it. He writes on the wrapper, in pencil, the date and the fact that it exists and nothing else. Then he makes a decision with a person in it: **he takes Nim Rook on as a runner**, at night, for nothing, and she states her price.

Emotional change: The batch's belief breaks. He has spent six days believing that holding the truth privately was the same as being able to use it, and he finds out that a procedure is stronger than a conviction and always has been. This is the first time in the volume he has been out-manoeuvred by a building rather than by a man.

Power or resource change: No power change and no growth. He gains a runner, a cost in his own hand, and the knowledge that his hoard is a liability. The price Nim states is not money and it is not the ward roll: she will carry anything, at any hour, for nothing, and in exchange he tells her the moment he finds out anything at all about **a name of hers**, and he says her sister's name out loud, once, in the copy room, because a name said in a room is in a room. He says it. It costs him nothing and it is the first thing he has done in this volume that is purely for somebody else.

Continuity fact: The unofficial page three exists in two copies in his own hand, both against the Trust's standing order, and is in neither the ward's hands nor the file's. **Nim Rook's sister is Ilen.** Nim's price is stated and paid. The ward roll in his coat is not touched in this chapter.

Ending type: reversal.

Next-chapter pull: A man who has kept clinics open for twenty-two years knows exactly what is in the Tallow file, and he knows that somebody has been reading it, and he is about to make Marek an offer.

### Chapter 0016 — Nineteen Years

POV: Marek.

Location/time: The supervisor's room, then the stair, the **seventeenth**, the second bell into the afternoon.

Immediate goal: Work out how much Hesper knows, and get through the conversation without lying in a way he will be caught in.

Resistance: Hesper is not subtle and he is not a liar in this scene. He says, without any pleasure, that somebody has been copying pages out of the Tallow file, that a suspended file cannot be copied by the tower, and that the only way a page of it can leave the building is a person. He does not threaten. He makes an offer, and the offer is genuinely good, and it is this: **the Vale settlement schedule is cleared.** Not the years. The road work — the supervised road work that would service Tomas's vigor loan after the fourteenth-day draw — will be written, named, scheduled and witnessed. The draw is not cancelled and the years already taken are not returned; what is offered is the schedule, which is the only thing anybody in this building can still give away. In exchange, the copy stays in a drawer.

Information revealed: Hesper is astonished that his private arithmetic has a witness in it. He has never met the person who is asking the questions. He thinks the person is **Neris Kade**, and he is wrong, and Marek does not correct him, and that is the first lie of the batch that costs something. Hesper also says, apparently without meaning to, that the ward's water was never the problem and that the ward's bill is the problem, and Marek hears it and cannot answer it. Optional, and only if it will not slow the chapter: the single licensed panel, as Hesper puts the ward file on the desk and the Sight confirms a column that is entirely correct.

Action or decision: **Marek does not accept and does not refuse in the room.** He asks for it in writing — one line, a date, a form number — because a verbal offer by a supervisor in a room with no second person in it is worth nothing and because he is, to his own horror, thinking like a clerk. Emotional change: The hinge of the batch. He discovers that the whole of what he has done is a thing two people can be reasonable about, and that the reason it cost Kelb his hand is that nobody in the conversation was talking about Kelb. He is not tempted by the road work. He is frightened of how much he wanted it.

Power or resource change: No power change. He gains a written offer he will not be able to use and cannot throw away, and he learns that Hesper's information is wrong in a specific and useful way.

Continuity fact: The offer is **the settlement schedule only** — the road work, not the years. It is not the same instrument as the ward levy and it is not Tomas's loan being forgiven. Hesper believes the questioner is Neris Kade.

Ending type: cost, and a decision postponed by one day.

Next-chapter pull: Whatever Marek does with the offer, he has to walk past the north road to get on with his life, and the north road is where his brother is.

### Chapter 0017 — The Vigor Bill

POV: Marek.

Location/time: The north road above Tallow, the Vale rooms, and a yard at the north end of the span, the **eighteenth**, first light to dusk.

Immediate goal: Get his brother to stop working, and then to accept help, in that order, which is the wrong order.

Resistance: **Tomas refuses in public, in front of the crew, using the same voice he uses for a specification.** He does not want the road work and he says why and the reason is not pride in the abstract: the road work comes with an independent witness and a named schedule, and a named schedule on a bridgewright's contract is a schedule that can be read aloud in a room, and he has been made legible his whole life. Meanwhile the **fourteenth-day draw has posted and the vigor has been working since Chapter 5**, and the bill for it is real: a north-road crewman, Ferun's brother-in-law or a named hand — use a new working name, **Bohum Ostrek**, 33 — has taken borrowed vigor on the same terms and his hands have gone in the middle of a lift, and the same clause is working exactly as it is written on a second person in the same crew. Nobody has done anything wrong. That is the horror.

Information revealed: The vigor form is not a fraud and does not become one because it is cruel. Two men in one crew have signed the same clause, one of them is Marek's brother, and the second one is now the reason the clause is a fact. The draw was lawful, the vigor is working, and the years are gone; nobody is going to be told otherwise. ### Chapter 0020 — A Recount of the Totals (Batch Climax)
