# Volume 04 Outline — The City of Witnesses

Volume number: 4
Chapter range: 151–200
Civic period: **MR 415–416**. The sixth through ninth months of MR 415, then MR 416.
Batch map: 0001 (151–160), 0002 (161–170), 0003 (171–180), 0004 (181–190), 0005 (191–200)

**The calendar this volume relies on, stated once so that a writer does not have to re-derive it.** The Morrowfen year turns on the last night of the **ninth month** and there is no bell for it and no office that keeps a calendar anybody in a lane has ever been shown. **The tenth month is the first month of the new year.** A reckoning year therefore runs tenth, first, second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, and a month is named by its ordinal and not by its season, which is why the ninth month is also the harvest month and why the tenth month is also a year's first. **Consequence for this volume: MR 415 ends on the last night of its ninth month, and the month after it is the first month of MR 416.** Both names for it are in use and there is a printed form for either. **No chapter may date a month for its length, may count days backwards across a month boundary, or may put the tenth month and the first month in one sentence as though they were two different months.** Bells are first to sixth, the sixth when the copy room shuts, the seventh in the evening, the eighth struck in the morning. **There is no ninth bell.**

Central pressure: **false-witness cases flood the Witness Rooms, because a form that was abolished in print is still the form in use, and a record loan sealed in the third month of MR 415 stopped anybody asking whether the paper was on it.** The plant is Volume 03's own, carried in its own hand to the page: *if a mark that names a month can be put on any day inside it, then a mark that names a person can be put on any day inside that person's life, and there is a form in this kingdom for that and it is older than the Trust.* That form is **the lifelong attestation**: one sheet, four ruled lines, no boxes, of which the third is printed and the fourth takes a month and not a day. **It does not name the paper.** It names a *line* — a ditch, a roof, a stall, a boundary — and a promise to speak to **any paper touching that line while the signer lives.** A witness on an ordinary sheet is a witness *to that sheet.* A witness on an attestation is a witness to everything on that line, for ever, including papers that did not exist when the signer put their name down.

**The volume's question, stated and not answered:** *why does a record loan make a coerced signature look valid?* **The volume's answer, partial and institutional:** because a signature is not a fact about a moment. A signature is a name against a thing, and the thing on this sheet is a line and not a paper, and **there is no box on the form for whether the signer was free.** A coerced signature is a true signature. The record does not test how it was obtained; it tests whether the name is there, and the name is there, and the name is the same name.

**A second answer the volume finds and does not like: the form is not the crime. The form was shortened.** The original printing carried **five** lines and the fifth was *the paper.* Nineteen years ago the fifth line was cut, in Bell Stair, to save a half-sheet on every copy, and the effect of cutting it is that every attestation ever signed since is good against every paper on that line. **No chapter of this volume may find a villain in the printing, and none exists: the person who cut it was twenty-four, did it because it was cheaper, and has thought about it every working day since.**

Starting state: The register of risk is live in a shed in Tallow Ward, four hours a day on the days the rota says, a levy of a hundred and forty a season in a notice in Sedge's hand, a tin with **no total and never a total**, a wall list with about forty lines and a column at the end of the fourth with **nothing at the head of it**, a fifth line for the hours that is empty, and a sixth that is empty for ever. **Six places in this basin have a column and nothing at the head of it and the count is closed and nothing may be added to it.** About nine people stopped coming in the first year of the register and about nine more stopped in the fourth month of MR 415, and the two lots cannot be told apart. **The tenth month of MR 415, the second month of MR 415 and the third month of MR 415 have all gone by and none of them is a month this volume can reopen.** On the low ground there is a district rate on about nine names, entered on the second of the fourth month of MR 415, and one of the nine is Petar Duss. A registry rule in the hills says a mark no officer of a district can read is not a witness, and it is in type in a drawer nobody in this basin will ever be shown, and **it is a rule about marks and it does nothing about a person.** The advance on the four terraces is eleven thousand and some hundreds of pounds and it is in the ground and it is not coming back and there is no figure for it in any mouth. The first terrace is first on a list of four for no reason and the woman who owns that ground has asked for all four facts to be said out loud in a row so that nobody can make a story of her. **There is no second telling: the eleven weeks ran out somewhere in the winter and no form in this kingdom on which a person may be told that eleven weeks have run out.** A sheet of paper is on a barge to Saltglass in a coil with a mark on it in a month nobody on the bank can name. A second letter to the harbour is paid, stamped and posted. **A door and not a rescue, and it may never be a rescue.**

### New Volume 04 local terms (locked)

- **the lifelong attestation** — one sheet, four ruled lines, no boxes. The name; the **line**; a printed third line reading *I will speak to any paper touching this line while I live*; and a date that is **a month and not a day.** **It does not name a paper.** The print was stopped and the year it was stopped is not named anywhere in this novel and may not be named.
- **the line** — what an attestation names: a ditch, a roof, a stall, a boundary, a bin, a boat. **A line is not a paper and there is no form in this kingdom in which a line is a paper.**
- **a challenge** — a request that a sheet be looked at before it is sealed. A challenge is free and anybody may bring one and there is no form for refusing one.
- **spent** — a person whose attestation has been produced once. The record says they have already spoken, and the record does not permit a second use, and a person cannot be re-witnessed because they cannot be asked again. **A spent witness comes back when the paper is set aside, and the paper is not going to be set aside this volume.**
- **the fifth line** — *the paper* — on the original printing. Cut nineteen years ago. **The whole of the volume's case is in one line of type that is not on the form any more.**
- **a counter-signature** — the act of a clerk putting a name against a paper he has read. **It is the institution's act. When a chain is false-witnessed it walks to the living clerk who countersigned in good faith.**

### Locked instruments in the Volume 04 file

The defect is that the fourth line is a month and the fifth line is not there.**
- **The record loan of the third month of MR 415.** Sealed at a regional records office to stop a district account being struck out, on a standing authority, by an office, correctly. **Its price is entered as fourteen attestations and its scope names a line of district accounts.** **It is not a fraud and no chapter may find one.** Its present effect is real and it worked: the account was not struck, and a rate that was going to come on the low ground in the fifth month did not come. **Its later cost falls on people who were not asked and cannot be named at the time.** The chain is false-witnessed by construction, because the price is a class of attestations and a class cannot consent, and the record loan is the only form in this kingdom whose price may be entered as a class of people's willingness.
- **The paired field sheet.** Cut in the copy room in Bell Stair in the seventh month of MR 415, ruled in two columns with **two names at the head of them** — the first headed column in three volumes of this manuscript. The rule is that neither may write in the other's column. **A sheet with two names on it is a sheet a man in a good coat can produce at either of them in nine months, and they do it anyway, and the cost is named out loud in the chapter that cuts it.**

Midpoint reversal (Chapters 155–156, with the setup beginning at 151): Marek enters this volume looking for a forger, because a coerced signature is the thing he can name and because a forger can be shown a hand. **The midpoint turns it around in two parts. There is no forger.** Every one of the nine people whose attestations have been produced in this basin signed something themselves, in type, at a counter, and every one of them signed about something else. The counters are not lying and the forms are not forged and the printers did nothing wrong, and the first half of the turn is that **a signature is not a fact about a moment, so coercion leaves no mark on the paper, because there is no box for it.** The second half is a discovery in a print shop drawer: **the original printing has five lines and the current one has four, and the fifth line is the paper.** And the discovery that makes it a midpoint rather than an answer is that **the cutting is nineteen years old and was done to save a half-sheet, and there is nobody in this basin to be angry with, and the fifth line cannot be found by anybody except a printer who keeps one of everything.**

Concrete resolution (Chapters 191–200):

- **A witness rotation exists** — independent, in rooms with no employer, no surety and no certifying institution, and **it is a practice and not a statute**, and the first three of its rooms are in places that are not on any map a clerk in Bell Stair can look at.
