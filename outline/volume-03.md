# Volume 03 Outline — The Borrowed Harvest

Volume number: 3
Chapter range: 101–150
Civic period: **MR 414–415**, the tenth month of MR 413, and the seed month, rains month, growth month, harvest month and ninth month of MR 414
Batch map: 0001 (101–110), 0002 (111–120), 0003 (121–130), 0004 (131–140), 0005 (141–150)

Central pressure: **A sequence loan sealed in MR 412 favoured the basin's season, and the Red Uplands' four terraces are carrying the counterweight. The counterweight is named as a class — *the seed-keepers of the four terraces after this season* — and a class is not a person, so nobody was asked.** The present consequence is the best harvest in nine years: the low ground's rate falls by about a third in nine days, granaries fill, barges go down the river, and Morrowfen looks like a city that has been saved. Underneath it, four terraces of the Red Uplands are sowing at the wrong time of year, a third of their lots are sand, and a Crown March granary office has **advanced money against a projection of their future yield** and sold the paper on. **The security on that paper is a future famine being used as collateral in the only direction available: the buyer is assured that the district will be able to pay later, because later there will be grain.** A granary official signs the underwriting clause. The signature on it is a Tide Reckoning mark, and nobody in Morrowfen can read one, and the same hand is on a Morrowfen bundle and on a board over a shed door and on a haulier's paper from the uplands.

**The volume's question, stated and not answered:** *why are signatures being copied across unrelated contracts?* **The volume's answer, partial and institutional:** a mark that names a **month** instead of a **day** cannot be checked against a day without a tide table, so it is the cheapest mark in this basin to put on a sheet and the most expensive one to refuse. A standing authority that certifies a *projection* has nothing to certify except a number, and a number can be carried forward from one season to the next in a hand that is nobody's.

Starting state: The tower is trusted because it works. The register of risk is **live in a shed in Tallow Ward**, six weeks old in the tenth month of MR 413 and about four months old by the middle of this volume, with four hours a day, a levy of one hundred and forty a season, a cap, a fifth line for the hours that is empty because the hours are nobody's paper, a sixth line that is empty for ever because there is no box in the kingdom for the days, and a column with nothing at the head of it that nineteen hands declined to name and a yard voted in four minutes not to fill and not to rule out. **The register has no wages in it and no way to record what it costs the people who use it, and the first cost to be named is the yard's water, which does not come down the Ropewalk standpipe on a queue morning.** Ovid Merrow has lost a monopoly and kept the money and is not going to count the people in his own book. **A district account is held by nobody.** A cut order of the ninth month is drafted and not entered. The lamp in Rainmarket is out until the third month. And a school off the north lane is the fourteenth and last line on a list of fourteen that nobody is sending anybody against.

### New Volume 03 local terms (locked)

- **the projection** — a granary's forward estimate of a district's yield, entered in a ledger in the year before the yield. It is a number, not a measurement, and it is entered in advance, and that is the ordinary practice of a book that has to balance.
- **the advance** — money paid out early against a projection. It comes back as a set-off when the delivery happens, and if the delivery does not happen there is no form in this kingdom for taking it back.
- **the underwriting clause** — the clause in which an office assures a buyer that a district will be able to deliver later. It is standard, it is lawful, it is printed, and it costs nothing until the season disagrees with the book.
- **the season works** — the Red Uplands' own list of the works and jobs of a season, made by the terraces and not by any office. **It has nine lines. The ninth is a seed store and it has no keyholder, because the man who held the key has been told in MR 413 that he is going to die this winter.**
- **a Tide Reckoning mark** — four parts and no day: **a month, a tide phase, a harbor seal, and a translator.** It is the only mark in this basin that names a period rather than a point, and a period can be put on any day inside it, and checking it requires a tide table.
- **calendar lag** — the interval between a local contract date and its recognised Morrowfen Reckoning date. A Tide Reckoning mark that has not been translated sits in lag and cannot be silently folded into an MR year. **Inherited from Volume 02 and untouched in Movement I.**

### Locked instruments in the Volume 03 file

- **The upland sequence instrument.** Sealed in MR 412 by a Crown March office on behalf of four terrace holdings, in eleven names. Five parts present. Need: a season in the basin. Pledge: **the seed-keepers of the four terraces after this season.** Scope: the sowing months. Witness: two, one of them a Crown March man who was not paid by the terraces and one of them a terrace holder who signed for a household that had already left. Settlement: out of the harvest. **It is well made. No chapter of this volume may find a defect in it, exactly as no chapter of Volume 02 found a defect in the Share's instrument.** Its counterweight is a class, and the number of persons inside the class is not stated and cannot be, **and there is no form in this kingdom in which anybody may be given that number** — which is Volume 02's finding, restated in a different mouth, and the reader is meant to hear it twice.
- **The Crown March granary underwriting clause.** One sheet. It names the four terraces, the season, and the advance, and it assures a buyer that the district will deliver. **It is signed in advance of the season, by an office, on a standing authority, and the sheet carries a seal and a Tide Reckoning mark before anybody has written a word on it.** The clause is not a fraud. **It is the correct form, correctly filled in, on a number nobody in the basin can check.**
- **The advance.** **Eleven thousand and some hundreds of pounds.** The exact figure is in a Crown March ledger and **the figure may not be fixed in this volume, by any chapter, in any mouth** — it stays *eleven thousand and some hundreds* the way forty-three and some months stays forty-three and some months. It is already in the four terraces and it is the volume's only money.
- **The register's form.** One sheet, six ruled lines, no boxes, headed with a tank and a date, cut in the copy room by Sedge. **Unchanged. A later chapter may not add a line to it.**
- **The form cut in this volume for a rate on a member's ground.** Five ruled lines and no boxes, cut in the same copy room by the same man, in the harvest month of MR 414, and **its first hole is that there is no box for the ground**, because a rate is charged to the ground and the ground is not a person, and the whole of the volume's last third happens in that hole. **Sedge says on the page, again, that a form has to be cut by a man who does not know whether it is right.**

Midpoint reversal (Chapters 105–106, with the setup beginning at 101): Marek enters this volume looking for a false instrument, because that is what he found the last two times and because a false term is contestable. **The midpoint turns it around in two parts. There is no false instrument.** Every figure in the Crown March stack balances, the underwriting clause is the correct form correctly filled in by a man doing his job, the advance is real money already spent, and Ondra Kelb — counting twice with a left hand that is a nine-year-old's and is not his — finds that the four terraces' entry in the granary ledger is internally consistent and says so and is again ashamed of how much he is relieved. **And the fourth fact is the counterseal.** A Tide Reckoning mark names a month, a tide phase, a harbor seal and a translator. **It does not name a day.** So it cannot be falsified by moving it, it cannot be checked against a day by anybody without a tide table, and there is one tide table in this basin and it belongs to a man who lives in two rooms on a lease and has said out loud that he does not know what the marks are. The volume's question stops being *who forged this* and becomes **what is a signature, and what happens when the thing a signature is made of names a period instead of a person.**

Concrete resolution (Chapters 141–150):

- **The present harvest is secured** and the four terraces go into MR 415 with seed, labour and a pool of money that was never clawed back.

- **Marek ends the volume able to direct an effect and unable to direct it to the right person**, with a piece of somebody else's counterweight in his own yearline, and still a clerk with no office, and with the register still four hours a day and the column still bare.
- **Ovid Merrow is not in the volume's resolution and is not dissolved and not convicted and is not a party to anything in it.** He keeps his money. His shed on the low-ground road is open and is not better than the Gate shed and he says so.
