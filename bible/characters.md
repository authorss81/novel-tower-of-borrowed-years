# Character Bible

## 1. Marek Vale — the settlement clerk

**Age at opening:** 28
**Occupation:** third-grade settlement clerk, Chamber of Accounts, Bell Stair
**Home:** a narrow rented room above a lockkeeper's shop in Tallow Ward
**Public reputation:** precise, unremarkable, and careful with forms
**Private reputation:** someone who will stay late to correct an entry rather than admit that the rule is the problem

### Want, need, flaw, and fear

- **External want:** clear the debt attached to his older brother Tomas after Tomas takes a body loan to repair a flood-damaged bridge. Marek wants a clean ledger, a secure job, and a family that is not at the mercy of a creditor.
- **Internal need:** accept that he cannot protect people by controlling every variable. He must share authority, disclose uncertainty, and let other people choose a cost he would not have chosen.
- **Flaw:** Marek turns relationships into procedures. He postpones difficult conversations, hoards evidence, and assumes that a clean category is a morally clean choice.
- **Fear:** becoming the person who decides that someone else should pay so that the city can continue.
- **False belief:** if he can find the missing line, he can find a solution that costs no one their agency.

### Skills and limits

Marek is unusually good at spotting mismatched ink, altered dates, repeated witness phrasing, and physical inconsistencies in a record. He knows municipal procedure, can work long hours without complaint, and remembers the names of clerks who have been promoted or disgraced. He is not a strong fighter, a gifted battlefield spellcaster, or a natural public speaker. His early victories come from preparation, allies, and a willingness to accept an inconvenient answer.

Ledger Sight appears after a settlement accident in Volume 1. It gives him a narrow read of current entries, not prophecy. Prolonged use leaves him with headaches, tunnel vision, and a temporary gap in recent memory. He has to recover his own past through people and objects, which makes the power an argument against his favorite habit of hoarding control.

### Contradictions and habits

- He keeps a small ledger of promises made to his mother, even though he calls personal obligations “unofficial.”
- He is generous with food and stingy with compliments.
- He cannot eat a meal while a page is unfinished, even when the page is not urgent.
- He respects boldness in theory and distrusts it in people.
- He lies most convincingly by giving a technically accurate answer that omits the question he knows matters.

### Arc

Marek begins as a man who believes fairness can be achieved by being more competent than the people around him. He becomes a public auditor, then a field negotiator, then a builder of voluntary compacts, and finally an ordinary citizen who must live in the system he helped repair. His final growth is not a greater ability to read contracts. It is the willingness to act when no record can guarantee the result.

## 2. Neris Kade — the field examiner

**Age:** 30
**Occupation:** independent field examiner for the Rainmarket and upland season works
**Home:** a rented room above a seed shop, later a shared workroom in Hallow Ground with a small public seed counter
**Public reputation:** blunt, difficult, and very good at identifying a false harvest
**Personal goal:** make every settlement that affects a farm, orchard, clinic, or school visible to the people who live there

Neris has a practical sensitivity to **year scars**: places where a loan's future cost has altered growth, health, or work without leaving an obvious injury. She cannot read every ledger. She knows how to stay in a field for twelve hours, ask questions people do not want to answer, and make a public official explain a decision in language that does not hide behind procedure.

Neris distrusts Marek because clerks repeatedly turn her communities' losses into clean entries. She is not immune to bitterness. When a settlement falls on the wrong district, she wants someone punished immediately, even if punishment makes the next harvest harder. Marek's need to distinguish fraud from structural failure slowly teaches her that anger needs a strategy. His need for procedure slowly teaches him that a strategy without anger becomes a quiet surrender.

Neris's private history is a village in the Red Uplands that used a craft loan to preserve its seed houses. The loan saved the current harvest but ensured that the next generation would have no trained seed-keepers. She does not want to see “responsible” people turn a shared loss into someone else's bad luck.

### Relationship with Marek

The relationship is a slow burn built on friction, professional respect, and shared risk.

- They meet as opposing witnesses in Volume 1.
- They are forced to share field work in Volume 4.
- They exchange a personal truth during the Hollow North expedition in Volume 8.
- Marek withholds a record that could clear him of an old audit error but would expose Tomas's original claim and endanger Neris's district in Volume 12; the breach damages trust.
- In Volume 14, both stop trying to protect the other from the truth and choose equal partnership.
- In Volume 16, they sign as independent witnesses to the districts' provisional year commons, and again in Volume 18 for the Open Charter itself, never as a romantic contract.
- In the final volume, they choose a shared life without making either one a guarantee for the other.

There is no competing romantic relationship. Their first physical affection is late, earned, and awkward around people who knew them only as coworkers.

## 3. Tomas Vale — the bridgewright

**Age:** 34
**Role:** Marek's older brother, a bridgewright and local contractor
**Need:** stop treating his own body as a repair budget
**Flaw:** pride makes him accept a dangerous loan before asking for help

Tomas takes a vigor loan after a flood wrecks the bridge over the family workshop. He wants his daughter Lio to have a future that does not begin with debt. He resents Marek's clerkship because he sees the ledger as the thing that made the danger legible after the fact, not before it.

Tomas is not a reckless fool. He is a skilled worker who knows exactly what the bridge needs and cannot bear watching his family leave the district. His choices force Marek to see that a contract can be understandable, necessary, and still unjust. He survives the final transformation without his extraordinary borrowed vigor. His later life is shorter and more ordinary, and he treats that as a fact to work with rather than a betrayal by Marek.

## 4. Lio Vale — the child who sees the cost

**Age:** 9 at the beginning
**Role:** Tomas's daughter and a recurring ordinary point of reference

Lio is not a magical child or a hidden oracle. She asks direct questions that adults avoid: who owns the year, why a dead person's name is missing, and why a grown person is willing to get old before breakfast. She likes drawing maps of places adults say are empty. Her story keeps the central conflict connected to a life that is still being lived.

## 5. Sera Holt — the physician and mentor

**Age:** 52
**Role:** chief physician of the Morrowfen clinic and a former senior witness
**Goal:** keep the clinic capable of treating people who cannot afford a public loan
**Flaw:** she accepts triage as a temporary necessity and becomes complicit in deciding which patients are “not worth the settlement”

Sera trains Marek in the physical consequences of power. She can tell when a body has paid for a borrowed season, and she knows that medicine cannot repair every social injury. She borrows years to keep the clinic's furnaces and staff alive during a winter shortage, then hides the debt from the board. She is both mentor and warning: expertise without accountability becomes another institution that decides who is expendable.

Her relationship with Marek moves from mentorship to a painful friendship. In Volume 12 she opposes his Open Charter plan because she believes an imperfect immediate loan is better than a principled collapse. In the final volume she supports the transformation after helping design a public medical reserve that makes triage less brutal.

## 6. Nim Rook — the courier and archive breaker

**Age:** 19
**Role:** night courier, former copy-room assistant, and occasional thief
**Goal:** prove that her missing sister Ilen existed
**Flaw:** she treats every institution as an obstacle and every stranger as either a liar or a possible ally

Nim can move through Bell Stair, rooftops, and drainage tunnels without being noticed. She is not a superpowerful infiltrator; she knows the city's routines because she once ran supplies through them. Her sister's disappearance gives the story a human answer to “unwritten”: a missing name is a missing relationship, not merely an administrative inconvenience.

Nim repeatedly steals evidence Marek needs and then gives it back after demanding a better plan. By the final volume she has become a public records courier whose work is recognized rather than punished.

## 7. Ilya Senn — the Lantern Warden

**Age:** 41
**Role:** senior Lantern Warden responsible for flood locks and tower lamps
**Goal:** keep the basin's physical systems alive without choosing a district to drown
**Flaw:** competence has taught him that he should make the terrible decision before anyone else can

Ilya knows the Yearspire as machinery, not a metaphor. He can maintain pumps, locks, heat exchangers, and emergency lamps without using a loan. His practical skill makes him indispensable and makes his silence morally dangerous. He first appears in Volume 1 as the unnamed Warden who has secretly disabled a settlement valve to save Tallow Ward; he knows the Trust will call that sabotage.

Ilya becomes the final engine's physical anchor: he does not solve the philosophical conflict, but he refuses to let the Deep Engine destroy the city while people are still inside it.

## 8. Hesper Rann — the compromised senior clerk

**Age:** 46
**Role:** Marek's immediate supervisor and a former audit clerk
**Goal:** keep Morrowfen functioning even if the official record has to bend
**Flaw:** believes that a small lie made for a public good is a practical sacrifice

Hesper falsifies a Tallow Ward water entry in Volume 1 so that a floodgate loan can be approved before the district runs dry. He believes the cost is hidden, folded into a reserve that appears on no public roll and can be paid off administratively. He is wrong in a way that matters: a falsified entry is false-witnessed, so the tower keeps the responsibility chain intact and looks for a living certifier instead of a ledger line. He is not a cartoon villain: he has kept clinics open, knows every clerk's weaknesses, believes the alternative is a corpse in a gutter, and is genuinely astonished that his private arithmetic has a witness in it.

Hesper is arrested after the first volume's local victory. He later becomes an unwilling witness against the Trust. He never becomes innocent; he becomes a person whose compromised choices are finally placed in public record.

## 9. Ovid Merrow — the Sable Compact broker

**Age:** 47
**Role:** head of the Sable Compact's Morrowfen house
**Goal:** turn future credit into a private market that no government can ignore
**Method:** buy distressed claims, combine them with unrelated obligations, and sell access before the risk is visible

Ovid is charismatic, patient, and genuinely good at predicting markets. He does not order storms or poison wells. He simply makes a desperate person sign a document that looks like a smaller version of the only available choice. His defeat is not a public execution; it is the loss of the legal monopoly that lets him separate a promise from its cost.

## 10. Chancellor Ysara Venn — the emergency governor

**Age:** 58
**Role:** chancellor of Crown March and defender of regional stability
**Goal:** prevent a repeat of the Last Meridian through centralized emergency authority
**Flaw:** treats informed consent as a luxury available only when the danger has passed

Ysara is not secretly cruel. She has seen famine and war, and she believes a city that cannot make an unpleasant decision quickly will die. She supports loans that save thousands while making sure no one can appeal them afterward. Her conflict with Marek is about who bears the cost of uncertainty, not about whether uncertainty exists.

## 11. Seren Calder — the settlement marshal

**Age:** 40
**Role:** public safety officer who enforces emergency settlements
**Goal:** ensure that a settlement happens, even if a person refuses to sign
**Flaw:** obedience to procedure is treated as moral innocence

Seren is a former public defender who has watched people die during appeals. She develops a hard belief that consent matters only when the river is not rising. She becomes the most effective field commander of the Crown of Continuation because she can enforce a charter without reading it.

## 12. Lyra Dain — the Quiet Choir leader

**Age:** 36
**Role:** archive historian, returnist, and leader of the Quiet Choir
**Goal:** restore the Last Meridian by closing all futures that diverge from its final record
**Flaw:** cannot distinguish love for a lost people from entitlement to decide who may live in the present

Lyra is sincere, educated, and willing to expose the Trust's crimes. She also treats ordinary people as “branch noise” when their choices make the approved future impossible. She is a foil to Neris: both have lost a home to institutional decisions, but Lyra responds by demanding one correct future while Neris insists on making room for more than one.

## 13. Edren Sable — the First Auditor

**Apparent age:** 50
**True nature:** a succession of copied memories and renewed bodies carrying the First Account's authority
**Public role:** absent, disputed, and signed only as **A-0** in the oldest records
**Personal goal:** close every future except one in which the Last Meridian survives
**Flaw:** he confuses survival with moral permission and has never allowed a future to reject him

Edren is the final antagonist and is seeded early through his signature, not introduced as a surprise. He was the Last Meridian's accounting officer and the custodian who helped convert the crossing into a bank. When the original body failed, the tower copied his memory and authority into a new body. Each successor believes itself to be Edren, but no single body is the whole of him. He can compare broad continuations and force a settlement's pressure, but he cannot invent a future or make genuine consent.

Edren offers a clean solution: one approved timeline, no more branching, no more year droughts, and the restoration of the lost world he remembers. That solution requires the current world to pay. His final defeat is not that he is proved evil; it is that his authority is made inseparable from the First Account, and the affected people choose to dismantle the account rather than accept his certainty.

## 14. Supporting community figures

These figures recur enough to give the world ordinary faces without taking over the central mystery:

- **Marda Senn**, Ilya's older sister, a Warden's ledger clerk who disappears into the Hollow Galleries during the false-witness crisis and later returns with a partial map.
- **The Tallow counter-signer**, a junior Chamber clerk who countersigned Hesper's falsified water entry in good faith because the numbers in front of them balanced and nobody asked who had produced them. Marek's first Boundary Hold stores its delay in his own body, and when the hold ends the contract's chain finds this clerk at the end of it, which is how Volume 1 teaches him that a Hold cannot be aimed and that a false-witnessed contract still has a living person standing at the far end of it. The Volume 01 outline names them and must name them early: the injury is the volume's proof that the system's cost is a person, so it cannot be played as an anonymous entry. They live. Their recovery is slow, partly Marek's fault and partly nobody's, and they are not written to forgive him quickly.
- **Oren Pike**, a Rainmarket grain factor who begins as Ovid's opponent and ends up testifying against the Sable Compact. In Volume 7, two-winter displacement leaves him physically alive but legally unwritten; he later chooses a new public name and works as a grain witness.
- **Pella Rusk**, a schoolteacher who teaches children whose yearlines have been shortened by public loans. She is skeptical of Marek until he agrees to let her class review a draft charter.
- **Tamsin Vale**, Marek's mother, a practical seamstress who signed an old body loan to keep Marek alive during childhood fever. She knows more about the first mark than she initially tells him.
- **Sabin Rell**, a Crown March granary official who signs Ovid's emergency underwriting clause in Volume 3. He later admits that he knew a projected famine was being used as collateral and accepts a public censure rather than a political pardon.
- **Councilor Bel Harrow**, a Crown March representative who believes any honest public process is preferable to another secret settlement. He is useful, not incorruptible.
- **The Unwritten children**, a group of young people who maintain their own names in notebooks because official rolls have failed them. They are not a hidden army; they are a community with ordinary grief and humor.

## 15. Relationship rules

- Marek has one slow-burn romantic relationship, with Neris Kade.
- Family relationships are not shortcuts to innocence. Tomas can make harmful choices; Tamsin's past can be truthful and still damaging.
- No antagonist gets a redemption that erases the people harmed by them.
- Supporting characters have goals that continue whether or not Marek agrees with them.
- A character's skill is tied to work and relationships, not to a private numerical rank.
