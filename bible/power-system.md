# Power System: Yearlines, Loans, and Settlements

## 1. The basic rule

Every living person has a **yearline**: a continuity of capacity, memory, bodily time, relationships, and possible choices. Calendar age is visible, but it is not the same as the amount of yearline a person possesses. A yearline is not a countdown. It changes according to how a life is lived and which commitments are made.

The Yearspire can draw a plausible future consequence into the present. This is called **seasoning**. The present effect is real and the future cost is real; neither cancels the other.

A loan can therefore make a bridge hold, a body endure labor, a hand perform a skill, or a record become legally usable. It cannot make a person immortal, bring back the dead, or create a result that no plausible future could support.

There is one later exception to the normal source of power: an **Open Account** can use present contributions instead of broad future claims. A worker may offer an hour of practiced labor, care, or conscious recovery while that act is happening. The effect cannot exceed the contribution, cannot use the same hour twice, cannot draw from an unborn or unchosen future, and disappears when the contribution ends. The Open Account may also honor a narrow future pledge made by a living person in person, with an independent witness and no uninvolved collateral, but it cannot draw from the First Account or the absent founders. This is a public utility method, not a loophole that makes impossible work possible.

## 2. Anatomy of a loan

A valid loan has five parts.

1. **Need:** a concrete present outcome, phrased as something that can be observed or tested.
2. **Pledge:** the future cost offered by the borrower or an independent surety.
3. **Scope:** what the borrowed effect may change, for how long, and where. A contract for one gate cannot secretly power an entire city.
4. **Witness:** at least one person who understands the terms and is not being paid by the borrower. The witness does not have to understand the entire political future, only the promise and the immediate cost.
5. **Settlement:** the event or date when the future consequence comes due. A settlement may be paid by life, labor, skill, care, restitution, or an approved transfer to a consenting public pool.

A loan that omits a material term is not automatically void. It becomes **false-witnessed**, which makes the contract's authority unstable and shifts the collection toward whoever certified the lie. A physical effect may still occur, but it carries a visible responsibility chain and can be challenged before settlement.

## 3. What can be borrowed

The tower recognizes four broad expressions of future capacity. The expressions can be combined, but each has a different practical limit.

### Vigor

A vigor loan draws on future endurance, recovery, or resistance. It can keep a person conscious through surgery, let a crew work through a dangerous shift, or hold a body together during a controlled exposure.

- **Present benefit:** stamina, healing, heat resistance, or temporary physical strength.
- **Later cost:** accelerated aging, chronic illness, lost recovery, or a shorter biological future.
- **Failure:** the body pays all postponed damage at once, often as a visible year scar rather than a convenient instant death.
- **Boundary:** vigor cannot bring back a severed limb, cure an unrelated disease, or make a body immune to violence.

### Craft

A craft loan draws on a future version of the borrower's working life. The borrower receives the reflexes, timing, or judgment of a practitioner they may not yet become. The skill feels familiar, but its source is a possible future, not an ancestor's ghost.

- **Present benefit:** a surgeon performs a difficult operation, a bridgewright sees a load path, or an apprentice repairs an unfamiliar mechanism.
- **Later cost:** the opportunity that would have made the skill develop may disappear; an apprenticeship, a workplace, or a relationship can be redirected. A craft loan can also create a **craft scar**, in which a community inherits a technique without its history or consent.
- **Failure:** the skill may become unusable, or it may be reproduced by someone who never agreed to inherit its obligations.
- **Boundary:** craft power cannot teach a moral judgment, grant social authority, or guarantee that the borrower keeps the skill after settlement.

### Sequence

A sequence loan changes which local event is most likely to occur next. It can make a storm pass a quarter, let a seed take root, or hold a bridge closed long enough for an evacuation.

- **Present benefit:** a narrow, observable change in the order of events.
- **Later cost:** a named counterweight loses future possibility. The cost can be a failed harvest, a lost relationship, a delayed injury, or a less favorable political outcome.
- **Failure:** the sequence snaps back at the worst available moment, or the counterweight becomes the target of the next loan.
- **Boundary:** sequence is not mind control, general luck, or a promise of victory. It can only favor an outcome that a plausible future could produce.

### Record

A record loan draws on the future legal and social consequences of a statement. It can make a disputed contract appear properly witnessed, place a person in the public roll, or keep a dangerous testimony from being heard.

- **Present benefit:** a change in what institutions will accept as evidence or identity.
- **Later cost:** a witness loses a memory, a family loses a legal relationship, or a person is marked as **unwritten** when the record cannot sustain the change.
- **Failure:** the record folds back, erasing the recent proof while leaving the social consequences of the altered record intact.
- **Boundary:** record power cannot erase every record, create a person's whole identity, or make a false statement true in every court. It changes institutional recognition, not physical fact.

## 4. What a year is not

A year is not a universally equal coin. The same future can be expensive or cheap depending on the need, the pledged cost, the witness quality, the stability of the local timeline, and whether the borrower accepts the consequence voluntarily.

The tower does not reveal a single numeric balance. It records relationships among a need, a pledge, a scope, a witness, and a settlement. That is why two identical-looking contracts can have radically different outcomes.

A year is also not necessarily a year of life. A person may offer a year of health, a year of craft, a year of freedom from a duty, or a year of a future child's dependence. The latter pledges are prohibited in a lawful contract because another person cannot be made property through a signature. Prohibited pledges still occur, and their illegality produces false-witnessed settlements.

## 5. Present effect and later consequence

A seasoned effect must obey four limits:

1. **It must be locally possible.** The tower can pull forward a consequence, not create an entirely new object or law.
2. **It must have a named scope.** The effect cannot silently expand to every similar person or place.
3. **It must have a live chain of consent.** A witness cannot be a person who was coerced, copied, or unaware of the material term.
4. **It must leave a settlement path.** If no responsible future holder can pay, the engine must create a visible shortfall rather than pretend the debt vanished.

Settlement happens at the due event, not at an arbitrary wizard's convenience. A borrower may settle early by performing the agreed work or by transferring the obligation to a consenting pool. Early settlement cancels further collection but cannot undo the present effect or every social consequence of that effect.

## 6. Failure and shortfall

When a future changes, the exact pledged consequence may no longer exist. The tower then recognizes a **shortfall**. A shortfall is not a random punishment. It follows the contract's responsibility chain.

1. It first looks for the named borrower.
2. If the borrower is dead or unable, it looks for a named surety.
3. If neither can pay, it looks for the institution that certified the contract.
4. If the institution has no public fund, the affected district receives a year scar and a recorded liability.

A year scar can be a season of missing harvests, a child absent from a school roll, a district's chronic fatigue, a lost legal relationship, or a skill that no longer has a living practitioner. It is a consequence, not a decorative monster.

If a contract is forged, the same chain can make a powerful person, family, or office visibly responsible. The purpose is to make coercion traceable, not to guarantee that the victim is the only one who suffers.

### Holds and the responsibility chain

A Boundary Hold is not a new debt and it does not create one. It does not add a party to the chain either. For the length of the hold the postponed consequence is stored in the holder's own yearline; when the hold ends, the collection resumes on the original contract and walks the chain in §6.

1. If the named borrower is alive and able, the borrower pays and the storage is returned to the holder undamaged.
2. If the borrower is dead, absent, or unable, the named surety pays.
3. If neither can pay, the certifying institution pays. In a false-witnessed contract that institution is usually a living clerk who countersigned in good faith, because a clerk's counter-signature is the institution's act and the tower holds the officer accountable for it.
4. If no one on the chain can pay, the tower records an ordinary shortfall under §6, which still ends with the affected district. The holder is not a new terminal party and does not become the district's debtor. What the holder carries meanwhile is the toll of having held the delay in his own body, and it stays as a year scar after the shortfall is settled.

This is the whole danger of a Hold, and it has hard edges:

- A Hold can never reach a person who did not borrow, guarantee, witness, or certify the contract. People who merely attended a hearing, and bystanders who were present when it went wrong, are unreachable.
- A Hold cannot be aimed. The holder chooses the settlement and the duration; the chain decides where the cost lands, and the holder finds out by watching.
- The holder pays for the delay and not for the debt, unless the debt is the holder's own. A Hold is not cheap; it is a loan of storage that may become a gift.
- A Hold cannot be released early by paying less. Ending it early ends the delay, not the debt.

A Hold therefore punishes exactly the mistake the tower rewards in a clerk: trusting a clean file.

## 7. Cancellation and limits

A loan can be:

- **settled:** the agreed future cost is paid;
- **transferred:** moved to a consenting surety or public pool;
- **appealed:** challenged in a Witness Room before settlement;
- **voided:** canceled by a qualified independent audit when a material term was false or impossible;
- **suspended:** held briefly by an auditor, always with a new due date and a cost to the auditor;
- **forgiven:** paid from a public restitution fund, never erased from the record.

There is no normal procedure for returning a person to a life they would have lived. A settlement can prevent a future cost, restore a missing public name, or return unused capacity to a named living person. It cannot resurrect a dead person or reconstruct an exact erased history.

## 8. Scaling

Scale is limited by witness, trust, and infrastructure.

- A household loan needs one witness and can affect a household.
- A district loan needs several independent witnesses, maintenance crews, and a public settlement fund.
- A city loan needs a council, a written charter, and a continuous flow of people who can sustain the present effect.
- A civilizational loan cannot be authorized by one ruler because there is no body capable of representing every person whose future would be spent.

Increasing power does not remove consent requirements. It makes the lie larger and the eventual settlement more dangerous.

## 9. The First Account exception

The First Account is not a lawful loan that simply escaped notice. It is an **unappealed founding claim** created under the **Founder's Clause** by the nine Last Convocation survivors before the public witness system existed. They used their own lost future as surety for the crossing, and they were the only people who consented to that arrangement. Their bodies and independent witness chain are gone, so the claim cannot be re-opened by a living witness.

The Yearspire treats those absent founders as permanent sureties. That treatment is why A-0 can draw on the First Account at all, and why every ordinary loan seems to borrow from a deeper reserve. It is also a permanent procedural violation: the account has no living consent chain, and every use of it is false-witnessed in the strict sense even when the present effect saves lives.

The final Open Account can be created only by releasing that absent claim and replacing it with present contributions made by living people in person. It may then accept a narrow future pledge from a living borrower who has explicitly chosen the capacity, scope, witness, and settlement. It cannot transfer the founders' future to a new chosen life, because that would repeat the original violation. It ends the absent claim, records the harm, and makes the public institution responsible for whatever present capacity it receives.

After the transition, an Open Account has two lawful sources: real-time present contributions and a narrow future pledge made by a living person in person. A present contribution expires when the work, care, or recovery ends. A future pledge must name the exact capacity, cannot name an unborn child or an absent ancestor, cannot use a third party as silent collateral, and may be refused before sealing. If the pledged future becomes impossible, the institution bears the shortfall; it cannot substitute another person's yearline. This is an explicit exception to the general shortfall chain: after the transition, the public institution's reserve pays first, and a district is not assigned the loss unless it has separately accepted a present contribution.

The transition also changes how the four forms pay, which is where the old system's cruelty was most ordinary:

- **Vigor and craft** costs are the borrower's own yearline, pledged narrowly and in person.
- **Sequence** may no longer use an uninvolved person as its counterweight. The counterweight must be a consequence the affected people were told about in the same witnessed proceeding, must be accepted by them as a group, and may be withdrawn before sealing. Where nobody has accepted it, the tower records a shortfall and the public reserve pays. A sequence loan can no longer decide that some stranger's harvest, child, or marriage is the price of a storm.
- **Record** may no longer take a witness's memory or a family's legal relationship as its price. The contractual cost falls on the borrower's own yearline and then the reserve. What a changed record does to other people—the sibling who loses an inheritance, the household that must be told, the name that no longer matches a door—remains a consequence of the change, honestly recorded, and can no longer be pledged away by a third party who was not asked.

The forms still work. What changes is that the cost is no longer somebody else's private future, and the difference is visible on the page.

Once the sealed founding copy has been opened and authenticated, a quorum of independent local witness rooms can record a finding that the Founder's Clause has no living consent chain. That finding ends the First Account's authority; it does not restore the crossing year or transfer its capacity to a replacement person. The quorum can be challenged, but no single auditor can overrule it.

A quorum is built, not assumed. It requires at least three rooms whose members share no employer, surety, or certifying institution, and the record must name the specific disqualifying tie when one is alleged. Authentication of the sealed founding copy is what makes a finding admissible; the window that follows governs who sits, not what the finding may say. The moment those rooms are seated to hear the claim, anyone with standing in it gets a **challenge window** of stated length—in the final volume, nine days, extendable once on a filed objection—in which to object to a room's independence. Standing belongs to the parties who can still be named and served: the current First Auditor and the administrators who certified the founding records. The founders themselves are absent, cannot be notified, and hold no standing, which is precisely why their claim is open to challenge at all.

A successful objection removes that room and forces a replacement drawn from a district with no other tie to the fund. Losing the window, or filling a room with a paid counter-signature, voids the finding. This is why Edren's side fights the composition of the rooms rather than the substance of the finding: the finding is sound, and a quorum that is bought is worthless.

## 10. Marek's progression

Marek begins as a trained clerk and gains Ledger Sight during Volume 1. His later permissions come through practice, failure, relationships, and changed decisions. His stages are not numerical levels and do not make him a conventional battlefield cultivator.

| Stage | What Marek can do | What it cannot do | Cost and practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ledger Sight** | See a current contract's visible entries, witness chain, and immediate mismatch after physical contact with the record. | Predict a future, know intent, or produce a missing entry. | Headaches, tunnel vision, and temporary loss of a recent ordinary memory after repeated use. He learns by copying and correcting real files. |
| **Boundary Hold** | Hold one settlement or one crossed boundary in place for a short interval, usually a few bells. | Cancel a debt permanently, reverse a completed effect, delay many contracts at once, or choose where the postponed cost lands. | The delay is stored in his own yearline. When the hold ends, the contract's chain collects: the borrower, then the surety, then the living clerk who certified the entry when the chain is false-witnessed. A Hold adds nobody to that chain, so an unpayable debt still ends with the affected district and Marek is left carrying the toll of the delay as a year scar. It can never reach an uninvolved bystander. Delays can bunch every debt into one crisis. This is Marek's ability, not a sequence loan's counterweight. |
| **Season Hand** | Direct one already-authorized vigor, craft, or sequence effect toward a named person or object within its stated scope. | Increase the effect, invent a new power, or force a recipient to accept it. | Incompatible directions attach the debt to Marek. He must learn the actual need rather than assuming the ledger's wording is enough. |
| **Returnpath** | Split a live obligation among consenting holders and redirect a portion of a future cost to a public pool or named surety. | Restore a lost life, erase the original debt, or transfer it to an unwilling person. | He carries the sensory and emotional echoes of each obligation and loses privacy about his own future. |
| **Branch Auditor** | Compare several plausible contract outcomes using physical evidence, testimony, and existing records. | See every possibility, distinguish moral truth from probability, or predict what people will freely choose. | Contradictory memories and vertigo can make him temporarily distrust the wrong account. He must gather independent human accounts. |
| **Charter Weaver** | Build a binding multi-party compact that pools present surplus and distributes a declared effect without drawing from an unborn future. | Command communities, bind silent minorities, or make a broken promise harmless. | Marek becomes responsible for the compact's collateral and loses the ability to keep his personal future private. |
| **Open Account** | Anchor a city-scale present effect through independent witnesses who knowingly accept only present, voluntary costs. | Restore the First Account, guarantee his own life, or force agreement. | He releases the future self that has sustained his ledger power and accepts a genuinely unreadable future. He remains alive but ordinary afterward. |

## 11. The System-like Ledger Sight

Marek's interface is a **Ledger Sight**, a read-only page of the tower's oldest accounting craft. It appears as sparse ink, lines, names, and a short instruction when he touches a live record under stress. It is part of the fictional world, not a game menu.

A panel may say things such as:

> The mark is fresh. The witness is absent.

> One settlement is waiting on two names.

> The next cost will fall on the street below.

It never gives a percentage, a quest marker, a moral verdict, or a prediction. It can point to a contradiction the clerk has overlooked, but Marek still has to find the physical evidence, ask difficult people, and choose what to do. The interface is incomplete because the First Account deliberately hides its own opening entry.

## 12. Worked example: the floodgate

A village needs a gate to hold for six hours. The village pledges ten years of collective road labor and offers a named farmer's future recovery. The tower returns six hours of exceptional structural endurance. The gate holds. At settlement, the farmer's body pays the first biological cost; the road labor funds the remaining obligation.

If the river later takes a different route and the gate never needed six hours, the tower still recognizes the original contract. It may refund unused capacity, but it cannot pretend the saved hours were not spent. Marek's first professional lesson is that a successful present effect does not prove the future pledge was fair.

## 13. Hard limits for drafting

- Never use “power points,” “mana bars,” or arbitrary stat increases.
- Never let Marek solve a problem by simply reading the answer. Evidence, relationships, and action remain necessary.
- Never let a borrower be forced to accept a contract through a single hidden System command; force requires human institutions or forged consent.
- Never resolve a moral problem by deleting the people who disagreed.
- Never use a final transformation to make the previous suffering unreal. The ending changes the system, not the memory of its victims.
