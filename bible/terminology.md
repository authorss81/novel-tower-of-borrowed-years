# Terminology Bible

These are the canonical terms for the novel. Use plain language in narration and reserve technical definitions for the bible, glossary moments, and documents characters actually need.

## Calendar and setting terms

### Morrowfen Reckoning (MR)

The civic calendar begins when the Yearspire first accepts public loans. The story begins in **MR 412**. The reckoning counts the tower's public opening, not the Last Meridian's lost future.

### Tide Reckoning (TR)

The Saltglass Coast calendar. It begins with the first full tide after the first public Yearspire loan and divides each year into thirteen named tide months. A valid coastal year-mark must name the month, tide phase, harbor seal, and translator. Settlement is triggered by the event or by a translated date; an untranslated mark remains in **calendar lag** and cannot be silently folded into an MR year.

### Calendar lag

The interval between a local contract date and its recognized Morrowfen Reckoning date. Calendar lag is not a free delay; it is a public dispute over when a settlement is due. A translator's certified correction, not a lender's preferred date, resolves it.

### Boundary

A named physical or legal line written into a contract: a floodgate line, a ward edge, a bridge span, a court boundary, or the line of an appeal. Boundary Hold uses a boundary already named in the record; it cannot invent one.

### Yearspire

The physical tower and public institution on Stillstep Island. “The tower” is common speech; “Yearspire” is the official name.

### Stillstep Island

The raised stone platform in Morrowfen's central flood channel. It contains the Yearspire and is connected to the city by a public causeway. No private dwelling is permitted on the island.

### The Threshold, Tally Halls, and Witness Rooms

The public intake desks, record halls, and independent hearing rooms where loans are registered, copied, and challenged. The Threshold is where petitioners first ask for help; the Tally Halls are where the Chamber works; the Witness Rooms are where terms are spoken aloud before sealing.

### Season Vaults, Hollow Galleries, and Deep Engine

The Season Vaults store materials and bounded effects whose future origin can be audited. The Hollow Galleries preserve records removed from official histories. The Deep Engine is the sealed machinery beneath the tower, including the First Account. These are maintenance and archive spaces, not a monster dungeon.

### Morrowfen Wards

Tallow Ward, Bell Stair, Rainmarket, Crown Terrace, Hallow Ground, and the Underbank are the principal city districts. Hallow Ground is locally known as the Unwritten Quarter but is omitted from the Tower's official map and rolls.

### The Red Uplands, Saltglass Coast, Crown March, Hollow North, and Quiet North

The Red Uplands are orchard and quarry country. The Saltglass Coast is the port region using the Tide Reckoning. Crown March is the regional administrative heartland. Hollow North contains places repeatedly erased from official maps. Quiet North is the hidden route network used by people who claim descent from the Last Meridian.

### Chamber of Accounts, Yearspire Trust, Sable Compact, and Lantern Wardens

The Chamber is the public audit office. The Trust operates the tower and owns its physical vaults. The Sable Compact is a private lending and trade network. Lantern Wardens maintain pumps, locks, bridges, heat exchangers, and emergency lamps.

### Saltglass Harbor Council

The port council that controls Tide Reckoning translations and harbor seals. It can delay recognition of a coastal year-mark, but its decision can be challenged by a certified multi-calendar witness procedure.

### Yearwell

A natural concentration of unrealized consequences beneath a place. It can make a plausible future temporarily available as a present effect, but it cannot create impossible outcomes.

### Last Meridian

The future era from which the Last Convocation escaped. It is not another accessible world. It is a historical era that was erased from the current timeline, known mainly through contradictory records and inherited practices.

### Last Convocation

The nine survivors who crossed backward from the Last Meridian and built the first version of the tower. Their crossing created the First Account.

### One-way year seam

The single pass the Last Convocation made by collapsing their own Yearwell inward, in the country that no longer exists. It is not a Yearwell use and not a door: it opens only where the collapse happened, cannot be aimed, cannot be reopened, and has never carried a living passenger twice. Its price, the lost future they pledged, is already spent and is the missing year at the head of every ledger. Every later “return” ritual in Morrowfen is a fraud worked with borrowed records.

## Contract terms

### Yearline

The continuity of a person's bodily capacity, memory, work, relationships, and possible choices. A yearline is not a visible countdown and does not equal lifespan.

### Loan

A recorded exchange in which a future consequence is pledged to create a present effect. A loan always has a need, pledge, scope, witness, and settlement.

### Pledge

The future cost offered by a borrower or surety. It can be biological age, labor, craft, care, freedom from a duty, or another clearly bounded capacity. Naming another person's life without independent consent is prohibited and produces a false-witnessed contract if attempted.

### Present contribution

A bounded amount of capacity offered in the present for an Open Account effect: an hour of practiced work, a period of conscious labor, a scheduled act of care, or a consciously chosen portion of present recovery. It creates no future claim, cannot be taken from an unborn person, and expires when the offered act ends. A worker cannot contribute the same hour of labor or skill twice at once.

### Witness

An independent person who understands the material terms of a loan and confirms them aloud. A witness is not a guarantee that the contract is fair. A witness can be pressured, deceived, or bribed; those are the cracks through which the larger system exploits people.

### Witness quorum

A finding recorded by at least three independent local witness rooms, with no two sharing an employer, surety, or certifying institution. Authenticating the sealed founding copy is what makes a finding admissible; the **challenge window** that follows governs who sits. It begins when the rooms are seated to hear the claim and belongs to the parties who can still be named and served — the current First Auditor and the administrators who certified the founding records. A quorum can end an absent founding claim, but it cannot rewrite a settled personal history or force a living person to pledge a future. A successful objection removes a room and forces a replacement from an untied district; a quorum bought with one paid counter-signature is void.

### Scope

The stated place, duration, and effect of a loan. A contract cannot legally expand from one gate to every gate in the city. False-witnessed contracts can leak beyond their scope, which is one source of disasters.

### Settlement

The moment the pledged future consequence comes due. Settlement can be paid through life, labor, care, restitution, or transfer to a consenting public pool. It can cancel further collection but cannot undo the present effect.

### Shortfall

A situation in which the future named by a contract no longer contains enough capacity to pay it. The tower traces responsibility to the borrower, surety, certifying institution, or affected district in that order. After the Open Account transition, its public reserve replaces the district in that chain for a narrowly pledged living-person future; a district pays only for a present contribution it separately accepted.

### Public reserve

The Open Yearspire's common fund of present contributions, seized Trust assets, fines, and restitution. It is what pays when a pledged future becomes impossible, and it is deliberately too small to cover a lie. It is a floor, not a promise, and its shortfalls are published every year.

### Year scar

A visible or civic consequence left by a shortfall: missing harvests, chronic fatigue, a lost legal name, an absent school year, a damaged craft, or a community-wide drought. A year scar is not a decorative monster and cannot be removed by simply fighting the person who caused it.

### Year drought

A widespread pattern of shortfalls in which several communities lose future capacity faster than they can replace it. It is the tower's regional failure state.

### Seasoning

The act of pulling a plausible future consequence into a present effect. The term comes from the idea that a life accumulates seasons of experience; seasoning is an accelerated, borrowed version of that process.

### Counterweight

A named future consequence that balances a sequence loan. If a storm is moved away from one district, a crop, relationship, or later decision elsewhere becomes more fragile. The counterweight can be visible or hidden depending on the quality of the witness chain. It is a property of a contract, not Marek's personal power.

### Boundary Hold

Marek's second power stage: briefly hold one settlement or one crossed boundary in place. It postpones rather than cancels a consequence. The delay is stored in his own yearline, and when the hold ends the collection walks the contract's responsibility chain — borrower, then surety, then the living clerk who certified the entry when the chain is false-witnessed. A Hold adds nobody to that chain: if nobody on it can pay, the shortfall still ends with the affected district, and the holder is left carrying the toll of the delay as a year scar. He chooses the settlement and the duration, never the destination, and the cost can never reach a bystander who did not borrow, guarantee, witness, or certify. This ability is not the same thing as a sequence loan's counterweight.

### Standing hold

A founding remedy written in the margin of the tower's oldest reference volume, in the same hand that signed its blank first line **A-0**. A standing hold postpones a settlement until the year is ended, and it is held by **the clerk of record**. It is not a new power, not a second Boundary Hold, and not a way to cancel a debt: it moves a due date to the end of the year and leaves every term of the contract standing. The chain behaves exactly as in any other hold — the delay is stored in the holder, nobody is added to the chain, and the toll stays as a year scar.

### Clerk of record

A founding-era office, not a title in the Trust's table. The clerk of record was the officer who could hold a settlement past its due date on the tower's own behalf, and the office was retired a few centuries after the opening. Nobody holds it in the present day, which is why founding remedies naming it survive only as taught history and why anyone who takes the duty on has taken a vacant office: there is no office behind the words to store the delay in, so the delay goes into the body of the person who took it. The office is not refilled in Volume 1. A clerk may describe himself as the clerk of record as a promise, and never as an appointment.

### The no-copy standing instruction

A standing Trust order on the sealed founding page of the oldest ledger: no clerk may erase the signature on it and no clerk may copy the page. Copying it into a public register is therefore an act against a live instruction, not an oversight, which is what makes a hand-made public copy of that page either a defiance or a crime depending on who is asked. The instruction is never explained or justified in the novel's opening volume; no one in Morrowfen knows whether it protects the tower or the signature.

### Returnpath

A lawful, difficult procedure for splitting or redirecting an unsettled obligation to consenting holders. It can prevent a collection and return unused capacity to a living person, but it cannot restore a lost life or exact history.

### Open Account

The final public form of the Yearspire. It uses independent witnesses, voluntary present contributions, and narrowly pledged future capacity from living people, while refusing extraction from any unborn, absent, or unchosen future. It keeps the first entry publicly visible. It is not a magic wish or a universal guarantee.

## Power forms

### Vigor loan

A loan of future endurance, healing, or resistance. Its cost appears as accelerated aging, illness, lost recovery, or reduced biological future.

### Craft loan

A loan of practiced skill or judgment from a possible future working life. Its cost may be a lost apprenticeship, a craft scar, or a skill separated from the community that created it.

### Sequence loan

A loan that favors one bounded local event over another. Its cost is a counterweight elsewhere. It is not general luck and cannot force a person to make a choice. Before the transition, a counterweight could land on people who were never asked. After it, the counterweight must be named to the people it will fall on, accepted by them as a group, and withdrawable before sealing; if nobody has accepted it, the tower records a shortfall and the public reserve pays.

### Record loan

A rare loan that changes what an institution will accept as identity, testimony, or legal history. Before the transition, its cost could be a witness's memory or a family's legal relationship. After it, the contractual cost falls on the borrower's own yearline and then the public reserve. The damage a changed record does to other people is still real and still recorded, but it can no longer be pledged away by someone who was not asked to pay it. It changes institutional recognition, not physical fact.

### Ledger Sight

Marek's limited System-like interface. A live record can show its visible entries, witness chain, and immediate contradiction after he touches it under stress. It cannot predict the future, read intent, or solve a mystery by itself. Messages are short and plain:

> The witness is absent.

> The cost has two names.

### Branch Auditor

A higher permission that lets Marek compare several plausible continuations using evidence and testimony. It is not prophecy. The more contradictory the evidence, the more likely he is to see a false continuation.

## Faction and historical terms

### First Account

The hidden reserve created during the Last Convocation's crossing. It is an aggregate of possible years, not a literal bank of unborn lives. It is the source of the tower's oldest and most dangerous loans. It is an unappealed founding claim, not a lawful public loan.

### Founder's Clause

The emergency agreement by which the nine Last Convocation survivors treated their own lost future as surety for the crossing. It was made before the current witness system existed, and no living person consented to its terms. The clause is the reason the First Account can be drawn on at all; it is also the moral and legal wound the final Open Account must resolve.

### Release

In the founding claim, release has one meaning and no other: the claim is **voided** and its entry is **returned to the public record** so that anyone may read what was taken and who took it. Release is disclosure and discharge, never recovery. The crossing year is not restored, no lost world is recovered, and no capacity is handed to a new chosen life. The same verb is used elsewhere for letting go of a claim or of a possible self — Marek releases the future self behind his Ledger Sight, the returnists release their claim to the remembered Last Meridian — and in those cases too nothing is recovered. Characters who speak loosely about “getting the year back” are expressing hope or a threat, and the ending corrects them.

### First Auditor

The title given to Edren Sable's copied authority. The first auditor maintains the First Account and decides which future claims may be settled. The title appears as the signature **A-0** in old records.

### Hollow Court

A loose mutual-aid network of people carrying year scars, confiscated marks, erased relatives, and counterfeit documents. It shelters people from collection parties and preserves evidence of Tower abuses. It is neither a saintly order nor a single crime syndicate.

### Quiet Choir

A returnist faction that wants to restore the Last Meridian by forcing all futures into one approved continuation. Its members call alternate lives “branch noise.” The name describes their ritual practice, not their moral nature.

### Crown of Continuation

The late coalition that combines Trust administrators, military officials, private lenders, and Quiet Choir cells. It treats one stable future as the only humane solution.

### Unwritten

A person or place removed from official recognition, not necessarily dead. Hallow Ground is locally known as the Unwritten Quarter, but the Tower omits it from official maps and civic rolls. The term can be imposed by an institution and reclaimed by the people who live there.

### Hallow Ground

The locally named district called the Unwritten Quarter. “Hallow” is an older spelling used by neighborhood records; residents use it because changing the spelling would change the legal district boundary and risk another erasure. The two names refer to the same place.

### Open Yearspire

The post-ending public institution. It offers bounded effects funded by present contributions or a narrow future pledge made by a living person in person, maintains an open archive, and recognizes a refusal or withdrawal before a compact is sealed. It is not a promise that no one will ever make a difficult bargain.

## Drafting usage

- Say **year** when describing the cultural or economic unit.
- Say **yearline** when explaining what the unit actually measures.
- Say **borrowed effect** in general narration; use **vigor**, **craft**, **sequence**, or **record** when the mechanism matters.
- Say **settlement** for the collection event and **year scar** for the lasting consequence.
- Never call a future person a resource, coin, or empty vessel in narration unless a character uses that language critically.
- Do not use game-stat terms or real software jargon in System messages.
