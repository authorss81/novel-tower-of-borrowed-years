## Setting

Morrowfen, a city on a black peat sink in a salt basin, in **Morrowfen Reckoning Year 412**. The Yearspire stands on Stillstep Island and is the city's bank, courthouse, emergency office, archive, and power plant. The six public sections are the Threshold, the Tally Halls, the Season Vaults, the Witness Rooms, the Hollow Galleries, and the sealed Deep Engine. Principal districts: Tallow Ward, Bell Stair, Rainmarket, Crown Terrace, Hallow Ground (the Unwritten Quarter, missing from the tower's map and rolls), and the Underbank.

## Rules that must never bend

- A **loan** has five parts: need, pledge, scope, witness, settlement. A missing material term makes it false-witnessed, which makes the responsibility chain visible and contestable.
- Four forms: **vigor** (endurance, healing, resistance), **craft** (practiced skill from a possible working life), **sequence** (a favored local event, paid by a counterweight), and **record** (institutional recognition, not physical fact).
- A year is not a coin and the tower shows no numeric balance. Effects are real now and the cost is real later; neither cancels the other.
- A Yearwell cannot bring back the dead, invent impossible outcomes, show the one true future, safely move a living person backward, or erase a settled past.
- A valid loan cannot make a person immortal, resurrect the dead, or promise a victory no plausible future supports.
- **Ledger Sight** reads visible entries, witness chain, and immediate contradiction on physical contact. It never predicts, never gives percentages or verdicts, and costs headaches, tunnel vision, and a recent memory gap after repeated use.
- **Boundary Hold** suspends one named settlement briefly. The delay is stored in Marek's own yearline. When the hold ends, the contract's chain collects: the borrower, then the surety, then the living clerk who certified the entry when the chain is false-witnessed. A Hold adds nobody to that chain, so an unpayable debt still ends with the affected district and Marek carries the toll of the delay as a year scar. He chooses the settlement and the duration, never the destination, and the cost can never reach an uninvolved bystander.
- The **First Account** is a reserve created from the Last Convocation's lost future, pledged under the **Founder's Clause** with no living consent. It is an unappealed founding claim, and every use of it is false-witnessed in the strict sense.
- The crossing was made through a **one-way year seam**: in the country that no longer exists, the founders collapsed their own well inward. It cannot be aimed, reopened, or used to carry a living passenger twice, and its price is the missing year. The oldest surviving copies bear the signature **A-0**. The seam can be disclosed and voided. It cannot be recovered.
- The **Open Account** can exist only after the founding claim is released in the Deep Engine and a quorum of three independent witness rooms records that the Clause has no living consent chain. After that, an effect is funded by present contributions or a narrow pledge made by a living person in person, and a shortfall is paid by the public reserve.
- No new cosmic threat, god, or prophecy enters late. The final enemy is Edren Sable and the First Account system, seeded in Volume 1 through the A-0 signature.

### New Volume 01 local terms (setting detail, not new world rules)

- **the Counter-Clerks' Fund** and **the Fund's book** (the discharge book) — the mutual the counter-desk clerks keep, which pays a dead or hurt clerk's household and is the only thing in the building that turns an injury into a claim.
- **a recount** — the fourth clause of the settlement desk's standing practice: a supervisor's re-addition of a certified total, with a third-grade clerk present, entered in the desk's book with the hour, the clerk's name and the reason. Lawful.
- **a cause line** — the sentence on a recount's form saying why the recount was done, written before the arithmetic is checked.
- **the fourth chair** — a waiting-room position at the Morrowfen clinic. Only Milena Kelb's use of it is on the page.
- **a notching** — a physician's word for a ruled line crossed with even notches. Osk's book has one line about it and the line is the shortest in the book and it does not say what it is for.

### What Chapter 14 is, structurally

It is the one chapter in the batch with a **deliberate point-of-view change at a section break**: the first two-thirds are close-third on **Ondra Kelb** (with Marek entirely absent from the first half, as the batch card requires), and the last third is close-third on **Marek**, who enters the Fund's room at the last of the light. This is intentional and is not a slip. It is also the chapter where **eleven counter-clerks learn what a counter-signature certifies** and where Marek acquires a debt of a kind he has no procedure for: his name is now in the room of the counter-desk in connection with the ward file, and one of them was in the room in Chapter 20.

### The ward's scar examination, Chapter 19 — the method, which is a ward document

Three piles: **the Silt's years** (nobody's instrument, owed by the river), **the vigor clauses** (form number, date of draw, witnesses; lawful, witnessed, informed; twelve already showing a season's scar and nine of the twelve fuel-yard or bridge hands), and **the hold's years** (the fourteenth, six bells, one clerk, no form number, no instrument, everybody's body). The founding rule: **a scar has to be matched to an instrument and an instrument has to be matched to a clerk; a scar with no instrument behind it is somebody's private business and a scar with a schedule behind it is a line in a case.** The form's four rules, as written on the back of Rusk's second complaint sheet: *examine the body before the instrument; a scar is dated, not counted; where there is no instrument the scar is not recorded and the person is not asked to say; a person may stop at any point and the stopping is recorded as a stopping; an independent witness not paid out of the district account and not living in the yard signs the sheet, and two sheets are never kept by one person.* It was left on a trestle table at the Gate yard, and it is the seed of the individual-consent work in Chapter 35 and the school-roll argument in Chapter 37. **Forty-one people were asked what to call the third pile and gave forty-one answers; the most common was *the sixth to the fourteenth*; there is no agreed heading and a blank space is left at the top of the column.** Marek's own scar is in the third pile, in his own hand, dated the fourteenth, and **nobody remarks on it**.

### Neris Kade and Marek (Chapter 19) — locked

Their first afternoon of shared work, done badly for three hours and then well, and they still do not like each other. The argument neither of them wins is about what the third pile is called: she will not write *the clerk's* and Marek will not write *the ward's* or *unattributed*, and when he turns her own founding rule against her — a scar with no instrument behind it is somebody's private business — she uses it better than he did and makes him see that the description he is reaching for is the same shape as the invented reserve. Forty-one people were asked; nothing was published; a method was left in the ward. She says she has not forgiven him for Thursday and is not going to, and he writes it down. **No warmth, no physical affection, no instant trust, and no panel and no Ledger Sight in the chapter.**
