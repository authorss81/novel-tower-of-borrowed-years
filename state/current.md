The batch opens on consequence, in a clinic lane: a physician tells Marek in three words that there is no form, because no office owes a yearline mark, and a laundress in the fourth chair makes him say out loud, in her order, that her son countersigned a column and a clerk held up a settlement for six bells with no authority. The ward is two things at once and will not be one thing. Aldis Kern reads two lists in a yard, and the second is longer, and Marek tells four hundred people that holding up a settlement does not make water: the water came off a valve a Warden has broken open against a sealed order since the second of the rains, the ward owes nobody for the hold, and it owes a debt to that man it has not yet agreed to name. On the fifteenth the Trust's third floor compares two copies of the same assessment and finds the hole between them: page three was never served, so eleven unsigned sureties are set aside, which is good for the ward today and bad for the ward later, and the file's stated cause is no longer a reserve but an operator's failure — a Crown Terrace irrigation contractor's maintenance failure on account 4-Crown, every word of it true, and load-bearing for the lie, because it puts the ward's thirst on somebody else's contract. Marek asks for one small thing and gets it: both copies entered, the difference noted and not adjudicated. In exchange the settlement is certified lawful, the ward's lawful requirement for the peak night is one hundred and sixty hundreds of pints, and the ward drank four hundred and forty, so from that afternoon the levy looks deserved everywhere except in that room. Then the batch's belief breaks against a procedure rather than a man: a hand-copy is a certification, and there is a class of page no clerk may copy, so the perfect copy of page three is a second document and nobody in any room is permitted to produce it. He hires a nineteen-year-old runner for nothing and says her sister's name out loud. A supervisor offers him his brother's road work, in writing, with no name on it but his own, and he is frightened of how much he wanted it. A crewman on the north road takes the same vigor clause his brother took and his hands lock in the middle of a lift, and nobody has done anything wrong, and Marek files the name in the public petition book because he is tired of writing things that go in a drawer. A schoolmistress has been counting what a levy does to a school year for nine years and has four names; Marek wins her one week on the strength of a permanent record, and then cannot answer her one real question. He and a hostile examiner spend an afternoon doing a ward's scar examination badly and then well and leave a method on a trestle table. And on the twenty-first a lawful recount of the counter-desk totals is performed in a supervisor's room with a junior clerk present, and the cause line is written before the arithmetic is checked, and it says the totals were added from a defective schedule at the counter-desk — which is true, and which makes a boy of twenty the cause of a ward's shortage. Marek asks one question out loud, in front of the witness, and Hesper answers it truthfully, because a man who has told the truth about a document has never once been punished for it. Then Marek copies the cause line into his own book, knowing exactly what a second document is worth, and his hand fails. The recount sits in a drawer. The ward's arithmetic is in four hands and none of them agree. And in the back margin of his own book, in pencil, a clerk who has found something on himself writes it down.
