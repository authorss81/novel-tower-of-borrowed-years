## The volume's close, in one paragraph

A ward of about four hundred people was short of water in flood season, and a Deputy Settlement Steward settled it lawfully on a standing reserve that does not exist, and the cost of that settlement was drawn from eleven named households of a ward that had never been served with the schedule naming them. A third-grade clerk put a one-word answer to a twenty-word query and made it collectible. A junior counter-clerk put a pencil to a column that was correct. Six bells of an unauthorized delay were held by a man with no authority, and the delay went into his own arm, and the collection went to the boy, and the six bells have never had a forum and still have not. The ward went out into nineteen lanes and knocked on forty-one doors and got twenty-nine answers and three refusals, and one of the refusals was the finding of the volume. A Crown March representative, who can call a meeting and cannot pay anybody out of it, made a room, and a woman from the Trust who was right about the law and wrong about the consequence, and wrong about the arithmetic in a way that turned out to be load-bearing, argued him down in procedure and lost in the yard. A supervisor of twenty-two years read his own entry aloud in a room built for thirty and said the number of people he had found dead in nine minutes, and was walked out of the room and asked, on his way, whether the ward had water. The entry was struck from the public copy and not from the book. The bill became work. The valve went into the record in one paragraph with the damage it did. A brother got a schedule and not a refund. A boy of twenty got a sum of money and a hand-book on the wrong hand. And at the last of the light a man copied a blank line out of the oldest book in the city into the public register by hand and wrote the signature underneath it in full, because a blank page is not a blank line, and nobody appointed him to anything.
