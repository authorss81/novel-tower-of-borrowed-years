Last batch summary: **What the Valve Held (Chapters 21–30).** MR 412, the twenty-second to the thirtieth of the rains. The batch is the volume's midpoint and it turns the volume over twice, in opposite directions from where the reader expects. It opens by closing the last procedural door: the Trust's investigation is opened on a sealed notice whose four terms are the account, the maintenance record, the yard counters and such other books as the Deputy Steward directs — arithmetic and nothing whatever about conduct, with the hold notice on the public board not in it — and Renata Vole, playing an administrator solving a scheduling problem rather than a villain, offers Marek the settlement desk's fourth clause: a twenty-nine-word statement that the Tallow file was never assigned to him, in exchange for striking his certificate of posting, worth exactly four households. He does the arithmetic on the temptation in about forty seconds, asks for the terms in writing, and on the twenty-fourth puts both papers on the *answered* side of her own tray and writes *not signed* — and the reason, stated in the prose, is not conscience but the discovery that **the statement is twenty-nine words long and every one of them is true**, and a man who signs a true sentence because a superior has told him it costs nobody anything will be told by the next man who reads it that he did. At the Threshold the ward's petition of three hundred and eleven signatures is refused on standing orders with no cruelty in it, in the sentence that carries the rest of the volume: **a ward cannot petition, appeal, swear or be cross-examined, and every form in the hall has a box for a person in it and there is no box for Tallow Ward.** The batch then spends the ward's clock. Marda Senn's two chalk columns are ruled out of use twice and she goes into the removed records after the figure nobody will write down, and comes out with it — an unmetered take-off above the ward meter, eleven years old, two hundred hundreds of pints a night climbing a hill into the Crown Terrace north gardens — and that evening **her name is struck off the warden's roll** by a Warden with a rule and no form for a cause. Ilya tells Marek on the platform that the intake has been drawing short since the second and cannot say by how much because he is not a clerk. Kern reads forty items of the night of the fourteenth to a yard with the Underbank on top: a condemned storehouse, and then Vasil Ferun, thirteen, with a shin broken in two places, who does not want it named and is named anyway, and then the sluice. When Marek offers to certify the sheet and get it into the investigation, **Kern refuses him, lawfully and correctly, in front of four hundred people, because a certified incomplete account sets the settlement aside and the ward would pay the whole bill instead of the excess** — and the sheet stays on a board in forty hands, a memory nobody can revise and nobody can produce. Then the fence comes down in public with no accusation in it: a reading is not a document, it is a column somebody copies off a dial; a maintenance is a thing somebody did; one does not cause the other; the screen really was foul, the account really was wrong, and two hundred hundreds of pints a night has been going into a walled garden since the second of the rains because that is what an open valve does to a garden. Vole corrects the account in the Threshold vestibule to 440 against a lawful 160, an excess of 280 against the ward's own roll of 279 hundreds and 40 pints — **sixty pints in nine days** — and the take-off's account turns out to be 4-Crown with nine gardeners' wages on it. The midpoint turns in 25 and 26 and turns in the direction nobody wants. Hesper names the four people he found in nine minutes on the ninth of the rains and does not apologise, and Marek goes and reads the plate on the Tallow Gate keystone, which says the notice is thirty days and that the current season's notice expired on the twelfth, and which has said so out loud on a wall for eleven years; **the man Marek has spent thirteen days trying to destroy was right about one enormous thing and did not care in the slightest.** Meanwhile the ward divides, and Kern gives evidence against the man who fed it — not lawfully delivered, and the ward will pay every hundred pints of it at the ordinary wage — because a beneficiary of somebody else's breach pays the whole of what it took, and because naming the valve is the surest way to get Ilya named; he does not enjoy it, and Ilya spends two hours of a second high water holding a board in the mouth of the Underbank passage with the water above his knee. Ilya's hearing is a maintenance account with a man attached, and he refuses the tidying sentence in front of the party, in front of the ward, and to his own wages, because the third part of it is false: signing it would make the tidiest instrument in Morrowfen the end of the list. **Neris Kade puts 441 into the hearing record beside the certified 440, because in this building things die when the only person holding them stops breathing.** At the yard that evening the ward settles on paying and not thanking, keeps its own roll in forty hands, and refuses to let either Kade or Marek hold it, and Ilya says he never wanted the frame, because in six weeks somebody will notice a rising bill, a rising river and a closed valve and work out that the sensible thing was to shut it. The batch's discovery is institutional and it is the volume's: the invented reserve is not only a line somebody can lean on, **it is a line a district has been paying for three hundred years** — a reserve carry of forty thousand nine hundred, entered every year, unapplied, unclaimable, held against a standing reserve nobody has ever found, with no form anywhere that asks whether the fund exists — and the office balances the account by cutting an order that has **no copy**, gets cheaper as it goes down, and puts the north gardens' crew ninth and the ward school last. The batch ends on the thirtieth with the finding served in an open yard: settlement lawful, reserve honoured against the water office's own account and not against the households, delivery corrected, excess 280, **the delay of the fourteenth not within the terms with no account of it arising** — because there is no office in the city that holds a delay held without authority against an instrument that does not exist — and the collection to proceed in the ordinary course **and it may not be entered before the cause is entered in the box at the head of the form**. Bohdan Rezek, whose hands seized on the ninth of the rains, asks for her name to be read before his and then for his yard, which has none. Marek is told the sitting is the twelfth of next month and that he is the clerk of the day for it. He has nine words in a book he cannot show. Hesper is kind to him on the stair and tells him to bring his book shut. **Nothing is filed. The box is empty. There are twelve days.**
