# Review — Volume 02, Batch 0003 (Chapters 71–80)

Reviewer pass on the finished batch, followed by a fix pass over the prose and the artefacts.
**Prose is authoritative.** Where a card, a state file or a prompt disagreed with a chapter,
the chapter won and the artefact was corrected. No planned plot movement was changed, no
chapter was restarted, no scene was cut, and no good prose was replaced — every repair is a
line-level substitution, and the two structural repairs (Ch 77's hold, Ch 79→80's date break)
keep their scenes, their order and their endings intact.

This is the third volume-02 review and the second in a row in which the prose was largely sound
and the **artefacts were asserting things the prose does not say**. Batch 0002 found six
instances of that in its own files. Batch 0003's writer found nine and closed them, and the
review then found **four more, three of them inherited from the writer's own pass and one of
them load-bearing for the next batch.**

## Verdict

Ten finished scenes, ten different engines, no long table, no document read out to a room as a
load-bearing event, **zero Ledger Sight panels and two lawful Boundary Holds**, no manufactured
villain, and a batch that ends on a cost rather than a resolution. **The batch stands.**

The prose defects were real but narrow: one chapter's calendar had collapsed, one month leaked
through two verification passes, one character had two ages, and a Rainmarket woman was quoting
Tallow Ward's numbers. **None of them touched plot, character or the volume's argument.** The
artefact defects were worse than the prose defects, because the artefacts are what the next
phase reads as fact, and one of them had put a romance guardrail into `batch-0004/PROMPT.md`
built on a statement that was not true.

## The pattern, stated once, for the third time

**A verification sentence in a state file is a claim about the prose, never a substitute for
reading it.** `state/continuity.md` for this batch opens with that rule in bold, written by the
same phase about the same risk, and then breaks it in this specific way three times:

1. It records the Ch 77 month fix as *I have known it since March* → *since the spring*. What
   had actually happened is that a **different sentence eleven lines earlier** had been changed
   and this one had not. *Since March* stood in Halda Brnec's mouth in the finished chapter.
   The file recorded the fix **by the shape of the sentence rather than by the sentence.**
2. `state/open-threads.md` said the Ch 77 hold ran *the eighth to the eleventh* — a start date
   the chapter never stated, silently chosen so that "three days" would work around the
   chapter's own "on the fourth." The artefact was right and the prose was wrong, which is worse
   than the reverse, because the artefact is what gets believed.
3. `state/current.md` and `batch-0004/PROMPT.md` both stated that **Kade and Marek were never
   in the same scene in Chapters 71–80.** They share the field in Ch 71 and **Ch 72 is a
   sustained two-hander.** The batch's central relationship beat was denied by the brief for
   the batch that follows it.

**The rule is now recorded three times over, in three files, and it is still worth writing a
reviewer for.** A batch 0004 reviewer should check whether its own artefacts survive contact
with its own prose, and should not assume this note was decoration.

## Prose repairs, in the review's order of priority

**1. The false Kade/Marek claim, and the prompt built on it.** Corrected in `state/current.md`,
in `state/open-threads.md` and in `workspace/volume-02/batch-0004/PROMPT.md`. The batch-0004
romance guardrail previously said *"if a second disagreement happens here it must be smaller
and quieter still"* on the premise that there had been only one. There have been two: Ch 68's,
and Ch 72's on a flat above a mill at night, where neither of them wins. **The guardrail now
names the real count.**

**2. `since March`, the month leak that survived two passes.** Ch 77 line 17 → *since the seed
month.* The chapter already says "the spring" in the sentence before and the sentence after, so
the leak was sitting in a hole cut for it. A grep of Chapters 71–80 for the twenty month and
weekday names now returns **only *Tuesday*, six times**, all of it Kade's Chapter 52 figure of
speech — which is the claim three artefacts were making, and which is now true rather than
asserted.

**3. Chapter 77's day arithmetic, broken in five places.** The hold was placed on the fourth,
ended on the eleventh, and was called three days, which is seven. Alongside it: *"he has been up
here four days"*, *"came up a road for one day and stayed four"*, *"you have been on this
holding four days"*, and *"about a sixth of what he wanted"* against the chapter's own *"three
days out of ten."*

**Repaired structurally, not cosmetically.** The hold is now placed on the **ninth** and ends at
the **second bell of the eleventh**, which is three days and preserves the thesis line
*What he bought was three days out of ten. What it cost was ten* untouched. The fraction is now
**a third**.

**The repair forced a real find.** Ch 74's cart hold is the second to the sixth bell of the
**eighth** of the growth month, and Ch 77 keeps Marek in the Red Uplands across that day — the
original chapter had him doing both. So Ch 77 now states the two days out: *On the seventh Marek
went down off the terrace with the borrowed boots in the bag, because there was a cart on the
low-ground road that wanted doing at the second bell and the road up is a day's road.* He is
back up on the ninth. **The four things the holding did while he was gone are no longer narrated
in his presence — Malnar tells him all four in ninety words on the evening of the ninth.** That
is a gain and not a loss: it is the first time anybody in this batch tells Marek what happened
to him. The growth month's day numbers are now load-bearing and are recorded as such in
`state/open-threads.md` and `state/current.md`.

**4. The Ch 79 → Ch 80 date break.** Ch 79 closed by promising a seed house yard on the morning
of **the twenty-first** — a day that cannot exist in a growth month that Ch 79 itself fixes at
twenty days — thirteen days before Ch 80's third day of the harvest month. Ch 79 now says **the
third day of next month**. Three stranded figures inside Ch 80 were re-anchored in the same
family and are logged in `state/continuity.md`: *I looked again on the eighteenth* (three days
into the month) → **yesterday**; *a sheet for three days* → **since the first of this month**;
*ten days ago at a gate on this road* (which resolves to the twenty-third of a twenty-day month)
→ **a month ago**. Two more of the same kind were caught in the same pass and are recorded:
*the twenty-first of last month* in Fisk's mouth → **the twenty-first of the seed month**, and
*in the second week* for a hired cart that had to have come down before the nineteenth sowing →
**in the second week of the growth month**.

**5. Chapter 72's two ages.** The boy's mother was *twenty-five now* in one speech and *she is
fifty-one* in three others. Three to one is not a reason, so the arithmetic was checked: a girl
of fourteen, eleven years to find out, and a boy of nineteen. At twenty-five she could not be his
mother. She is now **twenty-five when she came to Vrabec in the winter and fifty-one now** —
the same scene, the same refusal, and a longer grievance. The *"four of them are over fifty"* on
the valley's roll is untouched, because the roll is not the household.

**6. Chapter 75's six days.** *"I am going to do a thing in about six days"* for an event the
next paragraph dates to the eleventh, ten days out, in a chapter that now dates itself to the
first. → **in ten days**.

**7. Chapter 73's stolen numbers.** Ilma Tresch, a low-ground Share member in a Rainmarket
clinic, said she was *"one of the three hundred and seventy-seven that nobody has ever asked
anything"* and cited the door rounds. **377 of 406 is Tallow Ward's Gate tank figure** (Vol 01
Ch 42 and 46, Vol 02 Ch 60, 63, 64) and `state/current.md` says in terms that these are Tallow's
figures and not Rainmarket's. Replaced with the reason that is actually hers: *sixty-one people
put a thumb to a seal in the bad autumn and eleven of us got a line out of it in a field on the
sixth, and nobody asked one of the eleven anything.* **Same beat, same rhythm, her own facts.**

**8. Chapter 73's unnamed seamstress.** A seamstress mended a sleeve in the clinic and two lines
later Marek *"did not ask Tamsin Vale anything"*, with nothing on the page joining them. The
seamstress is now named in the sentence before he does not ask her, and the reader can place
her. **Tamsin is still asked nothing, which is the point of the scene and is unchanged.**

**9. Two fifth boxes and no sign they were different forms.** Ch 74's factor's pledge form puts
the settlement in its fifth box; the Trust's seasonal advance form puts a *return* in its fifth
box at the foot; **and Ch 79's climax turns on which box means what.** Not a contradiction —
different documents — but the page did not say so, and a reader who has held the first form is
silently handed half of the second. Ch 74 now says the form is a factor's and is not the
Trust's and never has been, and Ch 76 says the sheet in the cart yard is a different form cut in
the same five parts. **A third form exists and is already distinguished in Ch 75: the Crown
March house cut its own and its own had seven boxes.** All three are now listed in
`batch-0004/PROMPT.md`, because Ch 79's argument is Movement IV's inheritance.

## The two outline divergences, now logged

The review found that the batch logged one outline divergence and that Volume 01's practice is to
log each one. `outline/volume-02.md` is not this phase's file and was not edited. Both are now
recorded in `state/continuity.md`:

- **Ch 77's card says *A year later, in a body*.** The chapter is nine days later. *In a body* is
  delivered in Ch 73, *paid for by a man who did not read the schedule* is delivered exactly in
  Malnar's own yard, twice, and *longer* is delivered — three days against four bells. A year
  later was not available: Ch 76 is the twelfth of the same growth month, the frost is four days
  old, and the Share's eleven days of labour are on the sixth. **Nothing in Movements IV or V may
  inherit the year.**
- **Ch 80's card says *the cost lands where the chain says it lands*.** It lands on eleven Share
  households in the Rainmarket low ground, and pointedly not on Tallow Ward, which is where the
  chain's ninth link points. The card's other clause — *Marek watches it happen and cannot
  redirect it* — is delivered exactly, in a lane at the sixth bell. **The divergence is the
  batch's argument, not an accident: the low ground is charged by a cooperative's own rule out of
  a cooperative's own book, and the ninth link is a district account nobody can serve notice on,
  and Marek's silence is about the second and not the first.** Movement V owns the arrival and
  Ch 93 owns the finding out; neither may be moved forward to make Ch 80's cost land on the ward.

  2,566 · Ch 74 3,284 · Ch 75 2,905 · Ch 76 3,084 · Ch 77 3,176 · Ch 78 2,793 · Ch 79 3,168 · Ch 80

  `state/current.md`, `state/continuity.md`, `state/open-threads.md` and the batch-0004 prompt —
  was corrected in the same pass. **A repair that changes a counted thing must recount it, and this
  repository has now had a length figure wrong in four separate ways.**
- **A caution for the next reviewer, and it is a real trap.** *Crown March* is a street in Bell
  Stair. **A plain grep for `\bMarch\b` over Chapters 71–80 returns twenty-five hits and every one
  of them is the place name.** The month check has to exclude the proper noun first. Run
  correctly, the batch contains no real-world month name and no weekday other than *Tuesday*,
  six times. **The reviewer's own first pass at this check was confounded and had to be redone,
  and the artefacts' "the grep now returns only Tuesday" claim was only ever verifiable with the
  exclusion in place.**
- **No filler-word tics.** `held` 7, `steady`/`plain`/`breathed` 0 across 30,279 words.
- **Guardrail greps hold.** No meta phrasing, no Kallow, no district four, no A-0, no Choir, no
  Saltglass, no Pella Rusk, no markdown bold in narration. `forearm` absent; the two `wrist` hits

- **Panel budget met at zero**, both holds on lawful instruments, neither mark decoded, Tamsin
  asked nothing, the founding standing hold not wished for.
- **The Tuesday count checks out** — six, all Kade's.
- **Pipeline discipline is clean.** No controller file touched. Exactly one next artefact

- **The batch-0004 prompt is genuinely good** apart from the one false inheritance: it encodes the
  hold lesson, forbids a second Ledger Sight panel, names Ch 87 as a trap, and is explicit about
