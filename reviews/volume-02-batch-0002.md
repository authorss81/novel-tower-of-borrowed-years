# Review — Volume 02, Batch 0002 (Chapters 61–70)

Reviewer pass on the finished batch, followed by a fix pass over the prose and the artefacts.
**Prose is authoritative.** Where a card, a state file or a prompt disagreed with a chapter,
the chapter won and the artefact was corrected. No planned plot movement was changed, no
chapter was restarted, and no scene was cut, rewritten or padded.

The review's own findings are A1–A7 and B1–B3, in its order of priority. The fix pass found
**two more instances of B1** that the review had undercounted, and **one consequential fix
that the review's own correction forced** and did not see.

## Verdict

Ten finished scenes, 26,609 words inside the 24,000–28,000 plan, ten different engines, no
long table and no document read out to a room as a load-bearing event, the Ledger Sight and
Boundary Hold budgets met at **zero**, no manufactured villain, and a batch that ends on a
cost rather than a resolution. **The batch stands.** What was wrong was bookkeeping again, and
bookkeeping in this repository propagates: the artefacts the *next* phase reads were wrong in
ways that would have been inherited as fact, and in six cases the artefact was asserting
something **the chapter does not say**.

## The pattern, stated once

Every serious finding in this batch is the same failure. A state file stated a thing the prose
does not contain, and the statement was written in the confident register of a quotation.
Batch 0002's own `continuity.md` contains the sentence *"A self-reported pass in this file is
not evidence"* — written by the same phase, in the same section, about the same risk — and then
broke it six times inside two hundred lines.

**The rule now recorded in `state/continuity.md`: every clause in that section is a quotation
or it is a note about a quotation, and a note that cannot be pointed at a line does not go in.**

## A. Errors inside the phase's own artefacts

### A1 — Word counts were wrong, rounded *down*, and three derived claims were false

Recorded in both `state/current.md` and `state/continuity.md`: total 26,258, distribution
`61 3,146 · 62 2,958 · 63 2,343 · 64 2,341 · 65 2,384 · 66 2,210 · 67 3,105 · 68 2,432 ·
69 2,196 · 70 3,143`. Actual prose, title line excluded: `3,138 · 2,925 · 2,401 · 2,336 ·
2,425 · 2,200 · 3,249 · 2,659 · 2,115 · 3,161` = **26,609** after the fix pass. The total was
under-reported by 335 words; Ch 67 by 144 and Ch 68 by 225.

Three claims built on those numbers were therefore false, and all three are gone:

- *"none is over 3,150"* — Ch 67 is 3,249 and Ch 70 is 3,161.
- *"Chapter 70 is the only one over the 3,100 allowance Chapters 62, 66 and 70 were given,
  by 43 words"* — 61, 67 and 70 are all above 3,100, and 70 is 61 words over.
- `continuity.md`'s *"Chapters 62, 66 and 70 came in at or just over the 3,100 allowance"* —
  62 is 2,925 and 66 is 2,200, both under it.

`workspace/volume-02/batch-0003/PROMPT.md` says *"record the honest figure … and do not round it
down."* It was rounded down, in the same file family the prompt tells the next writer to
trust. Both state files now carry the counted figures, the three-chapter-above-3,100 fact, and
a note that the counts must be taken from the files. **The reviewer's figures were themselves
taken before the fix pass added words, so the numbers in the state files are the post-fix
ones and the review's list is the pre-fix one; both are recorded rather than reconciled to
each other.**

### A2 — `continuity.md` invented a calendar anchor Volume 01 does not contain

The batch 0002 section told batch 0003 to *"count from Volume 01's only anchor — the ninth of
the rains is a Wednesday."* Across all fifty Volume 01 chapters *Wednesday* appears ten
times, always as an ordinary weekday (*"on the Wednesday"*, *"competently, on a Wednesday"*).
Nothing ties it to the ninth of the rains.

**The anchor's origin is this repository's own batch 0001 review**, finding 15, which recorded
it as fact and told the next writer to use it. That review has been marked at the head rather
than edited, so the record stands and the correction is visible.

Two things were wrong with it, and the second is the one that mattered:

1. It is an inference from a review pass, not a fact in the prose.
2. **It could not have worked anyway.** Counting from the ninth of the rains through the
   thirty-one-day rains month reaches the seed month. The batch 0003 writer needs weekdays for
   the growth month and the ninth month, and **the lengths of the months in between are fixed
   nowhere in the outline, the bible or the manuscript.** A rule that cannot reach the dates a
   writer needs is worse than no rule, because it looks like a rule.

Both the batch 0002 section and the still-live batch 0001 section carried the instruction; both
are corrected, with the reasoning left in place so the error cannot be re-derived. **A later
batch names no weekday**, except as a figure of speech about a kind of occasion, which is how
Ch 52 and Ch 63 already use one.

### A3 — `continuity.md` cited a *Saturday* in Chapter 64 that is not there, and contradicted itself

The calendar bullet said the weekday words were *Tuesday* in Ch 63 **and** *Saturday* in Ch
64's account of a household that signed in a lane. **Ch 64 has no weekday word.** The same
file's later bullet — *"a grep for weekday words returns one instance"* — was the correct one,
so the two state files disagreed with each other. Corrected: one weekday word in the batch, and
the reason it is not a claim about a day.

### A4 — *"In about eleven days"* is a phantom figure, and it was in two state files

`continuity.md` and `chapter-summaries.md` both reported Droll telling Marek that the
usefulness would be asked about *"in about eleven days."* `chapter-0063.md` does not contain
it; her speech ends *ask the man in the yard, he had it first*. The only *eleven* in Ch 63 is
*"eleven minutes"* at line 17.

This is the most dangerous shape the pattern takes, because the phrase sounds like a
character's forecast and would have licensed a later batch to put a date on the Tuesday — the
one thing Ch 63 refuses to do. Corrected in both files, and **both now state that she names no
date and that no later artefact may put one there.** `open-threads.md` carried the same phrase
and is corrected too.

### A5 — `continuity.md` gave Droll a role the chapter gives to somebody else

The locked-names list had Sanne Droll as *"one of the yard's readers of the refusal."*
`chapter-0064.md:41` says the refusal was read out twice, *"once by the keeper and once by the
reader, and the reader was nineteen years old"* — that is **Teodor Lask**, named as such in
Ch 63 and 64. Droll holds her sheet at the trestle and later writes the Ropewalk woman's words
on the back of one. Corrected, with Lask's role stated explicitly so the two cannot swap again.

### A6 — `open-threads.md` turned a refusal into a hard deadline

Carried into batch 0003 as: *"the answer is wanted on the eleventh day after it came, which is
the last day of the seed month."* `chapter-0067.md:83` is built to refuse exactly that: the
referral *would be in the tray on the twenty-second and on the twenty-ninth, and the eleventh
day after it came was the eleventh day after it came.* **No deadline exists in the prose, and
nothing anywhere fixes the length of the seed month, so "the last day" was unsupported as
well.** A later writer told there was a due date would have manufactured one and then had to
resolve it. Corrected, with the sentence quoted so the intent cannot be misread, and with the
prohibition stated: nobody may put a due date on the referral that is not in the prose, and no
chapter may call any day the seed month's last.

### A7 — `continuity.md` gave Ivo Sarn an age nowhere in the prose

*"Ivo Sarn, 51, Crown March officer"* sat in a locked-names list where prose is supposed to be
authoritative. Ch 62 gives only *"eleven years at this"* and states no age. The age is removed
and the entry now says **do not give him one**, since a name list is exactly where an invented
figure would be inherited.

## B. Prose and world consistency

### B1 — Real-world month names were leaking into a world of ordinal and seasonal months

Morrowfen's calendar is the seed month, the rains month, the growth month, the harvest month,
and the second, third and ninth months. **No real-world month name appears anywhere in
`outline/` or `bible/`.** The review counted seven. A full grep finds **nine**, and all nine
are fixed: the review's seven, one more in a chapter the review had not opened (**Ch 64**), and
one in **Ch 58** that is a different kind of find — it is the same event as two chapters the
review *had* opened, and it is only visible once those two are renumbered.

| Chapter | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ch 55 (batch 0001) | borrow again **in August** | **in the growth month** |
| Ch 64 | clear nine hundred more **in August** | **in the growth month** |
| Ch 65 | dry it **in June instead of April** | **in the growth month instead of the seed month** |
| Ch 65 | a man in a good coat **in September** | **in the ninth month** |
| Ch 66 | **in September** somebody is going to stand in a room | **in the ninth month** |
| Ch 58 (batch 0001) | **shut up in June** | **shut up in the growth month** |
| Ch 68 | four feet of water, **dried in June** | **dried in the growth month** |
| Ch 70 | what was going to happen **in September** … about the ninth month | **in the ninth month** … about **the ninth month before** |

Three things are worth recording about how these were fixed.

**The replacements are not cosmetic.** *September* and *August* were standing in for the
volume's own clock, so they became **the ninth month** and **the growth month** — the two
months this volume is actually about. Ch 66's accountant telling Marek what this office is
going to be asked *in the ninth month* is sharper than *in September*, because the ninth month
is the month this volume's clock runs on.

**Ch 70 had both calendars in one sentence** — *going to happen in September* in the same
breath as *the only sentence anybody in this city had put on paper about the ninth month* — and
a straight substitution would have produced *in the ninth month … about the ninth month* twice
in one paragraph. It now reads *the ninth month* and *the ninth month before*, which keeps the
emphasis and loses the collision.

**Ch 58 had to move with Ch 65 and Ch 68, and the review did not see it.** Ch 58 says the shed
was *"dried out afterwards by being shut up in June"*; Ch 65 says the decision was *"to dry it
in June instead of April"*; Ch 68 reads the bench book's *dried in June* aloud to a girl of
sixteen. These are **one event in three chapters**. Renumbering two of them would have left the
third asserting a different month for the same shed, and the third is the chapter where the
truth is finally said out loud. All three now say the same thing: the stock was dried in the
growth month instead of at sowing, which is why eleven trays went to sand.

### B2 — Chapter 64 contradicted its own count two paragraphs apart

Line 49, a speaker at the trestle: *"we are four hundred and six people and three hundred and
seventy-seven of us have never been asked a question by anybody"* — which matches
`ch-0063:49`. Line 57, Sanne Droll: *"The four hundred and six of us have never been asked."*

**The twenty-nine the first round did ask disappear, in the same scene**, in the volume whose
engine is precisely who has and has not been asked. Now: *"The four hundred and six of us have
not all been asked."* The rest of her speech — the man from Bell Stair who cannot be asked
either, and the refusal having no man's hand on it anywhere — is untouched and still lands.

### B3 — Minor rounding habit

`state/current.md` said Merrow spoke *"in front of a hundred people"*; `ch-0069` says *about a
hundred and forty* twice. Corrected.

## C. Correctly flagged by the batch, and now closed in the prose

**Chapter 58's word count.** The batch recorded — accurately — that Ch 58 sets *A district is
not a person* in pencil and calls it *four words*, which is six, and correctly declined to
propagate the figure. Two batches have now flagged it and it was still open on disk, which
means flagging was not enough. **Both instances now read *six words*.** Chapters 61–70 never
repeat the count, and **Chapter 79 is the payoff of this line and may not depend on a word
count** — now recorded in three state files, because a later writer reading Ch 58 fresh would
otherwise be tempted to build the payoff on the number.

## What was working, and was not touched

**`workspace/volume-02/batch-0003/PROMPT.md` was checked against every finding and is clean of
them.** It never repeats the Wednesday anchor, never gives Sarn an age, never carries the
*eleven days*, and contains no real-world month name. Its one line on this subject —
*"record the honest figure in `state/current.md` and do not round it down"* — was correct and
was the rule the batch broke, so the prompt needed no change and the state files did. The
prompt is the next phase's artefact and has deliberately not been edited. **The calendar rule
for the next batch was put where the prompt already sends the writer: `state/open-threads.md`,
item 13 of *Carried into batch 0003*.**

Panel discipline is exact and better than the budget asked: **zero Ledger Sight panels, zero
Boundary Holds, the founding standing hold not referred to, and Marek's forearm not described,
referred to or looked at once in ten chapters.** The second cost of the power is paid entirely
in usefulness — a room withdrawn, a statement in someone else's record, a rope-maker saying a
true thing to his face in front of two hundred people — and the Sight is used for none of it.

There is no villain and none was manufactured. Merrow, Ferrow, Sarn, Vey, Grale, Fisk, Lund,
Droll and Kelb each act correctly and each cost the reader something, and the only criminal in
the batch is a rule that puts a cost in a box with no person in it. **Ovid Merrow ends the
batch more useful than he began it and is refuted by nothing**, which is the hardest thing in
this volume to do and the batch does it. No office is closed, no licence withdrawn, nobody
convicted, the Compact not dissolved, and the chain does not arrive.

The engine note from batch 0001 was met and is worth repeating as a standard: **ten chapters,
ten engines, no long table, and no chapter whose load-bearing event is a document read out to
a room.** The one reading in the batch is Sarn's minute in Ch 62, and it is the chapter's
institutional object rather than its set-piece.

The prose itself is good. The refusal in Ch 64 is the argument of the volume with no villain in
it. Ch 67's cut order is a physical institutional object and a man's hand failing on the last
letter of his own name. Ch 69 is a rival register that is better than the reader expects and
that nobody refutes. **Nothing in this pass touched a sentence for style.**

## What is still open, and now owned rather than overlooked

  54, 55, 59). The Volume 01 instances are inert. **The cluster is not, and it is deliberately
  left alone:** which ordinal month the seed market falls in decides whether it falls before or
  after sowing, and that in turn decides whether the instrument's eighteen months and the
  widow's tears in the Witness Room still sit where Ch 51 puts them. **A batch writer renaming
  it in passing would be making a canon decision, not fixing a typo.** It is recorded in
  `current.md`, `continuity.md` and `open-threads.md` as the outline owner's item, and a
  standing rule now says no batch may add a real-world month name anywhere.
- **The cut-order numbering divergence** between `outline/volume-02.md`'s Chapter 67 card and
  its concrete resolution — *second cut order* against *the water office's second cut of the
  year* — is unchanged, because the outline is authoritative and not this phase's to edit. The
  prose reads Ch 67 as **the first cut of MR 413 and Grale's second signed order in his life**,
  and leaves the second cut *of the year*, with no officer on it, to Ch 98. **Chapters 81–100
  must keep that reading.** Recorded in three places, as before.
- **No figure exists for how many of the twenty-two recertification files Marek finished, and
  none may be invented.** Ch 61 states the refusal in Kelb's mouth instead of a number, and the

- **The Trust's own instrument carrying a counterweight, and Renata Vole's first-person letter**
