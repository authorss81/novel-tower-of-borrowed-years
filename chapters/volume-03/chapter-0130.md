# Chapter 130 — The Two Harvests

The carts turned round on the ninth of the harvest month.

That is the whole of what happened in Rainmarket in the space of a week and nobody announced it. On the low-ground road there is a stone platform with a beam on two posts, and Ottmar Kuez has stood on it for twenty-two years, and he reads the rate out to every cart that crosses, and he will not say the figure aloud, because it is written in a bridge book with a day against every line and nobody has ever come to look at the book.

On the ninth of the harvest month a cart went up the low-ground road with a full bed of straw on it, and Kuez read the rate out to the driver, and about six other drivers waiting behind it heard the same number, and a cart that afternoon came *down* the same road with a full bed of barley on it, and the driver of that cart was on his way to a threshing floor four miles away and had come up out of a field that had been golden in the sixth month and had the look of a thing that had been left out in the rain.

The rate had been about a third lower than it had been nine days in the tenth month of MR 413, and it had stood for four days before the fifth of the second month of MR 414, and it had stood since, and it did not fall when the carts came up and it did not rise when they came down, and nobody in a lane said anything about it and nobody went and looked at the bridge book, and that has never once happened in this basin in nine years.

---

The low ground's second sowing did not come, and about a fifth of the first did not come either, and the third was thin and slow and about half of it never made a sheaf.

There was no announcement. There is no office in this basin that sends anybody to tell a district it is short, and no form on which a person may be told that a field was paid for a loan that the field did not know it was on, and the road out of Rainmarket was busy and the barges were running and the depot's flag was on the mast, and what a person in Rainmarket could have told you on the ninth of the harvest month — if you had asked them, and a good half of them had been asked — is that a chaff stall on the low-ground road had been shut four days running, and then a week, and then that it was not a stall any more and the man who kept it had gone to work on the loading floor, and that the rate had not moved.

Petar Duss was hauling straw up out of the low ground on the ninth with two carts and a rope round his wrist, and he was the only man in this basin with a mark in a foreign calendar on a paper of his own, and he could not get a line in about it in a shed eleven feet by nine, and he said nothing about either of those two things to anybody, and he has never once said that he was disappointed, and the office in the hills would want him in a room in about five weeks.

---

The four terraces had a harvest.

That is the other half and there is no figure for it either. The first terrace's barley came in off a ground that had gone brackish in the growth month and the owner of it stood at the rail with a jar on it for nine days and then took it inside and did not mention it again. The second terrace threshed on the shared floor on the sixth, the seventh and the eighth, and the floor is line three on a board in a loft at Hartlow and it is a shared floor and it has never belonged to anybody, and about a terrace and a half of straw went off it in three days.

The third terrace was cut by nine men who did not live on it, on the second to the fifth, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a cutting can be entered as having been done, and no name on the board, and no office that could be told.

The fourth terrace's return said the sowing went in on ground that had been warm for nine days, and that is the only sentence in the three of them that anybody in this basin has ever bothered to write down.

And the nine lots that had been sand since the seed month on a shoulder above a new drain were in it, and a woman of thirty-one with a store with a lock stood at the edge of a shared floor with a child on her hip and did not say one word to anybody about it, and there were nine rows on that shoulder that four people in Rainmarket had said out loud in eleven different kitchens that had come up for no reason anybody could give, and a field cannot be cross-examined, and a season can be pointed at a household, and there is no form on either of those.

---

Petrus Lenge came up eleven switchbacks on the tenth of the harvest month with a returns sheet in a satchel and a slate in the other hand, and he got as far as the bridge before Marek Vale, who had been in the valley since the sixth, and stood in the road and said, without preamble and without any of the four or five sentences he had prepared on the way up:

"It is a good harvest and it is nothing like a district, and I have got a bad one in my bag that is going to be a district, and both of them go on one line, and I would like you to tell me before we go up that road which of the two you think I should write first."

Lenge put the satchel down on the wall.

"I am going to tell you what I am going to write first," he said, "and it is the one I wrote first the last two years as well, and I am not going to change it this year for a reason, and the reason is that a book that has one number in it and a wrong number can be corrected in ink in about four seconds with two initials against it, and a book with a hole in it is a book that says out loud that the office cannot do its job, and the office can do its job. I have been at this four years and I have a man on the floor who has been at it nineteen who comes in at the second bell and says *is that the third one* and goes home, and neither of us has ever once closed a book with a hole in it, and I would like it noticed that nobody has ever asked us to."

"You have a district in that bag with no return in it."

"I have a district in that bag that returned a bad one and is going to be entered at the ten-year figure, and I am permitted to do that and I am not permitted to produce the ten-year figure, and I did it on the eleventh of the rains month at the second bell out of a slate keyed by number, and you stood four feet off that desk and watched me do it, and if you ask me again which of those two figures is the one I took off a page in a hand four years older I will answer you the way I answered you that night, which is that the man who wrote it is not in this basin and the man who wrote it this year is a man on a road having told you about it twice."

---

They went up the switchbacks and the four terraces had a harvest and he read the returns sheet at the top of it, in a yard, standing up, at about the fifth bell, to the people who had come out of it, and read the second terrace's figure out loud, and read the first terrace's, and did not have the third's, and read the fourth's, and then read the whole of it again because a depot reads a returns sheet twice.

He read it well. He has thirty-three years and six at Verrow and four on the column and he is the mechanism and not the villain, and every word of the sheet was correct.

And when he had finished, Zofia Teran, who had come up in the morning because a shared floor is a place a woman with a school exercise book goes, said the one thing anybody said all afternoon.

"You have written a good year."

"I have written what the four terraces sent me," said Petrus Lenge. "Which is a good year, and I have written it correctly, and I want you to understand before anybody says anything else that I have written it correctly and that the correctness of it is the entire of my trade and also the entire of the difficulty."

---

"I have one line," he said, on the way down, at the culvert, with about four feet of drop below the parapet and the ground drying out for the first time since the sixth of the third month. "That is what I have got and I want to say it once and then I am going to say it again in nine months to a man in a good coat and it will be the same sentence, and I have said it to a handful of people this year and you are one of them.

"The district is one line. The four terraces are inside it. A district with four terraces in it is one line, and the four terraces are not four lines, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a terrace is a line, and I have looked, twice, in four years, and both times I was told to stop looking by a man who outranked me and who was right.

"So this year the one line has to carry a district that has come in short and four terraces that have come in well, and there is nowhere on that sheet to put the second thing, and I am not permitted to make a second line, and if I make a second line then I have invented a district out of four terraces, and the district in this basin that is going to be short is not going to be four terraces, it is going to be a line that begins at the bridge you came over and goes four miles in every direction on a low ground, and it is a Crown March district and it has a name on a schedule in Verrow.

"And the number of persons in it is not on the sheet, and I have never seen it, and I am not permitted to ask for it."

---

They stood at the culvert for about a minute.

"The paper said the four terraces would deliver," said Marek.

"The paper said the four terraces would deliver *a district's figure*," said Petrus Lenge, "and the four terraces have delivered a good harvest, and I have written it, and a good harvest off four hundred acres in the top corner of this region is a part of one of those forty-one lines, and it is not a large part of it, and the short part of it is in a basin that feeds three regions and has a depot's flag on the mast this morning and a rate that has not moved since the second month.

"And that is where neither of them is," said Lenge. "That is the whole of your question and you did not ask it. Neither of them is where the paper said. The paper put a district's number on four terraces and the four terraces have done well and the district is going to be short, and the paper put a class of people on a form and the four hundred and some of them are going into a store with a lock, and the eleven thousand and some hundreds of that advance is already in the ground in four hundred acres, and it is in the ground in the *best* of the four terraces and it was put there in the second month of last year by men doing their jobs."

---

At the second bell of the eleventh of the harvest month, in an office nine feet by eleven off the long side of a yard, Petrus Lenge wrote the coming season's column first, out of a slate keyed by number and not by name, and then ruled the foot of the page and wrote nothing under it, because there is no total under that line and there never has been.

And then he went back up the column to the one line that carried both of this year's figures, and wrote the true one in beside the ten-year figure, in his own hand, with his initials against it, and left the ten-year figure on the page.

Two figures on one line is not a mistake. He says it to nobody in particular, and shuts the book, and puts the slate on top of it, and the slate has a ten-year figure on it that came out of a table in a room he has never been in, and the book has the true one in it with his initials against it, and neither of the two is going anywhere, and by the fourth month a district will have a rate on it.
