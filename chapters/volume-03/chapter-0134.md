# Chapter 134 — The Line He Could Not Fill

The fourth-terrace seed store is a stone building the size of a two-horse cart with a door that has been planed four times and a bar across it and a hasp and a padlock, and the keyway in the padlock is bright on the edge of it and black in the middle, which is what a keyway looks like after eleven years of a key going into it every morning.

Marek Vale had looked at the door four times between the growth month and the harvest month and had not knocked on it, and he came up to it on the eighteenth of the harvest month at about the fourth bell with a piece of chalk in his coat and a reason, and he stood in front of it for about four minutes without touching it.

He touched it in the end, because a man cannot stand in front of a lock for four minutes and not put his hand on it, and what he found was that the padlock was a good one and the hasp was a good one and the staple was set into stone and not into wood, and the bar was oak on two staples and it was heavy enough that getting it up took both hands, and the door behind it had not dropped a quarter of an inch in eleven years, and none of that was any use to him at all.

There is a bar on it. He had been told that by a man with a key on a nail in Tallow Ward and he had not believed the description until the eighteenth of the harvest month, because the bar on this door is an oak bar on two staples and it lies flat, and the door has not dropped, and the reason there is a padlock as well as a bar is that a bar is a thing that can be lifted and a padlock is a thing that cannot.

A padlock is the reason there is a key. A key is the reason there is a keyholder. A keyholder is a person, and a person can be asked, and that is the whole of what a store room with a lock is for.

The key was on a hook inside a seed room, and the seed room hook had a coat on it, and the coat was Janko Vrabec's, and it was on the hook because he was stood up, and the key had been on that hook for eleven years and was not on a list and was not on a nail in an office and was not in a hand, and about nine people in this valley knew where it was and not one of them had ever been asked.

---

He went up the ladder that was not a ladder into the loft and the works board was on the wall at the gable end where the light came in sideways, with this season's nine lines cut in above last season's, and the eighth line had a name against it — Stana Peric's mother, fifty-eight, the second terrace's store, a store with a lock and a holder — and the ninth line had nothing in it at all, and it had been cut in the second month of MR 414 by a man with a knife in about nine seconds, and the man had cut his own name out of it rather than write it and have it struck, and nobody had argued with him about it.

Marek Vale took the chalk out of his coat.

He put the point of it on the board in the ninth line. He held it there for about as long as it takes to count nine, and he did not write anything, and then he took it away and put it back in his coat, and that was the whole of what he did in that loft and nobody saw it.

Ruta Vrabec read the board from the bottom up the way a woman reads a board she has read every year of her life, out loud, without being asked, eight lines, and then she stopped at the ninth one and said the name of it, which is a seed store, and said nothing else, because there is nothing else to say about a line with nothing in it and she had been saying things about that line in this loft for about two months and nobody had asked her to.

The ninth line is the only line on that board that has ever had a person's name cut into it beside it. Marek looked at the shape of the space where the name went, and the shape was the right shape, and there was a knife-cut round the edge of where a name had been, and there was no name in it, and the space was not a mistake and it was not waiting for anybody, and there is no office in this kingdom in which anybody may be applied for.

---

Ruta Vrabec was in the loft with an account book under her arm, and she is fifty-eight and she is one of the four women over fifty on the roll at Hartlow, and she does the cooking, and Janko Vrabec eats at her table, and she is not a keeper of anything and has never once been asked to be.

"You have come up that ladder twice in four days," she said, "and the first time you stood at that door and did not knock and the second time you have stood at that door and come up here with a piece of chalk and put it on a line and taken it off again. I watched you do it through the gap. I do not know what a chalk is for."

"I was going to write a name in the ninth line," said Marek.

"No you were not," said Ruta Vrabec, "and if you had been, you would have written the wrong one, and I would like to know which one you had in your head so that I can put it out of it."

"I had his in my head."

"Right," she said. "And that is the answer, and it is why you have been standing at that door for four minutes instead of knocking on it, and neither of us is going to say the rest of it in this loft, because there is a man in the seed room twenty feet away who has heard the word *ninth* twice in four days and neither time did he come up, and that is his business and not ours."

---

She put the account book down on the boards of the loft floor and sat down on the boards, which is not what a woman of fifty-eight does in a loft.

"Now I am going to tell you the thing you have not asked anybody about," she said, "and then you can go down the ladder, because there are six people in my kitchen and I have a floor to think about.

"Line nine is not empty because there is nobody in it. It is empty because it is a seed store. It is the kept-back seed, and it is the only seed in this valley that will still be in the valley in the eleventh month, and the seed does not care whose name is on the line. The seed comes in off four terraces about a month from now, in sacks, on a cart, and it goes into that building, and it is going to go into that building whether there is a name on the board or not, because the other seed is in a second-terrace store with a lock on it too and a woman's name against it, and the third place is a room off a seed room that floods.

"So the ninth line is going to fill up in about a month with a lock on the door and a key on a hook in a coat, and nobody in four terraces will have been asked about any of it, and that has nothing to do with your paper and it never has, and I would like it noticed that I have said that to a man in a coat in a loft and I have not said it to anybody else, because the two things have nothing to do with each other and everybody in this valley is going to want them to."

---

"And you cannot go down to the third terrace," said Marek.

"No."

"You have not stopped me."

"You have not asked me to," said Ruta Vrabec, "and I have had about nine seconds to work out whether I was going to, and I was not, and the reason I was not is not mine, it is yours, and I am going to hand it back to you because you are going to go up this road and say it out loud to somebody and you may as well have it in the right words.

"You went up this road on the seventh of the growth month and you looked at that ground and you came back and you did not say one word to anybody about what you had done on it, and nine people have worked that out since, and one of them is a woman on a bench at the top of this terrace who has told you in a yard that you were in the room the first time and not the second time. And then on the second of the harvest month you stood on a bank for two and a half days with your hand on a channel and four people on that ground stood on it and watched the water not move, and you have not said a word about that either, and I am not asking you to, because there is no form for either.

"So go down and do it now if you like. There is nothing to stop you. Nobody has sent anybody to that terrace this year and nobody is going to, and there is no office in this kingdom in which anybody may be sent to ask a terrace why it has not sent anybody, and that has been the position for two years and it will be the position in the fourth month.

"And you can walk down there, and there are four people on it, and they are not on the roll and they are not on your paper and they are not a class that has been printed anywhere, and if you go down there and you tell them what a counterweight is then you will have told a household, and not nine people, and you will have got a name, and the name will be one person out of four and it will be yours and it will not be theirs, and that is what you did in the growth month with a hoe."

---

He went down the ladder and out of the yard and down the lane to where the lane forks and the lower fork goes to the third terrace, and he stood at the fork for about as long as it takes to count nine.

There is a wall on the left of that fork about chest high that somebody built out of the same stone as the store and did not finish, and he put his hand on it, and he stood there, and the third terrace is a quarter of an hour's walk and there is a channel in the way at the bottom of it with a bridge somebody made a bridgewright's thing out of in the flood year, and he has done all of it before, and he got as far as the wall and he turned round and went back up the lane.

There was no one watching him. There was no office in this kingdom that would have known he had done it, and no form on which it could be entered, and he could have gone down that lane and said nine words to four people and come back up it before the light went, and he did not, and the reason he did not is that a man with a season in him does not get to interview the people it came out of — and he said that sentence in a lane in the growth month and meant it then, and he meant it now, and the second time he said it out loud it did not feel like a rule and it felt like the ninth line of a board.

Then he stood in a loft four miles above a stone bridge with a piece of chalk in his coat and worked out, in about nine seconds, that he had a season in him and there were four people below him whose ground it had partly come out of, and that he could put his hand on it and name a name and it would go, and that he was not going to do it, and that this was the first time in four months that the thing he had been carefully given had been no use to him at all.

He said that out loud, to Ruta Vrabec, once, because he has learned that a thing said in a yard to the wrong person is worth four things said in a room.

"I could put it on that ground this afternoon," he said, "and I am not going to, and I would like it noticed that I am not going to for the reason you have just given me and not for a better one, and that I have had four months of a better one available and have not found it."

"That is a thing to be noticed in a yard," said Ruta Vrabec, "and not in a loft, and I am not going to carry it down that ladder after you."

---

Janko Vrabec came out of the seed room at about the sixth bell with a lamp in his hand that had a very small wick in it, and he was civil, and he asked Marek whether he had come up the road or over the shoulder, and Marek said the road, and Janko said that the ninth was better in the wet because of the four feet of it below the last turn, and neither of them mentioned the ninth line and neither of them mentioned the door.

Then he said one thing, and it was about seed.

"The kept-back comes off the first on the sixth or the seventh," he said, "and off the second on the ninth, and the fourth's is ours and ours is in a room off the seed room and comes off last. Four days of carting, and it wants a dry floor and a roof and a lock and it does not want a bar, because a bar is for a thing you are hiding and that store is not hiding anything, it is holding next year's seed, and next year's seed is the only thing in this valley that anybody in the hills has ever been sure about."

"You have not opened it since the second month," said Marek.

"I have not opened it since the second month," said Janko Vrabec, "and it is not shut, and the key has not moved, and I will tell you the same thing about that on another day and you may come back for it."

And he went back inside, and the door of the seed room was not shut all the way, and the coat was on its hook behind it, and there was a key on the hook, and the kept-back seed of four terraces comes up a road in sacks in about a month, and none of that can be entered, served, sworn to, or shown to anybody at all.
