# Chapter 143 — Eleven Times, Separately

The seed merchant off the low-ground road had been in the trade thirty-one years and had a good scale and a bad thumb on the beam, and on the twenty-sixth of the ninth month he had eleven sacks on a floor and a slate and a boy of about nine, and in about an hour he had written on the slate eleven times, which is nine more times than he had written anything in the previous six weeks.

Each of the eleven was a separate purchase. Each was paid for. None of them was a group and none of them was a pool and nobody in that building ever said the word.

The instrument of the second month of MR 412 was sealed in eleven names and there are eleven holdings, and Marek Vale had been saying four households for about a month because a man who has been in a valley twice since the harvest month counts terraces and not doors. Nobody in that shop knew that and the boy with the slate would not have cared.

The price on the slate was the depot's price, and the depot's price was in a letter that had come down a road in the growth month for a shilling, and the letter had a price in it and a quantity in it and no form on it, and no family that did not want to take it had ever had to be in a room to refuse it. Mirren Fisk had read it to the members of the Share in the growth month and had not answered it, and at the fourth month of MR 415 there were four terraces going to answer it eleven times between them.

---

The first of the eleven was bought at about the second bell on the twenty-sixth by a man of about fifty with a barrow and a list on a paper the length of his hand, and the merchant weighed the sacks and the boy wrote the number and the man paid and went, and that was the whole of it and it took about four minutes.

The second was bought at about the third bell on the same day by a woman of thirty-eight who had a jar of brackish water on a rail two hundred feet away and a rag in her hand, and she paid in coin and she did not ask for credit and she said one thing to the merchant while he was writing, which was that she wanted to know whether the seed had come off the low ground or up the river, and the merchant said it had come off the low ground and been bought at a depot's price, and she said that was the first straight answer she had had about a thing in a year.

The third and the fourth and the fifth were bought on the twenty-seventh and the sixth and the seventh and the eighth on the twenty-eighth, and the ninth and the tenth and the eleventh on the twenty-ninth, and between the second and the eleventh there was one morning on which three of them came, separately, in the same hour, and the merchant said so out loud to the boy and the boy wrote it down, and neither of them said why.

"There is going to be a queue of these," said the merchant, "and it will not be a queue, because three men do not make a queue, they make a coincidence, and a coincidence is a thing I can sell seed against and I am telling you I am not going to be able to do it twice."

"You will be able to do it once," said Marek Vale, who had been standing by the door for two days.

---

The merchant put the chalk down on the second morning.

"Eleven purchases," he said. "I have written eleven purchases and I have been in this trade thirty-one years and I have never once written eleven of the same seed in eleven days off one road, and I would like to ask the question once, and I am going to ask it badly and I am not going to be aggrieved by the answer.

"Is that money of mine."

"Which money."

"The money that came out of the depot in the second month of last year," said the merchant. "Because there is an advance against these four terraces and it is eleven thousand and some hundreds of pounds, and if a man with a slate comes into my shop and buys seed on the strength of it then I am selling on a pledge and I have done that twice in thirty-one years and both times I did not get paid and one time I lost a barn.

"And I am not asking because I think you are a bad man. I am asking because I have got a book and you have got a road and one of us is going to be asked a question in about a year and I would like to know now which of us it is going to be."

---

There was a pause of about four seconds at the door, and then Mirren Fisk said:

"No."

"That is a plain answer and I do not believe a word of it."

"It is a plain answer and it is a true one and you may write it on your slate," said Fisk, "and I am going to tell you what to write, and then I am going to say it again to the next one who comes in, and I am going to say it eleven times in this shop over three days, and I am not going to be thanked for any of it and neither are you.

"Write: this seed is bought and paid for, out of ground and not out of a pledge, and there is no line against the buyer's name in this book or any other, and the man buying it is the man paying for it."

"That is a line with a head on it."

"It is a line a woman who keeps a book has said out loud to a man who keeps a book, in a shop, on three days, and you may copy it down or you may not, and I have not come here to be believed and I have not come here to persuade you. I have come here because you are the only man in this district who will be asked about it, and because I would rather you were told a true thing by the person selling than work it out on your own in the third month and be angry about it."

The merchant wrote it down.

"That is the sixth one," he said.

---

It took three days.

The seventh of the eleven was bought at about the fourth bell on the twenty-seventh by a woman of thirty-one who held the ground in her mother's name and whose mother was fifty-eight and counted by nobody, and who had come down eleven switchbacks with a rope and a child's coat, and who said nothing at all to the merchant and paid and left.

The eighth was a man of thirty-four who drew the eleventh tray twice a day and had a cough in the small of his back and a certificate for it in a clinic book, and he said the sentence out loud because he had decided to in the shop and not on the road:

"I have been written out of eleven days by a physician and those days went into a book at the clinic as a cost and the balance of my own is a rate on my ground in the fourth month, and I am telling a seed merchant about it because there is no form on which I can tell anybody, and the seed I am buying now is not the seed for that, and I would like the two not to be confused in anybody's book, including mine."

The ninth and the tenth were bought on the twenty-ninth between the second bell and the fourth, and the eleventh was bought in cash at the fifth by a woman of fifty-eight who came down the road on her own and did the counting herself and who is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be, and who is the only person on the roll of the top terrace whose name is on a line anywhere.

She bought it for her son, who is nineteen and who stayed at the top of the road and did not come down and has not read anything and does not know that the seed was bought on the twenty-ninth.

The eleven refusals were said in three days by eleven people about their own money, and not one of them was said about anybody else's, and nobody in that shop kept a list of who had said which, and the merchant wrote all eleven on his slate because he keeps a slate, and then he rubbed five of them out at the end of the twenty-ninth because he had a house of his own and a family in it and a business that had to be worth something in a year's time.

He left six on the slate and put the six on the wall of the shop with a nail through the corner of it, and there was nothing at the head of the six.

---

The labour went up the road on the first of the tenth month.

It was found, not arranged, which is the only way anything in this basin has ever been found. Mirren Fisk put the Share's rule to the members in a yard — a lot of ours that does not come up becomes days, and the days are divided by trays, and the trays are the Share's and there are sixty-one of them and eleven of them came up as sand — and what came back in about nine days was nine members who could be away for a period and no more, and the number nine was nobody's decision. It came out of the yard the way a number comes out of a yard, which is that two people said they could and then five more said they could because it had been said out loud.

Nine people went up the eleven switchbacks on the first of the tenth month with scythes and a hand fork and two picks, and one of them was Petrik's mother, and Petrik was at the seed house with the slate and the string and did not write.

Hana Belic did not go. She had a certificate in a clinic book and the eleventh tray and a cough, and Fisk asked her twice and she said no twice, and the third time Fisk did not ask, and Belic said she had been asked twice for a thing she had already said no to twice and that she would like it noticed that the second no had cost her a tray.

The autumn work on the four terraces took about nine days and it was not the sowing. The kept-back of the first and the second terraces was already in its stores, having come up the road on the sixth and the seventh and the ninth, and the fourth terrace's came off last over four days of carting into a room off a seed room. What the nine went up for was the ground: the ditches, the drains, the second terrace's shoulder above a new cut, the cutting of the stubble, the mending of a wall that a wall has to have mended before the rain.

The sowing goes in in the second month and it went in on the ninth and the tenth, and the seed that had been bought off the low-ground road at a depot's price by eleven households in three days went into the ground on four hundred acres, and about nine rows on a shoulder above a drain came up in the second month of the year after that, and a field cannot be cross-examined.

---

The third terrace was in neither list.

That was the fourth morning and it came out of a conversation with a woman of thirty-one who was sitting on a wall above a new drain with a coil of rope in her lap, and it came out flat, and nobody argued with it, and Marek Vale wrote it on a slate of his own and then rubbed it out because he had been in a yard two days earlier and had watched a man do exactly that.

"You cannot buy them seed," said Stana Peric. "There is nobody to buy it. I have been up and down that lane four times this year and there are four people on the third terrace and I have spoken to one of them, and the one I spoke to is the mother of the two others, and she took the paper off me and read it and gave it back and said it was not hers to hold.

"And you cannot tell them, and you have not told them, and that is the correct thing and I have said so in three yards this season. And there is no form on which anybody may be sent. And so they are not on your list, and they have no seed, and you have known that since the eighteenth of the harvest month and you came down the road anyway."

"Yes."

"Then say the next part out loud, because I am not going to say it for you and I am not going to be the one who says it in a room that will be repeated."

"The next part is that a cart is not an office," said Marek.

Stana Peric looked at him for about four seconds.

"That is the first thing anybody has said up this road in four months that I have not thought of," she said, "and I have thought of very little. And I am going to tell you the three things it costs you, and then I am not going to say another word about it.

"A cart can be driven to a lane and a sack can be put down in a shed and nobody can be served with a sack and nobody can be asked about a sack. And a sack in a shed is the only way those four people are going to have anything in the third month, and you are right, and it is not a person and it is not an office and it is not a form.

"And the second thing is that they will not be told. Not by you and not by the cart and not by whoever takes it in. There will be seed in a shed on the third terrace and there is no person in this basin who will ever be able to say that they were told anything, and if anybody in a room in nine years asks how the third terrace came through the winter, the answer will be that they had seed, and the answer will be true, and it will not be the whole of it.

"And the third thing is that you have just found a way round the only rule in this basin that nobody was allowed to get round, and you have found it in nine seconds, and it was there the whole time, and I would like you to sit with that for about a day before you are pleased with yourself."

---

The last sack went on Petar Duss's cart on the third of the tenth month at about the second bell, and he charged his own rate, which was a number he said out loud once and did not improve, and Mirren Fisk argued with it for about four minutes and lost, and Duss said that he had been on the road thirty-one years and that the road went up eleven switchbacks and that a man who drove it up in the third month with somebody else's seed on the bed was not going to be cheap about it, and that if anybody in Rainmarket said he had been bought he would say so to their face.

Nobody said he had been bought.

He took the two carts up on the first and the third and did not come down between, and at the top of the road on the third he said the thing he has said since the tenth month of last year and has never had written down, which is that he would like somebody to write down that he knew the road was opened on purpose, and Marek Vale stood in a gateway with a slate in his hand and a list of nine names on it and did not write it down.

He has still not written it down, and the sheet about it is folded inside the left breast of his coat, and there is a person in this basin who has been owed that sentence for about a year.
