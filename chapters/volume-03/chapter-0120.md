# Chapter 120 — The Two Halves of It

Sabin Rell came down the river on the fourth of the growth month with a satchel and a good coat and about nine days' worth of being pleasant, and he came into the Elmsworth seed house at about the second bell in the afternoon, which is a thing nobody from a Crown March office has ever done, and the reason he gave for it was that it was the only room in Rainmarket he knew of where somebody was keeping seed on purpose.

The lamp was on. It had been on since the third of the third month, when the water office supplied the thirteenth of fourteen lines on a list in Bell Stair, and it was a lamp with a glass on it about nine inches above a bench of sixty-one trays, and it had made a difference to that room that nobody in it had said out loud.

Hana Belic had worked sixty-one trays twice a day in the dark from the sixth of the ninth month to the third of the third month, and had worked them twice a day in the light since, and had a cough in the small of her back, and had been written out of four tray-days by a physician in the tenth month and not written out of the other seven.

---

"You have got a count in your head," said Rell, looking at the trays.

"I have got sixty-one," said Hana Belic, "and I have had it since the seed month and it is a thing I say to make people stop asking."

"Then I will not ask what a tray is worth and I will tell you why, in advance, so that you do not have to spend the afternoon being polite about it. I cannot take a count off a cooperative and carry it into a book. There is no form on which I may, and I have not got a licence and I am not going to get one, and a season's advance is not a reason to invent an office in an afternoon."

Mirren Fisk, who was the keeper and who had been a keeper for nineteen years, said from the far end of the bench: "You are the first Crown March man who has stood in this room and said that."

"I am the first Crown March man who has been in this room," said Sabin Rell. "The rest of them send a slip."

---

He asked for the kept-back seed instead, because a kept-back store is not a cooperative's figure, it is seed on a floor, and seed on a floor can be looked at by a man in a good coat and reported as *present* and not as *sufficient*, and that is a thing a Crown March man is allowed to write down.

They counted for about an hour with a measure and a book, and Rell did the measuring himself and got it wrong twice and had it corrected twice by a woman of thirty-four without either of them raising their voice about it.

Then he asked for a bench to sit on, and there was no bench, and he sat on a sack, and he put his hands on his knees, and Marek Vale understood about four seconds afterwards that the man was about to say the thing he had come down the river to say, and that he was going to say it in a seed house to about nine people rather than in a room over a chandler's shop, and that he had chosen that on purpose and had not explained why.

---

"I want to say two things and they are in one sentence and they are about half right and half wrong, and I am going to tell you which half is which before either of you asks me, because I have been asked twice already this month by men in good coats who wanted the half that suited them and I am not going to give it to them separately.

"The right half is this. This basin feeds three regions. It fed them last year at a price that made a Crown March office in the hills nervous, and it is feeding them now better than it has in nine years, and the reason it is feeding them is a season in a valley four miles above a stone bridge that was granted to those four terraces in writing by people who knew what they were doing, with reasons, and I have read the reasons twice and they were good reasons and the ground was ready when they asked and it was not ready when the rain came. Nobody in this basin stole a harvest. I have been in this trade twenty-nine years and I would know, and I would have heard about it in Verrow before I heard about it in Rainmarket.

"The wrong half is a word. I have been saying *the ten-year mean* for four years in a room in the hills as though that were a measurement of a district, and it is not a measurement, it is a number, and a number is not a season. It is a number this year that was made out of ten numbers, and the ten numbers are in a table in a room I have never been in, and I read that table once and cannot swear to it and am not permitted to produce it. And a number made out of ten numbers by men in an office is not what those four terraces did this year or did not do this year, and when I stood on a stair in the tenth month and told you that a mean is the only figure in the book that cannot be argued with by anybody who has been in the district, I was telling you the truth about the arithmetic and I was selling you the wrong half, and the third terrace has proved it, because the third terrace has not returned anything for two years and the column in my office has a number in it for that terrace, and the number is not that terrace, it is a page from a year before."

He stopped there and let about nine people in a seed house do what they wanted with it.

---

"Now the thing I came down the river to say," he said, "and it took me nine days to say it to myself and I would like to say it in a room with nine people in it rather than in a room with one, and that is the only reason I am here and not at a desk.

"Taking the advance back is correct. It is cheap, it is quick, it is the whole of what the clause was for, and the office at Verrow has a box for it and a standing instruction about how it is done and a man whose whole business is doing it, and it can be done in nine days. And if it is done, then eleven thousand and some hundreds of pounds of bad autumn lands on four terraces in a season that has already failed once, and about a terrace's worth of that seed is in a cut on the second terrace and the rest of it is in the ground and the labour is spent and the ground has been worked.

"And that is the answer. And I am telling you it is the answer, and I am not going to pretend that a granary official has found a third thing, and I have looked for nine days.

"The only reason it is not the answer is that the money is not in a chest in Verrow. It has been spent. It is in seed and it is in labour and it is in four hundred acres that have been worked, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a pound that has been spent on a sowing is un-spent, and there is no office that can un-spend it, and I have gone looking for one for nine days and I have found that the only thing this kingdom can do with a pound that has been spent is to send somebody up a road to tell a family that it was."

He put his hands on his knees again.

"I am not going to be thanked for that and I am not going to be forgiven for the other thing, and I am not going to be asked about the mark in the corner of anything, and I have never known what is in the corner of anything and I have signed about two hundred a year."

---

Marek Vale had a thing he could have said at that point and did not say it, and it was the thing about a form, and about a hand, and about a name in the corner of a sheet, and about a man who does not know what the marks are and has not known for nine years.

He did not say it, and he was not going to be asked, and he sat on a sack in a seed house in Rainmarket and let a good season go on in front of him, and understood that he had become the second man since the year turned to decide on his own that a true thing was in the wrong week.

"You have got something you are not going to tell me," said Sabin Rell, about a minute later, without any edge on it at all. "I am not going to ask. If you ever want to tell me you will not have to come up a road about it, and that is the whole of what I have ever wanted from a man in a good coat, and I have wanted it about nine times in twenty-nine years and got it about once."

---

At about the fifth bell he asked Fisk for the name of the seed house and the name of the woman who drew the eleventh tray, and Fisk gave both, and Rell wrote them in a book he had brought out himself, in his own hand, in a column, and dated the line twice — once in his hand and once in his own column — and Marek Vale saw him do it and did not say a word about it, because it was not his.

"Eleven," said Hana Belic, from the bench, "is one of the eleven that came up as sand."

"I know. I have the first terrace's return in the satchel and the second's and the fourth's, and the second's says nine lots out of thirty-nine, and the first's says the ground was worked and does not use the word, and the fourth's says the sowing went in on ground that had been warm for nine days, which is the only sentence in the three of them that anybody in this basin has bothered to write down. The third terrace's is not in the satchel because it was never sent. I have read the word *sand* about nine times this month and I have never once seen a figure against it." He shut the book. "And I want to be exact about why I am writing a seed house's name in a depot book when I go back at the end of the week.

"It is not a favour and it is not a finding and it is not the beginning of anything. It is that a depot can send a letter to a seed house. Not a person. A letter, with a price in it and a quantity in it, and it goes down a road and it costs about a shilling, and there is no form in this kingdom that anybody can be served with, and a family that does not want to take it does not have to be in a room to refuse it. That is the whole of what I have got from where I am standing, and I have had it for nine days and I have not sent it because I did not know whether the shop was still trading, and now I do."

Nobody thanked him. Belic said the tray was hers and he could not have it, and he said he had not asked for the tray, and Fisk said she would read the letter to the members and not answer it, and he said that was the correct order, and Marek Vale stood in a seed house and watched two correct positions be held by two people who did not know each other and were not introduced.

---

Rell got onto the barge at the sixth bell and the lamp behind him burned till the seventh, and the count went onto the bench book at the end of the day with nothing at the head of it, and the four terraces had a set-off on a nail in a seed house, a wall in a cut, a figure in a column that came off a page nobody could produce, four names on a Crown March form, a letter that would go down a road for a shilling, and a depot that could not take a pound back, and not one of those nine things was a seed.

Hana Belic was still at the eleventh tray at the seventh bell with the lamp burning behind her, and the cough was in the small of her back, and about nine people had come into that shed since the third of the third month who had not come into it before that date, and nobody had sent anybody, and the register opened at the fourth bell in a shed eleven feet by nine four miles away with about eighty people in the lane and the fifth line empty.
