# Chapter 136 — The Box That Is Stock

In the depot yard at Verrow there is a scale with a man on it, and he is weighing a cart of barley against a figure in a book and getting the figure he expected, and there is a basket by the office door full of returned forms going grey along the top of it, and those two together are the whole of Marek Vale's errand, and it took him about a minute to explain.

The tide had gone up on the twenty-second of the harvest month and he had been on the water a night and a day and had come into Verrow on the third of the ninth month, and he had come up a main road in a book because a man in a copy room in Bell Stair had cut a form with five lines on it and no word at the top of the fifth one, and because there was one thing in this basin that he had never once been inside, and it was a place where the printed thing was read, in quantity, by somebody whose job was reading it in quantity.

He had not been going to see Sabin Rell, who was in that building and was three floors up, because a man with no office who goes up a flight of stairs to see the officer who signed the paper is a man asking for something, and there was no form on which the asking could be entered, and Rell had already given him the correct defence for free in the harvest month and would be within his rights to refuse it a second time.

---

Damir Pribis was forty-nine and had been filing seasonal advance returns at the Verrow depot for eleven years, and he was in a room off the long side of the yard that was nine feet by eleven and had a press in it and a table and two chairs and a window that looked at the wall of the next building, and he was the man who opened the wire basket, and he was not a clerk of the third grade and had never claimed to be, and he described himself, when anybody asked, as the man who opens the basket.

"Sit down," he said. "It is not a room anybody comes to. You are the first man to come up that road in about two years who was not selling me something, and I have got about nine minutes before the next cart, so ask."

Marek asked.

---

"Returned this season," said Damir Pribis. "Sixty-one. Sixty went into a press last week and the sixty-first is this one, and that one is the fourth terrace's and it came back up the road in a cart with two other people's sacks on it, which is a thing that happens and which I do not enter.

"And I have read all sixty-one. That is not a boast, it is the job. I open them and I go down the columns and I look for two things and the two things are a figure that is not a figure and a date that is not a date. I have been doing it eleven years and I do not read them any other way and I could not tell you what is written in the second box of one of them without going and looking, and neither could anybody else in this building, and that is not a criticism of anybody, it is a form."

"Then you have never read the fourth box," said Marek.

"No," said Damir Pribis.

He did not say it as a confession and he did not say it as a defence. He said it the way a man says the name of a column he does not fill in, and he pulled the sixty-first out of the basket and put it on the table between them, flat, squared to the edge, and put his thumb on the first box.

"The first box is the party. The second is the advance. The third is the delivery when it comes, or a rule at the foot when it does not, which is the ordinary case and which I do not think about. The fifth at the foot is a return, and it is the oldest box on the form and it goes back further than the paper is printed on, and in my eleven years I have seen a hundred and about forty entries in it and every one of them was a thing being handed on, and there has never been anything else in it, and if you want to know why, nobody who has ever come in here has wanted to know why."

---

"And the fourth," said Marek.

"And the fourth," said Damir Pribis, "is the counterweight, and it is in type, and it says that the district named in the schedule bears the failure of the season favoured, and the district is filled in by hand, and in sixty-one forms the hand has been the same office's hand and the district has been right every time, and the district is the only thing in that box that could ever have gone wrong, and in eleven years it has not."

"And here is what I am going to say about it, and I have never said it out loud in this room and I am not saying it to you because you are a better man than the last one, I am saying it because you have asked me a question about a column and I have answered it and this is the answer.

"I have never once had a question about the fourth box. Not one, in sixty-one, in eleven years. And the reason is not that the fourth box is careful. The reason is that the fourth box has never once asked me anything. It is a printed line with a number in it and the number has been right and the line has been the same on every sheet in the world, and there is nothing in it for a man to have an opinion about, so I have never had one, and there was never any occasion for me to have one.

"And I will tell you the other half of it, because you will go away with the first half and it is the half that makes a good story.

"I have never had a question about it, and I have also never had a reason to look at it and be frightened. It is stock. It is the same on every sheet of the run. A clerk reads a form by the heads of the boxes, and the head of the fourth box is a kind, and a kind is not a hole, and I have never in my life gone looking for a hole in a form. You do not go looking for a hole. You go looking for a figure that is wrong, and the fourth box does not have a figure that can be wrong, and that is the whole of it, and I would like it noticed that that is the best possible answer and it is not an answer to anything."

---

Marek Vale put his hand on the sixty-first and did not move it and looked at the first box and then at the fourth, which were about four inches apart on the sheet.

"Can I say a thing and you can tell me whether it is true," he said, "and it is not a question about the form, it is a question about the two boxes."

"Go on."

"There is a name in the first box on this sheet. The party's name. It is a person's name and it is a person's name because the party has to be somebody, and a set-off has to have somebody on it, and the office has a standing instruction that a party is served and not a holding, so somebody's name goes in there whether or not the thing is about them."

"There is," said Damir Pribis.

"And the fourth box names a class, and the class is made out of the first box by taking the person out of it."

Damir Pribis put his hand on the table flat, and did not pick the sheet up, and looked at the fourth box for about four seconds, which was a long time for a man who opens a basket.

"Yes," he said. "That is exactly what it is, and I have never said it, and I have never seen anybody say it, and I have been in this room eleven years.

"And I am not going to be pleased about it being said in here, because a man who has said a thing like that in a room has usually come in with a story, and you have not got a story, you have got a wet coat, and I have decided in the last four seconds that I believe you and I would like it entered that I took four seconds over it."

---

"But nobody ever does it," he said. "That is the part you have not asked me and it is the part you came up here for, and I am going to give it to you straight, and then I am going to open the next cart.

"Sixty-one forms. The first box has got a name in it sixty-one times. And there has never once been a name put in that first box about the fourth box, and there never will be, and it is not because a clerk decided not to. It is because if a clerk did it, then the name in the first box would be saying that that person is a party to the thing in the fourth box. And a granary cannot certify that. Not this office, not the board, not the man upstairs. A granary certifies paper and a granary certifies a number of holdings, and the moment a name in a party box has to mean *this person is the counterweight*, then somebody has to accept that a person is a counterweight, and no office in this kingdom has a box for that, and the man who put the name in would be certifying a thing he cannot check and cannot read and has not been asked about by anybody above him.

"So it is not that a box with a class in it does not get a person. It gets one sixty-one times, four inches away, in a different box, doing a different job. And a box that has a class in it cannot get a person, because there is no way on that sheet to tell the two boxes apart. About nine hundred of these come back through that basket in a year across the four offices, and not one clerk in any of them has ever tried, and I have thought about why not, and the reason is that nobody has ever asked them to."

---

Marek asked him one more thing, and it was not about the form, and it took about four seconds and got an answer that took about nine.

"Is a translator's name ever checked here."

"No," said Damir Pribis. "We are not the harbour and we cannot read one and we have never claimed to. A form comes up off a road with a mark in the corner of it and the mark is part of the paper and the paper is what we file. I have looked at a great many of them and I could not tell you what a translator is."

He said it in the voice of a man reading a price list and he did not know that it was the answer to a letter that had been on a shelf above a chandler's shop since the seventh of the second month, and Marek Vale did not tell him, and stood in a room nine feet by eleven in a depot at Verrow and worked out, for the second time since the growth month, that a thing not coming back was not a no, and that nothing between that night and the fourth month of next year turned on the answer, and that he had not gone and looked.

---

There was one other thing and it took nine seconds.

The returns book for the coming season was on the table with the slate on top of it, and on one line of the projection column there were two figures and not one, and the first was the ten-year and the second was the true one, in a young clerk's hand, with his initials against it.

Neither figure could be produced. Neither figure could be used. The ten-year mean was on a table in a room in that building and was not in the book and was not permitted out of the room where the table was, and the man who had carried it forward twice on a slate keyed by number had written the truth in beside it and left the ten-year standing and gone up eleven switchbacks to read a returns sheet out loud twice.

Damir Pribis did not remark on it. Marek did not ask him about it. There is no form on which either of those two things is a question, and a man who asks a question at a desk in a depot about a figure in a column gets a figure, and Marek had wanted a figure since the ninth of the second month and got the only one anybody in this basin has ever been given, and could do nothing whatever with it.

---

At the fourth bell Damir Pribis put the sixty-first into the press with the rest, pulled the lever, and squared the bundle on the edge of the table, and put it on a shelf with a label on the shelf that said a season and a district, and the label was the only thing in that room that had a name at the top of it.

"Six to a pound," he said. "You may carry it down the yard if you want and it will show you the fourth box sixty-one times on the way and it will not tell you one single thing about it, and that is the best thing anybody has ever done to a box."

Marek Vale carried a bundle of sixty-one returned seasonal advance forms four hundred yards across a granary yard in the ninth month of MR 414, and looked at the same printed line in the same box on sixty-one sheets, and it did not ask him anything on any of them, and the yard-man at the gate did not look up, and the tide was going down.
