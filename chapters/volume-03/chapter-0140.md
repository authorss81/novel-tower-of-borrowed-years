# Chapter 140 — Two Women and a Form

The count goes on late in the ninth month, because the lamp has been in since the third and the trays are on the second turn by the sixth bell, and the seed house was full at about the fourth on the twentieth and stayed full.

The form was on the bench under the slate with two sheets of Chamber paper on top of it, and both of the sheets had four ruled lines filled in and a fifth line with nothing in it, and Sedge was at the end of the bench with his coat still on because he had come down off the causeway on the same morning and had not taken it off, and nobody asked him to and he did not.

Nobody thanked him. He had come four miles to watch a thing he had cut be used, and the yard he came from does not thank anybody for anything, including him, and he had said on the way down that he was not to be thanked and had said it before anybody had thought of it.

Ilma Tresch was at the bench at the fifth bell, and Hana Belic was at the bench at about the sixth, and they came at different hours on purpose and not together, and neither of them knew the other was coming until she was already standing there, and neither of them said one word to the other about it, and both of them stayed.

---

Ilma Tresch is forty-one and she did the eleven days on ground that had already failed in the growth month and she has had a cough since the ninth of the seed month which went into the small of her back, and and a physician wrote her out of four of the days and then, in the same afternoon, wrote her out of the rest of them once, and the certificate went to the board of the clinic, and the board entered it as a cost, and the district's answer to a cost is a rate.

Her balance is the days she was written out of. That is all it is and there is nothing else in it, and they do not come back in kind, and the Share's own rule puts the balance on the ground of the line, and her ground is one lot in the low ground with a roll number on it.

She looked at the form for about a minute and then she said:

"Whose name goes against it."

"You do," said Mirren Fisk. "There is nowhere else for it to go and I have not come here to find out a better way. That is what a rate on a member's ground is. It is charged to the ground and the ground is held by a person, so the person is on the sheet."

"Then that is right," said Ilma Tresch, "and I am not going to argue with it, and I have not got anybody to argue with it with, so write it."

And it was written, and the second line was in her own words because Fisk writes what a person says and does not improve it, and it said that she had done eleven days and had been written out of the rest of them once, and that the days she was written out of were a cost to the clinic and the clinic had entered them, and the balance was on her ground, and Fisk read it back at the ordinary speed and Ilma Tresch said that was what she had said.

And then she said the other thing, and she said it in the plain voice of a woman who has been in this room since the third of the seed month and has been arguing with a doctor ever since.

"Before I go," she said. "There is going to be a rate on this low ground in the fourth month. There is a district and it has come in short and I have not seen the paper and nobody has shown it to me, and everybody in this room knows it and nobody has said it out loud until now.

"So there is going to be one of these on my lot. And there is going to be another one on the district. And they are both going to be on the same dirt, and one of them is mine and one of them is the low ground's, and there is nothing in this kingdom in which those two can be looked at together. Not a form, not a counter, not a room. If you bring me a piece of paper with both of them on it I will sign it, and there is no piece of paper, and that is not because anybody here has done anything wrong. It is because the rate on my lot is the size of my lot and the rate on the district is the size of a district, and the two sizes do not meet anywhere, and so there will be two charges on one piece of dirt and nobody alive will ever be shown both of them at once, including me, including you, including whoever sent you up here."

She got up off the bench and put her shawl over her head.

"That is not an objection," she said. "I am not refusing. I am telling you the size of it, and then I am going home to a house with a cough in it, and you are going to charge me either way, because the rule is right and I have said so twice, and I would like the whole of it minuted that I said the rule was right before I said the other thing."

---

Hana Belic came at about the sixth bell and she drew the eleventh tray and had worked it twice a day in the dark from the sixth of the ninth month, and a physician wrote her out of four tray-days in the harvest month and did not write her out of the other seven, and then a physician came back in the growth month to a cough in the small of her back and wrote her out of the evening trays for eleven days, and both certificates went to the board of the clinic and the board entered them as costs, and the district's answer to a cost is a rate.

Her balance is the eleven days. She did none of them, and she has not hidden that, and nobody in the room treated it as anything but a certificate.

She read the form standing up, which took her longer than it took Ilma Tresch, and she got to the fourth line and stopped.

"Who did the eleven days," she said.

"Nobody," said Mirren Fisk. "That is the whole of what a certificate is and you have one and I am not going to explain it to you as though you did not know it. You were written out of the days. The days are a cost to the clinic. The clinic has entered them. The balance of your own is a rate on your ground, and that is the rule and the rule is nineteen years old and it is mine and it is correct."

"I know it is correct," said Hana Belic. "That is not what I am asking. I am asking who did the work that was not done."

Silence at the end of a bench of sixty-one trays.

"The trays are the Share's," she said. "Sixty-one of them and mine is the eleventh. The rule takes a lot of ours that does not come up and divides it across the members by trays, and eleven of the trays came up as sand out of a shed that was shut up wet in the flood year, and the days those eleven would have been are the days I was written out of, and the days I was written out of went into a book at the clinic as a cost, and the clinic's costs go on the same account as everything else, and the district's answer to a cost is a rate, and the rate is on my ground.

"And there is no line on this piece of paper for the Share, and none for the rule, and none for the certificate, and none for eleven trays."

"So there is one woman on here and one figure, and the woman did the trays and the figure is hers, and she did not do the eleven days and the eleven days are not hers either, and they are nobody's, and nobody's is what a Share is when a thing has to be put on a piece of paper."

---

She put her hand flat on the table next to the form, the way people do when they have been lifting sacks for a season.

"I did the trays," she said. "That is the whole of what I did and I would do them again. I have done the eleventh tray in the dark since the sixth of the ninth month and I know what is in it better than I know what is in a kitchen, and I have a cough in the small of my back and a certificate for it, and I am not going to be a person who is ungrateful about a certificate in a seed house.

"And I am not going to be a rate. I am a woman who did the eleventh tray. Whatever the rule did, it did it, and I was one of the trays, and if you want to put a figure against my name because the paper has nowhere else to put it, then put it, and I will say yes, and I would like it noticed that I said yes before you asked me, and I would like it noticed that saying yes to a form is not the same as saying the form is right, and you told me that in a loft in a valley four months ago and I have thought about it about nine times."

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"It is not the same," said Marek Vale.

"That is the first true thing anybody has said about it in this room," said Hana Belic, "and you have been here four minutes, and I am going back to my tray, and somebody is going to have to turn the twenty-third at the fourth bell or you are going to get the middle of it wet."

---

Sedge had not spoken for the whole of it.

He stood at the end of the bench in his coat with his hands in his sleeves and looked at the two sheets for about four minutes after the second woman had gone back to her tray, and then he said:

"You want a finding."

"I have not said the word finding."

"You have had it in your face since the fifth bell and so have I," said Sedge. "Two sheets with the same failure on them at two different sizes and you have a form that was cut for one of the sizes, and you would like a piece of paper with a head at the top of it that says what the other size is, and you would like me to put my name under it in a clerk's hand so that it is a document and not a thing you said in a seed house. And you want it on my form."

"I want a finding."

"Then I am not going to give you one, and I am going to tell you why in about four sentences and then I am going to pick up my blank and go back up the hill.

"Finding is a form. I have never cut one and I am not going to cut one about mine, and if I did, then a man in a good coat in a room in nine years could produce it, and the head of it would be a kind, and the kind would be *a rate on a member's ground does not scale*, and that is a sentence with a space in it where a person is supposed to be, and you have spent a day and a half learning that you do not put a head on a column and I am not going to help you do it in a worse place.

"And the second thing is that a man who cuts a form is the man who ought to be told it is not right, and being told is not the same as signing, and signing is the thing you do to a form to make it into a document, and I have already made this one into a document twice this morning and it has cost two women a morning each, and I am not going to be a third time about the same piece of paper."

---

"And the third thing," he said, "and then I am picking it up.

"I said a sentence in a copy room on the twentieth of the last month and it came out flat, and I have thought about that every day since, and it came out flat because I have said it three times and a man who has said a thing three times cannot say it fresh.

"There is a shed in Tallow Ward where a line gets read out twice at the ordinary speed and the room does not talk over a line. And I have spent a fortnight deciding that I am not going to put that sentence at the bottom of a piece of paper to be read out twice, because when it is read out twice it will not be a thing a man said in a room any more, it will be a thing the room owns, and in about four years a man will produce it and say that the yard thought so."

He put the blank sheet back inside the fold of the form and squared it against the end of the bench.

"Take your two sheets," he said. "Do not let anybody head them. And when the fourth month comes and there is a rate on that low ground, do not bring me a finding, because I will not cut it, and if you cut it yourself in a room I will read about it and I will not be surprised and I will not write to you."

"Nobody is going to thank you for any of this," said Marek.

"I have been in this building long enough that twice in it," said Sedge, "and I did not come down here to be thanked, and if somebody in that lane thanks me on the day the days go on that board I will tell them it is a chair they are thanking and not a man."

---

Petrik came along the wall with the chalk and put the days on the oiled board, and there was nothing at the head of the board, and nobody told him to put anything there, and he did not, and the twenty-third tray was turned at the fourth bell by a boy of eleven who had been told by a woman of thirty-four and had not been told by anybody else.

The two filled sheets went face down under the slate, and the fifth line was empty on both of them, and the low ground's second sowing did not come and was not coming, and in the fourth month a rate was going on that ground anyway, correctly, out of a rule nineteen years old, by a woman who had walked to every kitchen in this district and had told two of them the size of it and had been thanked by neither.
