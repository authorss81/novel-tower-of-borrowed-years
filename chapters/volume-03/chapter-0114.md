# Chapter 114 — On the Back of an Envelope

The wall on the second terrace went in the third month, in a week nobody had a name for, and about a terrace's worth of seed went into the cut with it, and on the sixth day of that month Petrus Lenge came up eleven switchbacks with a second bundle in a different kind of wrapper and could not get a cart up the last of the road and had it carried the last four hundred yards by two men he paid out of his own hand and entered nowhere.

"You have come up twice in a month," said Zofia Teran, at the top of the lane, with her hands full of a rope and a child's coat.

"I have come up twice in a month," Lenge agreed, "and I would like it noticed that the depot considers this cheap and the second day of it is going to be the day I stop coming up cheap."

---

There is no desk in a lane.

They did it at the gate of the second terrace's holding, sitting on the low wall where the drain had gone out, with the notice on Lenge's knee and a depot envelope on the flat stone between them, because a clerk who has to do a thing on a holding has nowhere to put his elbows. About nine people were in the lane. Two of them were standing in the cut with a bar.

The notice was one sheet. It had four clauses and a rule at the foot and a mark in the bottom corner in four small parts.

"Read me the third clause," said Marek.

"That is the set-off," said Petrus Lenge. "I will read it standing up the way I read it at a door and I will not do it twice, because I have done it at six doors since I came up the road and my mouth is not what it was before I came up."

He read it standing up. It said, in the plain shape the plainest version of it can take, that a sum advanced against a delivery had not been recovered out of that delivery; that the sum remained outstanding; that it was to be recovered from the party standing behind the delivery; and that it was to be recovered by set-off against the next account the party had with the depot.

"Next account," said Zofia Teran.

"The next account. The projection for the coming season. If the sum is set off against the projection, then the projection is a smaller number, and a smaller projection is a smaller advance, and a smaller advance is what they give you for seed."

He put the sheet down on the stone.

"So the money is not coming back," said Marek. "It never was going to come back. It is going to be subtracted from the number you are entered at, and the number you are entered at is the number the next advance is worked out from, and the next advance is the seed."

"Yes."

"Then a set-off is a thing that does not take money off anybody. It takes seed off the four terraces, about eleven months before the seed wants to be in the ground."

"Yes," said Petrus Lenge, "and I have been trying since the day before I came up to think of a way to say that sentence to nine people who have just lost a wall, and I cannot find a way that is not that sentence."

---

Zofia Teran put the rope down on the wall.

"Now tell me who it is against," she said.

"The holding."

"That is a field with a number on a schedule."

"That is a field with a number on a schedule."

"And I am a woman standing in a lane." She did not say it as an accusation, and Marek Vale, who had come up with the clerk and had said four words to her at the gate, understood that she had been saying sentences to men in rooms for a year and did not need to raise her voice at one in a lane. "So which of those two is it against, and how does the notice get to it, and who is it served on?"

Lenge turned the sheet round and put his finger on the head of the third column.

"That is the party. And the party is the number. And the depot's standing instruction is that a party is served and not a holding, which I told you at the bottom of that road on the eleventh, and I have thought about it every day since and I have got nowhere."

"Because a party is a person."

"Because a party is a person. That is the whole of the instruction and there is no second half of it. A party is served by a person being present at the serving of it, in a place, on a day, and the person has to be able to say they took it. And a holding is four hundred acres with a number on it and it cannot be present anywhere, and the number is not a person, and the office that holds the schedule is in the hills and it is not coming up here, and there is no form in this kingdom in which anybody may be applied for."

---

"Then what is on that paper," said Marek, "under my finger."

Lenge looked at it for a while.

"It is the name of the holder of the holding, which is a person, and it is on there because a set-off has to have somebody on it, and the office will say, when the returns go up, that the holder was notified on the sixth of the third month at a place, and that is true, and it is also a way of saying that a field was charged, and a field cannot be told anything, and a woman standing in a lane is a witness and not a party."

"Am I the party."

"You are the holder's household, and the depot's form does not have a line for a household. It has a line for a party and I have put your name against it because there are eleven parties on this schedule and I have to serve eleven notices, and I am not going to write *a holding* on a line that is there for a person, and if I write a number on that line then I have served a schedule, and if I leave it blank then the year has a hole in it in a different place, and I have told you about the holes."

Marek looked at the two hands on the sheet: the clerk's, and the one that had been in a bundle on a barge in a box that was not lashed well.

"How much of it falls on this holding."

"I am not permitted to give you that figure and you already know the sum and you know the reason. The share is in the district schedule at Verrow. It is on the paper. The paper is the only place it is allowed to be, and I have said that to you in Morrowfen and I am not going to vary it on a wall in four hundred yards of a lane."

---

The child came down the lane at about the fifth bell and wanted to know where the seed was, and Zofia Teran said it was in the cut, and the child said the seed was in the ground, and Zofia Teran said yes, and stood still, and about nine people in a lane found that they had nothing to say, and two men in the cut with a bar found that they had better get on with it.

Then Lenge did the thing he had come up the road to do, and he did it on the low wall, and it took about a minute.

He read the whole notice out again from the top, all four clauses and the rule at the foot, in the plain voice, and then he wrote the date of the day and the place on the back of the depot envelope, and he wrote his own name under it, and he gave it to the woman standing in the lane, and said that she was not the party and he had served it on the person present, and that the office's position in about nine weeks would be that the holder was notified on the sixth of the third month at the second terrace of the Red Uplands.

"And if I put it in the school house?" said Zofia Teran. "There is a nail."

"There is a nail. There is a nail in a school house and there is a nail in a seed room and there are about nine nails in four terraces and every one of them belongs to somebody who is not the party." He tucked the pen away. "Put it where the people who have to see it will see it. That is not advice, that is the only instruction I have got and it is that the paper is served and not the holding, and I have discovered about two days ago that the two of those are the same sentence, and I have not got a better one."

She put it on the seed house nail, and it was there in the afternoon, and it was read by about forty people in a day, and by the fourth day there was a thing in this basin that had never been there before, which was a piece of paper with a date on it saying that a field had been charged.

---

Nobody thanked Petrus Lenge. He said on the way down that he had not expected to be, and that the depot's standing instruction about serving a party and not a holding was in a book in Verrow in about the same words, and that it was about four words longer than what he had just said in a lane, and that he had left the four words off because he had been up the road four times this month and had not the four words in him.

Marek Vale walked down the switchbacks with him and got about two hundred yards and then said the thing he had been carrying since the ninth, which was that there is no form in this kingdom in which a holding may be charged and that the whole of the set-off is a charge on a number, and that a number is not a person, and that a person who is served a notice about a number is a witness and not a party.

"I know all of that," said Lenge, "and I have known it since the second of the month, and I have got eleven sheets to serve and about four days of road left in me, and I would like you to tell me one thing before you tell me anything else I already know."

"Ask it."

"What am I supposed to put in the box at the foot?"

And Marek Vale walked eleven switchbacks down a road in the second month of the year in which the reckoning turned on the first, with a question that was not his, and had no answer, and did not pretend to have one, and did not say no.

He had asked Lenge on the ninth, in a room over a chandler's shop, what the head of the column was, and had been told that it was set in type in the hills, and printed in a run, and came down with the form. And he had asked a man in two rooms above a chandler's shop on the seventh whether anybody kept a record of the instructions a mark was made from, and had been told that they were kept until the tide and then burned. And he had been up a ladder in a loft on the eleventh and watched a board get nine lines cut into it with a knife and had watched the ninth line get cut out with the same knife, and none of it — not the type, not the burned sheet, not the empty line — was anybody's fault, and all of it was the same thing, and the thing was a form, and the form was the cheapest thing in this basin and the most expensive one to refuse.

The register in Tallow Ward opened at the fourth bell the next morning, in a shed eleven feet by nine, with a bench against the wall on the days the rota said, and the fifth line empty, and the sixth line empty and always would be, and a column on the wall list with nothing at the head of it, and about eighty people in the lane, and Teodor Lask reading somebody's second line out loud twice at the ordinary speed to about twenty people who had all come down off the terraces in the night.
