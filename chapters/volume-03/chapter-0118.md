# Chapter 118 — The Column at the Second Bell

The depot at Verrow is a long low building with a counting floor at one end and a yard at the other, and about nine offices off the long side, and the third office is the projection desk, and it is nine feet by eleven, and Petrus Lenge had it to himself on the eleventh of the rains month at the second bell because the projection desk is only used at night by one man and he was the man.

Marek Vale came up the yard road behind him with a bag and a coat and no authority of any kind, and had been let in by a door-keeper who looked at a Crown March clerk's slip for about four seconds and then at the man behind him, and said, "Are you his?"

"No."

"Then you can go up the stairs and not round the floor," said the door-keeper, "and be back in the yard at the fourth bell, and I have not written you down and I am not going to."

That is how a man with no office gets into a room: because the room's own door-keeper has no form on which to enter him, and a door-keeper is not going to invent one at the second bell in the rains month.

---

The desk had a lamp on it and a bound ledger and a rule and a pen and a blotter and a slate, and the slate was the reason Marek knew he was in the right room, because a man does not keep a slate on a desk for any reason except that a bound book cannot be laid flat with a thumb in it.

"Nobody may see the book," said Petrus Lenge, before he had the lamp up. "That is not the depot's rule, it is mine, and the reason is that if a man in a good coat looks into a bound ledger at night then for the rest of his life everybody who asks him anything will say *you were in the book*, and I would rather you had the slate."

He put the lamp at his elbow and turned the book round a quarter and opened it at a page, and the page had about forty ruled lines in it, and the third column had a head printed above it, and the lines under the head had figures in them in about nine different hands, which is what four years of men doing one column looks like.

"That is last season," he said. "Not last season's season. Last season's page, which is what I work from. The column for the coming season is clean, and it is on the facing page, and it took me from the second bell until about the fifth last night and about half of that was getting the returns in, because about nine districts have already sent sheets and about four of them have sent the same sheet twice."

---

This is what a projection is from the inside, and it is nine minutes and no speech.

A depot does not measure a district. A depot gets a sheet from a district and the sheet is the district's own account of itself, and the sheet is late or it is wrong or it is not sent, and a depot that waited for a sheet before it wrote anything into its book would close about nine months of every year with a hole in it and would be an office that could not do its job.

So the depot writes what it is going to write and then, when the sheets come in, it *corrects* what it wrote. That is the whole of the craft and there is nothing else in it.

"You write the number first," said Lenge, "and then you find out what you should have written, and then you put the second one in with two initials beside it and the first one stays on the page. And that is why a projection column is the only place in this building where you will find two figures on one line, and it is not a mistake, and if anybody in this building ever strikes one of them out I will want to know why."

He wrote the first figure in the first space. Then he went down a column of nine districts doing the same thing, and Marek stood four feet off the desk and watched the same nine digits go onto nine lines in a row in a hand that was not his and not anybody's, in about six minutes, and every one of them out of a slate.

---

"Marek," said Lenge, without looking up. "You are going to ask me what the slate is."

"I am going to ask you what the slate is."

"It is the mean." He turned the slate about ninety degrees so that it faced away, which was a thing a man did when he was being careful and not a thing a man did when he was being honest. "It is the ten-year figure for the districts in this year's book, taken once a season from the table, and the table is in the first office and it is not mine, and I do not copy it out in front of anybody, including you, and I am not being dramatic. If I copy a figure off a table onto a slate and a man reads it, then the man has the figure, and if the man is ever asked to produce it he has to produce a slate he copied in a room he was let into by a door-keeper who did not write him down. That is not a figure. That is a story with a number in it."

"I would not have written it down."

"I know. You have never written anything down since the year turned and it is the single most tiresome thing about you." He went back to the column. "And the districts on that slate are keyed by number, not by name, because a book that keys a district by name can be read out. A book that keys a district by number cannot, and that is not a secrecy. It is a grammar."

"Then I cannot tell you which of those is the four terraces."

"You cannot," said Petrus Lenge, "and neither can the man who wrote the number, and I have looked for that number four years and I can find it in about nine seconds and I am not going to, because if I say a number to a man in a good coat at the second bell then the number is in a man in a good coat, and about four months later a man in a good coat is standing in a room saying *the depot's own estimate*, and there is no such thing as the depot's own estimate, and if there were, then an offer can be sued, and I have read that rule and I have understood it and I did not understand it in the tenth month when a man explained it to me on a stair."

---

Marek said, "The third terrace has not sent a return for two years."

"Two years. It sent one for nineteen and then it stopped and nobody is permitted to send anybody, and I have written *district has not returned, cause not known, no officer in this office has standing to inquire* in my own report two years running, and I mean every word of it, and I have also carried the figure forward for it two years running, out of the page, and if you want to know whether I sleep I will tell you about that some other time."

"I am not accusing you of anything. You are the only person in this basin who has told me the truth about a number in about four days and I have come up here on purpose to be in the room while you do it."

"I know why you are here. You are here because a clerk from the Chancellor's office wrote four names on a form in Tallow Ward and went back to the hills with them, and the four names are on a page in that building, and you have not got the page and you are never going to get the page, and you have come eleven days up a road to stand in an office at the second bell so that when somebody asks you in a year what you have done about it you can say you were in the room." He put the pen down. "That is not a reason anybody would put in a minute and it is a real reason and I would have given it myself if I were you and had no other."

---

Neris Kade was in the yard at about the third bell with a satchel of returns under her arm, and she had been in the building since the eighth bell and had certified four sheets and had one sheet in her satchel that was not a return at all, and she was the season examiner for the Red Uplands, which is not an office and cannot vote anywhere and gives her a piece of paper that holds about four figures in the order they are called.

She came into the third office at about the third bell and put the satchel on the floor and said, in the voice of a woman who has been in a building all day and has eaten nothing:

"The third terrace has not sent me a return either."

"I know. He has told me."

"Then you know that I have put *not returned* in my own report for two years and that a season examiner's report is not a return and is not entered anywhere and does not go up to a board, and that I am the only person in this kingdom whose job it is to count a terrace that will not be counted, and that I have a piece of paper that says four figures in an order and I have never once been in a room where anybody called a figure out."

Marek looked at her for about two seconds and then said, "There is a figure in the column for the third terrace and it came off a page, and the page is not in this building."

"No," said Neris Kade. "It is not."

She picked the satchel up and went back out, and she did not say thank you, and he did not say anything, and neither of them mentioned the four names in Tallow Ward, which she had heard about and which she was right not to be told about in a room in the hills.

---

At the fourth bell Petrus Lenge blew the lamp at the top of the column, and ruled a line at the foot of the page, and wrote nothing under it, because there is no total under that line and there never has been, and then he shut the book and put it on the shelf and the slate on top of it.

"Forty-one lines," he said. "Two of them are the Crown March districts and the rest are this region's, and there are four terraces inside one of them, and a district with four terraces in it is one line, and the four terraces are not four lines, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a terrace is a line, and I have looked, twice, in four years, and both times I was told to stop looking by a man who outranked me and who was right."

"How long is the column, at the second bell, every night?"

"Three nights, usually. Tonight was the first and it was short because about nine districts sent in and I did not have to correct anything." He took the lamp off the desk. "And I have been doing it four years and there is a man on the floor who has been doing it nineteen, and he came in at the second bell tonight and put his coat on the back of his chair and said *is that the third one* and went home. That is the entire profession. Nineteen years of a man saying *is that the third one*."

Marek Vale went down the yard road at the fourth bell with nothing in his coat, because a number on a slate in a working room at night is not a record and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with no office may hold one, and the only thing he had come up here for was to watch a man do a night's work and find out what a projection is, and he had found out, and it could not be used, and the column was full and the book would close.
