# Chapter 144 — The Last Night of the Ninth Month

The first frost came in on the last night of the ninth month and the year turned in it, and the only thing in Rainmarket that knew the date was a woman of thirty-four with a bench book, because the seed house counts on the last night of every month whether or not anything has happened, and nothing had happened, and she wrote the number down and put the string across the top of the page.

The year turned at some point in the night and there is no bell in this basin for it and no office that keeps a calendar anybody in a lane has ever been shown.

The frost was thin enough to walk on and thick enough by the fifth bell to put a skim on the water in the trough at the Gate, and by the second bell in Rainmarket about nine people had broken it with a heel because a trough that has a skim on it in the first cold is a trough nobody can get a pail into, and one of the nine was Petrik, and he did it with a flat stone and not with his hands and did not say anything about having been told to.

---

By the last day of the ninth month the four terraces had been bought seed.

That is the plainest way of putting it and it is the way a keeper would put it, and the plainness is doing all the work, because the sentence has no office in it and no form under it and no figure anywhere, and there is no version of it that anybody in Crown March could be served with.

Eleven households on four terraces had each gone to a shop off the low-ground road in three days in the last week of the month and each had paid a depot's price in coin out of ground and not out of a pledge, and there was no line against any of them in any book, and a man with a slate had six of the eleven sentences on a sheet of his own hung on a nail in his shop with nothing at the head of them, and five of the eleven he had rubbed out at the end of the third day because he has a house and a family and a business that has to be worth something in a year's time.

The stores were shut. The kept-back of the first terrace had come up the road on the sixth and the seventh and the second's on the ninth and the fourth's had come off last over four days of carting into a room off a seed room. The bought seed went up on a cart on the third of the tenth month, and nine people from the Share went with it and spent about nine days on ditches and drains and a wall that a wall has to have mended before the rain, and the sowing goes in in the second month and nobody on four terraces is going to be short of seed in the second month.

The low ground's second sowing did not come. It did not come in the harvest month and it did not come in the ninth month and nobody sent anybody, and the carts turned round on the ninth of the harvest month, and about a fifth of the first sowing did not come either and the third was thin and slow and about half of it never made a sheaf. The chaff stall on the low-ground road is not a stall. The rate has not moved since the fifth of the second month and has not moved since, and Kuez still reads it out to every cart that crosses, and he will not say the figure, because it is in a bridge book with a day against every line and nobody has ever come to look at the book.

And a district that has come in short does not pay. A district answers a shortfall with a rate, and a rate goes on a person with a name, and by the fourth month there will be about nine names available, and none of them is anybody in a room with an office, and every one of them is on the low ground.

---

Mirren Fisk did the Share's arithmetic in the yard on the twenty-ninth of the ninth month and she did it out loud, twice, because she wanted it said in front of the people it was about and not in a room.

Nine members, nine days each, so eighty-one days of labour on a rule nineteen years old that divides a failed lot by trays. Paid in seed, at the depot's price, because a depot's price is a price and not a favour and a favour goes round a valley in about four hours.

The seed that came back to the Share was worth more than eighty-one days, by a good deal, and Fisk said so, and there was no cheering, because the seed came back and the days did not.

"That is the trade," she said, "and the trade is the right trade, and I want the two numbers said out loud in this yard one after the other without a word between them, because a man who hears the first one and not the second one is going to go home and be pleased about it.

"Eighty-one days. And a measure of seed at a depot's price. And the days are gone and the seed is in a drawer, and a day does not come back and a sack does, and I did the sum twice and I am not going to do it a third time in front of you because I would get the same answer and I would like to keep the room."

Somebody at the back said that the Share had done well out of it.

"The Share has done well out of it," said Mirren Fisk, "and the Share is the sixty-one of us, and the sixty-one of us are the ones with eleven lines on a piece of sack coming in the fourth month, and I am going to go to eleven kitchens and say a number out loud, and I have not asked that man for anything and he has not asked me for anything, and every pound that went up that road came out of this yard and went to four households that are not ours.

"And I would like it entered that I said that, and that nobody thanked me, and that if it is wrong then I did it anyway and I will do it again in the fourth month and I will not be stopped."

---

The thing that was said on the last day of the ninth month, and said once, and not in the yard, was said by a woman of thirty-eight at the end of a bench at about the fifth bell with a rag in her hand.

Marek Vale had come down on the foot of the road with nothing to say, which was new, and Mileva Vukic looked at him across sixty-one trays and said:

"You have come down eleven switchbacks with nothing to say."

"I have."

"Then you can say the thing you have come to say, which is that the first terrace is the first terrace in a list of four and it is not because of anything I have done, and the counterweight found this ground before it found any of the other three, and the seed for this ground was bought on the twenty-sixth of the month off the low-ground road at a price a man in a good coat set, and none of those four facts is a reason and I would like all of them said out loud in a row so that nobody in a room in nine years can pick one of them up on its own and make it into a story about me."

He said them out loud in a row, and she listened to all of them, and then she said:

"I am not going to be grateful to you, and I want the reason to be written down somewhere, and the reason is that I would have bought the seed. I have thirty-eight years and a spring that has come up in the same field every year of my life and a heifer that has not come back in heat, and I would have gone down that road and paid that price and carried it up on my back, and the only thing your room did was be a room.

"And I am not going to be grateful to the first terrace either, and I am not going to be careful with it, and I do not care who hears it, and the first terrace is going to have a good year in the second month and the second month of the year after that and the one after that, and about four hundred people on four terraces are going to go on sowing because a man in a good coat wrote a number in a room in MR 412 and nobody was asked, and the money that is not being taken back is not the seed.

"And I have got about eleven weeks' worth of that in my head," she said, "and about four of them are gone, and I have not been told anything since the fourteenth of the harvest month, and I would like to know whether a man says a thing like this twice or whether he says it once and then lets a woman find out on her own. I am not asking for it tonight. I am asking you to think about it up that road, because you are going to have to."

---

There are now six places in this basin with a column on them and nothing at the head of it.

One is on a wall list in a shed in Tallow Ward with about forty lines on it. One is a sheet of oiled board on the seed house wall with the days on it and no keeper's name, and there are sixty-one members and which one of the sixty-one is on the rota is a question the yard has no standing to ask and the Share has no form to ask it with. One is the back page of a bench book where a count goes every night. One is a piece of sack in a drawer in a front room under a folded apron. One is the fifth line of a form cut in a copy room in Bell Stair in the harvest month by a man who cut it on purpose and would not cut a head for it. And one is a sheet of six lines hung on a nail in a shop off the low-ground road, put up by the man who wrote it, who has a business that has to be worth something in a year's time.

Nobody has named any of the six, and no office in this kingdom has ever been told that any of the six exists, and the one on the nail is the only one of the six that a man in this basin has put up of his own free will and could take down in about four seconds and will not.

---

The year turned in the night and nobody was told, and the frost did not last, and on the first day of the tenth month a barrow came round the Gate tank at about the sixth bell with a hundred-pound sack on it and a yard-man took it off with his back, and a slate in an office had a wet mark on it that had gone from grey to nothing at all across about three days and had never been legible to anybody, and a man with no office walked up the low-ground road at the first bell with a bag on his shoulder to be on the first cart up the eleven switchbacks, and he had not told anybody he was going and he had not been asked to and there was no form on which anybody could have been asked to be told.

The last bell of the ninth month went at the seed house and Petrik said the number out loud and did not write it, and Hana Belic wrote it on the bench book with the string across the top of the page and nothing at the head of it, and the lamp went out at the seventh bell because the lamp goes out at the seventh bell.
