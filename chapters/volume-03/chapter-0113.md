# Chapter 113 — Nine Lines

There are four terraces and the fourth is the top and it is called Hartlow, and the season works is made in a loft over a seed room at Hartlow because the keeper of the roll keeps the roll and the roll is the only thing anybody in the valley has ever agreed to keep.

It was made on the eleventh of the second month, in about an hour, by four people and a knife.

The works is a board. It has been a board for longer than anybody can remember and it has the last season's lines still cut into it, and this season's lines are cut in above them, and you can read the old season if you stand at the right end of the loft in the light. Nine lines is the whole of it. Cutting. A drain. The shared threshing floor. A road. A water. Two store rooms. A seed store. And the fourth-terrace store, which is the biggest and the only one with a key.

Marek Vale came up the road on the eleventh with a Crown March clerk called Petrus Lenge, who had eleven sets of a paper in a bundle and a day of switchbacks behind him and the manner of a man who has been told to be pleasant to people and has decided to be tired instead, and they came into the yard at about the fifth bell and Lenge asked for the fourth terrace's holding-holder and Janko Vrabec came out of the low door with a lamp in his hand that had a very small wick in it.

"You are the one with the box," said Janko Vrabec.

"I am."

"Then you will want the fourth terrace, and I am the fourth terrace, and I am fifty-eight and I have a bench to sit on and you may sit down." He looked at Marek. "And you are the one from the shed with the four lines, and I have not forgotten which of those was mine, and I have not read about the other one and I am not going to."

"I have not written anything down," said Marek.

"That is not what I asked and I know it is not, and I have said the sentence and it is out of my mouth now, and we have a man from a depot in a yard and about nine other people in it."

---

The loft was over the seed room and it was reached by a ladder that was not a ladder, and the board was on the wall at the gable end where the light came in sideways, and there was a straight-edge and a knife and a lump of chalk and about nine people in a space built for four.

The making of it took forty minutes and it was not a discussion. A first-terrace man of about sixty cut the lines. A woman from the second terrace read the old season out loud, because he could not read, and Ruta Vrabec said what the third terrace had said the year before, which was nothing, because the third terrace had not said anything the year before either.

They went through the nine. Cutting, and the ground it is on. A drain, and the ground it is under. The threshing floor, and the wall it is holding up, which is the second terrace's wall and is about a terrace's worth of seed. A road, and the four miles of it, and a culvert in it that a bridgewright has looked at in a book and says is wrong. A water. Two store rooms, which are the sixth and the seventh, and are not the same store room. A seed store. And the ninth.

The eighth is the second terrace's store and it has a name on it because a store room with a lock has a holder and a holder is a person. The ninth is the fourth-terrace store, the kept-back seed, the only seed in the valley that will still be there in the eleventh month, and it is the only one with a key, and it is the only one that has ever had a name cut into the board beside it.

Janko Vrabec cut his own name out himself, in the second month, with the knife, in about nine seconds, in front of Ruta, and nobody argued with him about it.

"Do that again," said Zofia Teran, from the other side of the loft.

"I am not going to do it again."

"Then it is a line and there is nothing in it, and I want it said out loud in front of these people that the ninth line is empty and not that it is finished."

"It is empty because the ninth line has to have a person in it and the person is me, and I am going to be dead in the winter, and in the winter somebody is going to have to strike a man's name off a list, and there is no box in this kingdom for striking a name off a list." He was not angry and he had the lamp in his hand. "That is why there is nothing in it. It is not a hole anybody can work around and it is not a job anybody can be given to, and there is no form on which any of you can be applied for, and I have not got a second key and I am not going to have one made, because the day there are two keys on that store then there is a question about who holds the second one, and the answer to that question is a name, and the name would be on this board, and I am the only man in this valley who can stop that happening."

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and then Zofia Teran said, "Write the eight."

---

Then the argument happened, and it was short, and everybody in it was right, and Marek Vale wrote down two sentences and burned them later in a yard because they were not his.

"The third terrace is not on that board," said Zofia Teran.

"The third terrace is four lines of ground above the water and the cutting on it is done in the same week as the cutting on the first," said the man with the knife. "And the third terrace sent nobody. It has sent nobody to two of these and it sent a sheet up to the hills for nineteen years and this year it did not send the sheet, and I am not putting a terrace on a board because I would like it on the board."

"Then the third terrace's cutting gets done by whoever turns up."

"The third terrace's cutting has got to be done by somebody and it will be done by whoever turns up and the board is the board that says it is a job." The man with the knife put the straight-edge down. "And there is nothing on this board about who owns the line, and it never has been, and a line is a job and a job is done by whoever comes, and I do not want my name on a cutting and neither do you, and the only line on this board that ever has had a person's name on it is the ninth, and the ninth is empty for the reason that man has given and not for mine."

"That is the whole of the argument," said Janko Vrabec, "and it took nine minutes last season as well, and the answer was the same, and I would like it read out that the answer was the same."

---

Tomas Vale came into the loft at about the sixth bell with his coat off and a roll of pegs in his hand, having been out since the fifth looking at the culvert on the fourth line, and Lenge had by then served four sets and been turned away from one door, which was a door on the first terrace held by a man who had looked at the bundle and said the word *advance* and shut it.

Marek had something to tell him.

He had had it since the sixth, when a clerk in a room over a chandler's shop had turned back a page and copied a figure into a column, and it was a figure about four hundred acres of ground above the water on a road with a day of switchbacks in it, and Tomas was going to be standing in a loft in about an hour while a man from a depot had a returns sheet on his knee, and Tomas was going to be asked questions about a culvert and a wall.

He had had it for five days and he had carried it up eleven switchbacks twice and had not said it, and the reason he had not said it was not a reason. He had been waiting until he could say it in a form that could not be improved on, and there is no such form, and he had known that on the sixth.

Tomas put the pegs down on the boards of the loft floor and looked at the board.

"Two lines," he said. "Two and four."

"Two and four."

"Give me the board." He got a lump of chalk out of his own coat and wrote his bill on the board in the margin against the drain and against the road, in a bridgewright's hand, with a figure under it and nothing else — no date, because there is no date on the board, and he wrote what each figure was for and what it was not for. It took him about two minutes and he did not consult Lenge and he did not consult anybody.

"You cannot be paid for that," said Lenge, from the ladder head.

"No."

"It is not on a form. It is on a board in a loft in four hundred yards and it has no date on it and no name at the foot of it but yours, and the depot cannot order it and neither can the four terraces, because the four terraces are not a body and have never been a body and would not want to be one."

"Then it is a bill," said Tomas Vale, "and I will do the work and the four people who live under the wall will know I did it, and that is the whole of the payment and I am not going to be thanked for it, and if I am thanked I will take the thanking and I will not put it anywhere."

"Four feet too low," he said, to Marek, and he did not raise his voice, and it was the fourth line and he had the culvert in front of him. "Come down and look at it and then do not tell me about it in here, because I have been up this road thirty-four years and I do not need a plan for a drain and I have said so in a kitchen and I am not going to say it again in a loft."

---

They went down the ladder and then down the switchbacks, all of them, and got to the culvert at about the seventh bell, and there were about nine people there because there are always about nine people on a road on a terrace in the second month.

Tomas was right about the mouth of it. He got down into it with a lamp and came up and said four sentences, and none of them were a plan. *Whoever built that put the inlet four feet below the invert, and the invert is the floor, and the floor is the wrong place, and the whole of the water is going to stand in the pipe instead of going through it, and there is a wall on the second terrace that has been holding up nine trays' worth of ground since the flood year and it is being held up by a pipe that is four feet too low.* And then he said the thing about the drain that made the whole morning worth the walk: *And when the pipe fills, that is line two going in the third month, and line two is on that board, and I am not going to be the reason line two is on that board. The wall is going either way. I am just telling you which week.*

Nine feet down the road from the culvert there was about four feet of drain, and it had a wall on one side of it and about nine inches of silt in it, and a man with a bar could have got into it in an afternoon.

Marek Vale got the bar out and went down into it and worked for about an hour, and came up with silt in his hair, and was not thanked, and did not expect to be, and about four people told him where to put it and he did it, and Tomas Vale did the part where the bar went under the lip and levered, which is the only part of it that is craft, and which Marek could not have done in a year.

At the top of the drain, in the dark, with the lamp in his hand, Tomas asked him the question.

"Which column," he said, "and whose number in it."

And Marek Vale gave him a technically accurate answer about where the head of the column is set, and about who sets it, and about where it is printed, and it was all of it true, and it was not the question.

"The third terrace," said Marek, and stopped, about four seconds too late.

"The third terrace has not sent a sheet for a year."

"No."

"So there is a figure in it."

"There is a figure in it," said Marek, "and it is last season's figure, and the man who wrote it four years ago is not in this basin, and the man who wrote it this season is in a room over a chandler's shop in Morrowfen and did it on the ninth of the second month in front of me, and it was correct, and if he had not done it there would be a hole in the returns sheet, and the hole would have gone up that road to about nine doors and one of them would have had a piece of paper saying that a man in Verrow declined to write a number for their ground."

Tomas Vale stood on a road four miles above a stone bridge in the dark with a lamp in his hand and did not raise his voice and did not use the word *sorry*.

"You have known that since the sixth," he said.

"Yes."

"And you are up a ladder in a loft with about nine people and a man from a depot on the boards, and I am asking you what is in a column, and you told me where the head of it is set."

"Yes."

"All right," said Tomas Vale, and got the lamp back, and went down to the culvert to look at the lip of the pipe again, and did not raise it again for the rest of the night and not for the rest of the season.

Marek Vale stood on a road with silt in his hair and did not follow him and did not go after him about it. There is no form in this kingdom in which a man may be told afterwards that he was in the wrong week, and there is one in Tallow Ward that will read a sentence out twice at the ordinary speed to about twenty people on anybody's paper except his own, and he had known that since the ninth of the ninth month, and it was the fourth time in two years that knowing it had not been the same as being able to use it.
