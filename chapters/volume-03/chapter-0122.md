# Chapter 122 — The Woman Who Has to Be Asked

Zofia Teran's yard is four miles above a stone bridge and the road between has eleven switchbacks in it, and Marek Vale came up it on the seventh of the growth month at about the fourth bell, on foot, with the lamp he had not lit the night before still in his coat, and got to the top of it having been passed by two carts of stone going the other way and neither cart's driver having any opinion about him.

She was in the yard with a rope and a child's coat over the rail and about nine other people in it, and there was a bar leaning against the wall where it had been leaning for a week, and two men were in the cut nine feet down the lane with a bar of their own.

"You have not slept," she said.

"I have slept."

"You have done the thing where a man has slept," said Zofia Teran. "Sit down. You are not going to be helped up eleven switchbacks and I am not going to carry anything this season."

She put the child's coat on the rail because it was in the way, which was the only accommodation she made him all afternoon, and then she turned round and looked at him and said:

"Now. You have come to ask me who to give a season to."

---

Marek had prepared for it. He had got as far as the culvert the night before and had a sentence, and the sentence was *there is a paper and the paper names a class and I cannot ask a class anything, so I need the name of a person*, and it was true and it was the shape of the thing and he heard it come out of his own mouth sounding like a man in a shed in Tallow Ward explaining a rule to a queue, and he stopped about nine words in.

"That is not the question," said Zofia Teran.

"It is the question."

"It is the beginning of the question and it is the part you have already got, and you have come four miles to give me the part you have got." She put the rope down on the rail. "Try again and say the other part."

Marek said the other part.

*There is a season in this basin that has not been paid for. Its paper says it lands on the seed-keepers of the four terraces after this season. I can put my hand on it and I can point it. It will go where I point it. I do not know which of them actually needs a season, because the paper does not say, and the paper has never said, and a person who is named on a paper in this basin does not get asked. So I have to ask somebody who has been to the doors.*

Zofia Teran stood in her own yard with her hands on her apron and did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and about nine people in a lane four hundred yards above a stone bridge found that they had better get on with what they were doing.

"Say that last part again," she said.

"I have to ask somebody who has been to the doors."

"You have to ask *me*," said Zofia Teran. "You have not said that. You have said *somebody*. And I have been to the doors and you know I have been to the doors, and there is a woman in this yard who has been to the doors and has not said a name to anybody for a year, and you have known that since the ninth of the third month, when I told you the reason."

"I have."

"Then say why I will not."

---

She said it standing up, without being asked twice, and it took about a minute, and she did not raise her voice, because she had been saying sentences to men in rooms for a year.

"Because a season is not a thing anybody in this valley has ever been given, and the first time one of us is named out loud, that woman is not going to be the one it lands on. She is going to be the one it is said to have landed on. And I have got five households on this terrace whose ground came up as sand and about nine of them have children, and if I say a name to a man with no office in a yard with nine people in it, then for the rest of the year that household gets what nobody else gets. The neighbours' kindness. The depot's price. A cart when there is not a cart. And the season, whatever it is, will land about nine doors away, and there will be exactly one household in this valley that anybody thinks about, and it will not be because of what you do, it will be because of what I said in a yard."

"Mrs Teran—"

"And the second thing," she said, "which is worse, and I have thought about it on four walks down to the low ground since the seed month. If I give you a name, then I have decided who a class lands on, and the class is four hundred and some people, and I am one of forty-four-year-old women on a terrace who keeps a book. And there is no form in this kingdom in which anybody may be given that number, and I do not want it, and I have said in a yard in the third month that I decided not to and not that I forgot, and you were in that yard."

---

Marek Vale stood in the yard and let the two sentences stand, because there was nothing to be done with them.

That was the first honest thing that happened to him in nine days and it was not comfortable and it was not a relief, and about four seconds after she finished he understood that he had been waiting four months for somebody to be angry with him about it and there was going to be a great deal more of this before there was any, and that what she had just given him was not a name and was not an argument against giving him one.

It was a question, and it was hers, and it had been sitting in her own yard for nine months waiting for somebody with a hand.

"Ask me the one you have not asked," said Zofia Teran. "You have asked me whether I will. Everybody asks me whether I will. Ask me the other thing, and then I will answer the one you came for, and I want the nine words to be mine and not yours."

"Who is going to tell her."

"Ah," said Zofia Teran. "That took you a day and a half. I would have thought a man who reads out loud four hours a day for nothing would be quicker at it."

---

"There is nobody," said Marek.

"No."

"There is no form in this kingdom in which a person may be told that a season has moved. The paper is eleven miles up a road in a locked box in a depot in the hills. The nine words are printed on it. The party is a number. And there is no office in this city that could serve a notice on a household saying that a season of the valley's has been pointed at her, and there is no office in the hills that could serve one, because the season was not bought from them and it cannot be given back to them, and if it could there would be a form for it and there is not."

"That is four sentences and every one of them is right," said Zofia Teran, "and you have said them to me as though saying them was the work."

"It is not the work."

"It is not the work at all," she said. "You are going to pick a household. You are going to go into her ground and make a season happen in it that would not have happened, and she is going to find that out from her ground, on a day, in about four days, with her hands, and she is not going to be told. And if she comes down the lane and asks me what happened, and I say *a man with no office came up a road and I named you*, then what has she got? She has got me. That is all she has got. She has got a woman on a terrace and a road and nothing on a paper, and a woman on a terrace is not a thing anybody has to answer to, and by the fourth of the harvest month there will be a seed house in Rainmarket with a lamp on it telling that story and not hers."

Marek said nothing.

"So say the true thing out loud," said Zofia Teran, "in front of the two people who are going to have to look at you afterwards. Because I am about to do the thing you have come for and I am not doing it for a man and I want to hear you say first that the woman is going to find out from her ground."

"I am going to say it."

"Then say it before I answer, and say it in my yard, and if you say it in a loft in four days I will not answer you at all."

---

Marek Vale said it in her yard, standing up, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people and a rope on a rail, and it came out badly and he let it come out badly.

"A woman is going to find out from her ground," he said, "that somebody decided she needed a season, and nobody will be able to tell her who, and she is not going to be asked, and if she asks, the only person who chose is a woman of forty-four with a school exercise book who is not a clerk and does not want to be one. And the season does not belong to me and I cannot make it bigger and I cannot make it go where I did not point it and I cannot give it back. And I am telling you this in your yard and not in a room because there is no room."

Zofia Teran looked at him for about four seconds.

Then she said: "Say the last part again. *I cannot give it back.* That is the part I have not heard a man say out loud in a year and I want to be sure of the shape of it."

"I cannot give it back."

"Right," she said. "Then I will give you a name and I will not tell you why, and you will not ask me twice, and if you ask me twice I will not give you a second one and you will have the first one and it will be the wrong one."

---

She did not tell him why, and he did not ask twice, and about four people in the lane heard a household's name and about five did not and one of the five was a man with a bar who was in the cut.

**Stana Peric**, thirty-one, second terrace, on her mother's line, five children, the eldest nine, and nine lots out of the thirty-nine sown on that terrace that had come up as sand, and no kept-back store of her own because a kept-back store has a holder and a holder is a person and the person is her mother, who is fifty-eight and does not count.

Marek wrote the name nowhere. He said it back to her once, correctly, and she said *once is enough and if you write it down I will know*, and he did not write it down, and there are three things in this basin in which a man with no office may not put a name: the register, the bench book, and a sheet of his own with nothing at the head of it.

Zofia Teran then went and got the exercise book off a shelf inside the door and did not open it, and held it for about four seconds, and put it back.

"You do not need this," she said. "That is the second time this year somebody has come up this road wanting to look at it. The first time was a man in a good coat and he left it on a bench in Bell Stair and it is a record of about nine houses that somebody's children have not been dry since the seed month, and I would like it noticed that I gave it to him on purpose."

"I am not going to ask to see it."

"You are going to need to know what she said at her door in the third month and I am not going to tell you, and that is not me being difficult, that is me telling you what the book is." She put her hand flat on the door. "It is not a record. There is no name at the head of it and no name at the foot of it and it lives in an apron and you cannot check a word of it and it is a woman's account of what she was told, and what she was told is not what was said. And the only thing in it that anybody can check is the day, and the day is the only thing anybody has ever wanted off it."

Then she went back to the rail and picked the rope up, and Marek went down the eleven switchbacks with a name in his head and a lamp he had still not lit, and the two men in the cut were still in the cut, and the bar was still leaning against the wall, and nobody thanked anybody.

The register in Tallow Ward opened at the fourth bell the next morning and the fifth line of the form was empty and the sixth line was empty and always would be, and a column on a wall list in a shed eleven feet by nine had nothing at the head of it and nineteen hands had declined to name it and a yard had voted in four minutes not to fill it and not to rule it out, and about eighty people were in the lane, and a man with no office read somebody's second line out loud twice at the ordinary speed to about twenty people and had a household's name in his head and no form in this kingdom on which he could have entered it.
