# Chapter 121 — What a Season Is For

The eleventh tray had been sand since the seed month and Hana Belic had not thrown it out, and on the sixth of the growth month she had put it at the end of the bench nearest the door, where nobody stood to look at it, and the lamp had been burning since the third of the third month, and the difference the lamp made was that a person could now see that it was sand.

Marek Vale came in at about the ninth bell with his coat wet through and no lamp of his own and stood in the doorway for a while so that Belic could look at him and decide.

"You have come to look at a tray," she said.

"I have come to ask you a question and the question is not about the tray, and I am going to ask it wrong the first time because I do not know the right way yet, and I would like you to stop me when I am doing it."

"All right," said Hana Belic. "I will stop you when you are doing it, and I will not stop you when you are talking, because there is no use in that and I have got sixty-one of these to draw before the bell."

---

She worked while he talked. That is the whole of what he remembered about that night afterwards and it is the only part of it that was any use. She drew a tray along the front of a bench with two fingers, tipped it nine degrees, looked at the bottom of it, put it down, and went on, and the room went on being a room with sixty-one trays in it and a lamp nine inches above the middle of them.

Mirren Fisk came in at about half past and stood at the back with a letter in her hand that she did not put down, and it was a letter with a price and a quantity in it that had come up a road from a depot at Verrow at the beginning of the month, and Fisk had read it out to the members twice and had not answered it, and it was the third week that it had been sitting in her hand in a room.

"You have read that out," said Marek.

"I have read it out twice and I have not answered it and I am not going to answer it this month either," said Mirren Fisk, "and if you have come up here to help me answer it you can go back down four miles and tell the man who sent it that a cooperative of sixty-one is not a person and could not answer it if it were."

"I have not come to help you answer it."

"You have come to sit on my floor at the ninth bell in a wet coat," said Fisk, "and if you are going to be here for an hour, sit on the end bench, because that is the bench people sit on, and I will not have a clerk on the working bench."

---

The first thing he tried, he tried on the sand.

He got down on one knee in front of the eleventh tray with his right hand flat on the rim of it, and he did not shut his eyes and he did not hold his breath, and he did exactly what a man does when he puts his hand on a thing to find out whether a thing is as bad as it looks, which is to wait.

Nothing happened, and about four minutes passed, and nothing happened again, and at the end of the four minutes he got up off his knee and told Belic it was nothing and she said it was sand, and he said he knew it was sand, and she said that was the first correct thing he had said since he came in.

The second thing he tried was worse and he tried it because he had been told nothing happened and he was a man who does not take a thing at the first telling. He put both hands on the boards under the bench, where about nine sacks of kept-back seed stood on their sides against a wall that had been whitewashed so often it had gone the colour of a candle, and he asked for a season in that room.

Nothing happened either, and this time the nothing had a shape to it, because the boards were warm and the sacks were cold and his hands were wet, and he sat back on the end bench with them open on his knees like a man who has been holding something heavy and has put it down.

"You have come up here to do something to my seed," said Hana Belic.

"I have come up here to find out whether I can do something to your seed, and I cannot, and I am going to try one more thing and then I am going to stop, and if I hurt a tray I will carry it down myself and put it in the yard."

"All right," she said. "Do your third thing."

---

The third thing worked and it worked on the wrong object, and the man who had come up eleven switchbacks with a question did not get up off the floor for about a minute afterwards.

What he did was name her. That is the whole of what he did differently and it is four words long. He said the name of the holding she had been born on, which is not a person, and he said the word *there*, and he asked for the season to be where she was standing, at the end of the bench, on that floor, in that room, for that household, in that sowing.

And the eleventh tray came up.

Not all at once and not the way a thing comes up when you water it. The sand had a crust on it about a finger thick where it had been pressed flat by eleven fingers over four months, and the crust lifted at one corner, and under the crust there was about a quarter of an inch of something dark and not sand, and it was not green and it was not going to be green by morning, and it was the only living thing in the eleventh tray.

Hana Belic put her thumb in it and then took her thumb out and looked at it and then looked at him.

"Say that again," she said.

"Which part of it."

"The part where you said my name on the ground. Say it exactly the way you said it."

He said it exactly the way he had said it, and got nothing, which he had expected, and the tray stayed the way it was, and the quarter inch of dark stayed the quarter inch of dark and did not do a single thing.

"Once," said Belic. "That is how you said it. That is not twice. I have heard men try to be careful."

"It is not a matter of—"

"I know it is not a matter of care," said Hana Belic. "I have been drawing trays for nine years and I have watched about nine careful people ruin a bench. You said the holding and you said *there*, and you said it about me, and it did not do anything to me, and it did a quarter of an inch of something to nine months of sand."

She put her hand flat on the rim of the eleventh tray, which is what he had done, and left it there.

"You have got a hand," she said, "and you have not got a person."

---

Marek Vale sat on the end bench of a cooperative's germination bench in the low ground at about the tenth bell with a wet coat and a head that had a small hard place at the front of it, and worked out, slowly, over about a quarter of an hour, four things that he had not known an hour ago, in the order in which he found them.

The first was that the effect he had been able to put a hand on for years without knowing it was there is not a season. A season is not a thing that sits in a room and can be walked into a room by somebody who is not a season's keeper. A season is an effect that somebody obtained, in writing, from an office, on a form, in advance, in a place, for a scope, against a pledge, and it is still travelling, because it has not been paid for, and the paper it is on says *settlement: out of the harvest*, and the harvest of the instrument that bought it was in the tenth month of last year and this year's is not in yet.

The second was that the effect had a name and a direction and it did not have a person in it. He had been thinking of a hand. What he had was a season, and a season goes where it is pointed, and what he had been able to do since the ninth month of MR 413, when he first put his hand on a paper in a cellar eleven steps down and understood that a form could be copied and that a name at the foot of a copy is the whole of the authority in this kingdom, was not a reading. It was a *sighting*. He could see where an authorised effect was standing, the way a man can see where water stands, and he had spent four months believing that seeing was knowing and it was not, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a man with no office may put a sighting in a column.

The third was that the tray had been the wrong target and the reason it was the wrong target was not that a tray is not a person. It was that a tray of sand is the place where the effect was already standing, and the effect does not want to be told to arrive. A season does not answer to being told to go somewhere it is already.

The fourth was that he had got a quarter of an inch out of nine months of dead sand by accident, in a room where nobody was watching, about which nobody will ever be told anything, and that he could not do it again on purpose, and that if he could do it again on purpose then the correct target was going to be a person, and he did not have one.

---

"You are going to tell me something," said Hana Belic, "and I am going to make a guess about what it is and you can correct me or not."

"Go on."

"You have read something." She said it the way a woman says *it is raining* on a bench. "Not a page. There is no page. You have read a thing that is going to be true in the future, and it is true of a valley with about four hundred and some people in it, and it is going to land on about one of them, and you have come up eleven switchbacks in the wet because you have worked out that you can make it land on a different one, and you have come to a seed house because a seed house is the only room in this basin where somebody has ever told me what a tray is worth."

"You have had four Crown March men in this room since the year turned and not one of them asked you that."

"No," said Hana Belic. "That is why I have answered you and I am going to be sorry about it."

"It is the right question. It is the only question anybody has asked me this year that has a person in it."

"Then go and ask it," she said, "and go and ask it of somebody who knows the answer, and do not go and ask it of me, because I draw the eleventh tray and I have been in the dark with sixty-one of them since the sixth of the ninth month and I know what the trays are worth and I do not know what a household is worth and neither does anybody in this room."

She went back to the fourth tray and did not look at him again.

---

Marek Vale left a seed house in the low ground at about the eleventh bell with a head that had a small hard place at the front of it and a quarter of an inch of something dark in the eleventh tray, and he did not write it down, and there is nowhere in this kingdom he could have written it down, and if he had put it in the register at the fourth bell in the morning Teodor Lask would have read it out twice at the ordinary speed to about twenty people in a lane, because that is what the register is for and because a man with no office may enter a line about his own paper and nobody may enter a line about anybody else's and a man who can move a season into a valley has a paper, and the person behind it is in the room, and the line would have gone in.

He went down the low-ground road in the dark and then took the upland road and walked it for about an hour, and at about the first bell he stopped at the culvert on the fourth line, where the inlet is four feet below the invert, and stood in the wet and thought about a woman's voice on a bench.

*You have got a hand and you have not got a person.*

The instrument says *the seed-keepers of the four terraces after this season*, in nine words, on a printed form, in four parts, with a mark in the corner that names a month. There are four hundred and some people in those four terraces and about nine on any paper anybody has ever seen, and the nine words do not have a person in them, and they cannot be made to have one, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a class of people may be given a number.

A season that is going to land on four hundred and some people does not need a hand. It needs a name, and the name has to be a person's, because a hand cannot tell a field which field.

And a tray in Rainmarket was not a person, and that is what the sixth of the growth month had been about: the effect's own ground is the basin, and Rainmarket is in the basin, and a germination bench in the low ground is inside its scope the way a road is inside a road's scope. It had answered a holding, and it had answered a tray, and it had not answered the woman standing over either, and it would not have answered four hundred acres above a stone bridge until somebody said a name out loud and meant a person.

At about the second bell he started walking up the switchbacks with a lamp he had not lit, and by the time he was at the bridge he had worked out that there is exactly one person in this basin who has written down, in her own hand, what was said to her at each door in a season, and that she is four hundred yards above a stone bridge, and that she has told about nine people this year that she is not going to be the woman who came down the hill.

At the seventh bell of the next morning the count went onto the bench book with nothing at the head of it, and the count was sixty-one, and the eleventh tray was the eleventh, and the quarter of an inch of something dark in it had not moved in the night, and about nine people had come into that shed since the third of the third month who had not come into it before that date, and nobody had sent anybody.
