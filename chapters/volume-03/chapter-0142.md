# Chapter 142 — A Room With No Office

There were eleven sets of Crown March paper on the bench of the Elmsworth seed house on the morning of the twenty-fourth of the ninth month, laid out in a row the way a woman lays out eleven things she has counted twice, and nine of them had never been opened, and they had been on a nail in the corner of that room since the sixth of the third month.

The sixty-one trays stood along the long wall under oiled paper in two ranks. The lamp burned from the first bell and it had been in since the third month and nobody in that room had said out loud what it had done to the work. Petrik was at the far end with the slate and the string and was not writing.

Marek Vale had come down from Tallow Ward on the foot of the road and had been in the room for about an hour before he said anything, which was longer than he had been in any room in four months.

Zofia Teran had come down the eleven switchbacks on the twenty-third and was sitting on a sack with her hands in her lap. Mileva Vukic had come down on the same day by a different road and was standing at the end of the bench with a rag in her hand because there was mud on it.

Petrus Lenge had not come up.

That was the first fact about the room and it was the reason the room existed, and Marek put it on the bench before he put anything else on it.

"He came up in the second month and he came up on the tenth of the harvest month," he said, "and on the second day he said to me on the way down that the depot considers coming up cheap and the second day of it is going to be the day he stops coming up cheap. And he was right, and he stopped, and the reason he stopped is that a man in a good coat has told him twice what this basin costs to be visited by him, and I am not going to be the third reason."

"He has not sent anything," said Mirren Fisk.

"He has sent nothing," said Marek. "That is the depot in this room. A nail, eleven sets, nine of them shut, and a man who is not coming."

---

"What is it for," said Mirren Fisk.

She said it standing at the end of the bench in front of sixty-one trays, and she had said it to him once before in a room over a chandler's yard with a drawer shut, and she was not going to sit down for it.

"I have brought two things," said Marek Vale, "and I am going to say them once and I am not going to argue about either of them, and then I am going to ask the three of you one question and I am not going to answer it myself.

"The first thing is that the advance is eleven thousand and some hundreds of pounds and it is already in the ground in four hundred acres, and an office a night and a day up the river has a box for taking it back and a man whose whole business is taking it back, and it can be done in nine days. And I am not going to say that it would be right, because a man who has been in that office for twenty-nine years has already said in a seed house in the growth month that it is correct and cheap and quick, and he said it himself first, and I have not got a better sentence than his.

"The second thing is that the money does not have to be taken back. It can be left where it is and spent on seed in the four terraces, and that is not correct, it has no office behind it, it cannot be produced by anybody, and it is the only one of the two that does not turn a season that has already failed once into a famine."

He put his hand flat on the bench beside the eleven sets.

"And that is the whole of what I have. I have had it since the fourth of the growth month and I have carried it up and down a road about nine times and I have never once put both of them in a room at the same time, and this is the first room I have found that has nobody in it with a coat on."

---

Nobody in the room said anything for about four seconds, and the trays went on being what they were.

Then Mileva Vukic said, from the end of the bench, without turning round:

"You have not told us what it is *for*."

"I have told you what it is about."

"That is a different sentence and I have heard it said about nine times in four months and every time somebody said it they were about to want something," said Mileva Vukic, "and I am on the first terrace and I have a spring that has come up in the same field every year of my life and it is brackish, and I am not a member of anything and I did not come down this road to have a hand put on my ground again by a man who is very careful."

The room went quiet in a way it had not been quiet in the first hour.

"You did not," said Marek.

"No," said Vukic. "And that is not a thing you have to be told about, so I will tell you the other thing, which is that the second of your two is the only one of the two that is about seed, and I have four hundred acres of ground above a stone bridge that I do not own and have never owned and cannot sow with what I have got, and you have come down eleven switchbacks to tell a room of people who are not the four terraces about it. Ask the four terraces. The four terraces are not in this room."

"They are."

"Two of them are," said Zofia Teran.

---

She had not said anything for the whole of the first hour and she said it now sitting on a sack with her hands still in her lap.

"The first terrace is here and the second terrace is here and the third terrace has four people on it and nobody has been to them and nobody is going to be able to go this season, and the fourth terrace has a man in a seed room who is going to be dead in the winter and has said no to three things in nine days and I have watched three of them," she said, "and I have not come down this road to be in a room where a thing is decided about four terraces by people who are not on them, and I will tell you the reason and then I will sit down again.

"The reason is that the last time four terraces were in a room it was a room in this basin with a form in it and a column on the form for the seed-keepers, and the number of persons in that column is not on the form and cannot be, and four hundred and some people went under a word. I am not saying the room was wicked. I am saying the room had a shape, and this room has a shape too, and this room's shape is that we are all people who are already talking, and the two households above us are not."

"You are both talking," said Marek. "You came down the road."

"We came down the road because we were asked to come down the road by a man with no office," said Zofia Teran, "and that is not the same as being sent, and I have made sure I know the difference every single time, and I have come anyway, and that is mine to answer for and not yours."

---

Mirren Fisk took the rag out of Mileva Vukic's hand and put it on the bench and put the eleven sets out of their row and back into a heap, which is what she did with a thing when she was finished talking about it.

"Right," she said. "You want a room. You have not got one and I am not going to find you one, and I will tell you the reason and then I will tell you what there is.

"There is no room, because a room that could do this would have to be a body, and a body can be told things and can be asked things and can be written to, and a body is a thing that can be produced at a household in nine months, and you have understood that, because you have spent four months not making one.

"And the second thing is worse than the first and it is the reason there is no room and not merely a reason. Look at the nine."

She put her hand flat on the heap and pushed nine of the eleven sets out of it and spread them along the bench.

"Those nine came back unopened in the second month. Not refused. Unopened. A man's coat came to four doors and the doors sent him away, and at the doors he went to, the paper was taken off him and sent back down the road to the depot sealed, and nobody in this valley has ever been told whose door would not take it.

"And you cannot ask," she said. "There is no office in this kingdom in which a person may be asked why their own door sent a thing back. And I have been in four of those yards in my life and I can tell you that not one of the four women in them would tell you, and not one of them was hiding anything, and two of them told me to my face that the paper was not theirs to hold and were right.

"So you have a fact and you cannot get at it, and it has been on that nail since the sixth of the third month, and I have looked at it about nine times and I have never opened one."

---

The door at the bottom of the stairs was open and a man came in with a slate under his arm and put it on the bench end on end.

He was forty-one and he was a factor in Rainmarket and his name was Orren Vaas and he had bought a copy of the season schedule off a man in a good coat in the third month and had never been told in words what it was, and he had come up the road for seed.

"Which of you is the keeper," he said, "because I have been in this room for a quarter of an hour and four people have looked at my slate and nobody has told me what I am standing next to."

"I am," said Mirren Fisk. "And what you are standing next to is nine closed pieces of Crown March paper and a man's coat, and I am going to tell you that you are not going to be told what is in them, and that you are going to be asked to leave them alone, and that you may do either as you please because there is nothing in this room with a lock on it."

He ruled eleven lines on the slate without being asked. It took him about a minute and he ruled them evenly and he squared the end of it with the bench.

"Then I will put a figure on the slate," he said, "because I have come up a road for seed and I do not carry a slate out of a room with nothing on it, and I will tell you what I think it is so that you can correct me, because I would rather be corrected by a keeper than find out in nine months in another county.

"I think eleven thousand and some hundreds of pounds has been advanced against the four terraces and is about to be recovered, and I think it is going to be recovered as a set-off against the coming season, and I think that comes off the seed money, and I think that the four terraces are going to buy less seed in the ninth month than they did in the growth month, and I have a factor's business buying less seed in the ninth month, and that is why I am in this room."

"That is right," said Marek.

"Then it is the whole of what I came for, and I will tell you the other half, which is that I have been buying that paper since the third month because a house in Saltglass pays for a schedule of four terraces in a year that has been paid twice, and I have never been able to find out why and I have stopped trying, and it is the cheapest security I have ever had in a drawer, and it is not in your name and it is not mine, and if anybody in this room wants it back they may have it and I am going to write my name on it and date it and hand it over and be done."

---

The room did not want the paper and nobody said so.

"I have got one question," said Marek, "and I have not got to it before, and I am going to ask it now and I am not going to answer it.

"The party behind that advance is not a holding. It is a district. A district is one line in a book a night and a day up the river and the four terraces are inside it, and a terrace is not a line, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a terrace is a line, and a man in a good coat in that office has told me so twice and been right twice.

"So there is nothing up there to recover against. Not four households. Nothing. And the district is short — the low ground's second sowing did not come and the carts turned round on the ninth of the harvest month and nobody has announced it because there is no office in this basin that sends anybody to tell a district it is short.

"So a district that has come in short does not pay. A district answers a shortfall with a rate. And a rate goes on a person with a name, because a charge has to have somebody on it or it is not a charge, it is a note. And by the fourth month of MR 415 there will be about nine names available, and none of them will be anybody in this room, and every one of them will be on the low ground."

Nobody said anything.

"So there are not two ways of taking it back," said Marek. "There is one, and it is not either of the two I came down here with, and it is the one I have been carrying up and down a road about nine times without noticing, because I have been asking a man in a good coat whether the money can come back off four terraces and the answer has been no every time and I have been hearing the word no and putting it in my pocket instead of in this room."

---

Mileva Vukic put her hand on the bench.

"Say the other one," she said. "Say what is left after the two you brought, and say it once, and then do not say anything for a while, because I have been in this room for an hour and a half and a man has said the word *famine* in it once."

"Four households buy their own seed," said Marek Vale. "At the depot's price. Out of money that has already been spent, which is possible only because nobody is going to collect it, which is possible only because a terrace is not a line. And the Share's people go up and do the sowing, and the Share is paid in seed at the same price, and that is a return in kind, and it is not a gift, and it is not a favour, and I am not going to be able to prove any part of it to anybody in nine months."

"The Share does not spend its own seed on four hundred acres," said Mirren Fisk.

"No it does not."

"I have sixty-one members and I have eleven lines on a piece of sack in a drawer under an apron and I am going to go to eleven kitchens in the fourth month and say a number out loud, and the sixty-one of us are the ones who are short, and you want me to take my seed up a road and hand it to four households that are not mine that have just had the best year anybody in this valley can remember."

"And if they do not sow," said Mileva Vukic, "then the third of us is standing in a field in the fourth month with nothing, and I have been that, and I would rather be told a figure now."

Fisk put both hands flat on the bench and looked at the tray rank for about nine seconds.

"Then it is not the Share's seed," she said. "It is four households' seed, bought at a price, and the Share is paid in seed at the same price, and the price is the depot's price and not yours, because if it is yours it is a favour and a favour goes round this valley in about four hours and I will not have it going round.

"And nobody is to call it a pool," she said, "and I will not say the word in this room, and if any of you has written it on anything I would like to know where."

Nobody had written it on anything.

"And I am not going to be thanked for it," said Mirren Fisk, "by any of you, at any point, in any month, and I am not going to be thanked for it by the people it goes to either, because I am going to walk into four kitchens and be told no by at least two of them and I am going to do it anyway and I have not asked that man for anything."

---

Orren Vaas had eleven lines on his slate and a price in his head and he said:

"I have been in this room for two hours and I have two things I do not have."

"Which two."

"The first is a figure," said Vaas. "Nobody has said one. I have eleven lines and nothing at the head of them and I did that on purpose because a man who puts a head on a column is telling the next man what the column is, and I have been on the wrong end of that. And I am telling you that I am going to go down this road with a figure in my head that eleven thousand and some hundreds of pounds has been left where it is, and nobody in this room has told me that, and a man in a good coat in the hills has told me the other, and the other is the one I came up here to hear.

"The second is worse and I am going to say it because I buy paper and I am the only man in this room who does.

"Eleven sets have been on that nail since the sixth of the third month. Nine of them came back unopened. And I have bought a copy of a schedule from a man in a good coat, and I have a factor's habit, and my habit is that a thing nine people will not hold is a thing somebody does not want held."

"There is no villain in this room," said Zofia Teran from the sack.

"I know there is not," said Orren Vaas. "I have been in four of these rooms this year and I have not met one either. I am telling you that I do not care, because a man in a good coat paid me for a schedule and I did not know what it was and I would like to know now, and I am not going to leave here without it, and you cannot make me, and there is nothing in this kingdom that can make me, and that is not a threat, it is a morning in Rainmarket."

"You may have it," said Marek, "and I am going to write your name on the copy and the date and I am going to hand it across, and I am going to tell you that I have not read it and that if you ask me what is in it I am going to say that it is a district's figure and a district is one line, and that is true."

Vaas took it and put it inside his coat.

"Then that is a trade," he said, "and I will tell you what a trade is at about the rate it is, and I will not be thanked and you will not be thanked, and I have a house in Saltglass that has asked me twice this year for a security on the coming season and has not been told no, and I did not know that is what I had in a drawer. I know now. It does not change what I am going to do with it."

He put the slate with the eleven lines on it in his coat as well and went down the stairs.

---

The count went on until the seventh bell and Petrik came off the end of the bench with the slate and said the number to the room and did not write it, and Hana Belic wrote it on the bench book at the far end with the string across the top of the page and nothing at the head of it, the way it has been done every day since the seed month.

Orren Vaas went down the eleven switchbacks with a figure in his head that no room had given him and a piece of paper in his coat that four households had given up without being asked whether they wanted to, and neither of those two things was ever put in front of another human being for as long as anybody in this basin kept count.
