# Chapter 145 — Five and Two

Sabin Rell came down the river on the ninth of the second month of MR 415 with a satchel and a good coat and no lamp, and he came into the Elmsworth seed house at about the second bell in the afternoon, which is a thing nobody from a Crown March office has ever done, and the lamp had been burning since the third of the third month and was burning now.

He did not come because the harvest was good. He came on the depot's business, and the depot's business was a sheet of paper folded in four, and the fold had gone soft along the middle crease in a way that a fold does when a thing has been opened and shut about nine times in a fortnight by a man who lives alone.

The count was going. It goes from the first bell and the sixty-one trays stand along the long wall under oiled paper in two ranks, and Petrik was at the far end with the slate and the string, and Hana Belic was at the eleventh tray with her back to the door.

Rell put the satchel down on the bench at the end where the count does not go and said:

"I am going to read a sheet out in this room in about four minutes and I am not going to stop the count, and if I stop the count I would like a person to say so."

"Do not stop the count," said Mirren Fisk.

"I have been put under regional oversight," said Rell. "I do not know what that means and neither does the man who wrote it, and it means that a man in Verrow reads my returns now, and I have been told that he is a careful reader, and I have not seen him, and I am not going to ask to see him."

---

He read it out standing up, in the ordinary voice, at about the speed a man reads a thing he has read.

That a Crown March granary official of the region, being an officer of the granary board at the depot of Verrow, on the twenty-fourth day of the tenth month of MR 413 did certify the underwriting clause in respect of four upland holdings upon a projection of their yield for the season then following; that the projection was entered in advance of the season and was a figure carried forward out of a mean of ten years, and the mean is kept in a table in a room the certifying officer has not been in; that the officer could not produce the mean and could not swear to it; that the season then following did not agree with the figure, and the counterweight named in the instrument of the second month of MR 412 fell in the district in which those holdings lie; and that the certifying officer is censured accordingly and is not removed from his post, and is to be under regional oversight, and is not thereafter to certify an underwriting clause upon a projection.

Rell read all of it, including the last of it, and he read the last of it slower than the rest and did not look up.

Nobody in the room said anything for about four seconds and the count went on.

"That is the whole of it," he said. "Five sat. Two dissented. I was one of the five and I voted for it and I have not told anybody in this room how the other two went and I am not going to, and the reason I am not is a reason about papers and not about shame, and I am going to give it to you in a minute because you are going to ask me for it in about a minute and I would rather have it out of the way."

---

"A censure is a finding."

"It is a censure and it is entered and it is public, and there is a copy of it in Bell Stair on a wire and a copy of it in a registry in the hills, and I am not disgraced and I have not been tried and nobody has proved anything about me, because there is nothing to prove: I signed a correct form, correctly filled in, by the standing authority my office has given me for twenty-nine years, and the form was the correct form, and the clause was the fourth clause of a matter of public print."

"And you could not swear to the mean," said Marek.

"I could not produce the mean and I could not swear to it, and I told a man in a stair in the tenth month that I could not, before I signed anything, and I said it on a stair to a man with no office and nobody wrote it down, and the censure is right and it is right for a reason I put in my own mouth in a seed house in the growth month, and I would like that noticed in this room and I am not going to be thanked for it."

---

The count came round to the near end of the bench and Petrik said a number and did not write it, and Hana Belic wrote it on the bench book with the string across the top of the page.

Marek said: "How did the two vote."

"I have just told you I am not going to say."

"I am not asking you what they said. I am asking you for the number."

"The number is five and two," said Rell, "and it is on the sheet in my hand and in a registry in the hills, and you have got it.

"And I am not going to describe the room, and I have thought about it for about nine days and I want to tell you why, because you have a piece of paper in your coat and a habit, and I have watched the habit work in nine rooms in twenty-nine years.

"A description of that room is a thing that can be produced. Not by me — by whoever is holding the papers. And a count of how five people voted on a thing is not a fact about a censure. It is a fact about five men, and one day somebody in a room is going to want to know which two, and a man who has written the count is a man who will be asked which two, and he will not know, and he will have made himself into a man who knows a number he cannot stand behind. That is the whole of the trade and it is the only thing I know how to teach anybody in this office, and I have taught it to about nine men and I do not know that one of them remembered it."

---

"Then write it down," said Rell. "You may write *a censure was recorded, on a vote of five, with two dissenting.* That sentence is true and it is the only sentence about that room that will ever be true in a form anybody can use, and it is worth nothing.

"And you are not going to write it, and I want to say the true reason and not the good one, and the true reason is that you have worked out — you have worked it out in about nine seconds, I watched you do it — that if there is one line in this basin with your hand under it that says five and two, then a man in about nine years can put that line next to a clause in a ledger and ask which two sat out. And you cannot answer him, and you know you cannot answer him, and so the line is a door, and you have spent a year refusing to open doors that do not shut behind you."

Marek had the slate out and put it back in his coat.

"I have not written it down," he said.

"No. And I have not written down the part I know either, which is that I asked for the last of the three clauses and I was told no, and I asked for it twice, and the second time I was told that a man who has been right about the arithmetic for four years is not to be asked for anything twice. That is in a file and it is not in the censure and I am not going to say it in a seed house, and I would like it noticed that I decided that in about four seconds and not in nine days, because it is about a file and a file is the same everywhere."

---

Somebody at the far end of the bench asked him, without stopping work, whether the basin was going to be short.

"This basin is going to feed three regions for about two more years and then it is going to be short, and both of those are true, and the reason is a season in a valley four miles above a stone bridge and a seed house in a town where a man in a good coat has been writing figures in a room since the flood year," said Rell. "I said the first half of that to nine people in a seed house in the growth month and I said the second half out loud and then I let the room have the first half, and I have not said the second half since and I am not going to now, and I would like it noticed that I have said the same two halves out loud in a better order for twenty-nine years and that nobody has ever once put the two halves next to each other and asked me to."

"You are under oversight," said Mirren Fisk, "and you have come down a river on your own account to read a paper in my seed house."

"I have come down a river on the depot's account to read a paper in your seed house, and it is your seed house, and I asked at the tank a night and a day up the river and a man in an office said the seed house was shut to visitors, and I said the paper was going to be read whether the shop was trading or not, and he said the shop was trading, and here I am."

He put the sheet back in the satchel and did not fold it, which took him a moment longer than the folding had.

"Then what is it you want," said Fisk, "because you have read it out and the count has gone on through it, which is more than most men manage, and I am going to ask you for it plainly and I would like a plain answer."

"I want to know one thing and it is not about me," said Rell. "Did the four terraces sow."

"They sowed on the ninth and the tenth of the second month and I have four of my own people up there and nobody has thanked anybody and I have not sent a letter to your office about it and I am not going to."

"Then that is the whole of what I came down for," said Sabin Rell, "and I am going to go back up the river on the evening boat, and I have not been thanked for reading a paper, and I would like it noticed that a man who is censured is not a man who has been let off, and that the clause they have taken off me is the only thing in that sheet that will cost this basin anything, and it cost me the only thing I know how to do."

Nobody thanked him. Hana Belic asked him, at the far end of the bench and without turning round, whether the count on the tray ought to be the same as it was on the second of the month, and he said that it ought to be, and that he had read a column of figures off that bench for an hour in the growth month and had got two of them wrong, and that the tray was hers and he had not asked for it.

He got onto the barge at the sixth bell and the lamp behind him burned till the seventh, and the bench book at the far end of it had the number on it and the string across the top of the page and nothing at the head of it, and the notice went down the river with him in a satchel that had been opened and shut about nine times in a fortnight, and a district in a valley four miles above a stone bridge had seed in the ground and no letter, and a man with no office stood at a tank in the lane and understood that he was the only person in this basin who had heard the whole of a censure read out and had written none of it.
