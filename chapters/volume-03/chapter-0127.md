# Chapter 127 — She Will Not Say Whose It Is

Mirren Fisk sent for Sera Holt on the eighteenth of the growth month because Hana Belic had a cough in the small of her back that had come back in about nine days, and Sera Holt came to the Elmsworth seed house in the low ground on the nineteenth with her own bag, which is not a thing anybody from the clinic does, because the clinic sees people and a seed house is not a place anybody is ill in.

"I have been a physician in this basin for twenty-six years and I have never once been in this room," she said, at the door, "and I have signed things about sixty-one women for two years without ever having seen the bench, and that is not a thing a physician is entitled to do, and I have come to look at the bench and then I will do whatever else I came for."

Nobody thanked her and she did not expect to be thanked and she was in the seed house for about four hours.

---

Belic's cough was in the muscle and not in the chest and Sera Holt put a hand flat on the small of it and said that it was a muscle and that it was what a body does when it has been asked to do sixty-one trays twice a day since the seed month with a lamp nine inches above a bench, and that she was going to write her out of the evening trays for eleven days and no more, and that the eleventh day was the last one and the certificate would go to the board of the clinic and the board would enter it as a cost, and that a member's ground does not have a box for a cost either.

Then she gave Belic the eleventh tray to draw on the rest days and did not consult the keeper about it, and Fisk let her, and Belic asked whether it was a favour and Holt said it was a certificate and that a certificate is a thing with a form on it and a favour is a thing without one and she was not giving her one.

---

Marek Vale came into the seed house at about the seventh bell with a coat and a lamp he had not lit and about nine words he had carried down eleven switchbacks that morning and up again in the evening, which is a thing he has done four times this month, and Sera Holt was at the far end of the bench with her bag open on the floor, and he thought he had come at the wrong hour and then did not go out, and she said:

"Sit down. Turn the tray over and sit on it. I have been a physician for twenty-six years and I do not need a lamp to look at a man's face in a room with a lamp in it, and you are the only thing in this building that has come in out of the weather."

He sat on an upturned tray.

She did not take his wrist. That is the part he has never been able to explain to anybody, and it is the part that told him she had known what she was looking at before she looked at it.

She asked him what he had done on the day before yesterday.

He told her. The fourth bell, the shed, a second line, a lane, a bar in a cut, an hour with his shoulder against it.

"And the day before that."

He answered. And then he stopped, about four seconds into it, in the way a man stops when he has arrived at a place he has not been before.

"The day before that was the ninth," he said. "I was on the second terrace at the second bell and at the third bell and at the fifth and at the seventh, and I came down eleven switchbacks and I got back to my own room at about the eleventh bell and I put a hand on my own bed to steady myself and I could not for about nine seconds produce the fact of my own coat on a chair."

"The coat was on the chair."

"The coat was on the chair," said Marek. "It was on the back of the chair and it was on the back of the chair the whole time."

Sera Holt wrote nothing down. She put one hand on the edge of the bench, and she did not ask him anything else for about four minutes, and about nine people in a seed house carried on with what they were doing, because a seed house carries on.

---

"You have not had a hole," she said at last.

"No."

"Then that is worse, and I want to explain why I am saying that before you tell me anything else, because you are going to try to tell me something else in about nine seconds and I have known you since you were twenty-one and I know the shape of the four sentences.

"If it were a hole I would know what to do with it. There is a hole in this basin that has a name and a form and a length, and a body that has had a hole is a body that has paid and I can write *paid* against it and a clinic can enter a cost and a district can answer a cost with a rate, and I have never once in twenty-six years found the hole easier and I have found it unbearable." She took her hand off the bench. "A hole is a payment. What you have is not a payment. What you have is a season with a person still in it."

---

"What have you got, then," said Marek.

"You want me to describe it."

"I want to know whether I can read about it in a book."

"No," said Sera Holt, "and that is the answer to your question and I am going to give you the other one for nothing in about a minute and I would like you to sit still for it, because I have not decided to give it and I have been deciding for about four minutes."

He sat still.

"You are not being shown a mark of your own making," she said. "I want that in front of you in the plainest words I have got, because every man I have ever said this to has heard the rest of it as a bill. You have not borrowed. You have not pledged anything. Nobody is going to come and take anything out of you and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody could be given what is on you, and if you had asked me in a room in Bell Stair what to do about it I would have told you the same thing I am telling you now and you would have found it useless.

"And I am not going to tell you whose it is. That is the thing I have been deciding for four minutes and that is the thing I have decided."

---

He sat on an upturned tray in a seed house in the low ground at about the eighth bell of the nineteenth of the growth month, four miles and eleven switchbacks from the ground all of it was about, and understood that he had just been told no by a woman who had looked at him and taken less than four minutes over it, and that she was the only person alive who had ever told him anything about what his own hands were worth.

"What you can see," he said, "is that it is not mine."

"I can see that it is not yours," said Sera Holt. "What it is, and where it came from, and whose hands are in it, and how much of it there is, and whether it comes down, and whether you will live to be sixty: that is five things and I am giving you one of them and I am not giving you the other four, and I want it entered that a physician gave that away on purpose and not because she did not know."

"You have seen it before."

"I have seen something with this shape before. I am not going to tell you how many times. I am not going to tell you whether any of them is standing in a room somewhere, and I am not going to tell you what happened, and if you ever ask me any of those three things again in any year of your life I will answer you the way I am answering you now."

She got a sheet out of her bag. It was a clinic sheet and it was ruled and it had a head at the top of it with a date column and four figures on it, and she wrote on it — one line, a date, and nothing else — and then she put the pen down and, at the left-hand margin, opposite the line, she made a mark.

It was not a letter and it was not a figure. It was a small hooked stroke with a short bar under it, and she made it in about two seconds, and it is not in any of the four books in this kingdom that a physician keeps.

"I have invented that three times tonight and thrown away two of them," she said. "And I am not going to tell you what it means, and you are not going to ask me, and I am not going to write it in the book, because a book would make it a thing with a history and it has no history. It has a body."

---

"Who has it?"

"I am not going to say whose it is," said Sera Holt, "and I have been asked that question once tonight in that form and I have answered it and I am going to answer it the same way if you ask it in nine months or nine years. And I would like you to hear why, and then I would like the subject closed.

"You came up a road today to find out whether a thing on you can be got rid of. And in about four days you will find out whether it can be pointed at somebody, because that is what you did on the ninth and it worked on nine rows of a second-terrace holding, and I have been in this basin for twenty-six years and there is not one procedure in it that can do what you do and there is not one form in it that can do what you do, and the reason there is no form is that whoever writes a form has to have said out loud, in a room, that a season may be given to a person by a man who has not asked that person, and no office in this kingdom is going to say that out loud, and not one of them is a coward."

Marek said, "Somebody in a building in the hills would say it if it were put in front of him correctly."

"Somebody in a building in the hills would say a great many things if they were put in front of him correctly," said Sera Holt, "and one of them is that a class of four hundred and some people is not a person, and he would be right, and it would be the ninth time anybody has said it, and none of the nine would have pointed a hoe at anybody's drill rows."

---

She put the sheet back in her bag and did not give it to him, and he did not ask for it, and that was the last of it, and neither of them ever referred to it again.

At about the ninth bell she said one more thing, and she said it standing up with the bag in her hand.

"You asked me once whether the register in Tallow Ward could put a thing like this down," she said. "And I told you no, and I was right, and I would like it said tonight that I did not tell you no because the register is a yard's business. I told you no because about nine months of a season of a woman's hands are in you and the only place in this basin that could have said so out loud in a form of its own is the fifth line of a form in a shed with a bench in it, and that line is the hours, and the yard has been asked to fill it twice and has refused twice on the yard's own rule, and the yard is right, and the yard is right for a reason that has nothing to do with you.

"And I am not going to be the third voice that fills it. There is a bench in this room with sixty-one trays on it and about nine people in it and a woman of thirty-four who has done the eleventh tray in the dark since the sixth of the ninth month, and if her hours go on a line with a head at the top of it, then in nine months a man in a good coat can produce that board at that woman and say *what did you think these trays were*, and the yard knows it, and the yard said no, and I certified eleven of those women out of their days in the bad autumn and I did not certify the balance, and the balance is on their ground as a rate, and I am not going to unsign that this evening because a man walked in out of the weather."

She said it because the day was on the rota and because she knew the rota, and because the rota has days on it and nothing at the head of it, and she had looked at it from the doorway on her way in and had not said anything about it then either.

At about the seventh bell the next morning the count went onto the bench book with nothing at the head of it, and the count was sixty-one, and the lamp was burning, and the woman of thirty-four was at the eleventh tray and not at the first one, and a man in a wet coat went up eleven switchbacks with a thing in him that a physician had looked at for four minutes and had written on a sheet in a bag and had taken away, and about four hundred yards above a stone bridge nine drill rows on a shoulder above a drain were still standing, and the bench book had nothing at the head of it, and nobody had asked her for any of it, which was the correct arrangement and was also the whole of what had gone wrong.
