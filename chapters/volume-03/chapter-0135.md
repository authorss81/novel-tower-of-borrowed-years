# Chapter 135 — A Dying Man Is Not a Party

The lamp in the seed room at Hartlow has a very small wick in it and the smallness is deliberate and it is not a habit, because a wick that is too large in a lamp that has been burning since the seed month smokes, and a smoky lamp in a seed room is a lamp you cannot read a tray-count by, and a keeper of a roll of six lines has about four hundred tray-counts a year to read.

Janko Vrabec was at the table with the lamp and a piece of chamois, and he was not cleaning the glass and he was not trimming the wick. He was turning the wick up about a quarter of a turn and putting it back about a quarter of a turn, which he does, and which is the whole of what a man does with a lamp that he is not going to leave burning at full and is not going to let go out.

There is no work in that room for a man in a coat and he did it anyway.

Hartlow keeps cones of its own for the sowing in the seed month and there is a tray out of the rank and about nine hundred cones to fill in a morning, and Marek Vale had filled cones for a cooperative in Rainmarket in the seed month of the year before last for four hours a day for about nine days and had got so good at it that a woman of thirty-four had said he could have stood at the bench, and he sat down at this bench with a stranger's stock in front of him and filled cones for about an hour with Ruta Vrabec at the other end saying nothing at all.

He was slow. He was slow because he counted — he counted the seed going in and he counted the fingers that were doing it, and about the fourth cone he caught himself at it, and Ruta Vrabec said, from the far end of the bench, in the voice of a woman who does not waste a morning: *You will be here till noon at that, and the count is not in the cone.*

So he stopped counting and was still slow, and between them they filled about four hundred cones in an hour, and at about the fourth bell he put the lid back on the tray and carried it to the rank and stood it down in the gap he had made, which is the whole of a seed room's idea of where a tray goes. The seed in it was Hartlow's own and it was for the sowing in the seed month of next year, and he had not been told which of the six lines on the roll it had come out of, and he had not asked.

He had one question. He had carried it from Tallow Ward by way of the first terrace and the second terrace and Hartlow, and it was the only question he had, and it was not good, and he said it standing up.

"You will not put your name in the ninth line."

"No," said Janko Vrabec.

That is the whole of the refusal and it took about a second and a half, and Marek stood there with the rest of it in his mouth and the man at the table did not look up and did not fill the silence and did not help him.

Then Janko Vrabec said, in the ordinary voice of a man who has been saying sentences to men in rooms for a year:

"A dying man is not a party to anything."

---

He turned the wick up a quarter of a turn.

"That is nine words and I have wanted to say them since the growth month, and I am not going to say anything else about it and you are not going to get a second speech out of me in this room, and if you have come up that ladder with three paragraphs then you have wasted the walk and I am sorry for the road and not for anything else.

"I am not a party. Not to your paper, not to the ninth line, not to the store, not to the roll, and not to the four people on the third terrace, and there is not one of those five things I can be asked about, because a party is somebody it can be put to, and there is nobody here it can be put to.

"You have come to me because a store room with a lock has a keyholder and a keyholder is a person and a person can be asked, and that is right, and I have been the ninth line of that board for eleven years and I have never once been asked anything about it, and the reason nobody has asked me is not that they are frightened of me. It is that there was never any reason to, until a man with no office wanted a sentence."

---

"You want me to say what happens to the name in the winter," he said, after a while, "and it is the only thing in this conversation that is any of my business, and it is a thing about a list and not about me.

"If my name goes in the ninth line in the second month of next year, then in the eleventh month there has to be a name struck off that line. And there is no box in this kingdom for striking a man's name off a list, and no room, and no office that has ever had to do it, so the line would be emptied in a loft by whoever was in the loft, with a knife or a wet cloth, and badly, because nobody can do a thing properly that they have no standing to do at all."

He got up from the table and went and opened the seed room door and stood in it with his hand on the coat on the hook, and he did not take the key off the hook, and Marek watched him not take it off, and after about four seconds he shut the door and came back and turned the wick up a quarter of a turn.

"And then the ninth line has a dead man's name scratched out of it in a hurry," he said, "and it is not a holder any more, it is a hole with a scrape through it, and the empty line at least was honest. And you cannot tell anybody anything through a hole with a scrape through it, because a dead man cannot be asked whether he will bear it, and cannot say no, and cannot be told afterwards. Three days of a road, and you have built a room with a person in it, and you have put a grave in it."

---

He let that sit for about as long as it took him to turn the wick back the quarter turn.

Marek Vale found that he had a second question, and it was a good one, and it had a shape. It was *is there anybody in this valley who could be asked instead of you*, and it was the correct question, and it was the one he had come eleven switchbacks for.

He did not ask it.

He worked out why in about nine seconds, and the reason was that the question was for him and not for Janko Vrabec, and that a man who has been refused four times in five days does not get to put his own refusal inside somebody else's mouth so that somebody else can be shown to have heard it. And in the growth month a woman of forty-four on a terrace had made him say the true part out loud in her yard before she would answer him, and he had done it, and he was not going to be taught it twice by two different women.

So he sat on the bench and did not ask, and Janko Vrabec looked at him not asking, and neither of them said one word about the not asking, and the lamp went on being a lamp with a very small wick in it.

---

"You may tell them that," he said. "It is true and I would like it told, because I have been sitting on this yard for two months waiting for somebody to need an answer about that line, and I have not been able to say so to anybody without it sounding like a man refusing work.

"That is the refusal and that is all of it and it has taken about four minutes and it is the third time I have said no to you and the three of them are not the same and I would like the third one counted."

---

Then he got up and went and got the roll down off its nail, because it was on a nail by the table and it is always on the nail by the table, and he put it on the table and squared it, and it is a piece of oiled board with six lines cut in it and a line of nails at the top.

"Four of these are women over fifty," he said. "One is a child. The sixth is a season examiner's name and she is not a member and she does not live here and she has never been up this road in her life. That is six and it has been six for eleven years and Ruta is one of the four and I am not going to say which one because you do not need it.

"Two people on this paper are going to bear what your paper bears. I have known that since the ninth month of last year and I have counted it more than once and I have never told a living soul, and I am telling you, and you are not to write it down, and if you write it down I will find out and I will not be angry about it, I will simply not have told you.

"One of the two is on this board and she is fifty-eight and she will say whatever she says when you ask her, and you are not going to ask her, and I have known you for three days and I can tell you that you are not going to ask her.

"The other one is not on this board. He is nineteen, and he is a person, and he is not a line on this roll because the roll is a roll of a cooperative and he is a man and there is no form for him either, which I told a man in a good coat in the tenth month of last year and he wrote nothing down, and I have been glad of that for months and I am still glad of it."

He put his thumbnail on the board and moved it down the six lines, one at a time, the way a keeper does.

"So that is the count," he said, "and the count is the only number in this valley that anybody has ever been able to put on a counterweight, and it does not help, and you may have it.

"I can count and I cannot ask. Those are two different trades and I only have one of them and I have had it for thirty-one years."

---

He put the roll back on the nail and it hung there at the angle it hangs at, and he sat down again, and Marek Vale did not ask him one question about his health and had not asked him one question about his health since the sixth of the ninth month, and knew that he was not going to get an answer if he did, and had decided in a lane above a stone bridge in the growth month that he would not ask, and had told his brother about the decision in four minutes in the open on a bend.

There is a rule in this basin about what a room does with a person who cannot be asked, and the rule was made by a man with no office in a yard in the third month, and it is that the room gets on with what it is doing. It does not lower its voice. It does not go round a chair. It does not appoint anybody.

So the seed room went on. The lamp went on being a lamp with a very small wick in it, and the cones went on being filled at the other end of the bench, and outside there was a bar across a stone door and a hasp and a padlock with a bright keyway, and at about the eighth bell Janko Vrabec got up and went and looked at that bar, the way he has looked at it most mornings for eleven years, to see whether the door had dropped any further.

It had not.
