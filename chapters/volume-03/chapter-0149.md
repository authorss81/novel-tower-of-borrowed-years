# Chapter 149 — The Key in a Dead Man's Coat

The fourth-terrace seed store is a stone building the size of a two-horse cart with a door that has been planed four times, and the bar is oak on two staples, and the hasp is on the staple, and the padlock is a good one with a keyway that is bright on the edge and black in the middle, and on the ninth of the fourth month of MR 415 the door was shut and the bar was on it and the store was empty and dry and had been since the ninth of the second month.

Marek Vale came up the last of the road at about the fourth bell on foot, having left Rainmarket at the first bell, and he had a bag on his shoulder and nothing in it, which is the whole of what he had come up with.

Janko Vrabec had died in the frost at the end of the ninth month of last year.

Nobody in that valley said a sentence about it. Ruta Vrabec told Marek about it in about nine words, standing at the door of the store with her coat on, and then she said the other thing, which was that she was not going to tell him what anybody had said to her husband in the growth month two years ago, because she had not heard it from her husband and he had never said it to her, and that if a man in a good coat wanted it he could come and ask her husband, and her husband was under a stone on the shoulder about two hundred yards off with his own name on it cut in by a man of nineteen who was not the stonecutter and had done it in an afternoon because there was nobody else.

---

"The key was on the hook in his coat," she said, "and it was on the hook for eleven years and it was on the hook when he died, and it was in the pocket of the coat on the ninth of the second month when Tomek took the seed out of that store, and he did it himself, and I was in the seed room and I heard the hasp and I went out and told him to stop and he stopped and he said *the seed has to come out today* and I said *there is nobody to say you may* and he said *there is nobody to say I may not.*

"And that is all of what happened and I have told about nine people that in a year and about nine of them have asked me whether a boy of nineteen may open a store, and I have said the same nine words to about nine people, and I have not given a different answer once, and I am not going to."

"Who has the key now."

"It is in the pocket of the coat and the coat is on the hook in the seed room and the seed room is not locked, because a seed room in this valley is not a thing anybody locks, and it has not been locked since the flood year," said Ruta Vrabec, "and it will not be, and that is how the ninth line came to have a key on a hook and no name beside it, and I would like the two of those put next to each other in somebody's head, because they are the same thing and they have been the same thing for eleven years and nobody has ever said so in a room."

---

They went up the ladder that was not a ladder into the loft, and the works board was on the wall at the gable end with this season's nine lines cut in above last season's, and the eighth line had a name against it, and the ninth line was empty.

Marek Vale stood in front of it and did not take anything out of his coat.

The ninth line had been cut in the second month of MR 414 by a man with a knife in about nine seconds, and the man had cut his own name out of it rather than write it and then have it struck, and the shape of the space where the name went was the right shape, and there was a knife-cut round the edge of it, and there was nothing in it.

A dying man is not a party to anything. He had said that in a seed room on the nineteenth of the harvest month in nine words, and he had not been asked for it and he had not been thanked, and Marek Vale had gone to a lane fork on the same road on the same day and had stood at an unfinished wall and had turned round, and neither of those two facts had anything to do with the ninth line and both of them had been made by a man with no office who had been asked to write a name and had not.

"It is not going to be filled," said Ruta Vrabec from the ladder.

"No."

"Not by me and not by him and not in the fourth month and not in the ninth," she said. "I am fifty-eight and I do the cooking and I have never been asked to be anything and I am not going to start on a morning because there is a nail on a wall in Tallow Ward that a man in a good coat would be pleased with."

---

They came down at about the sixth bell and the road was there in front of them, eleven switchbacks, four miles, and a day, and Marek Vale had nothing in his bag and no reason to be in a hurry with it, and he walked down because it was the only way down.

On the third terrace there is a lane off the fourth turn and a shed at the end of it that is not a store and has never been locked, and in the second week of the third month of MR 415 somebody put eleven sacks of seed into it and drove away and told nobody, and there are four people living on that ground and every one of them knew the seed was there by the second day because it is in a shed on a lane and a lane is not a private place.

And nobody in this basin has ever been able to say that they were told anything. There is a cart and a road and a man who is not an office, and no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be sent to ask a terrace why it has not sent anybody, and that has been the position for two years and it is the position now, and the third terrace had seed in a shed in the winter of MR 415 and will have a crop or will not, and there is no person in this kingdom who can be produced to say that anybody was asked.

---

Ottmar Kuez had stood on the low-ground weighbridge for twenty-two years and he read the rate out to every cart that crossed it, and at about the second bell on the ninth of the fourth month he read one out to a cart coming down, and the number he read was not the number he read in the tenth month of last year.

It was not the market's rate. The market's rate had stopped falling on about the first of the second month of MR 414 and had stood for four days and had stood since. What Kuez read at the second bell on the ninth of the fourth month of MR 415 was a district rate off a roll with a day against it in the bridge book, and the district was the one that begins at the bridge and goes four miles in every direction on a low ground, and the rate was entered in the fourth month, and the book had a column of nine names against it, and the bridge reads what it is given.

A cart came down the low-ground road at about the second bell with a full bed of straw on it and a rope round the driver's wrist and a lame near hind, and the driver was Petar Duss, who had been on the road for thirty-one years and had four children and a house with a new roof on one side, and who had a mark in a calendar in the corner of a paper of his own that he had paid four shillings for in the growth month of MR 413 to a man in two rooms above a chandler's shop.

"Petar," said Kuez.

"Ottmar."

"That is the district's and not the market's and I am not going to hold it against you and I am not going to say the figure twice, and it is in the book with a day against it and nobody has ever come to look at the book."

"I have not come to look at the book," said Petar Duss. "I have come down a road with straw on the bed and I have got a wife and four and the rate came on the roll on the second of this month and there is nobody in that building at the far end of the river who can tell me whose name it is."

"It is your name."

"I know it is my name. I have had nine days with it."

---

Marek Vale came down the last four hundred yards on foot behind the cart and caught up with it at the platform and did not say anything for about half a minute, and then he took a piece of paper out of the left breast of his coat and held it out, and it was folded in four and it was not a form and it had no head on it.

"Petar Duss," he said. "I have had this since the tenth month of last year. It is about what you said to me on a bridge and it is in my own hand and it is not evidence and it is not producible and it is not in the register and nobody has ever seen it."

Duss took it with his right hand because the rope is on the left. He opened it and turned it about ninety degrees, the way a man does who has been on a road for thirty-one years and does not read standing up, and he read it, and it took him about nine seconds.

"That is what I said on the bridge."

"Yes."

"Where did you write it."

"In a lane in Tallow Ward on the day you said it, in a book of my own, and I have carried it since, and you asked me in the first chapter of this story for it twice and I have not given it to you twice, and I have not given it to you until now because I wanted to be able to prove it was true, and it is true, and I can still not prove it, and that is what I have had for a year."

---

Duss folded it once and put it back in the other man's coat, and not in his own.

"That is worth nothing," he said.

"I know."

"You do not know it. I am going to tell you what it is worth and then I am going to get back on the road," said Petar Duss. "I have been waiting about a year for a man to write that down and you have done it and it is worth nothing, and I want that said out loud by you and not by me, because you are the one who is going to keep it.

"I knew the road was opened on purpose. I said so on a bridge in the tenth month and I have said it since to about nine men and no office in this kingdom will ever send anybody to ask me about it, and there is no form on which I can be served with the question, and there is no form on which you can be served with the answer. You have written it down in a book of your own with a date on it and no name on it, and in about nine years a man in a good coat is going to come to that counter in Tallow Ward and put a piece of paper on the trestle and four people in that shed are going to say no to it, and your piece of paper is going to be in a lane and it is not going to be anything at all. And that is correct. And I would like it written that I asked for it and I got it, and I am not going to be one of the nine who is thanked for it, and I am not going to be one of the nine who is sorry for you either, because you have kept it for a year and you have got a district rate on a straw cart and those two things are the same size."

"I put your name on a list in a drawer in the hills about a year ago," said Marek Vale, "in a piece of work of somebody else's, and I found out in the third month of this year that your name was on it, and I did not come and tell you."

"I know you did not."

"You do not know that."

"I know it because I did not hear it, and I have been on this road for thirty-one years and I have never once heard anything from an office a day and a night up the river, and I did not hear it," said Petar Duss, "and I am not going to make a thing of it, and I am not going to thank you and I am not going to say one word to anybody about it, and I would like that minuted, and there is nowhere to minute it, which is also correct."

---

He got back on the cart and took the rope in his left hand and the reins in the other and went on down the road, and the rate was in the bridge book with a day against it, and the column of nine names in that book is the only place in this kingdom where a haulier and a mark and a figure are in a line with each other, and it will be there in nine years, and the man who put it there did his job and is not a villain and does not know.

---

He was in the Gate yard at nightfall with the lamp lit and Petrik along the wall with the chalk, and the days went on the oiled board and there was nothing at the head of the board, and nobody told the boy to put anything there.

Vray was on the trestle with a sack on it and no slate and said good evening, which he does.

Ovid Merrow's shed was open on the low-ground road with a lamp in it and nothing standing at nothing in front of it, and Merrow was in the door with his arms folded, and he said the shed was not better than the Gate shed and had not become better, and that the Gate shed had water on a queue morning and his had a roof, and that a man could have both and would not, and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody had asked him and he was not thanked at the end of it.

And in Rainmarket at the fourth bell the twenty-third tray was turned, because a woman of thirty-four had said so in the growth month and a boy of eleven had been told once, and the lamp burned behind her until the seventh bell, and a girl with a broom was at the far end of sixty-one trays and was not spoken to by anybody, and the bench book had the number on it with the string across the top of the page and nothing at the head of it, and the sack on the trestle at the Gate and the straw on the cart and the key in a dead man's coat were three of the things that were not in any book in this basin that night, and the fourth was on a low-ground road four miles away in a door of a stone building on the fourth terrace with a bar on it that is not there to hide anything, because it is holding next year's seed, and next year's seed is the only thing in that valley anybody in the hills has ever been sure about.
