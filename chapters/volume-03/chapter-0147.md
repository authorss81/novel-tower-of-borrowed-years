# Chapter 147 — A Slip on a Wire

There was a slip of paper on a wire at the posting desk in Bell Stair on the eighteenth of the third month of MR 415, and the wire had been put up in the tenth month of MR 413 for notices about water and had been used since for about nine different things by four different offices, and the slip was four inches by two and had been printed in Bell Stair on the back of a form run that nobody has ever wanted.

The duty clerk at the desk was sixty and had twenty-two years in the copy room and had pinned the slip up himself because nobody sent anybody to do it, and he did not read it, and he said so when a man asked him what it was, which was on the same morning.

"It came off the evening post," he said. "That is what is on the wire. I do not read them. I have never read one and I am not going to start on an old man's morning."

---

It was headed *Extract of a minute of the granary board of Crown March, registry of district instruments*, and it said that the board had sat five and two had dissented, and that the papers of the sitting were in a drawer in that building, and that the finding was carried into the registry in the following terms, and the terms were these.

That a mark which no officer of a district can read is not a witness to the paper it is on, and that a paper whose counterweight, security or assurance is carried by such a mark alone shall not stand alone for any purpose touching the future of a district, and that a copy, translation or certified duplicate of any such paper is of no effect for any such purpose unless the mark is read and certified by an officer of this registry.

Six lines of type. There is no date on it in this basin that anybody can use, and no name on it, and the name of the office that carries the registry is on it in type and the office is four miles and a day up the river.

Marek Vale read it at the posting desk at about the third bell and then read it a second time, and then stood at the desk for about a minute, and then said:

"Duty clerk. I am going to ask you a question and you are going to tell me that you have never read them, and I am asking anyway because I have nowhere else to ask."

"That is the first sensible thing anybody has said at that desk in about four years," said the duty clerk, "and the answer is that I have never read one, and that there are eleven of them on that wire and I do not know what any of the eleven is, and that I would not tell you if I did, because the man who pins a thing up does not get to be the man who explains it."

"That was the correct answer and I want it written down that I was told it before I asked it."

"It is not written down and it is not going to be."

---

The second-floor hearing room was built for thirty and there were nine in it on the evening of the eighteenth, and one of the nine was Bel Harrow, who chaired, and who was not going to vote on what came up, and who had no clerk, and who served the minutes himself at about four minutes a page.

The table of five had four people at it. Harrow in the chair. Sedge for the Chamber, who said on the way in that he had been clerk of the day for nothing and had come. Zorka Vendl for the Fund, who declined money for a sitting, twice, in the first person, and asked for it to be minuted that she was still in the room. Aldis Kern, for the ward, in his own name, under a standing provision he could recite in about nine words and did not recite.

And the Trust's seat, empty, for the sixth time, with *in the ordinary course* still in quotation marks in a minute in a drawer in that building.

Also in the room: Neris Kade, not a member and not entitled to a vote, holding a sheet whose only function is to hold four figures in the order they are called. Ilse Ferrow, who has a lapsed licence and a right to be in a room where Crown March paper is being dealt with, and who used it and is not a member. Marek Vale, who is not a member of anything and is the only person in the basin who can be in a room without being on a list, and who was let in by a door-keeper who did not write him down. And two clerks of that building, who took no part and were not thanked and went out at the fourth bell.

The Chair wrote the question down before anybody answered it, which is what a chair does, and then he read the four terms, which take about eleven minutes.

The four terms have been confirmed at more sittings than anybody has counted. The chair presides until the board is satisfied it can sit without him, reviewed at each sitting and written. The Fund cannot pay a sitting and a ward cannot be assessed a rate at all. Attendance and declining is standard practice and is not going to be discussed. And term four: a member who dissents may have the grounds written in their own words, in the finding, and the duty to write them is the board's and not the member's.

"Term four stands," said Sedge.

"Stands."

"Stands," said Vendl, "and I have been asked three times to take money for this evening and I have declined three times and I am still in the room."

"Stands," said Kern.

---

"This is not ours," said Ilse Ferrow, before anybody had put it in words.

"Then the Chair will put it in words," said Harrow, "and then you will say it again, and the Chair writes it down before it is said, which is the correct order and which nobody in this building likes."

He wrote for about four minutes and read it back.

"Is a rule carried into a registry in the hills a thing that this board can do anything with."

The room thought about it for about nine seconds.

"It does something," said Ilse Ferrow. "That is the first half and nobody is going to get me to give it up. A mark that no officer of a district can read is not a witness, and that was in a drawer in this building since the sixteenth of the rains month last year with a Chair's hand on it, and it is now in type in a registry and it has a number of five and two behind it and it will be produced at every seed house, factor's office and holding in this region by a man in a good coat in about four years, and that is more than the drawer will ever do, and the drawer is going to be found in about nine years by a building that has read its own minutes.

"So it does something. And it is not nothing. And I have sat in this room since the tenth month and I have not once wanted a rule and I am telling you that I want this one, and I would like it written down that I wanted it, because I am about to say the other half and I do not want the other half to be the whole of what is on the page."

---

"It is in type," said Zorka Vendl.

She said it flatly and she said it once and then she let about nine seconds go by, which is not a thing she usually does.

"It is in type, and a minute in a drawer in this building is a thing that a Chair writes out in longhand with a fountain that skips, and every one of us in this room knows the difference between a thing that has to be copied out and a thing that is printed. And the difference is the whole of the danger.

"A finding in a drawer is produced at somebody by a person who has to find it. A rule in a registry is produced at somebody by anybody who can read, and there is no finding in it about whether it is right, and there never will be one, because a registry does not hold findings. It holds entries.

"And in about four years a man will take six lines of type to a woman on a holding and say that the paper she signed in the second month of MR 412 is of no effect because of a mark on it, and she will not be able to answer him, and she will not be able to answer him because there is no box in that sentence for her, and I have been in nineteen years of rooms in this city and I have never once been able to make a man take a thing back that is in type."

"It is not to be taken back," said Sedge. "That is the other half of what the Chair wrote and nobody has said it. It is a rule about a mark. It does not touch a woman's paper. It touches what may be done with the mark on it. A person who signed a thing in MR 412 signed it, and the rule does not say they did not sign it, and if a man in a good coat tells a woman on a holding that she did not sign it, that man is doing a thing this rule does not say, and he will do it in about four years, and there will be nothing in the room to put in front of him."

"Unless somebody puts the six lines in front of him," said Vendl, "and the six lines are about marks, and he will have read them, and they will be in his hand."

---

Kern did not wait to be asked, and he did not use term four, and he said so, and it took him about nine words.

"I am not asking for my grounds to be written this evening and I am not going to say why, and the Chair is not to ask me, and the yard is in a record in this building already and I put it there on purpose."

Which record he meant was in the minute in his own hand, and nobody asked him, and the Chair did not ask him, and the Chair is not going to.

---

Then the Chair asked Marek Vale whether there was anything in the room that was not in the finding, and Marek said there was, and got his own paper out and read nine lines off it, and then said:

"That slip on the wire at the posting desk is four inches by two and it has been on that wire since the eighth of the month. On the twenty-sixth of the ninth month of last year a man bought eleven times over three days off a seed shop off the low-ground road, at a depot's price, out of ground and not out of a pledge, and there was no line against any of the eleven in any book, and the eleven households paid for it in coin and took it up a road in sacks, and a cart carried a quantity of it to a store on the fourth terrace which nobody has been able to ask about and which is the ninth line of a works board made by four terraces for themselves.

"And the sowing went into the ground on the ninth and the tenth of the second month, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which any of that was done, and it was done because a set-off could not be entered, and a set-off could not be entered because a terrace is not a line, and a class is not a person, and a district is not a holding, and a man in a good coat has said all of those to me twice and been right twice.

"So. Six lines of type in a registry in the hills now say that a paper that carries a counterweight on a mark no officer of this district can read shall not stand alone for any purpose touching the future of a district. And the paper in the file of those four terraces does carry its counterweight on a mark that no officer in this kingdom can read.

"Which means that the advance cannot be recovered. Which means that what I have just described is not a decision anybody took in the ninth month. It is a consequence of a rule that was printed three months afterwards by five men in a room four miles and a day up the river, none of whom has heard of a yard in Tallow Ward, or of a register, or of a seed shop off the low-ground road, or of a man with a barrow and a bad thumb on a beam."

---

The room was quiet for about four seconds.

"Now the part I have not said," said Marek, "and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be able to say it again, and I have had a year to work out the shape of it and here it is.

"If anybody in nine years puts those two things side by side — six lines of type in a registry, and eleven households buying seed in the ninth month — it will look like a plan. It will look like a man who knew a thing was coming and got in first. And there was no plan. The seed was bought because a woman of thirty-four would not let a cooperative hand its own seed to four hundred acres, and a man of fifty-six with a bad thumb on a beam was asked one question in his own shop and gave one answer, and a woman of thirty-eight said no to a thing nobody had offered her, and a man of nineteen did not know what he was buying.

"And I am the only person alive who can say that, and I cannot prove one word of it, and I have not got a piece of paper that says any of it, and I could not produce it in this room if I had one, because the room it happened in had no office and wrote no minutes and does not exist, and a thing that happened in a room that does not exist can only be established by about nine people saying so, and four of them will not, because two of them are right not to and one of them is dead and one of them is a man who is not going to be asked because he is not a clerk and is not a member and is not in this basin.

"So in about nine years somebody is going to say a man in a good coat arranged it, and there is no office in this kingdom in which anybody may be produced to say he did not."

---

The Chair wrote for about eleven minutes, which is longer than four minutes a page, and then he put the pen down.

"I am going to write it," he said, "and I am going to write it in the finding, and I am going to write it in my own hand, and then this sheet goes down to the box with the other four, and the box has a lock on it and there is no date on the box and nobody has ever been sent to it.

"And the sentence is worth nothing, and I am putting it in the drawer anyway, and I have been a chair long enough to know what the alternative is. The alternative is that a true thing was said out loud in a room and not written down, and a true thing said out loud and not written down is a thing that gets said again louder by somebody who heard it and did not write it, and I would rather be one of five in a drawer than be the man who was not in the drawer."

Neris Kade had the four figures on her sheet in the order they had been called out and she said nothing and was not thanked for the sheet and had not expected to be, and Sedge said nothing after the Chair had written, and Vendl asked for it to be minuted that she was still in the room and it was minuted.

The room got up at about the seventh bell. The Trust's chair stayed as a chair and stayed empty. Bel Harrow wrote the date at the foot of the last page in his own hand, because there is no clerk and there has never been a clerk, and the reason there is no clerk is in the fourth term and nobody has ever asked about it, and the fifth minute went into a locked box in a drawer in a building, in a long clerk's hand, on the back of a notice about a water office's list of fourteen lines, and it says in the fifth paragraph that no mark in the finding is a witness, and it says in the seventh that the finding of the board is that a room with no office cannot be declared the wrong room, and nobody in that building will ever read either sentence to anybody in Morrowfen.
