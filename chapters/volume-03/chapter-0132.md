# Chapter 132 — Nine People and a Jar

The jar came back out on to the rail on the morning of the fourteenth of the harvest month, about nine days after Mileva Vukic had carried it inside and had not mentioned it again, and it came out because the first terrace was threshing and the rail is where a thing gets put when a floor is going and a house has not got room.

She is thirty-eight, and she has a spring in the field behind her house that has come up in that field every year of her life, a pear tree her father planted that is forty years old, a boundary stone at the top of her upper lot, and a heifer that has not come back in heat. She is not a member of anything. There is no cooperative on the first terrace, there is no roll with her name on it, and she has never been asked to sign a line in her life and has not wanted to.

The floor is not the shared floor. The shared floor is line three on a board in a loft on the second terrace and belongs to nobody. The first terrace has nine people on it and they thresh where the barley is stacked, on a beaten floor of stone and ground chalk at the top of the upper lot, and the whole of it took about four days.

Marek Vale came up the last of the road at about the fourth bell on the fourteenth, on foot, having left Tallow Ward at the first bell on the thirteenth, and he came into the yard where the floor was and nine people stopped what they were doing for two seconds and then went back to it.

Nobody stopped for longer than two seconds. That was the first thing he understood about the first terrace and he understood it standing in a gateway with his bag on his shoulder: they have been threshing since the sixth and there are four days of it and a stranger with a bag is not a thing a floor stops for.

---

She was on the rail side of the floor with her sleeves up and she had been the one who told the nine of them to go back to it, and she came out of the yard to him and said:

"You are the one who was in a ditch."

"Yes."

"Zofia Teran came down here in the growth month and said four things and I asked her for one of them and she wrote it in a book with nothing at the head of it and I have not seen her since." She looked at his boots. "Have you eaten."

"No."

"Then that is the first thing," said Mileva Vukic, "and I am not going to say the other thing until you have eaten, and you can stand there and be cross about it."

He ate. It was bread and cheese and a heel of something at the wall in the shade, sitting on a stone, and it took about nine minutes, and nine people threshed barley about four feet from him the whole time and one of them asked him twice whether he was the man who reads in the shed, and he said that he did on the days a board said so, and the man said that he had heard there was a boy who read it, and Marek said that there was, and the man said that the boy read it twice, and Marek said that he did, and that was the end of it.

---

Then she came back and stood in front of him with her arms wet to the elbow and said:

"Now say it."

"I am going to say it and it is going to be about four minutes and I am not going to do it well."

"I do not know what well is," said Mileva Vukic. "I have not had one before. Go on."

Marek Vale stood in a gateway on the first terrace with the smell of a threshing floor in his clothes and said the true part out loud, in about four minutes, to a yard, with a threshing floor going four feet away and about nine people on it, and it came out flat and in the wrong order and without one single sentence in it that he had written down.

"There is a paper. It was sealed in a locked box in a depot in the hills two years ago and it is not mine and I cannot take it back. It bought this basin a season. Nine words on it say the cost of that season is paid by the seed-keepers of the four terraces after this season. The nine words are printed. The number in front of them was written by a man in a room. There is no name in it anywhere on the paper, and there has never been one, and nobody was asked.

"I can read it. I can put my hand on the season it bought, and I can point it at one name, and in the growth month I pointed it at a household on the second terrace at the third bell before light and I did not ask that household, and about nine people up there have told that story eleven times this month and every version of it is wrong.

"So I have come four miles to say it to you before I do anything else, and that is the whole of why I am standing in your gateway. And I cannot tell you it is going to land on you, because nobody can, and I am not going to guess in front of nine people.

"And I am not going to say it in a room, because there is no room, and there is no office in this kingdom that can put nine people in a room, and I have spent four days in a yard in Tallow Ward finding that out from a man with a key on a nail, and he is right, and I have come up here with his sentence and not with my own."

He stopped, because there was nothing after that.

Mileva Vukic had not moved and had not looked at the floor.

"Say the bit about the spring," she said.

"No."

"Say it."

"No," said Marek, "and that is the first thing I have got right in four days and I want you to know it was deliberate."

---

She went and got the jar off the rail and looked at it and put it back on the rail, and that took her about four seconds, and then she came back and stood in front of him and said the thing that was better than anything he had carried up the road.

"You came up here to be told yes."

"Yes."

"And I am not going to say yes," said Mileva Vukic, "and I want you to hear that in the plainest words I have got, because you have come four miles and a night for it and you are not going to leave with it, and if I let you leave thinking I was going to say yes in a week then I will have done the same thing to you that somebody has done to about nine people in a form somewhere and I have watched it done and I am not going to do it.

"What I am going to do is leave the jar on the rail. That is the whole of what nine people on this floor have done since the twelfth of the growth month and I am not going to do one thing more than that, and it is not acceptance, and it is not a favour to you, and if you go back down that road and tell somebody on the second terrace that the first terrace accepted a counterweight then I will have said yes, and I have not, and I will not be able to say that I did not.

"And then there is the part you did not come for, and you can go after it.

"The paper does not land on me. My spring is brackish and there is a jar on this rail and a pear tree has come out twice and a heifer has not come back, and if you go down that road and say *the paper did that*, then in nine years a man in a good coat will come to this floor with a figure on a piece of paper, and I will have said it first, and I will not have earned it, and my girl will have heard me say it, and she is thirteen and she will still be living here when it is not true any more.

"So you are not to say it. Not on the second terrace, not to the examiner, not in a room in Bell Stair, not to a woman with a book. You are to say the nine words and you are to say who printed them and you are to say that nobody was asked, and then you are to go away and not come back up this road for a year, and if you cannot do that then you should not have said the nine words at all, because the nine words on their own are worth something and the nine words plus a cause are worth nothing at all."

---

He stood where he had been standing when she started and found that he had nothing, and that the nothing was not a relief.

"Somebody in this basin is going to ask you in the fourth month why your spring went," he said.

"Then they will ask me," said Mileva Vukic, "and I will say a spring came up brackish on the twelfth of the growth month, and I will say it to a woman she has known nineteen years, because there is nobody else to say it to, and I have had that ready since the seventeenth and I have not needed you and I am not going to pretend I did."

"What will you tell them," said Marek, "when they ask you what it was?"

"That I do not know," said Mileva Vukic. "In those words. To a man with a figure. And it will not satisfy anybody and it is the only thing I have got that is worth anything, and I would like you to go down that road able to say the same, and I do not know whether you can, and you have not tried it yet."

---

"You have not looked at it," she said then. "You have come up a road about a paper and you have not once looked at the thing the paper is about, and I would like you to look at it before you go, because you are going to be a man who says things about this floor for the next nine years and it costs you about a minute."

The spring comes up in the field behind her house at about the second bell and has come up there every year of her life. It comes through about two feet of gravel and there is a stone lip round it that her father set and her mother chipped and that is the only dressed stone on the first terrace.

Marek put his hand in it, and it was cold, and it was not water in the ordinary way of it. He put it in a second time, and then he put it to his mouth, and it was not the water of a drain and it was not the water of a river and it was a mouthful of something that had got into a spring in a month in which nothing in that household had changed.

"Well," said Mileva Vukic. "Now you have looked at it."

"Yes."

"And what are you going to write down."

"Nothing," said Marek. "There is no form for a spring, and there is no form anywhere for next year's ground, and if I put it on a sheet of my own with nothing at the head of it then in nine months a man in a good coat will read it out in a room and ask me which of the four things I said was the cause. And I will have handed him four things to choose between, and he will choose the one that costs the least to act on, and it will be one of the four, and I will not be in the room when he chooses."

"You have thought about that before."

"I have thought about nothing else since the growth month," said Marek, "and it has not once helped, and I would like it said out loud in a yard somewhere, and I have not got a yard."

She looked at him for about four seconds, which was longer than she had looked at him all afternoon.

"Then that is the first thing you have said today that I did not have to work at getting out of you," said Mileva Vukic, "and I am going to leave you alone with it. Go and stand at the rail. I have to be in the floor in a minute and you can watch a floor work, and I do not think you have ever watched one, and I can tell."

He stood at the rail for about an hour and a half and watched nine people thresh barley and did not say anything at all, and that was not a thing he had done since the seed month, and about the ninth minute of it a man of about fifty came past with a sheaf on his shoulder and said, without stopping, *you can take that end if you want it*, and Marek took that end, and they carried about nine pairs of sheaves to the headland and stacked them, and nobody asked him a single question the whole time, and that was the last thing that happened to him on the first terrace and it is the only part of it he has ever been able to repeat to himself without it sounding like a story.

---

"And how long does it last," she said then. "Because I would like to know how long I have to be the woman with the jar."

That was the question he had not expected and it was the only one she asked all afternoon, and it was the practical question of a woman with a rail and a floor and four days of barley in front of her.

"About eleven weeks," said Marek. "I have not got anything better. It is not a term and it is not a date and it is not on anything, and the woman who has been in a room about it in this city says that is about how long anything of this shape survives being told once."

"Then I will know by the season when it has stopped," said Mileva Vukic, "and I will not have to ask you, and you will not have to come up here and find out."

She turned round and went back to the floor, and the nine of them went on threshing, and the jar stood on the rail with the sun on one side of it and the shade on the other, and it stayed on the rail for the rest of that week and for the rest of the harvest month and it was still on the rail in the ninth month when the low ground's second sowing did not come, and nobody on the first terrace ever thanked Marek Vale for anything and nobody on the first terrace ever said the word *counterweight* again in his hearing, and a woman with a school exercise book two terraces away was told by nobody about any of it.

He went down the road at about the sixth bell with his bag on his shoulder and the second terrace in front of him, and he still had four words left to say about it, and his hand was in his pocket and the thing he had gone up there to do with it was in a county in a locked box and eleven switchbacks away, and he did not take his hand out of his pocket once on the way down, and at the bottom of the road he stopped at the stone bridge and put it flat on the parapet for a moment the way a man does who has nothing to put it on and wants to feel that it is still there.
