# Chapter 128 — Three Days at the Third Terrace

The water on the low ground stood in the second month and did not go down through the growth month, because a sowing that is thin takes its water in a thin way and a field that has come up wrong does not draw a channel dry the way a field that has come up right does, and there was a channel that had been cut in the flood year along the foot of the third terrace and it had held a dry bed every summer since.

On the second of the harvest month it was four feet deep at the bend and rising about a hand an hour, and at that rate it would have crossed the bend at about the fifth bell that evening and been four feet up the slope by the second bell on the third, and the third terrace's cutting is line one, and there are about a fortnight of it left in the ground.

---

Line one is *cutting*, and it is on a board in a loft at Hartlow, and a line on that board is a job and not a duty and it has never had a name against it, and the argument about the third terrace was had in a loft in the second month and the answer was the same as it is every year: **the third terrace's cutting gets done by whoever turns up.** The third terrace is four lines of ground above the water and its cutting is done in the same week as the cutting on the first, and the third terrace has sent nobody to two of these and a sheet up to the hills for nineteen years and then none, neither this year nor the year before.

Nobody had been sent. There is no form in this kingdom in which anybody may be sent to ask a terrace why it has not sent anybody.

So on the morning of the second there were nine men on the third terrace with scythes and two bars and a rope, and they had come up eleven switchbacks on their own time with their own food, and about four hundred yards down the slope there was four feet of rising water in a channel about nine feet wide with a bank on both sides of it, and a scythe cannot be got to the third terrace's cutting through a channel, and there is no way round it that a man with a scythe can use.

---

Janko Vrabec came up at about the fourth bell with a bar and did not say why he had come and was not asked, and he is fifty-eight and he has a cough in the winter and he came up eleven switchbacks in a growth month for no reason anybody could put on a works.

The first-terrace man of about sixty came up with him. He has cut the first terrace's line every year since the flood year and he has a knife in a sheath and he is one of the people who read the old season out of a board in a loft because he cannot read.

Tomas Vale came up at about the third bell with a coil of rope and eleven stakes in his hand, because a bridgewright who cannot get a bar to a field will build a thing across a channel that will hold a bar's end, and he did not say so and he did it, and it took him until the sixth bell and it is the only piece of craft in this chapter.

And four people from the third terrace were on the ground.

They were there because the ground is theirs and it is the second of the harvest month and there is about a fortnight of cutting left in it. Marek Vale did not ask any of them anything, at any hour, on any of the three days, and the reason is not a rule and not a form and is written here because it is the only thing he did that week that was correct and he did it on purpose: he could have asked, and there was nobody to stop him, and the reason he did not is that a man with a season in him does not get to interview the people it came out of.

---

He got to the bend at about the seventh bell and stood on the bank on the upper side and looked at the water for about four minutes.

It is a boundary, that is the thing he could not get past for two days afterwards. Not a wall and not a line and not a fence. A channel has two banks and the water crosses from one to the other every year and the crossing is the boundary and everything about a channel is a fact about its boundary — where it is, how deep it is, how fast, and when it will next be on the other side.

The water was going to cross that bend at about the second bell on the third and be four feet up the slope by the sixth, and the cutting above the slope was nine days old and about two days off being uncuttable, and there was a fortnight of it and nine men and a rope and a bar on a bridge that was nine hours old.

He put his left hand flat on the bank.

He did not close his eyes and he did not hold his breath and he did not say anything, and about nine men on a slope behind him and four people on a terrace above them went on with what they were doing, because there is nothing to see.

---

The water stopped.

That is the plain of it and there is no other way to put it that is not a lie. The surface of it went still from his hand to about nine feet out and then it was still all the way to the far bank, and the four feet at the bend stayed four feet, and the hand an hour that it had been rising at became nothing at all.

Marek Vale stood on a bank with his hand on it for a long time and did not move it, and he watched a rising thing stop rising, and the only thing he felt about it, then and afterwards, was how *boring* it was. There is no moment in it. There is no heat and no light and no sound in a man who is holding a crossed boundary in place, and the whole of what he got to look at for about nine hours of the first night was nine men with scythes on a slope and a rope and a bar and a channel with a thing in it that was not moving.

He did not let go for two and a half days. That is the entire shape of it and there is very little else to report: a hand on a bank from about the sixth bell on the second of the harvest month until about the fourth on the fifth, in the cold, with the light going and coming, by a man who does not sleep, because a man who sleeps is a hand that is not there. Somebody brought him up bread and a pot of something on the second night and left it at his feet. Tomas Vale brought him a coat at about the second bell on the fourth and Marek did not put it on and Tomas did not ask him to, and neither of them said one word about it, and that was the whole of the conversation the two of them had in three days.

He chose the length. That is the entire power and the whole of its shame: **he chose how long, and he did not choose where it went when it stopped.** The water was not gone. It was held, the way a door is held, and it was still four feet deep at the bend and it was still on the low side of a boundary that had been crossed every summer for nineteen years, and on the sixth it was going to be on the other side of it three days and some hours later than it would have been, and the ground above the slope was going to take it, because a channel takes water back when the water is let go and it does not consult anybody about where.

Nobody in this valley was going to be charged for the three days. Not one of them. Not a household, not a holding, not a terrace, not a district, and the reason is not an arrangement and not a kindness: the cost of a hold can never reach a person who did not borrow, guarantee, witness, or certify, and not one of the nine men with scythes did, and not one of the four people on the third terrace did, and not a single acre of that ground has ever borrowed anything off anybody.

He was the only party. He had made himself the only party on the ninth of the growth month by putting a season of somebody else's hands into a woman's field, and he had not known that he had done it, and now there was a second entry under it.

---

They cut for three days.

They cut the third terrace's line from the sixth bell on the second to about the fourth on the fifth, and it is the best-cut ground any of them had ever cut and they said so about nine times without meaning anything by it, and they could not afterwards agree why. The scythes went and the barley stood in a shock and the ground under it went in properly for the first time in about four years, and a first-terrace man of about sixty said that he could not understand what had come into it and that he would not try, and that there was no point in a man of his age starting on things he could not explain.

Tomas Vale pulled the stakes out of the bank on the fifth and coiled the rope and did not say one word about the three days to anybody, and Marek Vale understood about halfway through the third night that he was going to have to tell his brother, and that there was no form on which to do it, and that the only place left to say a thing like that in this basin is a lane with nobody in it, and that his brother would be on a road in about nine hours with a case in his hand.

Janko Vrabec sat on a wall on the third terrace on the second night with a lamp with a very small wick in it, and did not ask about the water, and did not say anything at all about it, and at about the fourth bell he said one thing which was:

"You have got the key to my store in your hand and you have been carrying it about this valley for three days and you have not asked me for it, and I would like it noticed that you have not asked me for it, and I am not going to give it to you, and I would like it noticed twice."

"I have not got it."

"You have got the key to it in your hand," said Janko Vrabec, "which is the same as having it, and I have been in this valley for fifty-eight years and there are four people on this ground and I know what is not coming up it and I know what is, and I have not asked you about the water and you have not asked me about the key, and there is not a form in this kingdom in either of those, and that is the ninth line of a works and it is empty and it is going to stay empty until the winter, and after the winter I am not going to be here to fill it, and I would like it noticed that I have said that out loud to a man on a wall at the fourth bell and he has not written it down."

---

At about the fifth bell on the fifth, with the last of the barley standing and the stakes out of the bank and about nine men going down eleven switchbacks with their scythes over their shoulders, Marek Vale took his hand off the bank, and stood up, and could not for about four seconds produce his brother's face.

It came. It came the way it had come on the tenth of the growth month, four seconds late and intact, and it was Tomas Vale's face in the light with a rope over his shoulder and no expression on it at all, and Marek had seen it in about nine doors and a kitchen and a loft and a wall, and it was there, and he had had to go and look for it.

The water came back to the bend at the rate it had been coming, because the volume of it had not changed and only the crossing had, and it was four feet up the slope by about the ninth bell on the sixth, which is three days and four hours later than it would have been and is the same four feet. The ground took it. That ground had a fortnight of barley standing in a shock on it and a crop on it that four people own and no name on a board anywhere in four terraces, and a cutting that was got in by nine men who do not live there in three days, and none of that can be entered in a form in this kingdom, and neither can the three days.

At Hartlow the works board still has nine lines cut in it and a name against the eighth and nothing at all against the ninth, and the ninth is the fourth-terrace seed store, and it is the biggest and the only one with a key, and the key is in a coat in a house four miles away, and nobody can be sent to ask, and the man who cut his own name out of that line in the second month is going to be dead in the winter.
