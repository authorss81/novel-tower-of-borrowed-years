# Chapter 72 — Nothing Is Charged

The Share had eleven households with sand in eleven trays and a season with a frost on it, and the rule it keeps its own seed by is that a member's lot failing is settled out of the next sowing, and the next sowing's kept-back has to be in the ground by the end of the growth month or the eleven households get nothing at all this year and are paid for in the next one.

There was one lot of kept-back left on this side of the ridge. It was in a village called Hartlow, four terraces up in the Red Uplands, four miles from a stone bridge, and it belonged to a seed house that had been selling saved stock out of its own drawers for sixty years.

The lot was forty-one sacks and it had to be examined before it came down, and the rule that governs that is not the Trust's and not the Share's and is older than both.

*A season examiner may not carry a lot she has examined.*

Neris Kade read it out in the seed house at the second bell of the second day of the growth month, off the inside of a board where somebody had written it out in a bad hand, and then she put the board down and waited, because everybody in the room could do the arithmetic and she was letting them do it in front of her.

"The examiner is Rainmarket's examiner and she is upland season works," said Mirren Fisk. "The lot is in her own district. The lot is coming to a party in another district. So she may not examine it and she may not carry it, and neither may I because the Share is a party, and nobody at the Trust window may carry it because the Trust is the certifier, and every one of the eleven men on Pike's slate is being paid to be somewhere else at the end of the week."

"So who," said Marek.

"Forty-one sacks and a cart and a road," said Neris Kade, "to a man who is not the Share, and not the district, and not the Trust, and not an examiner, and who has no office and therefore cannot be sent for in the ninth month by anybody." She had her arms folded and she was looking at him and she was not enjoying it. "You have been telling me for a fortnight that usefulness is not standing. It is not. It is the only qualification there is. That is what I have been arguing with you about since the third of the month and I have never once had to use it on you before and I am not going to pretend this is anything else."

"Send him," said Mirren Fisk.

"Forty-one sacks down the upland road in a hired cart with a man in it and no pay," said the keeper of the Hartlow house from the door, "and he is on the cart and the cart is his liability, and the road is frozen and will be worse at the top, and I will not send a lot down to a man with no office on a cart in his own care. It is too much to ask of a stranger."

"He is not a stranger," said Neris Kade. "He has been let into my work five times since the ninth and he is on the second-eye line of an instrument with sixty-one names on it and he is a man who has been carrying a flat board under his arm up a causeway for a fortnight because there is nowhere in this city to put it down. He will take it. Ask him."

He was asked. He said yes, and he said it before she had finished being uncomfortable about having said it, and that was the third Tuesday of the month and none of the three had been malicious and none of the three had been arranged.

---

The upland road went up out of the basin in eleven switchbacks and the frost had taken the middle out of it in three places, and they went up it at the speed of a cart, and Marek walked in front of the off horse for two hours and a half and had more to think about at the fifth hour than he had had in the building in six weeks.

The lot was in sacks against the north wall of a stone building with a slate roof and a smell in it that he recognised from the seed house at home and did not have a word for, and it was stacked eleven high and it was the oldest seed in the basin, and a man of fifty-eight came out of the back of the building to shake his hand.

"Janko Vrabec," he said. "I keep it. There is nobody else. Come in, do not stand in that with your mouth open, the frost is going straight through you."

He was the last of them and he knew it, and he said so in the first minute without being asked, and then he said the rest of it while he made tea, in the flat voice of a man laying out the position.

"There were six of us in this house in my father's day and there are me and a boy of nineteen and the boy's mother, and she is not a seed keeper and she does not want to be and she is fifty-one. The boy is called Tomek and he is good at it. He is better at it than I was at his age and I have told him so twice this year, once too often, because there is nothing after him. If I die this winter there is one keeper in this village in ten years and it is a boy, and if he goes, there is none, and the seed houses on these four terraces go over to bought stock in a generation, which is a slow way for a place to die and is exactly as slow as a year a drought."

"Who certifies you now," said Neris Kade.

"A woman from Rainmarket, four years back, and before her her mother, and the seed has come down this road without a break in it since my grandfather's time. That is not a boast. It is a thing that people in a valley are proud of and that nobody in a hall can put on a page."

---

There was a room off the seed room with a fire in it and a bench down one side and a table down the other, and a door with a notice nailed to the outside of it, and Marek read the notice twice standing in the snow, and then went and got Neris Kade and made her read it as well.

*ANYBODY MAY COME IN AND SIT AND LOOK. NOTHING IS CHARGED. NOTHING IS SIGNED.*

"Fourteen months ago," said Janko Vrabec. "That is the whole of it and there is nothing on the table but paper and a fire.

"We had a bad thing in this village in the year before and the instrument was in a chest and the chest was in a room and the room was shut, and a girl of fourteen in this village found out eleven years afterwards that she had been on a roll her whole life and had never been shown the paper. Eleven years. She is twenty-five now and she is the mother of the boy and she came to me in the winter and asked to see it and I said no, and she said it was her life, and I said it was a cooperative's paper and not a thing for one person, and she said, then whose thing is it for.

"So we opened the room. The keeper stands in it if anybody asks him to and he does not say anything unless he is asked a question and then he answers it in words and not out of a paper. Nothing is charged, because the day we charge a person to look at their own paper is the day this room is a thing the rich can buy. And nothing is signed, because a signature is how you get a thing into a column, and this paper has been in a column since my father's day and look where it has got us."

He put the tea down.

"That notice is the first thing in this house that was not in a book, and there are two hundred people in this valley and I could not tell you one of them who has been to that bank in Bell Stair, ever, about anything, and there is a woman in this village who would not go if the room were on fire, and I am not blaming her for that, I am telling you what the paper is for."

---

The instrument was on the table under a sheet of oiled cloth, and it was five leaves and a schedule and it was folded twice, and it was in a fair hand and it was badly made in one respect only, which was that it was written by a man in a hurry who was not a crook and who had put the cost in the only place the form left him.

Kade did not touch it.

"I certified it," she said. "Two years back, at the second bell, in this room, before the room was open, because there was no room. It is on the back of the second leaf. My name, my seal, the day, and one sentence underneath, which is that the pledge is accepted as a class and not as an individual, and I wrote that sentence and I wrote it because I thought it was the honest way of putting it, and I have read it about four hundred times since."

She turned the leaf with the side of her hand.

*And the seed-keepers of this village after us bear the keeping.*

"A class is not a person," she said. "That is my sentence. That is what I put in a box that the form did not have, and the box is the back of a leaf and the box is not a box at all, it is a place where a thing is said once by an examiner with a seal and never looked at again by anybody. And it was in there eighteen months before a man in a lane in Morrowfen sold a schedule with fifty-two lines on it and nobody had read our one in sixty years either."

She did not say anything for a while and the fire did the thing that a fire does.

"Two of us witnessed it. One was a woman called Benda Horvat who sold seed out of the shed by the bridge and is dead. The other is a miller called Stasek and he is eighty-one and he does not live here any more and I have his address and I have not written to him in a year and a half. Neither of them was paid by us. The witnesses are good. I checked them twice and I have gone over the whole of it since, in the nights, for a year and a half, looking for the thing I did wrong, and there is nothing wrong with it, and I would like that understood before anything else is said in this room: I have looked for it and it is not there."

"Then what," said Marek.

"Then a village kept its seed and paid for it in nineteen years instead of in one, and the nineteen years are now, and there is a boy of nineteen and a woman of fifty-one who does not want it and a man of fifty-eight who is going to die this winter." She put her hand flat on the table, not on the paper, beside it. "There are six women on this valley's roll and four of them are over fifty and the fifth is a child and the sixth is me and I am not a member of this village and I have never been a member of anything, and my name is on the paper and it is not on the wall."

On the wall by the fire there was a sheet, and on the sheet there were names in a fair hand with dates against them, and against the last name there was a date, and the last name was Vrabec, and the hand was a different hand.

"Nineteen," said Janko Vrabec. "She writes it up when it changes. That is not an office. There is no office. She is a woman of fifty-one who would not go to a bank in Bell Stair and she writes names on a wall with a date against them and nothing else and I would like you to leave that sheet where it is and I would like you to have understood that I have shown it to you."

---

They went down the road after dark and got as far as the bridge, and the argument happened on the flat part above the mill where there was a wind off the water.

"You are going to say the sentence," said Neris Kade, "so say it in the dark where I do not have to be a person about it."

"I am not going to say anything about your village," said Marek.

"You are going to say something about a class and I have had two days to work out what it is and here it is and it is worse than the one I have been carrying, so say it." She was not angry. She had been angry for most of this year and had got through it into something on the other side, and what was on the other side was much more tiring. "You are going to say that a class is a district. That *the seed-keepers of this village after us* is a box with no person in it, exactly like the low ground, exactly like the fourth clause, and that I signed the box, and that I have spent six years telling this basin that a district is not a person and that a class is not either, and that if a class is allowed then a district is allowed, and if a district is allowed then this whole month has been me arguing for a thing with two faces."

"Yes," said Marek.

"And you are wrong about one half of it and I am not going to help you with the other half."

"Which half."

"You are right that a class is not a person. I have known that for two years and it is the reason I wrote the sentence and the sentence is a confession and it is the only honest thing on the paper." She stopped walking. "The other half is that a person named in a paper is a person, and that when the class was not a person somebody still read the paper, because a man of fifty-eight read it to a woman of fifty-one and she wrote a name on a wall with a date against it, and it is on that wall and not in a roll and there is no form for it and nobody was paid and I did not send one of them. That is not a class being a person. That is fourteen months of a village doing a thing themselves with a sheet of oiled cloth on a table, and I have not written one word about it in a book, and if I had, it would have had a heading on it and my seal under the heading and in four years a person would have come with a form and asked her to prove it."

Neither of them said anything for a while, and it went on longer than either of them had expected, and neither of them won it, and neither of them was ever going to refer to it again.

"Nobody has thanked you for that room," said Marek.

"No," said Neris Kade. "Nobody is going to, and nobody should, and I did not come up this road to be thanked and I did not come up this road to be forgiven either, and I want to say that in the dark now so that neither of us has to say anything about it in the ninth month. There is nothing to forgive. I did the work, the paper is good, the witnesses were real, and I put a cost in a place that could not object, and I signed it, and I was right about the mechanism, and I was the only person in the room who could see what it was going to do, and I saw it."

She turned and went down to the cart.

"And on Tuesday," she said, over her shoulder, "if anybody comes up this road and asks you to carry something again, and there is nobody else, and you go — I want you to understand that the rule that made you carry it is a rule I enforce, and that I will enforce it again, and that it is still a Tuesday, and that you will still go. And then I will still be right about you, and you will still be a man who went."

Then she walked off down the road in the dark to see to the off horse, and Marek Vale stood on the flat above the mill with a sentence in his coat and no way to put it anywhere, and the forty-one sacks went down to the low ground on the hired cart at the third bell two days later, and the Share's rule took the eleven households and the frost took the second crop, and neither of those things had anybody's name on it.
