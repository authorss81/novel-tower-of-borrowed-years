# Chapter 96 — Held by Nobody

He proved it on the twelfth of the ninth month in about four hours, which is the figure he has given to two people since, neither of them in a room, and it is the only figure about himself he has volunteered in nineteen years.

He came in at the first bell and did not say one word to anybody, and at about the second he cleared the corner of his own desk, which is the second time in three hundred years that that corner has been cleared for anything but a tray.

There are eleven pages in the fifth box older than the eight-year practice. He had said that in a yard in the rains and had not repeated it. He went through all eleven at about the rate of eleven minutes a page, which is slow, and he went through them once down and once from the other end, which is how he has counted every figure he has entered since before Ostrowsky came to this desk.

What is in the fifth box at the foot of the settlement desk's form, under a rule that a district may hand over a returnable sum at a discount, is this.

Every page has a day at the top of it, in this office's hand, because you put the day at the top of a page or you cannot find the page again, and that has been this office's practice for longer than the flood years.

Not one of the eleven entries has a date of its own.

A levy is dated. A schedule is dated. A posting is dated. A return carries the day it was received. An entry with no date of its own has no day on which it was dealt with, and a claim that has been dealt with is not owed, and a claim that is not dealt with and has no day on which it could be is not owed either. It is a line standing in a box. It is entered once a year by whoever is at this desk and it is never applied, because there is nothing to apply it to, and a sum handed over to a receiving party comes off a page and a sum handed over to nothing stays on it for ever.

And the receiving party at the end of those eleven entries is not a party. It is a word. It is a kind. It is the kind of rate that offsets a reserve and is recited on an assessment and is never itself charged to anybody, and it has been in the fifth box under a rule about returnable sums since before there was a flood year in this city's memory, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a rate of that kind is charged to a person.

The day at the head of the fourth of them, which the man in the cell in Crown March would not name, is the day Grale wrote, in the fourth year of his hand in this office, on an ordinary morning of the second month, and he is not going to describe that morning to anybody.

And that is the line. The account says there is a sum standing against a district that nobody can apply. The box says the sum was handed over and came off the page. Both books are correct, both are in this building, and for three hundred years they have been saying opposite things about the same figure, and the reason they can go on saying it is that there is no fourth box on the form, and there never has been, and an entry that is not a levy, not a schedule, not a posting and not a return goes in the fifth box for eight years' worth of luck and then has no home at all.

He sat with his hands flat on the desk for about a minute and did not write anything, and then he told Ostrowsky, because Ostrowsky was at the next desk and had hung his coat on the same nail since before the flood and had asked him a question about the day at the top of a page on the fourth of the ninth month and had been answered honestly and had not known what he had been given.

"You have been in this room since the flood," said Grale. "I am going to say one thing and then I am going to write it in a book and then I am going to stop, and you are going to have to work in a room where a man of fifty-eight has said one thing, and I would like you to be the one who hears it and not the nine people in the copy room."

"I am the one who hears it," said Ostrowsky.

Then Grale said the thing, and it took about ninety seconds, and it was the shortest statement of a fact that has been made in this basin in three hundred years, and he made it in the plain voice he reads forms in, and he did not improve it, and he did not say *I think* or *it appears* or *in my opinion*, and when he was asked afterwards to soften it he said no.

And then he said the other one, and it is the reason the ninth month goes the way it goes, and it cost him more than the first one.

"Now the other," he said. "I have wanted one thing since I was thirty-five and I have said it out loud twice in yards in this city and both times to a person who did not repeat it, and I am telling you the same person in a copy room and no more than that, so listen to the shape of it rather than the words.

"I have believed for twenty-three years that the order in a frame gets cheaper as it goes down, and that somebody worked that out about sixty years ago and arranged the order of the lines, and that the school is at the bottom of it because of that. That is what I have believed. I have just proved the line I told a yard about in the rains, and the line I have proved is a fifth box, and the line and the frame are in the same book.

"They are not the same thing. I have wanted them to be the same thing since I was thirty-five, and they are not, and I am telling you that at about eleven in the morning on a day when I have just found out the thing I have been looking for, because if I do not say it out loud to one person in the next ten minutes I will spend the rest of the month arranging to have said it, and a man who goes looking for his own condition is a man who has already decided the answer."

"That is the only thing I have ever taught anybody in this building," said Grale, "and it took me nineteen years and I have never said it out loud before."

Ostrowsky did not say anything for a while, and then he said: "Then what was the sixty years about."

"I do not know," said Grale. "I am fifty-eight. I have four orders in a frame and I have signed two of them and I have wanted to stop since I was thirty-five and I am stopping today, and the thing about the order of the lines is not going to be proved by me now, and it is not going to be proved by anybody after me, and that is the price of my condition and I set the condition myself in a yard in the rains in front of about forty people, and I would set it again."

---

He wrote the finding at about the fourth bell in the margin of the district account, in his own hand, because there is no form for a sentence and there is no form for a finding by a levy office officer and there is no clerk in Crown March or in Bell Stair who would take a thing an officer wrote about his own hand.

He wrote it in about two hundred words. Then he wrote four lines underneath it, which were a notice, and the notice was dated the twelfth of the ninth month and took effect on the day, and the reason given in it was in the first person and was that the officer had established a fact about this office's own practice and was no longer able to certify in the terms of the office, and that no allegation of any kind was made against any person, and that there was nothing for anybody to investigate.

Then he went and stood at the frame for a while.

There is one cut order permitted in this basin. It is in a frame nailed to the wall of this office and there is no copy of it anywhere in the kingdom, and the water office may issue it and may not let anybody have one. It is read, in the words of the office, to every party it names, before it is entered. It is not entered at the close of a month. It carries the day and not the month. And it carries the name of the officer who enters it.

Under the last line of the order in the frame there is one name, in a hand that got heavier on the final letter than on the other five, and the letter came out at a different angle, and nobody saw it happen, and the man who wrote it had decided, four miles away in a ward, in the twelfth month of a year in which he had proved the only thing he had ever wanted to prove, that he was going to leave.

---

Marek Vale came up the hill at about the second bell, which is the wrong time of day to come up the hill, and he had the recertification list of twenty-two files in his hand with a pencil line on the back of it, and the pencil line was a sentence and a question, and the question was two words long and it had been two words long for nine days.

He put it on the corner of the desk, flat, the way a clerk puts a thing down when he is not going to argue about it, and he said, "There is a rate in your account with a kind at the head of it and nine names under it that are not on the Gate tank's count, and I have checked it twice, and I have got nothing."

"Then you have got nothing," said Grale, "and I am going to tell you why, and it is not a mystery and it is not anybody's fault. A kind is not a person. There is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be asked whether they are well, or whether they are the kind of thing a line names, and if there were, the nine names under that head would be nine households in four yards who have never been told that a line exists, and they would be told it by somebody standing at a tank, and I would not be that somebody and neither would you and neither would anybody else, and the only difference between that and the last thing that happened to those nine households is the coat."

"I know that," said Marek. "I am not asking to be able to ask. I am asking whether a true sentence about it can go anywhere at all, and the answer has been no in six different rooms since the seed month, and I have stopped asking it in rooms."

"Then stop asking it everywhere," said Grale. "There is a room in Tallow Ward with a form in it and a rule that anybody may enter a line about their own paper. You want to put an officer of this office's own admission into that room in the first person, and I am going to tell you why I will not let you, and then I am going to leave and you can do what you like with it, because I am a man with nineteen years in a chair and I have earned the right to be unhelpful for four hours.

"That form has one rule and it works. A bundle may not be entered as one line. A counterweight has to be named to the people who will bear it. Every one of those words is about a person being in a room. If a levy officer of nineteen years puts a sentence about a kind into it, then in a year a thing that costs four hundred and six people money is a thing a yard's room said, and a yard's room is a thing a man in a good coat can produce at anybody, and in nine months there will be a man in a good coat up that road who will be able to say that the ward's own method says a kind of rate is proper, because a clerk wrote it down in a shed and nobody was in the room but him and a rope-maker."

He picked up his coat at about the fifth bell, which is early, and Marek said the only thing he said in that office that day.

"That is the correct answer and it is the fourth time this year somebody has told me that to my face and every one of the four of them was somebody who was not going to have to carry it."

"Take it up with the yard," said Grale. "I have got one thing to say to a man who has no office in my last hour and I am going to say it now, so it is out of me and you can do as you like with it.

"I have wanted to stop since I was thirty-five. I have proved a line this morning in a book and the condition I gave a yard in the rains is the day I prove it I stop, and I am stopping at noon, and I have written it in the margin of the account in my own hand because there is nowhere else to write it. The ninth month's demand is now unanswerable. The forty-three and some months on that plate is on the district account and it cannot be taken off it, because taking a sum off an account is a cut, and a cut carries the name of the officer who enters it, and there is going to be no officer in this building from noon today. And that is my own doing, and it is the second time in my life I have done a thing that was correct and cost somebody else, and the first time I was thirty-five and I was not talking to anybody and I am talking to you now, and that is the difference, and I would like it noticed that it is the whole of the difference."

---

The water office wrote on the fifteenth. It was one line and it was not unkind and the young man who brought it up the hill did not apologise for the tube that day, because there was no tube.

*To whom it may concern: the district account of Tallow Ward is asked to exhibit the recited carry and to advise the position of the district claim.*

Nobody in this basin knows what a district account is held in. It is a page, in a book, on a shelf in the levy office on Bell Stair. The shelf is there and the book is on it and the page is legible and about nine people in this city could read it this morning.

And a district account is held by a person. Not by a book, and not by a room, and not by a wall. It is held by the officer of the office that keeps it, because the officer can be asked a question about it, and can be asked to produce it, and can be asked to enter a thing against it, and can be asked to be shown the eight steps between a sum in Rainmarket and a line on a page in Bell Stair, and can answer yes or no or *I do not know, and here is the form I lack.*

There is no form in this kingdom for a district account without an officer. A building is not a holder. A page is not a holder. A shelf is not a holder. There is no box for it, and if there were a box it would be in the same position as a counterweight named as a district, and it would be a cost in a box with no person in it, and every person in this basin would recognise it on sight.

On the seventeenth of the ninth month the water office wrote again, to the same address, in the same hand, and the address is the address of an office and not of a person, and the letter says that the office is not able to advise and will write again, and it did write again, in the tenth month, and in the month after that, and there is no record in this basin of anybody being annoyed about it, because being annoyed requires somebody to be annoyed and there was nobody there to be annoyed.

In the meantime, on the fifteenth, the office did exactly what it is required to do, which was to read a second cut order in the words of the office to every party it names, in seven places and one of them on foot, and then not to enter it, because the fourth of the four things a cut order says is the name of the officer who enters it, and the frame on the wall of the levy office on Bell Stair has one name under its last line and it is a name that was written on the twentieth of the seed month in a hand that got heavier on the last letter than it should have.

The district account is held by nobody. It is a page, in a book, on a shelf, in a room that is locked by a man who comes in at the eighth bell and goes out at the seventh, and about nine people in this basin could read the line about the recited carry if anybody thought to ask them to, and the water office has asked three times, and there is no person to whom the question can be put.
