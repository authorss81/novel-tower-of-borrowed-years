# Chapter 99 — The Sixteenth

The countersignature came back down the coast road on the seventeenth of the ninth month in a bundle of other things and it was dated the sixteenth, and the finding that made the schedule lawful was made on the seventeenth, at a table in Bell Stair, by a board of five, with the Chair serving the minute in his own hand because he had no clerk and could not get one.

There are six named men on the north-road span and three seasons of eleven days on the schedule and an independent witness who is a physician and who ruled a clerk out in the same breath and signed one side of it and not the other, and the schedule is lawful, and the schedule has been lawful since a room in Bell Stair said so in the plainest sentence anybody in this basin has ever written about road work.

The countersignature is a Crown March document. Supervised road work on a Crown March road is Crown March money, and Crown March money is released by an office in Crown March, and the office that releases it countersigns the document it releases the money against, and that is a custom and not a form, and it has been a custom in that part of the kingdom for longer than this basin has kept a roll.

It dates the day the money went out.

It does not date the day the work was found lawful, because the office in Crown March that released the money had not heard of the board of five in Bell Stair, and had no way of hearing of it, and there is no form on which a room in Bell Stair advises an office in Crown March that a schedule under its consideration has been found lawful, and there is no box for it in either place.

Both dates are true. That is the whole of it. The money went out on the sixteenth. The schedule was found lawful on the seventeenth. And the piece of paper in Tomas Vale's hand carries one date, and it is the first of the two, and it is on the front where anybody can see it.

He read it at about the fourth bell on the north-road span with the wind off the low ground and the crew standing about eleven feet away doing what they do, and he read it twice, and then he took it in the cab and sat down and did not say anything for about four minutes, and then he came back out.

"I am not going to be angry in front of you," he said. "I have been angry for two days and there is nobody to be angry at, which is worse. There is an office in a town I have never been to and a man in it who has never heard of this ward and has done his job exactly as it is written, and the money came out on the sixteenth because the money is what he was given to send, and I would not have sent it a day later if I were him."

"So we are where we were," said Bohum Ostrek, who is on the schedule and who has been on this road six years.

"No," said Tomas Vale. "That is the other thing and I am coming to it and I have not got it in the right order yet. We are not where we were, because there is a piece of Crown March paper in this cab with my own work on it and a date on it that says the day before a room in Bell Stair made this lawful, and in about two years somebody in that town is going to open two files and read both and one of them is going to be wrong and it is not going to be mine that is wrong, it is going to be a clerk's, and there are about nine clerks in Bell Stair and I have met two of them and one of them is a man with no office and a coat he does not need."

---

His hands are the reason he stopped building and the reason he is on the span, and they are worse than they were in the rains and better than they will be, and the crew knows it and does not talk about it, and on the seventeenth of the ninth month it was cold enough that he could not hold the paper and had to put it down on the tail-board four times.

"You are going to write to somebody," said Bohum Ostrek.

"I am going to write to three somebodies and I am going to be refused by all three and I want you to know the order I am going to try them in," said Tomas Vale. "First, a letter to that office, and a letter is a thing a person says once, and if they change a date in a letter then a man in a town I have never been to has said that a date was wrong because a bridgewright asked him nicely in a hand he can read. No.

"Second, a complaint, or an inquiry, or whatever the hall calls it when a paper is not right. And that is a person in a room asking whether a Crown March document is correct. And I am a bridgewright and the answer will be no, and the no will be correct, and the no will be in a record for ever with my name at the bottom of it asking whether Crown March money is dated properly. No.

"Third: a clerk, in a record, saying so. That is what I want and that is the only thing I want and I have been told it does not exist, and I would like it in writing and I would like it from a person who can be shown to have said it."

Then he said the thing he had got in the right order, and he said it to the six of them, because the six of them are named on the paper that is wrong.

"None of this touches the work," he said. "Three seasons of eleven days. Sixty-six days across three seasons, six men, named, and the residual days on the board at the Gate, and the days are days. The money came out on the sixteenth and the work started on the twentieth and the road is where it is going to be, and there is not a form in this kingdom that can un-lay stone, and I would not want one.

"And I will say the other thing once, because there are six of us and I would rather say it on this span than in a yard. Twenty-six months of my own recovery came out of my own body and it posted on the fourteenth and nobody cancelled it and nobody is going to, and it does not come back to anybody. Bohum signed a counter-desk in the rains and lost two weeks of the middle of his hand, lawfully, under a clause nobody has ever questioned and nobody is going to be asked to question today, and the clerk who countersigned it was a boy of twenty who did it correctly and is at his desk in Bell Stair this morning. None of that is the date's fault, and none of it is fixed by a date."

"And I want the date fixed, and I am going to be a man about it in a way you have not seen me be about anything, and I would like you to know beforehand that I know that. The money came out on the sixteenth and the finding was on the seventeenth, and both of them are true, and a piece of paper has one date on it, and the date on it is the wrong one for what the paper is."

---

Marek Vale came out to the span on the low-ground road a little after the fourth bell, on foot, with his coat buttoned, and he had been sent for by a nine-year-old with a slate, which is how most things happen in this ward.

He had known about the countersignature since the second month and had done nothing about it, because there is no box for a sentence and no box for a date and no office in this basin that holds a Crown March date.

"There is one," he said. "There is exactly one person in this city who can put a Crown March date in a Crown March record, and he is a representative, and he has no clerk, and he cannot get one, and there is no office that will serve the record of a body it is a party to, so he writes it himself at about four minutes a page. He is in Bell Stair and he will be asked to do it as a favour by a man he has met four times, and he will not, because a representative who corrects a document on request is a representative with a queue, and he has said in his own hand that he would rather be unapproachable than be a man people can get things from. So it has to be a finding, or it has to be nothing, and it cannot be a finding because the board cannot sit on a date and there is no date for anything."

"He was the Chair."

"He was the Chair and he wrote the finding out in his own hand and he has never met the countersignature and did not know it was coming, and that is the last part and it is the part that is going to be hard for both of us," said Marek. "A representative who has to say in his own minute that an office in his own region put a date on a piece of paper that does not say what the date means, is a representative who has said that a thing in Crown March is careless. There is no other way to say it and I have tried four ways on this road in the last two hours."

---

The girl was on the tail-board with her slate and a stick of chalk and she had written down nine things that had happened on this road since the morning, which is what she does, and she had been at the seed house and at the tank and at the yard and now she was here, and nobody had asked her to be anywhere and she had told them on the road that she was going to be at all three.

She had heard the date twice.

"What is a date for," said Lio Vale.

"It is so that afterwards," said Tomas Vale, "when somebody wants to say that the work on this road was not allowed to be done, there is a piece of paper with a day on it that says it was."

"Afterwards is us," said the girl.

"Yes," said her father.

She looked at the slate for a while and then she wrote something on it and turned it round so that both of them could read it, and it said, in a child's hand, *afterwards is us, so tell them the true day.*

Nobody said anything to her about it and nobody stopped her, which is the ordinary arrangement in this ward, and she put the chalk in her pocket and went and sat on the windward side of the cab with her back to the wind, and the six men went back to work, and Tomas Vale picked the countersignature up off the tail-board and put it inside his coat, against his chest, where it was, and said he would have it up the hill before the fifth bell and would not be in the room for more than the four minutes it takes, and then he took it back out of his own coat and gave it to the man with no office, who was going up the hill anyway and wanted nothing for it.

---

A man with no office carried the countersignature up the hill in the inside pocket of a coat at about the fifth bell on the seventeenth of the ninth month and put it on the table in front of a Crown March representative and said four sentences and left, and the four sentences were that a Crown March office had dated a document the day before a room in Bell Stair found the work lawful, that both dates were true, that there was no form in this basin on which a Crown March date could be corrected, and that the man bringing it was a party to the work and not an office and would be thanked for nothing.

Bel Harrow wrote it at about the sixth bell on the seventeenth of the ninth month, on the second floor of a building in Bell Stair, in a room built for thirty, at a table with a hat on, because there was nobody else in the building and there had not been anybody else in the building for four days.

It is the last of five paragraphs and it has no name at the foot of it, and the paragraph before it has no name at the foot of it either, and the one before that has a Fund secretary's name under it, and the minute is a minute of a board of five with the Trust's seat empty for the fifth time and in the ordinary course still in quotation marks in a drawer in that building.

The paragraph records that a Crown March office has countersigned a schedule of supervised road work on the north-road span and has dated it the sixteenth, and that the schedule was found lawful by this board on the seventeenth, and that both dates are correct and that the countersignature dates the release of the money and not the finding of the board, and that no office in this basin can amend a date on a Crown March instrument and that this board cannot sit upon a date and has no standing to do so, and that the finding of the seventeenth stands and is not disturbed, and that a record exists in which a Crown March date and a finding of this board may be read together and will be read together by somebody.

And then, in the same hand, and slower, and taking about as long as the rest of it: that the Chair has before him a Crown March document and a minute of this board bearing on the same work, and that the two bear dates one day apart, and that the document has been on his table about an hour and he has established nothing with it, and that he does not know whether the office which set the date knows that a board sat, and that he does not know whether the office would regard the date as wrong, and that he is not going to find out in a way that would put a bridgewright's question into a Crown March file, and that he records the discrepancy at the request of a party to the work and not at the request of any office, and that he is the Crown March representative in this city and he has made no enquiry and has no authority to make one and is not going to have one, and that a thing in his own region is capable of being read two ways and he has read it two ways and has written down which way he read it.

Nobody countersigned it, because no office will serve the record of a body it is a party to, and it went into a drawer in a building on Bell Stair at about the seventh bell on the seventeenth of the ninth month, and it is in that drawer, and it can be produced by one person, and he has said out loud that he will produce it, and nobody has asked him to and he was not thanked and he did not mention it again.

Marek was not in the room. He was on the low-ground road at about the seventh bell that evening, walking down it with a slate-less pair of hands, and a Crown March representative had put a Crown March date in a Crown March record in his own hand and had not been asked to and had not said so, and the man with no office who had set all of it in motion was told about it at a tank at the seventh bell by a rope-maker who had heard it at the trestle, and Marek Vale said *that is very good of him*, and Sanne Droll said *do not*, and neither of them explained it, and the man at the counter heard both sentences and has never worked out which of the two was the kindness.
