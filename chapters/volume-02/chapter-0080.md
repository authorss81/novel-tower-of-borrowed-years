# Chapter 80 — Which Clause

The harvest month came in cold and clear, and the low ground got four days of it, which was the best weather anybody had had since the flood year and the worst possible weather for a second sowing, because a second sowing wants a frost to break the crust and not four days of a low sun on a crust that was already made.

The kept-back had to be in the ground by the last day of the growth month, which was the twentieth, and it went in on the nineteenth, in nine hundred and forty acres of it, off the forty-one sacks that came down the upland road on a hired cart in the second week of the growth month, and it went in well, and nobody was pleased, because a second sowing is a thing you do because the first one failed.

On the third day of the harvest month Mirren Fisk did the thing she had said in a yard on the twenty-first of the seed month that she would have to do.

---

She did it in the seed house, standing, at the second bell, with the bench behind her and about thirty people in it, and eleven of the eleven were in the room and the other nineteen were not, and that was on purpose, because the rule it settles is the Share's own rule and is not a matter for the low ground.

There was one sheet of paper. It was the length of a seed sack's label and had been cut off a sack, and there was nothing at the top of it, and eleven lines on it, and each line was a household's ground and a figure, and no rule at the head and no column names and no total at the foot, and the reason for all three absences is the same reason and she said it out loud before she put the pen down.

"There is no form," said Mirren Fisk. "I looked on the first of this month with a man from Bell Stair sitting in that corner and I looked again yesterday on my own, in an hour, with the door shut. There is no form in this kingdom in which a rate on a member's ground goes, and none for a member in illness, and none for a member who cannot work, and there is not going to be one, and I am not going to cut one on the back of a seed sack and pretend to the eleven people in this room that I have. The absence is not an oversight. The absence is the rule working exactly as it was cut."

"Then where is it going," said a man at the back.

"Onto that, and into the back of the bench book under the date. And it is not a schedule and it is not a levy and it is not a return and it is not a posting, and none of those four is going to be the word for it, because if I use one of them then in nine months somebody in a hall asks me which one it was and I will have said it, and the day I say one of the four is the day this piece of sack has become a thing the Share is owed by somebody instead of a thing the Share did."

She wrote the date and nothing else for a minute and a half while about four of the thirty talked at once, and then she wrote eleven lines and put the pen down and did not read them out, which is the correct thing to do with a sheet with nothing at the head of it.

"You did the days," she said. "Every one of you did the days, in the growth month, with the frost in the shadow of the ditch and nothing in the ground worth lifting, and I will not have it said that those days were not work. The days are not the whole of it. The whole of it is a third of somebody's kept-back, and eleven of you could not bring a third in, and the balance is a rate, and the rate is on your ground, in your own name or your mother's, and it makes no difference to that sheet which.

"And I am going to say the thing I have said once in this yard and am saying again because there are thirty people here and one of them heard it. There is no box on a rate on which anybody is asked whether you are well. Not one. Not by the Share, not by the Compact, not by the water office, and not by the form, because the form was cut for ground, and ground is a thing that can be described once and stayed described. If you are entirely well you are on that sheet. If you cannot get out of bed you are on it by exactly the same figure, and nobody will ever know which you were, and in about four years neither will you."

---

Marek Vale was in the corner with the flat board under his arm and there was nothing in the city he could do with it, and he stood up anyway.

"Four facts and I am not going to choose between them, and I would like the room to know that I cannot."

Nobody objected, which was worse than objection.

"A shower went over the ridge on the ninth of the seed month and missed this ground by four miles. Eleven of sixty-one trays went to sand off the oldest kept-back stock in the basin, out of a shed that was shut up wet in the flood year. The district's flood-quarters rate was restated on the eighteenth on a rule that was correct, by an officer who had it open again in two days and has said out loud in a lane that four households lost a fortnight while it was shut. And the fourth clause of the instrument you all signed in the bad autumn names the low ground as the district that bears the failure of the season favoured.

"I cannot show any of you that the fourth clause did it and neither can she, and I have been in a field trying since the twenty-fourth and I have failed and I am going to keep failing at it. And the rate on that sack is not the fourth clause. It is your own rule, out of your own book, written by the woman who keeps it, and in the ninth month a man in a good coat will come up this road and he will be right about half of it, and it will be the half that is easier to say."

Fisk had the pen in her hand and looked at him over it.

"I have had a sheet with nothing at the head of it since the first of this month," she said, "waiting for somebody to come up this road and put something at the top of it, and you have come up the road, and I am glad, and I am not going to use it."

"I know."

"You do not, and here is why. If there is a word at the top of this, then this is a thing of a kind, and a thing of a kind is a thing somebody can be asked about at a counter and answered in a form and administered, and the eleven names on it will be in a column in Bell Stair with a date against them, and the day somebody comes with a form for it is the day one of these eleven women is told something by an office about her own ground.

"You told me yourself, a month ago, at a gate on this road, why that is the thing you have been frightened of since the ninth of the seed month. I am not taking your sentence. I am telling you what it is for. It does not need somewhere to go. It needs to stay where it is, in a keeper's day book on an island, in a hand that is not yours, where it cannot be used by anybody, including you."

Marek stood in the corner of the seed house and had nothing to say to that, which has happened to him four times in six weeks and has never once been pleasant.

---

The person it landed on first was not the worst of it and nobody had any way of ranking them, which is the point.

Ilma Tresch was forty-one and had done all eleven days on ground that had already failed, and had been at the low ground clinic on the seventh with a cough since the ninth of the seed month, and Sera Holt had certified her out of the remaining days, and the certificate went to the board of the clinic, and the board entered it as a cost, and a clinic's costs go on the same account as everything else, and the district's answer to a cost is a rate.

Which is the whole of a physician's discovery of the last month in one sentence, and Marek had been in the room and had not been able to improve on it, and there it was, on a piece of sack.

"Then I have paid twice," said Ilma Tresch. "I did the days, and the doctor wrote me out of four of them, and that cost the clinic something, and now I am on that."

"Yes."

"And what do I do."

"You can read your own line at the water office counter," said Marek Vale, "any day, in person, at the ordinary hours, and they will read it to you and nobody charges for that and it is your right."

"And will it be on it."

He did not answer for a moment, and the moment was the whole of the chapter, and he has thought about it since.

"It will not," he said. "A district's roll shows what is owed to the district. Your lot failed and the rule took the cost out of eleven households, and the Share is not a district and this is not a district's rate. It is a cooperative's own right against a member's ground, and there is no roll in this city with your figure on it and no office that holds it, and nobody can produce a copy of a thing nobody has. So you have a piece of paper and your own memory of every day you worked, and that is all of it, and I am not going to tell you it is a fair position, because it is not, and in four years you will not remember the days either, and the only thing left will be the paper."

She folded her hands.

"I am not asking you to do anything," she said. "I have watched you not do it for a month. I would like to say that my mother was one of the four that lost a fortnight when the window was shut, and she has never said a word to me about you, and that is not why I have come. It is because I am about to hand you something and I would rather you knew what it is not."

She said it to the middle of the room, which was the correct place to say it.

---

Wenna Drel was not on the sheet, and her mother was forty-one and had walked down from the low-ground road for it and should not have.

"That is my ground and my daughter is not on it, and I have been told the reason twice and I would like to hear it a third time in a building. The ground is in my name and a rate is a charge on ground, and a charge on ground does not know there is a girl of sixteen at the end of my family who has drawn seed off your bench since the third of the seed month and signed a line in a lane in the rain where I sign, and nobody has ever asked her anything, and she is on no roll of yours and on no paper and not on that."

"Her name is on the roll because yours is," said Fisk, "and the roll is a list of households and it is correct, and it is the fourth line of your line, and it is a list of who the seed is owed to and not a list of who is affected."

"That is the same sentence with the words the other way round," said Marek.

"It is," said Fisk, "and I have known it since the twenty-first of the seed month and have been the keeper of it since the third of the seed month, and there is a month between a thing being known and a keeper being able to do anything at all about it, and that month is the whole of what happened this summer. Nobody has ever asked me to write down that a girl of sixteen has been on this and I have not written it, and if somebody hands me a piece of paper tonight I will put her name at the top of it with the date, and I would like to be asked to do it by somebody who has to live in this valley after the ninth month."

Nobody asked her to do it.

And that was the morning. There were nine others, and Marek learned out of them what he was going to learn and wrote none of it down, and what he learned was that there are eleven houses in this basin where the ground is in a name and there is nobody in the house, and that this is not a fifth fact about the frost and is not the fourth clause and is not a rule in the Share's book. It is just true in a valley. It will be in the ninth month too.

---

Ovid Merrow came up the low-ground road at the fourth bell and stood in the yard with his hands behind his back, and Ilse Ferrow was with him and did not come past the step, and about forty people were in the lane and none of them inside, because a member's meeting is a members' meeting and a man with a licence is not a member of anything, and he knew it and said so before anybody could.

"I am not coming in. I have been to a yard once in this basin and read a sheet off a nail and I did it well and I am not doing it again. Your keeper has said I may say this at the door.

"Every one of the fifty-two letters went into my register on the nineteenth with the fact of it and the words of the Share, and the register is open four hours a day and nothing is charged. From the twentieth I have had people in asking whether a letter came for them, and for about two in three it did, and for the rest it did not, because eight households are not at the house the letter went to, and I have written that in the second hand and I am not going to tell you what it is a number of.

"And from this morning I have had a question I have never had in nine years. Nobody has ever come into my shed and asked me what the law says instead of what the paper says. They are standing on my floor asking me whether a rate on a member's ground is a rate, and I have told them the truth, which is that I do not know and have been licensed here for nine years and never had to, because nobody has ever asked me and because there is no line on any roll in this basin that would tell a person where to ask. And the price of not knowing in my trade is that I buy a piece of paper instead of an answer, and I have made a very good living out of the fact that nobody else in this basin will answer a question for nothing."

He put his hands in his coat.

"I will say the other thing once. On the twenty-second I open at the fourth bell as usual, and the register will carry a line for each of the eleven households the Share has written to, and against it, in the second hand, in the words the household used, what it has been told it owes. Nothing is charged. It is the same question I have been asking for a month and it is worth a great deal more now, and I am not going to pretend I have not noticed that, and I am not going to pretend I am being kind, because I am not being kind. I am a man in a lane with a bench and a column with nothing at the head of it, and the only honest thing left for me is to be the second person in this basin who writes down what a person said when they were asked what they owed."

Fisk said nothing.

"Put it in your own minutes," said Ovid Merrow, "and I will be glad if you do."

Then he went down the lane, and about six people who had heard him from the step went in and looked at the bench, and the sixth column had gone up on the nineteenth for the letters and the seventh was still bare, and four people stood in front of it not knowing what they were looking at, which is the only thing that has kept it bare for a month.

---

Marek walked down to the lane at the sixth bell, stood where the queue stands, and did not go in.

On the third bench along the right-hand wall the low ground's line was where it had been for a month: a total, two dates, a receiving party, and in the second hand what the district said when it was asked. And beside it, added on the sixth of the growth month in a hand that was not Merrow's, there was a further line, because a sum handed over to a receiving party is a returnable sum, and a returnable sum is the fifth box, and the fifth box is the oldest box on the form, and the sixth of the month was after the frost.

It named a party in Crown March and a date. It did not say where the thing had gone after that, and it was not a thing anybody in that shed could be shown, and about nine people in the lane had asked about the low ground that week and had got that far and had stopped, which is exactly as far as it is designed to carry anybody.

And further along the same wall the Tallow Gate line said a licensed carrier and forty-three and some months and a date and a party and *asked, and no answer given*, and there was nothing after it.

Marek looked at both of them and knew the whole of it, all nine of it, and could not have said one word of it to a person.

The reason was not the ward and was not loyalty and was not a rule. The next word after the low ground is a place, and the next word after that place is the end of it, and if he said the two halves out loud in a lane in Morrowfen then forty people would have them by the evening and a district account would be entered against before any office in Crown March or at the Tally Halls had been asked a question. A ward is not a person. Nobody can serve a notice on a yard. So a thing that goes into a roll that way goes in with nobody in the room and nobody able to refuse it, and he had said that sentence out loud on the twentieth, in a room, with four people in it, and had not been thanked for it and was not going to be.

So he stood in a lane at the sixth bell with the true end of the thing in his head and the ninth link over at the Tallow Gate, collectable, uncollected, and he told no one, and that was the correct decision, and it was also the exact one Neris Kade had named in a corridor in the seed month, and he knew that too.

---

He walked up the road out of the low ground in the dark. The sheet was not in the shed. It was in Fisk's own house on a table, because a sheet with nothing at the head of it does not stay in a building, and because she had learned that in a bad autumn, and Marek knew that she had done it and did not know how he knew it.

There was a space in the seed house yard about the size of a long leaf, where the lamp of the Share is, and the lamp has been cut after every line above it and is supplied in the third month, and the cut had not come off the ground, because a cut is not a date, and in the ninth month the office will send somebody.

On the third day of the harvest month eleven houses in the Rainmarket low ground were handed a figure on a piece of sack with nothing at the head of it, out of a correct rule, on a cooperative's own word, by a woman who keeps the book and had told the room there was no form; and over at the Tallow Gate a plate on a shed door stood for forty-three and some months that a man of fifty-eight had offered to pay out of his own and had not been asked for; and on an island a day book had a girl's own words in it in a keeper's hand, initialled in the margin, and a copy would be ready on the second day of next month and would not be worth anything in a room; and forty feet off the road a column a hand's width wide was still bare at the head of it; and in a hall in Bell Stair a vault book on the second floor had two entries in it in a shorthand that a man who has not been hurt at work cannot read, and neither of them said four bells and neither said three days.

And the sentence he had said out loud in a room on the twentieth, which had been the whole of the answer to the only question anybody had put to him in a month, was in a book on a table on an island across the causeway, under a weight, in a keeper's hand, and it could not be used by anybody, and it was not going to stop one single thing that was going to happen in the ninth month.

Marek Vale walked up the road at the seventh bell with a card in his coat he had not used and will not use, and a flat board with four ruled sheets in it, and no office, and no form, and no room, and no work, and four bells and three days of a year gone, and a sentence in a keeper's book. He was lighter than he had been in the seed month by exactly one thing, which was the belief that if he got the words right it would make a difference. He had got the words right. It had not made a difference. He had said so out loud on the twentieth, in a room, to two people and a bell and a queue, and the ninth month was under four weeks off.
