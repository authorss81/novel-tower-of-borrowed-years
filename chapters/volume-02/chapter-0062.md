# Chapter 62 — The First Thing in Three Hundred Years

The Crown March room is over a granary with a barred window and one door, and there is no bell in it, and a man sitting in it for five weeks loses the sense of the bells in his own city, which Hesper Rann said was the first thing he had noticed and the last thing anybody had asked him about.

Ivo Sarn had a minute book and a straight chair and no clerk, because a Crown March officer takes a statement himself or does not take it at all, and he came in at the second bell of the fourteenth with the book under his arm and put it on the table square to Hesper Rann so that both of them could see the page.

"I am going to read you what is on my paper before I ask you one question," he said, "and I am going to read it as a list, because it is a list, and because the moment I start asking questions the list stops being what you said and starts being what we agreed you said. Do you understand the difference."

"Yes," said Hesper Rann.

"Four dates," said Ivo Sarn, and read them. "The ninth day of the rains, at the sixth bell. The fifteenth day of the rains, in the afternoon. The twenty-second day of the rains, at the fourth bell. And a fourth date which my paper does not give."

The room was quiet for a moment, and then a man who has been twenty-two years in a building forty miles up this road said nothing at all, because the fourth date is not his to give.

"The second one is wrong," said Hesper Rann.

Sarn wrote it in. He did not ask why and he did not argue; he put a small stroke at the side of the line and went on, and the going on without asking was the thing that made Hesper Rann sit up off the wall for the first time in an hour.

"The second is the fifteenth at the second bell, not the afternoon."

"Second bell."

"And I was not alone on it."

"You are not asked that," said Sarn. "If you wish to be asked it, say so and I will ask it, and you will be answering a different question from the one you have been answering, and I will open a second minute. I want to be exact about that, because men in this room have been given a second minute by an officer who did not mean to make one, and eleven months later there are two documents and one of them is about something else."

"Do not open it," said Hesper Rann, and sat back down.

---

The form was on the table because it had come with the Crown March file. Hesper Rann's commitment record cited a Morrowfen form by number and a box, and Ivo Sarn had been sent to identify the box, and the box was the whole of the errand and had been the whole of the errand for about three days.

"How many boxes on this form," said Ivo Sarn.

"Five."

"Name them."

Hesper Rann named them. A levy, a schedule, a posting, a return, and at the foot, under a rule that a district may hand over a returnable sum to another party at a discount, one that had not been cut for this century and was the oldest thing on it.

"Which of those five is the entry of the ninth."

"The box for a return."

"Which of those five is the entry of the fifteenth, as you have now corrected it."

"The box for a return."

"And the twenty-second."

"The box for a return," said Hesper Rann. "All three of them. Every one of the three went in as a return, and the return is the correct box, and a rate arrears is a returnable sum, and the rule at the foot of the form says a district may hand it over at a discount, which is what I was doing, and which is the only reason it was lawful at all."

"Then what is the entry of the ninth," said Ivo Sarn, "in words."

---

And Hesper Rann said it.

He did not confess it and he did not lower his head and he did not ask for anything. He was forty-six years old and he had been twenty-two years a supervisor and he had kept clinics open and he was not going to be told by a Crown March officer with a straight chair how to say a word.

"An assignment," he said. "A ratepayer in arrears hands the collection right in the arrears to whoever pays him at a discount. One line. Dated. Signed by him. It goes into the return box, and it is not a lie, and it has not been a lie for eight years, and the form was cut before anybody was doing it, and I have never once been asked to explain which of the four kinds it was, because there were four kinds on that desk and an assignment is not one of them."

"You have said it now."

"I have said it now to a man with a minute book," said Hesper Rann, "because you asked me a direct question in a room, and I have been in a cell five weeks with nothing to do but work out what the four kinds are, and I have got to a fifth, and the fifth has been in this city for eight years and nobody has said it out loud anywhere, and I would like it in your book while there is still a clerk in the kingdom who will write one down."

Sarn wrote. He wrote for a long time, because a minute is not a summary and he had been eleven years at this and the habit of a Crown March officer is that a thing said in a room is kept in the room's words and not in the officer's.

---

"And the fourth date," said Ivo Sarn, when the pen was down.

"No."

"You have given me three out of four."

"I have given you three out of four," said Hesper Rann. "The fourth is not mine."

"It is a date in an account desk's book. It is in a book kept by your own office, and you kept the account, and a date in a book is a fact and not a confidence."

"It is a line in that account," said Hesper Rann, "and there is a man in this city who has said out loud in a yard that the day he proves that line he has to stop, and if I say the date in a room it is in a record in Crown March inside a week, and the man who gave the line under oath is not going to be asked whether he gave it to me first." He turned his head and looked at the barred window, which showed a wall. "You will have it in nine months from somebody who can afford to be wrong about it in public. I am not going to be the one."

Sarn looked at him for a moment longer than a man taking a statement usually does.

"The declining is entered," he said. "A person declines to answer and the declining is written down as a declining and nothing is made of it, and I will tell you that that rule is not a Crown March courtesy. It is in the standing orders of my office, and it was put there nine years ago by a representative who could not get anybody to serve a ward, and I have never once had anybody in this room try to use it against me." He turned the page. "I am also going to say the other thing, because I have read the same four dates in two other files and I am tired of them."

"Then say it."

"The gate was not my best work," said Hesper Rann. "The water entry on the ninth was. If anybody in Crown March has told you that the man who did the ninth is sorry about the gate, they have got a file with one date in it and not the other, and it is their file and not mine."

He did not say he had been wrong. He said the other thing, in that order, and it was a distinction and not an apology, and Ivo Sarn entered both halves and neither half was softer than the man who said it.

"Would you do it again."

"Yes," said Hesper Rann. "That is the part I would like somebody to write down after the other parts, and nobody ever does, because the two do not sit on a page together. I chose a person. I have chosen a person before and I will choose a person again and I would do it the same way, and the day somebody asks me what I learned from it I have nothing to give them."

---

Marek had written the letter. He wrote it on the twelfth, after the corridor with Sedge, at the sixth bell, and it was the only thing he had done for the Crown March office in five weeks, and it had no office under his name and no letter beside it and it went to a person whose name he had got out of the finding of the seventeenth, and it said, in nine lines: *Is there a box on the settlement desk's form for an entry that is not a levy, a schedule, a posting or a return. If there is, I would like to know the heading. If there is not, then the four kinds are the four boxes and there is a fifth thing going into a public account in this city, and I would like somebody in an office to write it down in words before the ninth month.*

It took three days to come back as a man standing in a corridor at Bell Stair at the eighth bell of the fifteenth with a minute book under his arm, and a Crown March officer does not come up this hill for a nine-line letter.

"There is no box," said Ivo Sarn. "That is the answer and it took me two days in Crown March to establish it and it is the reason I am on this hill. And the officer who told me that is a man who has been doing this for eleven years and who did not have to come to Morrowfen to tell me, because a form is a form and the form is the same in both places."

---

He read the minute out in the second-floor corridor, and he read it as a list, and there were four people there and none of them were the right four.

Marek was one. Sedge was one, because a minute out of Crown March that named a form used at this desk was going to be on that desk by the fourth bell whatever anybody did. Ostrowsky was not there and had not been told and was not to be credited with it. The duty clerk from the posting desk was there because she was in the corridor and always is.

The fourth was Neris Kade, and she was there because the officer had asked the Rainmarket district to produce the form, and the Rainmarket district had sent its examiner, and the examiner had a seal.

Sarn read all of it. He read the three dates, and the correction, and the word *assignment*, and the rule at the foot of the form with a district's returnable sum in it, and the three sentences about the gate and the water entry, and then he stopped at the fifth box and read the referral, which was the part he had come for and which was the shortest thing in the book.

"*The respondent states that the fifth box has been in use for the entering of sums handed over by a district at a discount, and that the use of it does not appear to be confined to the eight years of the present practice. The respondent further states that the respondent does not know, and will not say, at what date the use began. The declining is entered. This minute refers the fifth box, and the entries in it earlier than the eighth year, to the office at Bell Stair which keeps the form, for identification of the hands in them.*"

Nobody in the corridor had said anything for about four seconds.

"That is the first time in three hundred years," said Sedge, "that a Crown March minute has recorded a kind of entry."

"Yes," said Ivo Sarn.

"Three hundred years of what."

"Three hundred years of a form with four boxes on it and a foot of the form that nobody read." He closed the book and held it against his chest, the way a man holds a thing he has decided is worth two hands. "You are all going to read this as a small thing about a form. It is not. A conviction ends in a sentence, and a sentence is a thing you can serve and be done with, and I have four of those a year and none of them has ever changed a form." He looked at the duty clerk. "A practice ends when the people doing it stop doing it, and the only people who can stop a practice are the people who are doing it. So I have written down a kind of entry, and it is in a Crown March book, and a Crown March book is a thing that offices are obliged to answer, and in about five days the form is going to be sent back to this hill with a question on it, and there is a person in this corridor who is going to have to answer it."

Nobody looked at Marek, and Marek did not look at anybody, because there was no letter beside his name and a man who says nothing in a corridor where a Crown March officer has just said *there is a person in this corridor* has not denied anything and has not been brave about it either.

---

Neris Kade asked one question.

She asked it in the ordinary way, standing up, without asking permission, and Sarn wrote it down and read it back twice, which she made him do, and which is the reason it is in the minute in the words of an examiner and not in the words of a Crown March officer.

"Does the office know whether the instrument on the Morrowfen side, the one with the counterweight in its fourth clause, has ever been read out to the body the counterweight is named against?"

"No such question has been put to the office," said Sarn.

"Then it is not known," said Neris Kade, "and I will say the rest of it myself, because if I do not say it here it goes in a lane and dies in a lane. The Elmsworth Seed Share is a cooperative of sixty-one households and its instrument is sealed and the fourth clause names the Rainmarket low ground as the district that bears the failure of the season favoured, and in nineteen months of my being its examiner I have never found one member of that cooperative who has read the instrument from end to end, and I have said so out loud once, on the ninth of this month, to a man in this building who has no office, and he is standing here, and I would like that in your book in my words and not in yours."

Sarn wrote it. He read it back twice. And then he said the thing that made the corridor go quiet the second time, and he said it to her and not to Marek.

"Examiner, you have just made a statement, in a room, in Crown March, with my book open, about a cooperative of sixty-one households and a counterweight, eleven months before either of those things is decided by anybody."

"Yes."

"I want to be fair to you and I am going to be fair to you," said Ivo Sarn. "It is a good question and it is the right question and I have taken it. And it is now in a record in Crown March in a form that will be read out in a year by a person who is not you and not me, and you cannot take it out of it, and nobody is going to thank you for it, and I would not be in your place."

"I did not do it to be thanked," said Neris Kade, "and I did it because I have been standing eleven feet away from things for six years."

She put the seal back in her bag. She did not look at Marek on the way down the stair and Marek did not follow her down it, and the thing that had happened to her in that corridor was going to happen to her about eleven months later in a room with a district's name at the top of it, and neither of them knew that yet, and she had already learned in the rains month what a kindness in a corridor can be taken back for.
