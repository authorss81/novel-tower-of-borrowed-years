# Chapter 90 — The Fourth Morning

The register was open to look for the fourth and last morning before it went live, and by the fourth bell on the twenty-ninth day of the harvest month there was a queue in the lane outside the Gate shed that went past the end of the building and out along the causeway, and the yard had run out of things to say about that by about twenty past.

There were forty-one lines in it. On the twenty-first of the harvest month there had been one, a grain factor's own book entered as its parts, and it had balanced, and the man who counted it had been relieved. In the eight days since, forty people had come and entered a line about their own paper in their own hand, which is the only thing the form allows a person to do, and the fortieth had come on the second of this week with a page torn out of a book and read it out from a doorway while about eleven people listened, and the reader wrote it down as it was said.

Forty-one lines, in forty-one hands. The reader, who was nineteen, said so out loud to the lane about six times in an hour because it was the only true thing anybody had that morning, and it was not a rule and it was not a number, it was just what a room full of people had done.

The shed is eleven feet by nine and it is built for a seed bin. The counter is two trestles and a board. At the fourth bell on the fourth morning there were about twenty-two people in it and about sixty in the lane, and the queue had sorted itself by nothing at all, and that was not an accident and it was not a decision either: there are three people who have been at the front of it since the first bell and nobody in that building, the man at the board included, could have told you which three or why, because a register with no list in it cannot put anybody anywhere, and the yard had agreed that on the fourteenth of this month in about forty minutes and had put it in writing nowhere, being a yard.

---

What a queue does to a building is that it takes the road.

The Gate yard is four yards' width of standing room and a tank, and the water comes up a standpipe at the end of Ropewalk carried by two people, and at the fourth bell a queue of sixty people was standing in the lane they came down, and one of them stood in it for four minutes with two pails and then turned round and carried them back up the lane and did not come down again that day, and nobody in the queue was asked to move and nobody in the yard was going to ask them, and that is the arrangement and it is the register's first real cost and it has not been paid yet and will be.

Somebody on the causeway said to somebody else that the register was going to make credit safe, and said it twice, and it went along the lane about forty feet before it reached somebody who had been in the seed house, and what that somebody said about it is not in this account because he said it to a woman on a causeway and she was the only person who heard it.

Marek Vale stood behind the board and read out and entered nothing.

He read the first line four times in a morning, which was the fourth time since the twenty-first, and the person at the front of the queue on the fourth morning was a woman of about sixty with a folded paper in her hand which was the counterfoil of an assignment dated the fourteenth of the second month, and she had carried it since the bad spring, and she said, twice, that she had never been told what it was.

"You may have it read out," he said. "It is your line and you may ask as many times as you like and nothing is charged. And I can read it out and I cannot give you a paper, and I want to be exact with you about why, because a man at a water office counter will tell you the same thing in the same words and you will think I copied him.

"A copy of a rate is a return. A return is the office's own document. There is no form in this kingdom on which a person may carry away a copy of a rate out of a room that is not an office, and this is not an office, and I have no office, and if I put a paper in your hand it would be a second document and nobody in any room would be permitted to produce it, and you would have a piece of paper that nobody could use in front of anybody."

The woman said that was the second time anybody had told her that and the first time she had understood it, and folded the counterfoil away, and did not go, and stood at the front of the queue for another two hours, and came back on the fifth morning when there were not supposed to be a fifth morning.

---

The trestle for the levy was out in the yard and not in the shed, because a levy is not a secret and a shed is eleven feet by nine.

It was two people of the Share at a trestle and a box, and a slate, and about forty people came over and put something in the box between the fourth bell and the sixth. It was coin, mostly, and it was seed, in the bottom of sacks, and the two collectors were Bessok Tan, who was twenty-nine and had been the secretary for four years and had a string round his wrist, and a woman of the low ground whose name is not in this account because she has not been asked for it.

There was no total, and the reason there was no total is the yard's reason and it had been given out loud in a yard in the seed month and it was repeated at the trestle about nine times in two hours: a total read in a room in six months is a number of people who agreed with something, and none of them had agreed to anything, and all of them had been asked.

A man at the trestle asked what the day had come to.

"I can tell you what you paid," said Bessok Tan, "and I cannot tell you what the day has come to, and if you ask me that in a fortnight I will give you the same answer, and the reason is not that I do not trust you with a number. It is that there is a number on the slate and a number in the slate is a total, and a total is a thing that gets carried into a room in six months by somebody who was not at this trestle, and I have been the secretary of that Share for four years and I have said the word *households* out loud in that shed about three times in my life and every one of them was a mistake I did not know I was making until a man from Bell Stair said it in a lane."

The levy was capped. A member who could not pay in money paid in kind. A member who had nothing paid nothing and was not entered.

Nobody had told that last part to the room in the yard. It was in a paragraph of a finding in a Crown March hand in Bell Stair, in a Fund secretary's own words, on the twenty-fourth of this month, and it had been on a nail in a yard ever since, and about nine people in Tallow Ward had read it.

---

The girl came at about the fifth bell, alone, and she was sixteen and she had her mother's coat and a hood she did not need.

She stood at the trestle for a while. The collectors were busy and there were four people in front of her, and one of them was a man who was arguing about seed. Then one of the collectors looked up and saw her and stopped what she was saying, because that is what people in this basin do about a girl of sixteen who is standing at a trestle on her own.

The roll was in Bessok Tan's hand, because it is always in Bessok Tan's hand, and it is a piece of oiled board with sixty-one lines on it and a line of nails at the top, and every line is a household, and her mother's line is the fourth from the end of the board, and Wenna Drel is on that board because her mother is, and has been since the third of the seed month, and had drawn seed and filled cones every day of the sowing and had never once put a hand up in that shed in her life.

"Whatever it is," said the girl, "I have got nothing, and my mother cannot do days, and the book says that is allowed, and I would like it written down that I said it here and that nobody asked me whether I minded."

Nobody asked her.

The cap was in a paragraph on a nail a hundred feet away and it was the correct cap, and it produced exactly what it was designed to produce, which is a member who has paid nothing and is not entered; and the roll in Bessok Tan's hand is a list of households and does not have a place for a girl of sixteen who is on her mother's line because her mother is, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody asks her, and the levy agreed on the twenty-fourth of this month in a hearing room in Bell Stair is a levy on a cooperative's members and not on a person, and she is not a member, and she is a person.

Bessok Tan put the board down on the trestle, which is the only time in four years he has put it down in front of a person.

"I am not going to write your name on this. Your mother is on the board and she is a member and the cap is hers and I would give it to her again this afternoon, and there is nothing on this trestle for you, and there is nothing at this trestle that can ask you anything, and I would like you to go and see the line with your mother's ground on it before the sixth bell, because it is in the shed and it is the second one in and it is the only thing I have got to give anybody today."

The girl went and stood in the shed and did not ask anybody anything, and read nothing, and stood about four feet off the counter for about a quarter of an hour with a room of strangers in it, and then went home.

Nobody followed her and nobody wrote anything down, and Teodor Lask, the reader, said afterwards that the whole of the morning went out of the room while she was standing in it and came back in about as strong as it had been, and that he had been in rooms for thirty years and had not seen that happen except once before, at a gate in the rains.

---

Ilma Tresch came at about the sixth.

She is forty-one and she drew one of the eleven trays in the fourth week of the seed month and filled cones and carried two sacks, and she did the eleven days in the growth month on ground that had already failed, and she has had a cough since the ninth of the seed month which is now in the small of her back, and a physician called Sera Holt wrote her out of four of them, and the certificate went to the clinic board and the board entered it as a cost and the district's answer to a cost is a rate.

On the third day of the harvest month a woman who keeps a seed house wrote eleven lines on a piece of sack with nothing at the head of it, and there was no form in the kingdom in which a rate on a member's ground goes, and the piece of sack went into the back of a bench book under the date and then went home with the woman who wrote it, because a sheet with nothing at the head of it does not stay in a building.

On the twenty-second of this month the Share entered it, because the keeper of a seed house may enter a line about her own paper, which is the first rule of the form and the only rule anybody has been given by anybody.

Mirren Fisk read her own four lines out in the shed in front of about forty people and did not improve one word of them, and asked that the fourth line carry *a seed does not fail because of a schedule*, in her words, because somebody will take it out of context and it will be the half that is easier to say. It is on the wall, in her words. About nine people who had come in out of curiosity went out again before she got to the fourth line.

She was the second entry in the register. There are forty-one.

The second person asked the question that gets asked at that counter, which is: is that your line, is that the person behind it, is it your words.

"Yes," said Mirren Fisk. "All three. And I want the fourth line to say that a seed does not fail because of a schedule, in my words, because somebody will take it out of context and it will be the half that is easier to say."

It is on the wall, in her words, and it has been since the twenty-second.

---

Ilma Tresch stood at the counter and had her own line read out to her, twice, because the reader reads it twice, and the second time slower, which is a thing this room does because of a girl of sixteen and a keeper in a witness room on an island and a day in the growth month.

Her ground is the sixth of the eleven. The figure is the figure. The words in the second column are the keeper's words, and the words in the fourth column are the words the household used when it was asked, at a door, in the growth month, by a pair out of a yard on a round that is over and will not be repeated.

"Yes," she said, when the reader finished. "That is right."

"That is your line?"

"That is my line."

"Is it your words in the fourth column?"

"That is my words," said Ilma Tresch, "and I said them at a door to two people of a yard who wrote them down and did not improve them, and I have been thinking about them ever since, and I have not had a single thing done about them since, and there is no roll in this city with my figure on it, and no office that holds it, and no way to produce a copy of a thing nobody has."

She looked at the board for a while.

"I came here to find out whether my own line was right," she said. "I would like it understood that that is all I came for and that I have got what I came for. It is right. The figure is the figure and the words are the words and the ground is the ground and it is all in one room in public and a person I have never met can find it and read it out for nothing.

"And I have not got the faintest idea what to do about it," said Ilma Tresch, "and I have been standing here for about ten minutes working out whether there is a person in this room I could say that to, and there is not one, because the man at this counter cannot enter anything, and the woman at the trestle is a member of the Share and not of my household, and the boy reads it out and goes home to his mother, and I would not want to say it to a stranger in a shed in front of forty people.

"So I am going to say it to the room," she said, "and then I am going to go home, and I would like nobody to stop me and nobody to thank me, because I have had a very good morning and I am not going to be a woman with a grievance in a shed."

Nobody stopped her and nobody thanked her.

Marek Vale stood behind two trestles and a board with a form on it and could not enter one word of any of it, and it was given to him to read out by a yard on the ninth of this month in the yard's own words, and recorded on the last of five paragraphs of a minute in a room in Bell Stair with no name at the foot of it, and it was the only thing in his working life in MR 413 and it was not a post.

The flat board had been under his arm since the second of the seed month and it had four ruled sheets in it and oiled paper over them, and it was a copy and not an instrument and not evidence, and it had been entered nowhere, and it was the only thing in this basin that could not be produced in any room by anybody, and he was the man who had made it.

He put it down on the trestle at the end of the morning, flat, under the form, where anybody could lift it off, and nobody in that shed ever picked it up, and he has never been able to say clearly why he did it and has stopped trying.

---

They shut the door at the seventh bell because the shed is not a warm building, and the register was not going live until the ninth month, and the fourth morning was the last morning it was open to look.

There was a queue in the lane. It went past the end of the building and out along the causeway, and there was no water at the Ropewalk standpipe below it, and a trestle with a box on it and a slate with no total on it, and a form with six ruled lines and forty-one entries in forty-one hands and a fifth line for the hours, which is empty because the hours are nobody's paper, and a sixth line that was empty because there is no box in this kingdom for the days a person loses by standing in a room.

The register kept no total, and the yard had said out loud in a yard in the seed month why, and nobody in Tallow Ward had ever once been able to find out how many people were standing in a lane outside a shed, and they were not going to be able to find out this time either, and that was the first thing the room was good at and the only one anybody had been able to pay for.

And the ninth month was almost on top of them, and a cut is not a date, and the lamp had not come off the ground, and nobody can serve a notice on a yard, and a woman in a shed on the low-ground road had said out loud on the twenty-sixth of this month that there was no number in his trade and no form in this kingdom for it, and that was the last thing anybody in the basin had learned this month and it was learned in the rain to a boy of fourteen.

And the lane filled up again at about the seventh bell when about twenty people who had been sent away by the crowd came back, and the reader put the lamp on, and the man with no office read out the first line of the first morning, four times, which was the fourth time since the twenty-first.
