# Chapter 98 — No Officer

The second cut order of MR 413 was drafted on the fifteenth of the ninth month by a man who is not an officer, in the words of an office, on a form that has not changed since before the flood years, and it took him about an hour, and it is the same wording as the order in the frame because there is one form for a cut order and the office is not permitted a second.

There are four things a cut order says, and they have not changed, and the office writes all four without being asked.

It is read, in the words of the office, to every party it names, before it is entered. It is not entered at the close of a month. It carries the day and not the month. And it carries the name of the officer who enters it.

Ostrowsky wrote all four out on a sheet and then went and looked at the frame on the wall, which has a pale rectangle in the plaster where it was turned in the rains, and under the last line of the order in the frame there is one name in a hand that got heavier on the final letter than it should have.

The last of the four is the one that stops it.

"A cut order," said Ostrowsky, to the empty copy room, at about the first bell, "carries the name of the officer who enters it. That is the office's own rule. It has been the office's own rule for longer than I have been in this room, and I have been in this room nineteen years, and I have signed nine hundred settlement countersignatures, and I have never once in nineteen years asked where a fourth clause went, and I am asking it now, at the first bell, on the fifteenth of the ninth month, because there is nobody left in this building to ask."

Nobody was left in the building to ask. That is the sentence that is true in that room on that morning, and it was true because a man of fifty-eight had walked out of it at noon on the twelfth with a coat and a finding written in a book's margin in his own hand, and because the Trust has never sent anybody to anything, and because the Fund cannot pay a sitting, and because a ward cannot be assessed a rate at all.

So the office read the order.

It went out at the second bell with the first party, and the first party is the low ground's night lamp, which is the second line from the bottom of the office's list, and a person wrote beside the line that it had been read to them, in their own hand, as the rule requires. The clinic's coal was the same row of lines and the same signature. The river gauge at the second junction is one man and a candle and a float and he asked what the order did, and was told what it did, in the words of the office, and he wrote his own hand beside the line and went back to his float.

The market's weighbridge lamps, which are what the second bell is called on and there are four of them, and the pumps at the sluice, where the pumps man said *so the sluice is above the gauge* and was told *so the sluice is above the gauge* and wrote his hand beside the line and went back to his pumps.

The Elmsworth seed house's lamp was the thirteenth line and it was the last line of the order in the frame, and on the sixth of the month Grale had walked up the low-ground road on foot and read it to a woman with her hands in the sand of a tray, and there is no form on which a business can write beside its own line, and the office has never pretended otherwise, and it was read again on the fifteenth because the rule is that it is read and not because anybody has to be told twice.

Then there was one left, and it was the last line of the office's list, and the list has fourteen lines on it, and the school is the fourteenth.

---

Pella Rusk teaches in a room off the north lane in Tallow Ward with about ninety children on the roll and fourteen places, and the fourteen and the ninety-one are the number she said out loud once in a yard at the fourth bell in the rains month and once in a room in Bell Stair, and she has said it twice, and this was going to be the occasion of neither and was the worst of the three.

Ostrowsky read the last line of the order to her at about the sixth bell in the corridor outside the schoolroom because the room is full of children at the sixth bell and it is not a room in which an office reads a thing.

The last line is the school. It is cut after every line above it. The school is last.

"I am not able to answer you," said Pella Rusk.

"You are not required to answer," said Ostrowsky. "I am required to read. I have read. And I am required to write down what you said, and I am going to write down what you said in your own words, and then I am going to read it back to you, and then I am going to leave."

"Then write this," said Pella Rusk. "They have named the school twice. Once in a finding in a room on a hill with a Chair writing it out in his own hand, and once on the last line of an order in a frame on a wall in a building I have never been inside, and neither of the two times was anybody asking me anything, and both of the two times the school was the bottom of the list.

"Write this as well, and it is the whole of what I have: a school is not a party. I know that. You know that. That is why you could read a line to me and I could not answer it, and if I answer it, it is not a declining, it is a woman in a corridor, because term three of that board lets a party decline and the declining gets written down as a declining, and there is no form in this kingdom in which a school declines. A school is a thing the ward has got. You cannot serve a notice on a yard and you cannot take a declining from a school, and I have been standing in this corridor for four minutes trying to say no to an order in the only way a person can say no to a thing that is not a party, which is by saying it to a man's face, and a man's face is not a record and it is gone at the seventh bell.

"So the school is named. The school is last. And in about a year somebody in a hall is going to open a book and find that the school was named as a party and was never asked, and they are going to write that down as a thing that was done to the school. And I want it on the sheet, in my words, that we were named and not asked, and that a school cannot say no, and that the not being able to say no is the whole of what has been done to us and it is not a figure and it is not a rate and it is not on any roll, and I have got ninety-one children and fourteen places and the number is the same as it was in the rains and I have said it out loud three times now in front of people who did not ask for it."

Marek Vale was in the corridor at the other end, and he had come up the hill to find out what a levy clerk was doing in a school corridor at the sixth bell, and he was at the other end because a man who has no standing in a building is the sort of person who stands at the other end of a corridor.

He was not asked about page three.

She did not ask him about page three, and he noticed that she had chosen not to, and it took him about four seconds to work out that it was a decision and not a courtesy, and that the decision was that she had given up on getting an answer out of a man in a corridor in exchange for getting a number into a sheet, and that she had decided which of the two was worth more to her, and that it was not the one he had.

So he said the only thing he had.

"There is a room in Tallow Ward," he said, from the other end of the corridor, to nobody in particular, "that opened eight days ago, and anybody may enter a line about their own paper in their own hand, and be read out in public, and it costs nothing, and a queue stands outside it from the second bell. And I have to tell you what it cannot do before you ask me, which is that this order is not the school's paper. It is a district's account, and a district is not the school, and the school is not a person, and so a line about it would have to be entered by the ward, and a ward is not a person either, and the yard has already found that out in ninety seconds in front of about eighty people and it is on a nail."

"Thank you," said Pella Rusk, which was the correct thing to say and was not a thing either of them wanted to hear.

"I have not done anything," said Marek.

"You have told me the truth about a room instead of about a man, which is the first time in about a year that anybody has done that to me, and I am not going to be pleasant about it, so do not wait."

She went back into the schoolroom. Ostrowsky read back what he had written and she corrected one word and did not correct anything else, and then she went and stood in the doorway and watched him walk away, and Marek Vale walked away in the other direction, and neither of them said one word to the other, and the answer she was owed in a schoolroom in the rains month was not given, and this time she had not asked for it, and that was the whole of the difference and it was hers.

---

The order was read to every party it names, in the words of the office, before it is entered.

There were seven parties and all seven had been read to, and one of the seven was a business with nobody to write beside its own line and one of the seven was a school that cannot decline, and the rule is the rule and it was satisfied completely and to the letter, and that was the last time in the history of that office that a cut order got as far as the end of the fourth thing.

It is not entered.

It is not entered at the close of a month, and the close of the month is nine days away, and that is not the reason. The reason is the fourth of the four, which is written on the sheet in the office's hand in about the same ink as the other three, and the fourth of the four is that a cut order carries the name of the officer who enters it, and the officer of this office entered his last one on the twentieth of the seed month with a hand that got heavier on the last letter of his own name, and told the woman at the last line of it that he had signed two of these in his life and that this was the second.

There is no third. There is no fourth. Not because anybody has forbidden one, and not because the water office has withdrawn it, and not because the Trust has decided anything, and not because a board of five in Bell Stair has found anything at all. Because the name at the foot of the frame is the thing that makes the order an order, and a man walked out of a building on the twelfth of the ninth month, and nobody has been sent to replace him, and there is no form on which anybody may be applied for.

A cut is not a date.

Nothing falls on a day. Nothing falls at the close of a month. A cut holds the district account to its presented figure and the lines go in the order they stand, and the lines go when somebody sends somebody, and this year nobody has sent anybody, and the fourteenth line of the list is the school, and the school is the last line of a cut order, and there are ninety-one children on the roll of a room off the north lane and about fourteen places, and a cut that has not come off the ground is not a threat, it is a thing that has not happened yet.

At about the seventh bell Ostrowsky put the sheet in a drawer in the levy office, and the frame went back on the wall over the pale rectangle, and the room was locked, and about eight days later a man who comes in at the eighth bell and goes out at the seventh noticed that the frame on the wall of the levy office on Bell Stair had one name under its last line and that there had not been a second, and that the first name was four days old, and that nobody in the building could tell him what was going to happen next, and that nobody else in the building was there to ask.
