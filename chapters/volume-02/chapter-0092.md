# Chapter 92 — Three and a Half

The answer to the referral came up the hill on the fourth of the ninth month in an envelope with a Crown March seal on it and no name on the outside, and Grale put it in the tray, and then he took it out of the tray about twenty minutes later and read it standing at the end of the copy room with the copy room going on around him, which is the way he reads anything that matters to him and has been for nineteen years.

The envelope held one sheet and one sheet held a minute, and the minute was taken on the fourteenth of the second month by an officer in Crown March with eleven years of minutes and no clerk, and the officer had read the list before he discussed it, which is the only thing about that office anybody in this basin has ever been willing to say out loud.

A man in a cell had given that office four dates on which he had entered something in this desk's account that was not a levy, not a schedule, not a posting and not a return. He had named the kind, and the kind is an assignment. He had given three of the four dates. He had not given the fourth, and the minute recorded that in the officer's own words, and the officer had not gone and found it out, and had written down that he had not gone and found it out.

And then, in the middle of the fourth line, at the point where a fourth date would have gone, there is a day and a month and no year, and then the word *the*, and then nothing.

Beneath it, the minute-taker had written that the witness stopped him there, in the middle of the word, and had then read the unfinished line back to the witness and the witness had said it was correct, and the officer had closed the minute there.

Three and a half.

Grale read the whole sheet twice. He did not take it to the frame on the wall, and he did not take it to a desk with a drawer, and he put it back in the tray for about four minutes and then took it out again and put it on the corner of his own desk face up, and it sat there for the rest of the day where anybody coming up the aisle could see it, and he did that on purpose and did not examine why.

---

Ostrowsky came in at the quarter and hung his coat on the nail he had hung it on for nineteen years and did not look at the corner of Grale's desk, because in nineteen years nobody in this room has ever looked at a corner unless they were told to, and being told is a thing that happens about once a year.

He is fifty-eight. He has signed nine hundred settlement countersignatures. He has never in his life asked where a fourth clause went, and he is not a bad man for it, and there is no sentence in this city that is more careful than the one that says so.

"The Crown March envelope," he said, at about the fifth bell, not asking.

"A referral answered."

"Answered or sent back."

"Answered."

Ostrowsky waited, which he has never once in nineteen years had the patience to do, and Grale looked at him and decided that the whole of the rest of his working life had just been spent on a decision, and that the decision was to ask a man a question he had never asked him, at the fifth bell, in a copy room, on the ninth month of a year in which both of them were about to be asked questions by offices that were worse than each other.

"Who answered it."

"A man with eleven years of minutes," said Grale. "He took it in the second month. He read the list first."

"That is Ivo Sarn," said Ostrowsky. "He has never read a list second in his life. He did it once in Crown March in the flood year and had it in his minute and was proud of it for years."

"Yes."

"And he has given you three."

"He has given me three and a half."

---

Then he asked the question, and it was the question he had wanted to ask since the rains and had refused to ask in a yard, and he asked it in the worst possible room in the city, which is the only room he has.

"Ostrowsky. When you begin a page in the settlement book, what goes at the top of it."

"The day," said Ostrowsky.

"Not the party. The day."

"The day. Then the ruling, then the kind." He said it the way a man says a thing he has done nine hundred times. "Day at the top. You do not put a party at the top of a page because a page is not a claim, a page is a page. You put the day because a page is a day. There is a reason for it and the reason is you cannot find a page again, and the day is the only thing on it that finds it for you. Nineteen years, that is the whole of the practice."

Grale said, "Thank you," which he had not said to him since the floods, and Ostrowsky looked at the floor, and then said, "What."

"Nothing," said Grale. "Go and do the quarter's postings."

---

At the sixth bell he opened the book.

The fifth box is at the foot of the settlement desk's form and it is the oldest box on the form and the only one not dated in this century, and it sits under a rule that a district may hand over a returnable sum at a discount. The form is four boxes and a fifth at the foot. For eight years an assignment has gone into it, lawfully, because a rate arrears is a returnable sum and the fifth box is the box for a returnable sum. That is not a trick. That is the correct box, and there was no box for an assignment, and the form is older than the practice.

The question put to Crown March was narrower than that. The question was: whose hands are in the entries in that box from before the eight-year practice began.

There are not many pages. That is the thing nobody who has not been in the book knows and everybody who has been in the book knows. In this basin the fifth box has been in use for eight years and has taken an assignment perhaps twice a season, and before that it went in and out of use for a century in whatever office kept the district's returns, and there are eleven pages in it older than the eight years, and Grale knew that and had known it for longer than the practice of putting anything in it has existed, and he had said so in a yard in the rains in a sentence about a line standing since before the flood years and had not repeated it since.

He put a lamp on the desk because the copy room shuts at the sixth and the lamps on the north wall come on by degrees, and he went through the eleven pages, and he did it the way he does everything, once down and then once from the other end.

On the first three of the four that mattered, the day at the top of the page was one of the three dates in the minute.

Not a day in the middle. Not a day an entry was made. The day at the top of the page, in his own hand, in the practice of a man who has written the day at the top of a page since before you were at this desk.

He sat back.

He had not proved anything. What he had found was smaller and worse than a proof: that the three dates a man gave in a cell in Crown March were the dates on three pages, and the pages were begun, and the entries on them had no date of their own at all.

An entry with no date is not a claim that has been dealt with and is not a claim that is owed. It is a line standing in a box, and a line standing in a box is entered once a year and is never applied, because there is no day on it on which anybody could apply it, and a sum that cannot be applied against a thing is not money and not a debt. It is a line of type.

The fourth of the four had a day at the top of it too, and the day at the top of that page was not in the minute, and it is the only one of the four that nobody has ever asked about, and it is the only one of the four that Grale wrote.

---

He could have finished it in four hours. He has said since, to two people and neither of them in a room, that it was four hours' work and that he did the arithmetic of it standing up and that the arithmetic was four hours of work.

It was: take the eleven older pages, and find how many of them have a day at the top and an entry with no date. Then take the four that Hesper named and see whether they are the same eleven. Then write down, in one line, that the standing carry has been carried every year since before the flood years by an entry in a box for a returnable sum that names no party and bears no date and has therefore never been applied and never could be, and that the hand in it is his.

And then stop, because the day he proves the line in the account desk he has to stop.

He put the lamp back on its hook and put the four pages square again at the top of the pile, in order, with the days showing, which is how they were before he touched them, and he shut the book, and he wrote the four sentences he had to write, standing, in about a minute and a half.

*That the box named is the fifth box at the foot of this office's form and is used for a returnable sum at a discount. That the box has been in use longer than the eight-year practice. That of the four entries named in the minute, three stand on pages begun on the three days given, the day being written at the head of the page in this office's practice and not being the date of an entry. That the fourth page was begun by the officer of this office and I will not certify what is on it.*

He read it back. It was the truest thing he has ever written and it is four sentences about a form and about himself, and it is the first thing in nineteen years he has put in an official record about his own hand, and there is no way to take the fourth sentence back out of it now that it has gone.

He addressed it to Ivo Sarn, who is in the coastal returns and is not the officer who wrote the minute, and he sealed it, and it went down the hill at the seventh bell with a boy, and the boy did not know what it was.

---

He stood at the frame for a while after that, the way he has done four times in nineteen years and never in front of anybody, and the frame is the only instruction in this city that a person cannot be given a copy of, and there is one name under the last line of the order in it, and it is the first cut of the year, and it is his, and the last letter of it came out at a different angle from the other five and about a third heavier and nobody saw it.

The referral is about the fifth box. The order in the frame is about the district account. They are the same book.

He had known that since the second month and had said it to nobody and had not once let the two of them be in his head at the same time on purpose, and on the fourth of the ninth month at the seventh bell, in an empty copy room, with one lamp still on, he let them be in his head at the same time on purpose, and what he got for it was a feeling he had not had since he was thirty-five, and the feeling was not satisfaction.

The condition was never *stop when you can prove it.* The condition was *stop when you prove it.* He had been keeping it for twenty-three years by not looking, and not looking had cost him nothing at all, because a thing you do not look at is not a thing you have proved, and a man who has not proved a thing can go on doing a job in the ordinary course. And the ninth month had put a sheet on his desk that wanted a cut, and the cut wanted the line, and the line was in the fifth box, and the only way to be the officer who did not do a cut in the ninth month was to be an officer who had not looked.

He had stopped himself, on purpose, from the only thing that would have let him go on.

And the price of it, which he could put a number on and which is the reason he went home at the seventh bell and did not open the shutter on the Gate tank for another three days: the ninth month's demand cannot be answered by anybody, the ward is going to be charged a thing nobody in the ward was asked about, and the two charges are going to land in the same week, and the man who let them land is a man of fifty-eight who wanted to stop and has found a rule that lets him stay and call it duty.
