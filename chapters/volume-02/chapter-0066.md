# Chapter 66 — One Sentence

Neris Kade wrote the request on the fifteenth and it went to two offices, and it was the first piece of paper she had sent anywhere in six years that had her seal on it, and she told Marek about it on the causeway on the way down and told him the shape of it and not the wording, which is the correct discipline and one she had learned by having watched other people not do it.

The first office was the house over the chandler's shop, and the answer came back the next day, by hand, in an envelope with a licence number on it, and inside the envelope there was one page and the page was one line.

*Received: 16th day of the seed month, MR 413. One instrument. The Sable Compact, house of Morrowfen. Schedule and counterweight at the foot of the seller's paper, per licence.*

And underneath, in a different hand, which was not a clerk's and was not an officer's, four sentences.

"I am told you want the parts. There are no parts on our side, because the instrument is entered as one line, and I entered it as one line because the licence is a licence to buy claims and to sell them, and one claim is one line, and if I put fifty-two lines in a register where a buyer comes to see whether his own claim is sound, I have built a book that a man can be shown his own name in, and you cannot unbuild that in a year. If you want a claim to sound, show me the party. That is the whole service and it is free. I will read you a rate in this city as many times as you like, in this house, in a chair, at any hour, and I will read it to you in the words of the roll and not in mine, and I will not charge you for it and I would rather you did not thank me. Examiner, I have been doing this for nine years and I have watched four offices try to make me give up a schedule and I have never once been able to explain the reason to one of them, and you are the first person who has written to me as though the reason were worth saying, and here it is: a schedule is a list of people, and a register is a place people come to look for themselves, and a person who finds themselves in a register has been in it, and a person who is not in it has not, and I am not going to build the second thing in order to sell the first."

---

The second office was the district, and the district refused, and the refusal is the whole of the ninth month and it is one sentence long.

It came on the nineteenth, at about the fourth bell, in the district accountant's own hand, which is a small upright hand that has written the same six words a hundred thousand times.

*Asked for: the schedule of returnable sums received into the Rainmarket low ground district account between the second of the second month and the twelfth of the current month, with the names of the parties from whom they were received. Answered: none held. A district's roll shows what is owed to the district; a sum handed over is no longer owed to the district; and there is therefore nothing on the roll for the district to show.*

Neris Kade read it standing up in the lane outside the water office and then read it a second time, which is not what other people do with a refusal.

"They have answered the question I did not ask," she said.

"You asked for a schedule and there is no schedule, because a schedule is the seller's and the seller is a cooperative and fifty-two households, and not one of those fifty-two knows the other fifty-one's rate." Marek was looking at the paper over her arm, not touching it. "You asked the seller for the buyer's paper, and the seller has it, and the seller is a low ground cooperative of sixty-one households that reads its roll out at every meeting and does not have a copy of anything else."

"That is not what I asked either."

"It is what came back."

"It came back with a rule," said Neris Kade, "and a rule is the one thing you cannot appeal, and I want you to understand what has just happened to me, because it is going to happen to me about four more times before the harvest and I am not going to be able to do anything about any of them." She handed him the paper. "Look at the bottom. He has written a line under the answer, and the line is not part of the refusal, and I want you to read it properly."

The line said:

*The entry is shown in the return, and the return is exhibited on application, in one day, under seal, to a person who is a party to the account or an examiner for the season works. It shows the total received, the two dates of receipt, and the receiving party. I am sorry that it does not show your names. I have had this request eleven times in four years and eleven times for the total.*

"One word," said Marek.

"One word."

"You asked for the *names*. He offered you the *sum* in the same breath and he offered it in one day under seal, and he has offered it eleven times, and you did not ask for it, and I have been in this business long enough to know that I would have asked for the sum in the morning and been pleased with myself by the afternoon." He gave the paper back. "I am not saying that to be hard. I am saying it because in the ninth month somebody is going to stand in a room and ask what anybody asked this office for between the second of the second month and the twelfth, and the answer is going to be *a schedule with the names of the parties*, and there is going to be a seal on it, and it is going to be the right question for a man who wants a thief caught, and it is going to be the wrong question for a woman who wants to know whose rate it is."

"And which do you want."

"I want the sum, and I want the two dates, and I want the receiving party in my own hand on a piece of paper that I cannot enter anywhere," said Marek Vale, "and it will be worth exactly nothing, and it is more than I had on the twelfth of this month."

---

Then he went and read a rate.

That is not a metaphor and there was no difficulty about it. A district roll is a public record. It hangs on a rail behind a counter with a string on it and anybody in the city may stand at the counter and turn the string and read a ratepayer's own rate for any period in the roll, and the office does it four times a day and does not enjoy it.

Berta Lund was at the counter, and she was forty-four and had been at that counter a long time, and she did not stop Marek, and she did not help him either, and when he asked her for a copy she said the sentence that is the reason a register in this city is going to be necessary and not merely a good idea.

"You can read it as long as you like, sir. I cannot give you a copy of it, and I could not give you a copy of it if you were a clerk, unless you had the counter-clerk's letter, and there is one letter in this office and it is mine and it goes to the parties."

"Why not."

"Because a copy of a rate is a return," said Berta Lund, "and a return is an office's own document, and an office's own document is issued by the office, and I have issued about nine thousand of them in this city and not one of them has ever gone to a man who was not a party. Read it. Take it in. If you write it out on that paper, that paper is a piece of paper in your coat and it is worth nothing at all and it will be worth nothing at all in nine months, and if you are thinking of taking it somewhere to read it out, then somebody with a door and a seal is going to ask you where you got it, and you are going to have to say *I stood at a counter and asked*, and there is no form for *I stood at a counter and asked*."

"I know there is no form for it," said Marek. "I have been not having a form for five weeks and it has not been a novelty yet."

"No," said the woman at the counter. "It stops being a novelty when it costs you something. You will notice it has not cost you anything yet and you should not trust that."

---

What he had read was the low ground's rate for the third quarter, and it was larger than he had expected, and it was larger in a way that had a component in it that was not water, and the component was entered on the same line in the same column, because there is no box on the roll for a member's standing under a cooperative's own rule at the quarter, and the roll is a roll.

He did not copy it out. He stood at the counter with his hand flat on the page for about as long as it took him to be sure of it, and then he took his hand off, and he thanked the woman, and he went out into the lane.

The paper was in Rainmarket. The paper was one line at three hundred, with a stated rate and a counterweight at the foot of the seller's copy, which the buyer was entitled to read on the day he bought it and had not asked about since, and it carried the counterseal of a Saltglass harbor notary on it, and that seal was the reason four houses that will not buy Morrowfen paper would take it, and it was the reason an audit in this city could go nowhere at all.

He looked at it for a long time, on a trestle in a lane, in the rain, and the counter-clerk's sentence and Merrow's four sentences and the district accountant's one sentence all sat in his head at once and arranged themselves into a shape without any help from him, which is the worst kind.

A mark in the Tide Reckoning names a month, a tide phase, a harbor seal and a translator. Translating it into a Morrowfen year is a harbor notary's work, and there is no harbor notary in Morrowfen, and the office that would have one is not this office and does not answer to it, and no clerk in Bell Stair may read a Tide Reckoning mark or check one or say whether one is what it claims to be. So the audit goes as far as the seal and stops there, and the seal is the entire reason the paper is portable.

Nobody in this city could check the one mark on it. That was not a hole somebody had made in a wall. It was a wall, and it was three hundred years old, and it was the difference between a lender in Rainmarket and a lender at a coast, and Merrow had not built it and could not take it down and did not need to, because the market for what he was selling did not require the paper to be checked. It required it to be *accepted*, and a counterseal from the coast is what acceptance looks like, and a ratepayer in the low ground who is named in a schedule fifty-two lines long has no way of knowing and no office to ask.

He had come down to the lane to be angry at somebody and the somebody had told him the truth in three letters and a day.

The lane emptied around five. Over on the low-ground road, in front of the seed house, there were eleven trays with a mark on the end of them and a woman of thirty-four turning a bench twice a day, and the frost was coming in the last week of the month, and a man with no office stood in the wet with a rolled list of twenty-two files and a card in his coat he had not used, and had read a rate and taken nothing away, and that was the entire result of the nineteenth of the seed month and it was not nothing.
