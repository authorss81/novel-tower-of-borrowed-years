# Chapter 64 — In the Yard's Own Words

The letter came to the Gate yard on the seventeenth in a hand that was not a clerk's and was not a lawyer's either, and it was dated the fourteenth, which meant it had been written before Marek had been on the carriers' row, and the yard made a point of that at the tank within ten minutes and was right to.

It offered the ward about nine hundred and forty, as a write-off, that day, with no discount and no rate, and it asked one thing in return, and the one thing was on the second line of the letter and was not hidden: that the ward would take on, as a levy, a rate arrears of forty-three and some months against a licensed carrier at the Tallow Gate, together with the low ground's uncollectable rate for the flood quarters, in two parts, on two dates.

The carrier's name was not in it. There was a box for the district and a box for the officer who signs for the district, and the second box was the reason the offer could not be taken up by anybody, and the yard spent the first ten minutes on that and the yard was wrong to.

---

Ferrow came herself, and she came in a working coat and not a good one, and she set a ledger on the trestle and did not open it.

"I am going to say three things and then I am going to sit down and let the yard do whatever the yard does," she said, "and the first two are numbers and the third is not a number and you are going to want it more than the numbers, and that is how I make my living."

She put a finger on the first line.

"Nine hundred and forty is the sum of two uncollectable rates and one ratepayer's arrears, and the ratepayer has already said he will pay his own, so the offer is really about nine hundred, and the nine hundred is a good offer. It is the flood years' assessments on the low ground, and there is no household on the low ground who will ever pay them, and that is not because those households are wicked, it is because the years they were assessed for are years that did not happen for them, and a rate for a year that did not happen is not collectable at any price. The Sable Compact's house would like to clear them. That is what the house is for. It has cleared nine hundred of a dead district's rate this month and it will clear nine hundred more in the growth month and nobody will ever thank us and we do not expect it."

"And the second thing," said Aldis Kern, from the step of the tank.

"The second thing is that the offer needs one signature from a person who can sign for a district, and there is one on this page, and you are not going to be able to fill it, and I did not come here to have you fill it. I came here because a person who cannot fill a box is a person who has somebody else in the room."

"There is nobody else in this room," said Kern.

"I know," said Ilse Ferrow. "That is why I have come and that is why I am going to say the third thing in a yard with two hundred people in it rather than in a lane, because a lane is where this sentence goes to be repeated by people who did not hear me say it and get it slightly wrong in the direction of the person who repeats it."

---

The yard went quiet in the way that two hundred people who are actually listening go quiet, and it is a different quiet from four hundred people who are waiting.

"Take the low ground's fourth clause," said Ferrow. "You have not read it. Forty hands read it out in the seed house in the autumn and twenty-one of the sixty-one were not in the room, and the name of the thing in it is the Rainmarket low ground, and the sentence is that if the sowing takes, the district named bears the failure of the season favoured." She let it sit. "That district is not a person. There is no form on which a district is asked. And the offer on this table is the fourth clause coming due at the bottom of eight steps, and the eight steps end in a shed on a carrier's row, and if this yard signs this paper then Tallow Ward is the district, and a ward is the only account in this basin that can take money it is not owed without anybody's name going anywhere, and I have come to this yard to tell you that myself, at the top of my voice, in a yard, because nobody else in this city is going to and because I would like there to be one honest record of when you were told."

She shut the ledger she had not opened.

"That is the third thing," she said. "The nine hundred is real and the nine hundred is yours and the third thing is also yours, and I have never in my life been able to sell both halves of a thing to the same person, and this week I have."

---

The yard refused it in nine minutes.

It refused it in the yard's own words and not in Ferrow's and not in anybody's, and the refusal was written on a second-round sheet by two people of the yard with a piece of charcoal, and it was read out twice before the trestle was cleared, once by the keeper and once by the reader, and the reader was nineteen years old and had carried pails in the second week of the rains and had asked the yard a question at the tank the day before that had changed what the round was for.

Teodor Lask read it, and it went like this.

"We are asked to take on a rate of forty-three and some months against a licensed carrier at the Tallow Gate, and about nine hundred of the low ground's flood years' rate, so that the sum stops walking and comes to rest here. We will not.

"There is a licensed carrier at this Gate with forty-three and some months against his yard, and we have not spoken to him, and we are not going to be the second office in this city to ask a man for money, and we are not going to take a thing off him because a man in a good coat has offered to pay it for him. A levy is a thing a ward raises on itself. We have one. It is not drawn. We are not raising a second one to clear a dead district's rate, and if we did it would be the same levy with another name on it and the name would be the only difference.

"Here is the other reason and it is the one we would like written down properly. The carrier's yard is in this ward. If a carrier in this ward is paying a stranger's rate then his plate on his door is not a plate any more, it is a sign that says this yard takes money, and we are four hundred and six people and three hundred and seventy-seven of us have never been asked a question by anybody, and we are not going to start by being the yard that says yes to things out loud.

"We are not saying this yard cannot be reached. We are saying we can see it, and we are writing it down in our own words on our own sheet, and it goes on the nail, and we are not going to be asked about it again."

---

The yard said one more thing and the one more thing was the reason the whole round had been worth doing.

"The four hundred and six of us have not all been asked," said Sanne Droll, standing at the trestle with her sheet in her hand. "There is a man from Bell Stair in this yard who has not been asked either, and he has been here five weeks, and I want to say that the reason he was not asked is that we cannot ask him, and that is not a complaint about him. It is the first thing in five weeks that anybody from a hall has been in this yard and not been able to be written on a sheet, and I would like it noted that this refusal is ours and that there is no man's hand on it anywhere."

Marek stood at the end of the yard for all nine minutes and did not say one word, and there was no part of him that wanted to. He had been in Rainmarket for five weeks and he had learned in five weeks that a ward's own answers could not be produced if a man from a hall had a hand on them, and what he was watching was a sheet with two hands on it from the yard, in the yard's words, in a yard, being read out twice by a boy of nineteen who had not been asked a question by anybody until the fifth of the rains month.

He could have stopped it. That was the thing he went over on the causeway that afternoon, and he went over it four or five times and it came out the same every time. If he had stood up at the third minute and explained what the eight steps were, and read the schedule aloud, and named the fifth box, and put the chain in a yard the way he had wanted to put it in a yard since the eleventh — a young man from Bell Stair with no office and no form would have taken a ward's decision away from a yard in the rain, and the ward would have taken it because he had been useful, and the sheet would have had his hand on it, and in the ninth month the office would have come to this tank with a form and a district's name at the top of it and asked this yard to show what it had decided, and the yard would have shown them a sheet that a man from Bell Stair had made them refuse.

Nobody came back to it. Ferrow took her unopened ledger off the trestle and put it under her arm and said, "That is fair, and I am not going to argue with it, and I have got a hundred and eleven other offers to make this week and I shall be back on this road in a week, and I shall not come to the tank again, because I have been told where the tank is and I do not need it."

At the fourth bell on the twentieth the refusal went on the nail with the nineteen working sheets and the strings, and about sixty people stood in the wet to read it, and it was the first thing this ward had ever published that nobody in a hall had written any part of, and it is still on that nail, and it is still in those words, and no office has come to that tank about it.

Marek did not read it twice. He read it once and then he stood back and let four other people read it, which is what a man does at a nail when he knows the yard has decided that a man from Bell Stair does not get a second reading.

On the day the refusal went on the nail, a woman from the second pair told Sanne Droll on the Ropewalk side that her household had been to the trestle on the low-ground road the day before, which was the day after the yard said no, and had signed for the nine hundred, and had done it in a lane and not in the yard, and had not told the pair, because a pair is not the yard and the yard had said no and nobody in that household had been asked by the yard about anything in her life.

Droll wrote it down in the woman's words on the back of a sheet, which is the only place it exists, and then she did a thing the yard argued about for two days and did not resolve, which was to ask the woman once whether the yard's saying no had had anything to do with it.

The woman said it had not. She said she had signed because her daughter needed shoes and the yard's no had had nothing to do with it, and she said it without any anger at anybody at all, and Droll wrote that down too, in her words, and the yard did not send anybody to the low-ground road and did not write to anybody.

A yard's refusal binds the yard. It has never in the history of this city bound a household, there is no form on which it could, and the yard had said so in its own words on the seventeenth, and it made no difference whatever.

He walked up the causeway in the rain at the sixth bell with nothing in his coat except a folded list of twenty-two files and a pencil line on the back of it, and the flat board with the four ruled sheets was under his arm, and it was still going to be under his arm that night and the next night and the night after that, because a man with no office and no work and a bed above a lockkeeper's shop cannot put a flat board anywhere a lock exists.
