# Chapter 63 — The Same Four Questions

The yard met at the Gate tank at the first bell on the sixteenth with about two hundred people in it, and the tank is in Tallow Ward and the low ground is in Rainmarket, and between the two of them is a causeway and a bridge, and the seed is in the ground on the far side of the water and so is the ninth month.

The nineteen working sheets from the first round were still on the nail with their strings on, where they had been since the eighth of the rains month, and about forty people had lifted one off that nail and read it in the last six weeks, which was the entire point of a nail.

Marek stood at the end of the yard with no coat on, in his brother's second pair of boots, which were a size too big and had been since the second of the month, and did not offer to help, and was not asked, and did not expect to be, and had told Lio on the ninth of the month that he did not know whether the yard would go back out and had found out in about two hundred people's hearing, which was not the way he had wanted to find out.

Nobody in the yard was unkind to him. That has to be said, because the thing that happened next to him was not a humiliation and it is going to be written down in a state file in about forty years by a person who did not watch it, and they will get it wrong.

---

The argument was about the question.

Four of them, from the first round, on two long sheets that had come off a complaint sheet off a trestle table five weeks ago and that the yard had been careful not to improve on since, and the second sheets on the sixteenth were copied out by two children from the first with a charcoal between them and were not permitted one word of difference.

That took eleven minutes and produced two sentences that are now the rule of the second round and will outlive every one of the people in the yard.

*Ask the same four questions. Write the answer in the words it is given in. Where the answer is not the answer of the first round, write the first answer underneath it, in its own words, and write the date.*

"No totals," said Sanne Droll, a rope-maker of thirty-nine who had been one of the nineteen in the first round and had kept her sheet, which was on the nail, with its string. "If there is a total on this, somebody is going to read it in a room in six months as a number of people who agreed with something, and it will not be that, and we will not be here."

"There is a total on the roll at the tank," said somebody at the back.

"That is four columns and two hundred names and it is the ward's water and it is a different thing and it is not ours to add to."

Then Teodor Lask, who was nineteen and had carried pails in the second week of the rains and had been asked nothing since, put his hand up and asked the question that took the yard half an hour and is the reason the round was shortened.

"If we go round again in a month, and again in a month after that, is that not a series?"

Milan Vray, who was the yard-man and who had eaten at Vasil Ferun's table for six weeks, said: "Say what you mean by a series, Teodor, because if you mean a *schedule* then you have just said the only thing anybody in this yard has had to say this month, and I would rather you said it before I did. I will say it for you and then you can agree with me or not, and it is four sentences, and the first one is: a series of people is not a series. A series of asks, on a sheet, at a stated interval, on a named set of households, is a schedule. A schedule is the third box on the settlement desk's form, and the third box is the oldest working box the Chamber has, and the four kinds that go in the four boxes are a levy, a schedule, a posting and a return. And there is a man in a cell in Crown March who has spent a fortnight working out that a fifth thing is going into a public account in this city, and it is going into the box for a return, which is the correct box, and there is nobody left to look after the third."

---

"A round that repeats is a schedule," said Vray, "and a schedule is a thing the office sends round, and a thing the office sends round is a thing the office can send round again without asking, and in a year somebody in Bell Stair is going to be able to say *we have this house on a schedule* and nobody is going to be able to say we did not agree to it, because we agreed to the first one and we did not read the second one, and that is exactly how this yard got its name on a roll in the first place."

"So we do not go round again."

"We go round again exactly once," said Aldis Kern, from the step of the tank, "and we go round because I said we would, in a yard, five weeks ago, and a yard does not get to be quiet about a thing it has said out loud. And we go round once, and then we stop, and the stopping is not a third round, and I am not going to be asked again by anybody about this ward's doors in a year, and if I am, the answer is that there were two rounds and there were two rounds because we said there would be, and here is the nail."

"Put the number on the board," said Droll. "Two."

"Put two on the board," said Kern, "and put nothing under it, because there is nothing under it, and a number with nothing under it is the only kind that has never lied to anybody in this city."

---

The two hundred people in the yard broke into nineteen pairs in about four minutes, the way water goes into nineteen channels, and the pairs went out.

Three hundred and seventy-seven of four hundred and six had never been asked a question by anybody, and those were the doors, and the nineteen pairs had four days and would come back to the tank on the twentieth.

Marek watched them go. Nineteen pairs up nineteen lanes, in rain that had not started yet and did start at about the third bell, and a keeper's sheet and a reader's sheet in each pair and a piece of charcoal apiece and a bit of string, and not one sheet going into a third person's house.

Then Sanne Droll came back down the yard, which she had no reason to do, and stopped four feet off him and looked at him for about as long as it takes to count to nine, and the yard saw it happen, which was the part that made it permanent.

"Mr Vale. I have got a rope to sell on the twentieth and I am not going to be here to sell it, and I have been asked a question by four people in five weeks and one of them was you."

"You have my word that I have not got anything of yours and never will have."

"I know you have not." She did not soften it and she was not unfriendly. "I want you to hear the rest of it in the yard instead of in a lane later, because a lane is where a thing like this gets its shape changed on the way to somebody who matters. There are two hundred people here and about forty of them heard what I am going to say and they will say it the same way, and that is the only defence I have got, and it is the same defence the yard had in the rains when it put the Underbank at the top of the sheet."

"I am listening."

"You have read four figures out loud in this yard in five weeks," said Sanne Droll. "The four that do not close. You read them because somebody asked what our roll meant and you had them in your head, and you gave them to us, and we have told our children the numbers, and the yard thinks you were right to and I think you were right to. And you are a man from Bell Stair with no office who knows what four hundred and six people owe before they have been asked, and if in the ninth month a piece of paper comes up this road with a district's name at the top of it, there is going to be somebody in the city who says *ask the man in the yard, he had it first*."

"I have never had it first. I have read it off a wall you had already been given."

"I did not say you had it wrong," said Droll. "I said you were the reason we have to be careful." She shifted her weight and put her hand on the string round her wrist, which is what a rope-maker does with a hand that has nothing else to hold. "You are useful. You have been useful to me and to about nine people in this yard that I could name in front of and I am not going to name them in a yard. And in about four days the usefulness is going to be the thing that gets asked about, and it will not be anybody's fault, and there will not be a villain, and we will all be very reasonable about it, and the yard will be the one that cannot produce its own answers, because a yard's own answers cannot be produced when one of the hands on them is a man from a hall who is not on the roll and is not paid out of the district account and is not living in the yard."

She was gone before he had worked out a sentence.

And that was the Tuesday, and it arrived at the tank in front of two hundred people who had not asked for it, and it was not malicious, and it was not addressed to him, and it had been five weeks in the making, and it cost a rope-maker a day's sale of rope that she did not make on the last day of the round because she was not in the yard.

---

What it cost the people who answered is a thing nobody put on a sheet, and it is the reason the yard argues about the fourth question every year.

The first round took three days and the second took four, and the four days are four days of a wage, in a ward where the wage is coming out of a line of forty thousand nine hundred and where a foreman's day at the fuel basin is a day of food for four people. Nineteen pairs, four days, and there are about two hundred and sixty households that have somebody who can be spared and about a hundred and forty that have not.

The woman in the second pair on the Ropewalk side lost a day and a half out of it and did not say so, and the yard found out in the fourth week and wrote it on the back of a sheet in her words, which is the only place it exists.

The man in the fourth pair at the Tanner's yard was taken off the vat on the second day of the round and put back on the third and came off again on the fourth, and the twelve days of lost work are in the yard's memory and not on any roll, because there is no roll at the Tanner's yard for being asked a question.

And on the fourth day of the second round, a household at the back of the Tanner's yard said no.

They had said yes five weeks ago, in one sentence, without heat, and they had said *take the work and leave the days alone*, and the yard had written that down in their words and had drawn a line through the tally at their door in charcoal and had told the tank that the line meant *asked once and the answer stands.*

The second time, at about the fourth bell on the nineteenth day, the reader said their name and the keeper put the charcoal on the board, and the woman in the doorway said, before either of them had asked anything, "We said no to the days and we have thought about it for five weeks and now we say no to the work as well, and you are not to ask us why either time, and that is twice and you may not come a third time."

Nobody asked her why. Kern's rule is that nobody is asked why they are refusing, twice, and the yard has argued about whether the word *twice* means the second refusal or the second asking, and it has never settled it, and on the twentieth of this month it did not need to be settled, because the sheet said *no, given at the fourth bell, and no reason asked, and no reason to be given*, and that was the end of it.

The tally at their door was not drawn through. Kern came out at the sixth and drew a second line under the first and the yard did not know what the second line meant and voted at the tank, in four minutes and against advice, to leave it unexplained, on the grounds that a yard that invents a mark for something it cannot ask about will be doing in a year what it has spent a month refusing to do.
