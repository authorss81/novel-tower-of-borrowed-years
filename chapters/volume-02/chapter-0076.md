# Chapter 76 — Printed in Crown March

Marta Brel was forty-six and had a rate of her own she could read at the counter and had read twice, and on the twelfth of the growth month she stood in the lane off the low-ground road and asked Marek Vale to come and read a piece of paper with her in her own kitchen, and she gave her reason before he could say yes, which was that she could not read it and had never tried, because the man at the window had read it out to her and she had signed where he put his finger.

He said yes. That is the third time in eleven days and he had stopped keeping track of the number, and the thing he was learning, and could not say to anybody, was that there was a kind of usefulness which arrives at your door in the shape of a favour and which is not the Tuesday, because the Tuesday is the day somebody takes it off you, and nothing has been taken off him yet, and the road is downhill in both directions.

---

The kitchen was warm and it was small and there was a chair with a cushion in it that she gave him and took back, and she put the paper on the table between the bread and the salt and turned it round so that it faced him, and she did that in a way that made it clear she had been practising which way round it went.

"It is one sheet," she said. "That is what I want. I want to know that it is one sheet and I want somebody to read it to me and I am not going to write anything down, and I would like it understood that I have not written anything down, because I have heard that a thing in a house dies with the house, and it will, and I have a woman coming on Thursday to mend a roof and no use talking to her about water."

It was one sheet. The Trust's seasonal advance form, wet-season and spring, one sheet, four boxes and a fifth at the foot, and it was very well made, and every part of it was filled in her husband's hand except the parts the counter filled in, and the witnesses were two men who worked the weighbridge at the market and had put a mark beside their names in the office, and the settlement said eleven days of her husband's labour out of the harvest at the ordinary wage and a third of the seed the household kept back, and the scope said the low ground and a stated holding on it, and the need said the seed for eleven quarters.

And in the fourth box, in the middle of a printed form, in type, with the words of the trade in them, was this.

*The district named in the schedule bears the failure of the season favoured.*

He read it twice and put his finger on it and did not say anything for about ten seconds.

"Now you have found it," said Marta Brel, "and I have watched your face do about four things and I would like you to do the fourth one out loud, because you have been in my lane about nine times in six weeks and I have got a fairly good idea of what your silences are for."

"Your window shut on the sixth," said Marek. "You were in the queue. You lost a fortnight."

"I lost a fortnight," she said, "and I have not made a fuss about it, and my husband's half of a seed order went into a neighbour's ground at a price that was not a price. And then the assessments were restated on the eighteenth and the window came open on the twentieth and I took an advance off this man on the twenty-first, and I want you to notice the shape of that before you notice anything else. The rule that shut the window was correct. The rule that opened it was correct. My fortnight is gone and it is never coming back, and it cost me the season, and out of the season came this sheet, and the sheet is lawful, and I would like to know what is on it and I would like to know it today."

---

Here is what was on it, and it is the whole of this chapter.

Clause four of a cooperative's seed instrument, sealed in the bad autumn, names a district as the counterweight and was not the Compact's invention, and nobody has ever found a defect in it, and it is eighteen months old.

Clause four of the Trust's own printed seasonal advance form says the same thing in the same words, in type, in the middle of the form, where every advance taken on it for nineteen years has had it.

It is not a copy. A cooperative's clause is drafted by whoever drafts a cooperative's clause. This is set by a printer.

"The wording is not even close to the wording," said Marek. "That is what I have been looking for since the fourth of the month. A copy of one would be a wrong word somewhere and a man could show you the wrong word. This is not a copy. It is stock. Somebody in a shop in Crown March had it in his hand as a line of type and set it, and he was not thinking about it, and it is on a form that is used in every season office in the kingdom, and it is the fourth clause, in the fourth box, in the place clause four goes."

"You have said *in the fourth box* twice as though it were in the wrong place."

"I have said it because it is in the right place, and I have said it twice because for six weeks I have been the only person in this basin who could read a counterweight out of a paper and I have been extremely proud of it, and there has been a form in a drawer in a hall in Bell Stair with four boxes on it the whole time."

He put it flat and smoothed it with the side of his hand, which is not a thing you do to somebody's own paper, and she let him.

"Read me the rest of it," she said. "And read the second and third clauses as well, and I want to know what a witness is meant to be doing there."

So he read it. The need, the pledge, the scope, the two witnesses and what they had signed, and the settlement, and he read the fifth box at the foot, which was a return and was the right box, and he read the assignor's column, which was on the form and was the only heading on it that was not old, and he read the counter-stamp, and the date, and the number.

"It is a good sheet," he said. "It is better made than most. There is no lie in it, there is nothing missing out of it, and every box is the right box."

"Then I have signed a thing that puts my ground on somebody else's bad year and I have been told about it, and I could not tell you that if I had asked anybody, could I."

"You could have read it yourself. You had a right to read it. It is your paper."

"I am forty-six years old and I have a household and a debt and I have never once in my life asked a man at a counter to give me a thing to read, and the reason is that when I have asked for a thing to read I have been made to feel like a person who wants something, and I would rather be a person who has paid." She said it without heat, and then she said, "And the reason you have come up this lane nine times in six weeks is that you cannot read either, and I want that said in this kitchen, because I have been watching you come up this lane and I would like you to know that I have noticed it and I have not mentioned it and I am mentioning it now."

---

They walked down to the office at the low ground at the third bell, and Rustam Vey was at his window with the board out and the queue of four behind the two clerks, and he read the sheet in about eleven seconds standing in his own doorway with his hat off, and he was not surprised, and that was the thing Marek had been wrong about in his own prediction.

"How long has that clause been on that form," said Marek.

"Nineteen years that it has been on every one of them and I have certified about four hundred of them," said Rustam Vey. "I could not tell you the date it was first printed. It has the form number in the corner and a run number under it and a run number on a hand press tells you how many were pulled, not how many were used, and the number in front of me is eleven hundred and something and there is no office in this kingdom that knows what was done with eleven hundred copies of a form."

"You have read the fourth clause."

"I have certified the fourth clause," said Vey, "which is not the same, and I will tell you the difference, because you are a clerk and you will understand it. To certify a clause is to say that all the parts are present and that the parties went before a witness and that the thing may stand. Nobody asks the officer to certify that a clause is a good one. And I have been in the post nineteen years and I have never in my life drafted an instrument, and there are nine people in the Crown March road who draft instruments and I have met four of them and I would not tell you which of the four."

"And you do not think it should be there."

"I think it is a question for a person with an office and I am a man with a window," said Rustam Vey, "and I will say the other thing, since you have not asked it and I would rather somebody did. Four households lost a fortnight on the sixth and I have said out loud, in a lane, in front of more people than I would have chosen, that I was right and it cost a fortnight of somebody's seed. On the twenty-first one of those four took an advance off my window off a sheet with that clause in it, and she is standing there and she can hear me, and I would like her to hear me say that I have not thought about the fourth clause once in nineteen years, and that if the office wants to know whether a form should say that, then the office has been printing it for nineteen years without asking the man who uses it, and that is not a thing I can put right from a window, and I would like it minuted wherever you put the rest of it."

---

Kelb was asked about it because Marek asked him, in a corridor, standing up, and because a man who reads a counterweight out of a paper for a living is the only person in Bell Stair who had been asked to.

Kelb said no, he had never seen it, and then he asked for the form number and wrote it on the back of his own hand with a carpenter's pencil he had taken to using, and then he said:

"I have certified sixty of these forms. Sixty. I have put my mark beside sixty clauses I have never read, and I did it because the mark is a mark on the whole sheet and there is a rule that says you may not put a mark on a sheet with a gap in it, and there is no gap in it. There is no gap in it. That is the finding. There is no gap in it and I have been putting my name on sixty sheets with a clause in the fourth box that names a district."

He put the carpenter's pencil in his coat.

"You may write that down," he said, "because I have said it out loud in a corridor and there are three people in this building who have heard me and none of them are in a position to be asked anything about it by an office. And I am not going to be sorry about it tomorrow, and I am not going to be glad about it either, and if anybody asks me what I feel about sixty marks on a sheet I never read, I will tell them I have not been able to find out yet and that is a worse thing to say out loud than the other one."

---

The letter went up the hill to the Crown March road at the sixth bell, and it was four hundred miles to the coastal returns, and Renata Vole has been a Deputy Settlement Steward for longer than Marek has been alive and is the officer whose counter-signature makes a Trust instrument an instrument, and she is in the coastal returns and has not been in this city since the rains.

It went as first post, addressed by name and not by office, because he had no office to address it to.

It is one sheet and there is one question in it, and he wrote it in the first person because a man with no office writing to an officer in the first person has got exactly one thing to be and it is not a clerk, and he did not sign it as a clerk and did not sign it as an examiner and did not sign it on behalf of the ward or the seed house or the Chamber, and at the foot of it he wrote his name and *no office*, which is the form he has been putting at the foot of everything he has done since the eleventh of the seed month.

*Deputy. I am a clerk with no office in Morrowfen and I certify nothing and I am not writing as one. In the growth month I read the fourth box of a seasonal advance taken off the Rainmarket credit window on the twenty-first of last month by a ratepayer of the low ground, and the clause is yours. It is the Trust's own printed form and it is the same in its wording as the fourth clause of a cooperative instrument sealed in this basin in the bad autumn, and the cooperative's is not the Compact's, and nobody has found anything wrong with the cooperative's, and I have read it end to end this month and it is well made.*

*I am not complaining about it. I want three things and only three, and I will take an answer to one of them and be grateful for it.*

*One. Is the fourth box of that form the founding hand's clause or is it a clause somebody added to the form, and if it was added, by whose office and in what year.*

*Two. When you certify a seasonal advance, are you certifying that a counterweight named as a district is named correctly, and if so to whom the district answers; and if not, what it is that you are certifying.*

*Three. Is there any form in the Trust in which a person may enter a true sentence about a thing that is not a levy, a schedule, a posting or a return. I have asked that in a different letter and the answer I have is no, and if it is still no I would like it in writing from your hand rather than out of a corridor, because a man with no office is going to be asked in a room in about a month whether a thing he said is true, and I have nothing to put in his hand.*

*I am not asking you to answer it to me. I am asking you to answer it in the ordinary course, and if the ordinary course is a year, then a year, and I will hold the letter and I will not send it again.*

*There is a box on that form and the box is the fourth one and it has been printed in it for nineteen years and I have had four hundred people in four counties read it in that time without knowing it was there.*

He read it twice, and folded it, and put it in the post, and stood at the counter in a hall that was closed for the day with the clerk's boy holding the bag open.

Then he went back down the causeway in the wet with a figure in his head he had not written anywhere and could not have entered anywhere, and the figure was the number of sheets of that form that had gone out of that window in nineteen years, and it was a number nobody in Morrowfen could produce, and he understood as he walked that this was not a thing that needed a hero or a raid or a hearing, and that it was only the same shape as the cart in the yard and the bed and the gear and the fifty-two lines and the file of the form: a fact about the paper, and the paper's own shape, and the number of people on the wrong side of it not being on the paper at all.

The post went at the sixth bell and four hundred miles is four hundred miles and there is no version of this in which anybody in this city hears anything about it inside a month.
