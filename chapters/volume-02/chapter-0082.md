# Chapter 82 — The Twentieth of the Rains

The sheet went up on the Gate tank on the morning of the ninth day of the harvest month, under the board that said two and had nothing under it, and there was no name on it anywhere and that was the first thing the yard noticed and it took the yard about a minute and a half to understand what it meant.

Teodor Lask, who was nineteen and read things out twice because that was his office now and nobody had appointed him to it, read it standing on the step of the tank with about a hundred people in the yard and did not have to read it out twice, because it was four lines long and he had it by the fourth of the month.

*Ask at a door, one household at a time. Ask the household, not the yard.*
*Write the answer in the words it was given in. Do not improve it.*
*A stopping is a stopping. It is written down as a stopping and nobody comes back to it.*
*Two of you. A keeper's sheet and a reader's sheet. Never one keeper, never one reader, and a sheet does not go to a third person's house.*

And then, at the foot, in the same type and the same size and with nothing above it either:

*Tallow Ward. Gate yard. The twentieth day of the rains month.*

And under that, a fifth line, and it was the fifth line that put a hand on everybody's neck.

*An independent witness signs. Not paid out of the district account. Not living in the yard.*

The box under it was empty. Somebody had ruled a box and had not filled it, and they had printed the box, and printing the box was the loudest thing anybody had done in Tallow Ward in four months.

---

The yard did the only thing it ever does, which is to argue about the thing in front of it for a while before anybody says what they feel.

It took fifty minutes. Milan Vray said the printer on the low-ground road had done it and wanted paying, which was a thing he knew and nobody else did, and then said the printer had not been, because the type was not a printer's type, it was a copy from a sheet that had been set by somebody with a hand. Sanne Droll said it did not matter whose hand it was. Teodor Lask said, in a voice that four people later described as the voice he had used at the tank on the sixteenth of the seed month, that it mattered entirely, and that the yard had spent two months insisting on a rule that a keeper's sheet and a reader's sheet are two people and a third person's house does not get it, and that the reason for that rule was so that nobody could ever say whose hand was on a thing, and that the yard had just built the best possible reason for not asking.

Then it went quiet, and Aldis Kern came out and said the thing.

"Nineteen sheets," he said. "On the nail, with the strings on. And about a hundred and forty of us in this ward have put a hand on one of them in four months, and every one of those hands could have copied four lines off it, and there is no rule in this yard that stops a person reading a nail, because the whole point of a nail is that a person can."

Nobody said anything.

"So somebody in this ward has done it," said Kern, "and we are not going to find out who, and I want the yard to understand that we are not going to find out on purpose. Not because the person is somebody we would be hard on. Because the day we start asking, that is a schedule. You ask a named set of people a named question at a stated interval and you have the third box, and this yard said that sentence out loud on the sixteenth of the seed month and it was the truest thing anybody said in here all summer. So we do not ask."

"You printed a rule with no name on it," said Droll.

"We did not print anything."

"Somebody used our four lines and our date and did not put a name on it, and there is not a person in this city who could be shown to be the seller of it," said Aldis Kern, "and that is not an accident either. Do you know who taught that? Not me. Vray taught it in the rains when he said that a record a person can lift off a nail is a record and a record a person has to ask somebody for is a rumour. And before that the yard taught it, in a trestle, in a yard, in front of four hundred people, when we wrote the Underbank at the top of the sheet and put a boy's own fault beside the water."

---

Then he said the part that made the morning, and he said it in the yard's own register and not to Marek Vale, who was standing at the end of it with his hands in his coat.

"I am going to say where it came from, once, and then it is said, and then nobody is to ask me who held the pen. The twentieth day of the rains month. This tank. Four lines, on the back of a complaint sheet, off a trestle table that had been standing at the end of the tank for about a week, in a hand that was not a clerk's and not a lawyer's either, because there was nothing else to write on and somebody in two hundred wanted it in ink.

"I am not going to say the name and I am not going to be asked, and that is not loyalty. It is that the day this yard starts naming the hands on its own sheets is the day the sheets are a thing this yard keeps, and a thing a yard keeps is a thing a yard can be asked to produce, and I would rather have four lines in a hundred and forty hands than a rule in a building that a man in a good coat can produce at me in a room. The date is the yard's. The tank is the yard's. The four lines are the yard's because the yard sent nineteen pairs up nineteen lanes with them, and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it and I have said it."

"And you are not going to be thanked," said Marek Vale.

"No," said Kern. "And you are not going to be thanked either, and I want to say that in the yard and not in a lane, because a lane is where a thing like that gets its shape changed on the way to somebody who matters. You have been in this yard five weeks and you have not written a word of it and you have not asked a single one of the hundred and forty people here a question, and that is the only reason those sheets could be lifted off a nail by anybody at all. You have not done it. That is not humility. I would have done it in a week if I had thought of it, and I have thought of it since the sixth, and I did not, because I am the captain of this ward's water and I am frightened of a printer."

---

The yard granted one thing, and it took two hours, and the two hours were not about the register at all.

Kern put it the way he put everything, from the step, as a thing the yard was going to do and not a thing the yard was going to allow.

"Here is what we will do and here is what we will not do, and I am going to say both in the same breath because that is what a yard is.

"We will let our four lines be used. We will not be asked and we will not be thanked and we will not be a party to it, and if anybody in this city asks who owns the method, the answer is that it was written at this tank on the twentieth of the rains month and there is no owner and that is the design.

"And we will do three things about it, and the three things are conditions, and a yard can refuse anybody.

"First. It is copied in our words and with our date on it and nothing else. No hall. No office. No man. If a name goes at the foot of it in this city, we will nail the twentieth of the rains month up beside it the same day and let the two sheets argue.

"Second. The empty box stays empty. Not because we cannot fill it any more. Because we can fill it now and that is worse."

"Then why," said somebody at the back.

"Because a levy is agreed by two bodies," said Sanne Droll, "and an independent witness has to be neither of the two bodies, and a person paid out of a levy is one of the two bodies. In the rains we could not fill the box because a yard cannot reach or pay a person and there is no form for asking the city for one. That was humiliating and it was true. The new reason is decent and it is a rate, and I would rather have the old one, and I have voted for the old one, and I would like it minuted that I have voted for the worse reason because I would like somebody in the ninth month to be able to point at it."

"Third," said Kern.

Nobody said anything.

"Third," said Kern. "The person who keeps the register is a person of this yard. Not an hour's work, not a favour, not somebody who will be glad of the standing. The whole of what the yard has learned this summer is that a keeper of a thing is a person who can be asked, and if the yard's keeper cannot be asked then there is no keeper, there is a building. So the yard will name a second person to stand at that counter beside whoever is reading, and the second person is of the yard, and she is to be asked the four questions like anybody else, in her own words, and written down in her own words, and put on the wall with the rest."

The yard knew who it was before he had finished. It had known for about a quarter of an hour, in fact, and about four people had looked at her while he was talking.

"No," said Sanne Droll.

"Yes," said Kern, "and the yard has voted, and it was eleven to four, and your four are the four who thought it should be somebody who is not of the yard, which is the same four who said the independent witness should be in the box, and I have told them the same thing about that."

---

She said what it cost, because the yard had made her say what it cost, and she said it without any temper in it at all, which is what made the yard go quiet.

"I do not sell rope on a Tuesday because I am on a tank at the second bell and I have not sold rope on a Tuesday since the rains," she said. "That is not a noble thing and I would like it written down as a rope-maker's complaint and not as anything else.

"The second round cost four days of a wage in this yard and there are about a hundred and forty houses here that have not got a day in them to give, and everybody in this yard knows the number and nobody has ever written it down on a sheet, and it is not going on a sheet now either, because a total is a number of people who agreed with something and I am not going to hand that to a room in town.

"And the third thing, which nobody is going to like. I am going to be asked. Not asked what I think of the register. Asked the same four, at a door, in the yard's words, about my own household, and there are two people of this yard who will come to my door and read the sheet, and whatever I say goes on the wall with the other answers, and there is not one thing I can say in this yard this morning that will change that, and I have thought about it for two days, and I am not going to pretend I have not."

"Then say no," said a man at the back, "and it will be somebody else."

"And it will be somebody else," said Sanne Droll, "and in four months it will be a thing in a building that a person is paid to do, and it will be paid out of a levy, and the levy will be agreed by two bodies, and we will be back to a box in a form and I will have lost a day of rope for nothing. So no, I am not saying no, and I am not saying yes either, and both of those are things I would like the yard to hear me not say, because I have not got to the end of it and I have got to the end of it enough."

---

They voted again at the sixth bell and it carried, and the vote was not counted out loud and nobody wrote a number on anything, and what came out of it was four lines of conditions on the back of the yard's own refusal from the nail, in two hands, and both of them of the yard, and it went back on the nail under the new sheet with a bit of string through it.

Marek Vale was at the end of the yard for all of it and was asked nothing and helped with nothing and was thanked by nobody, and the reason he was not missed is four lines on a nail and about a hundred and forty hands that are not his, and he stood there and understood that this was the second time in six weeks that a rule handed to him by somebody in this yard had turned out to be a rule about him, and that he was not going to be thanked for it either time.

They put the shutter over the tank at the seventh bell because the wind had gone round to the north, and the new sheet was inside the shed on the nail with the strings on, where anybody could get at it, and the box under the four rules was still empty and would be printed empty for as long as the yard allowed the thing to exist.

A woman at the back of the yard, on her way out, said to the man beside her that there was a register now, and the man beside her said was there, and the woman said it was going to be a room like Merrow's, and the man said it was not, and the woman said how would he know, and the man said because Merrow's had a licence over the door and this one had a tank.
