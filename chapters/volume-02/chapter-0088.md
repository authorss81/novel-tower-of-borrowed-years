# Chapter 88 — A Dissent, Written Out

The second-floor hearing room in Bell Stair was built for thirty and there were nine in it, and the table was the same table, and the four terms were the four terms, and they were confirmed in about eleven minutes because they had been confirmed twice before and used three times and nobody in the room wanted to spend a morning on them.

Harrow presided and was ineligible to be chair, which he has said in his own hand at the top of every minute since the ninth of the seed month. Sedge for the Chamber. Kern for the ward, in his own name, under a standing provision that is older than anybody in the room. Vendl for the Fund. And the Trust's seat empty, and the Trust had not sent anybody, and the office's second refusal still used the words *in the ordinary course* and was still in quotation marks in a minute in a drawer in this building.

Harrow had no clerk and could not get one, and served the minutes himself, in his own hand, and it takes him about four minutes a page.

Neris Kade had the second seat, which is not a membership and has never been a membership, and her sheet's one function was to hold four figures in the order they were called out, and her sheet was blank, and it was blank because nothing was called out, because the matter before the table was not a figure.

---

"What is before us," said Bel Harrow, "is a question of a word. I want that said first, because the room has spent two months being brought in here to look at figures and there is nothing on the table but a word, and I have been in this city four months and I have learnt that a question of a word in this basin is a question about who is going to pay.

"The room we are talking about is in a shed in Tallow Ward. It is open four hours a day. Nothing is charged. A grain factor has put his own book in it and a man with no office reads it out, and there is one entry and it balances.

"It has no money. Nobody is paid. A boy of nineteen reads out for nothing and a rope-maker stands at the counter for nothing because her yard said she would, and a man from the hall in this building who is not a member of anything cannot enter a line in it and can only stand behind a board and say words.

"Every office in this basin has refused to hold it, in a sentence each, and the Fund's woman told him on the ninth of the month that the reason it has no money is that there is no box anywhere for a man who reads. And there is no box, and there is not going to be a box, because a box is a form and a form is a thing an office issues, and there is no office in this city that would issue a form for being paid to say true things.

"So it is going to be a levy. And I cannot levy anybody. I have told this table twice that a Crown March representative can call a meeting and cannot pay out of one, and neither has anything changed, and the Trust will not fill a chair, so we are five people and a hole."

"Then what is the table for," said Kern.

"To say what the word means," said Harrow. "That is all, and it is not nothing, and I have thought about it for three days and I am not pleased with it."

---

It took about an hour and a half and it went in the way those things go, which is that four people who all knew the answer argued about a word for an hour and a half and then one of them said it out loud and the other three agreed and none of them enjoyed it.

The figures came from Oren Pike's counting floor and they came in a hand, on a sheet, unsigned, because a factor who puts a number in a room wants it known that it is his, and Pike's man on the slate had written them and had not improved them.

A market pays two a reading. That is the going price and it has been two for eleven years, and a man in this city counted it in a lane on the twelfth of the month and gave the count to about forty people and is not in this room.

A levy, said the same sheet, cannot carry two. It carries about four a week, which is the going rate for a person sitting in a shed four hours a day answering a stranger, and which is what the girl in Merrow's shed is paid, and which Pike said he would not pay his own daughter less than.

And a season of that comes to one hundred and forty, and one hundred and forty on sixty-one households is a little over two pounds each.

"Then that is the answer," said Sedge. "A hundred and forty a season, out of sixty-one households. It is not a large figure."

"It is not a large figure," said Kern, "and I am the only man at this table who can tell you what it is in the place it is going to be collected, and I would like it said to the Share by somebody who has weighed it at that tank and not by a man in a good coat. Two pounds is what a household of four eats in a week at the Gate. I have not got a form for what I am saying and I have said it anyway."

"And it comes to the households that have already had a rate put on their ground out of a piece of sack with nothing at the head of it," said Bel Harrow, "and I would like somebody in this room to say that out loud in a sentence and let us hear how it sounds, and then let us carry on."

Nobody said it out loud. The Chair does not vote in a money matter and there were only two of them who could and both of them had written the objection already.

---

Kern's was the first, and he gave it in the first person, in about four sentences, from the position of a man who had refused nine hundred and forty pounds in a yard in the seed month for exactly this reason and had not enjoyed that either.

"You have got a ward using a room and a cooperative paying for it," he said. "I am not going to pretend to you that I can assess this yard. A ward cannot be assessed a rate at all; that was proved out in this basin in a room in the rains by a woman from the Fund and nobody has ever said otherwise. So the Share carries the whole of it. And a levy is a thing a ward raises on itself, and the Share is not a ward, and the Share's members are not a yard, and eleven of them have already had a rate put on their ground out of a piece of sack with nothing at the head of it.

"So what we are agreeing is a body raising a levy on itself out of its own members to pay for a thing it does not use and cannot pay for and is not a party to. Take the names off that and it is the offer that came to my tank in the seed month. About nine hundred and forty, written off, in exchange for the ward taking on somebody else's rate. I refused it in nine minutes and it is on a nail, and I would like this table to tell me in a sentence how a levy of two pounds and a bit a head is different, because I have not found it and I have been thinking about it for three days."

"It is different in three places," said Harrow, "and I will give you two of them now and the third one is going to be the thing that saves you and I have not got to it yet. One: the levy is capped and a member who cannot pay is not charged and is not entered. Two: it is paid into a room that anybody may look at and the wall is in the open and there is no office behind it and a thing with no office behind it cannot be produced at anybody.

"The third one I have not got, and I would rather we went on without it than have me invent it at a table."

---

Zorka Vendl had the dissent and she had had it for two days and she asked for it to be taken at the end, which is not what anybody expected, and the reason she gave was that she wanted the room to have been in the middle of something else when she said it.

"Term four," she said. "A member who dissents may have the grounds written in her own words, and the duty to write them is the board's and not the member's. That is the term and I have refused to let anybody make it a courtesy, and I am going to use it now and I am going to use it about money for the first time, and I would like it understood that I have been a member of a mutual for nineteen years and I have never used it for anything, and I am using it now because I have run out of other ways of saying a thing I have to say.

"The word is *levy*, and nobody in this basin has one on paper, and that is not because the word is new. It is because a levy is the only kind of money that is not owed. A rate is owed. A sum is owed. A return is owed. A levy is a figure a body decides to take off itself in order to keep something running, and the whole difference between that and everything else in the list is that the body has agreed to it before anybody asks, and the agreeing is the instrument, and there is no form for the agreeing and there is no office that can check it and there is no seal for it.

"And I am the person in this room who knows what happens when a levy cannot be carried, because I have one. The Fund's levy is a rate on the hall out of every clerk's wage and it has run at a deficit every year since the flood years, and the Fund cannot pay a sitting, and I have been asked twice to pay for one and have declined twice, and I am still at this table.

"So here is the grounds and I want them in my words and I want them to be a definition and not a complaint, because a complaint gets minuted and a definition gets used.

"A levy is a figure a body puts on itself, in a room, on a day, in the words of the body, without an office, without a seal and without anybody able to produce it afterwards against anybody. And the first rule of it is that a member who cannot pay in money may pay in kind, and the second rule of it is that a member who has nothing pays nothing and is not entered. Both of those are caps. And a cap is the only part of a levy that anybody in this basin can recognise as a levy, because a rate has never had one."

Somebody read the paragraph back to her and she corrected one word and did not correct anything else.

Then Harrow wrote it into the finding in his own hand, in full, with her name at the foot of it, and read out what term four required him to do, which is that the grounds go in the finding and the finding goes on a nail in a yard.

"It is the first definition of a levy anybody in this city can produce," he said, "and it is a Fund secretary's, in a Fund secretary's words, about the Fund's own money, and nobody in this room paid for it. I want the room to sit with that for a minute, because I have been in this city four months and I have been trying to get a room to pay a clerk and this is the first time anything has come out of one of these sittings that is worth more than the sitting."

---

Then the Chair did the last thing, and it was small, and Marek Vale was at the back of a room built for thirty with no standing and it was about him.

"Mr Vale," said Bel Harrow. "The Gate yard has given you a standing to read the register out. That is the yard's to give and it has given it and it is theirs. You may not enter a line in it, and I am told the form has no box for you, and I am told the reason, and I accept the reason, and I am not going to be the man who puts a clerk's name on a Crown March page when a woman on a posting desk has told him in the corridor that reading is not entering.

"I am going to write it down anyway, in my own hand, in a minute of a board of five, because the duty is a real duty and somebody is doing it for nothing, and a record that leaves it out is a record that says the only person standing behind that counter is a woman out of Ropewalk who is also not being paid.

"Note what you have got, Mr Vale. Not an office, not a form, not a pay, and not a right of entry, and a duty given by a yard in its own words which no office has granted and no office can take away and which is not in the finding, because a Crown March representative has no business granting standing to a man who has none, and which is the only thing in this building today that nobody can be told they got from us."

Neris Kade spoke twice in the whole of the sitting, and both times she was from a seat that was not a membership, and neither time was a vote.

"Two things and neither of them is a motion," she said.

"The first is that the register will be open four hours a day for a levy of about a hundred and forty a season, and that the levy will be collected by two people of the Share at a trestle in a yard, and that nobody in this room has the standing to say it cannot be collected, and that being right about that is worth exactly nothing and I have been right about a market for four months.

"And the second is that the levy is capped, and the cap is right, and the cap will not be enough, because a member who pays in kind pays in seed, and a member who has nothing pays nothing and is not entered, and a member who is not entered is a member who has not been asked, and this room has just written down a rule that produces a person who does not exist, and I would like somebody in a month to look for her, and I cannot do it and I have said so in a record in a room in Crown March already this year and it cannot be taken out."

Marek Vale said nothing at all, and did not look at her, and understood that she had said it to the table and not to him and that she was not going to be thanked in this room or out of it.

The finding was read out and signed by the Chair, and the Trust's seat was minuted as empty for the fourth time, and there was still no date for anything.

At the fifth bell Harrow read his own minute back to himself in the empty room afterwards, at the table, with his hat on, and the paragraph about the levy was the second of five and had a name at the foot of it and the name was not an office, and the paragraph about a man with no office reading a register out in Tallow Ward was the last of five and had no name at the foot of it at all, and the Chair looked at that for a while and then put the pen down and went down the stair.
