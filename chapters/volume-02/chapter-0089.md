# Chapter 89 — How Many People

The shed on the low-ground road lane was eleven feet by nine and had been taken on a week's notice from a man who sells pitch, and on the twenty-sixth day of the harvest month from the fourth bell it had a painted board over the door with a counterseal on it and a woman sitting in it, and the board said what it had said since the twenty-second of the seed month.

*A REGISTER OF ASSIGNED CLAIMS. OPEN FOUR HOURS A DAY. NO CHARGE. ANY PERSON MAY ASK.*

Signe Alve was twenty-nine and had a licence of no kind and four a week and had been in that chair since the twenty-second of the seed month, and by the fourth bell there was a queue on the lane, and about a third of it was not the queue it had been in the seed month, which was a queue of people who wanted to know what they had signed, and was now mostly a queue of people who had heard about a shed at the Tallow Gate that had been open five days.

The bench along the wall had fifty-two lines on it and then eleven more, and a second hand beside them, and eight days of work in the second hand, and the sixth column had gone up on the nineteenth of the growth month for the fifty-two letters, and the eleventh of the eleven went on on the twenty-second of this month at the fourth bell, in the words the households used and not one word of them the factor's, and the seventh was bare at the head of it and had been since the day the painters were sent home.

Ovid Merrow came down at the fifth bell, in a good coat, in the rain, and stood outside the door of his own shed with his hands behind his back for about four minutes before he went in, which is what he has always done, and which is not a thing a man does who is about to be helpful to anybody.

---

The boy who came up to the Gate with the message was about nine and had been paid a penny for it, and the message was four words in a factor's hand and it said *come down and stand there*, and Marek Vale had read it once, and had not written anything on the back of it, and had come down the low-ground road on foot in the rain at the fifth bell and told nobody why he was going, which is a thing a man with no office can do and is one of about four things he can still do.

---

The boy was fourteen and had a tin under his arm and a name that half the lane knew and the other half did not, and he had been holding a queue for a shilling a morning out of that tin since the seed month, for a house on the low-ground road, and he came into the shed at about half past the fifth bell on his own account.

He looked at the bench for a while and then at the woman in the chair, and then he said:

"You are the one with the sheet of everybody's business in here."

"I am," said Ovid Merrow. "That is a register of assigned claims, it is open four hours a day, nothing is charged, and any person may ask. What is your name."

"Tam Rell. I hold a queue for the house on the low-ground road for a shilling a morning and I have four sisters and my father is not here and has not been since the flood year. And I want to ask you three things and I have asked my mother whether I could and she said I could not and I am doing it anyway, so you may tell her you were not asked."

"I was asked," said Ovid Merrow. "Ask."

---

"How much do you get out of it."

The shed got quieter than a shed with nine people in it usually is.

Merrow did not put his hands in his coat and he did not smile and he did not look at the door to see who was outside.

"The paper is sold at three hundred," he said, "against a seed cost of eleven hundred and a second-hand plough at forty, and the house's margin is in there, and I am not going to pretend to a boy of fourteen that I am paid nothing for my trouble, because there are about eleven people on the low-ground road who would tell you the opposite and they would be telling you true.

"There is a number for the year. I am not going to say the number for the year in this room, and the reason is not modesty. The reason is that the year is not finished, and the ninth month is days away, and a man who says a year's figure in the middle of a year is a man who is doing a thing I would not do in a room, and I have watched four people do it this year and two of them were lying and one of them was not and one of them was me in a different month.

"So: three hundred on a piece of paper, and about nine hundred of a dead district's rate cleared this month at a discount, and a bundle of nine sheets and a schedule of fifty-two lines that a man in a good coat can sell to a house that will not buy Morrowfen paper. That is the whole of what I will stand behind in a room with you in it."

---

"Did you know what it was."

"No," said Ovid Merrow. "And I am going to tell you what that costs, because you asked it straight and you are fourteen.

"I have not known in nine years. Not once. And the price of not knowing in my trade is that I have to buy somebody else's knowledge of it, and I have been buying it for nine years, and I have got a figure for it and it is about a hundred and sixty a year, and that is a clerk's wage to a man in a Crown March office who reads a schedule and tells me what a thing is, and I have never met him, and I do not know his name, and he has cost me a hundred and sixty pounds this year.

"That is what not knowing is worth. I have never said it out loud in a yard because nobody in this basin has ever asked me for a figure, and they have all asked me for a sentence, and a sentence is free."

"And you do not want to say it any more," said Tam Rell, "because there is a room at the Gate."

"I have wanted to stop paying that man for four years," said Ovid Merrow, "and I could not, because he was the only person who would tell me, and now there is a shed with a boy in it who reads things out. So you have taken about a hundred and sixty pounds a year off my costs in five days and you have not taken one penny off my house, and I would like you to understand that I am not complaining, and I am also not going to pretend that I would not have built it if a man in a good coat had not."

---

The third question was the one he had been in a room about twice in nine years and had never once been asked by anybody who was going to keep the answer.

"How many people signed it."

"There is not a number," said Ovid Merrow. "And I have known there is not a number for nine years, and I have never been asked for it by anybody who was not a factor or a clerk, and I am going to tell you what I have instead, and then I am going to tell you why neither of them is the thing you asked for, and it is going to take about a minute and a half and I am doing it in the open because the boy came in on his own and did not send anybody.

"Fifty-two lines about assignments. There are sixty-three lines on that bench and fifty-two of them are a date, a rate period, a sum, a discount and the party it went to, and every one of those fifty-two has a signature on it, and the signature is the assignor's. That is the figure I sell and that is the figure I have always sold and it is a good figure and it is true.

"The other eleven went in on the twenty-second of this month at the fourth bell, in the households' own words, off a piece of sack with nothing at the head of it that a woman in the low ground has in her own house and will not give to anybody, and I wrote none of them out myself and had them written and read out in a room like a fool, and I expect to do it again, and I have not decided yet whether that is a good decision.

"Thirty-eight of the fifty-two households answered when they were asked in words at their own door what they thought they had signed. Fourteen did not, and those fourteen are entered as *asked, and no answer given, and asked once*, which is the yard's rule and not mine, and my house adopted it in a week without deciding to, which is the most useful thing that has ever happened to me.

"Thirty-eight and fourteen out of fifty-two. Those are the two numbers I have got and I will stand behind both of them in any room in this kingdom.

"And now I am going to say the thing, and there are about nine people at that door and I can see all of them, and I would like it noticed that I am not saying it to you."

He put one finger on the back of the bench.

"A line is a household. Not a person. Fifty-two households is not fifty-two people and it has never been fifty-two people, and the roll the cooperative keeps is households, and the instrument was carried on a count of hands in a shed and forty of them went up and one went down, and forty of forty-one is not a majority and nobody has ever said it was.

"I have been selling lines for nine years and calling them claims. A line with a date and a sum on it is the smallest thing a person will sign and it is the largest thing you can carry out of a house without the house, and I have never once in nine years been able to tell you how many people are inside a claim of mine, and I could not tell you this morning, and I have not been able to tell anybody since the eighth year."

Nobody in the shed said anything at all, and about four people at the door had come in and were standing against the wall.

"That is the number I do not have," said Ovid Merrow, "and there is no form in this kingdom in which anybody may be given it, and I have looked, and I have had nine years."

---

"Then what about the one at the Gate," said Tam Rell. "Is yours better than that one."

"No," said Ovid Merrow, without any difficulty whatever, which is a thing he is good at and which about four people in that shed had not expected. "Mine has been open since the twenty-second of the seed month and it has sixty-three lines in it and about nine people come into it on a morning. That shed has been open five days and there is a line in it for every person who has come through that door and nobody in it has been told a figure by an office that they could not go and read themselves, and the queue outside it is longer than the queue outside mine on a wet day.

"And I am going to be in front of it at the fourth bell in about a month and there is going to be nobody in this basin who does not know where to go, and I have said that in this lane in front of a hundred and forty people since the twenty-second of the seed month and I have not been wrong yet about anything, including this.

"And I am not going to be a martyr about it, because I built the first one. I built it because a register is a place people come to look for themselves and a schedule is a list of people, and the reason it worked is that nobody in this basin had ever had to write down what a person said when they were asked what they owed. I have made a very good living out of being the only man in the basin who will explain a piece of paper, and in about a month the price of an explanation in this city is going to be nothing, and there is no form on which anybody may be charged for asking, and I am entitled to be annoyed about that and I am entitled to say so in a lane and I am going to.

"And the thing that is going to beat me is not a register," he said. "It is a yard's method with a name on it, and I have been trying for nine years to get hold of a thing with nobody behind it, and the Gate shed is a method with a tank and a date on it and no name, and I could not buy that in a morning if it were in this shed on a bench in front of me. That is not a defeat and it is not a complaint. That is the trade."

---

Then Merrow held out his hand and said, *the card*, and Marek Vale took it out of the inside pocket of a coat that had been on a table on a causeway on the fifth of the seed month, and put it on the bench, and Merrow turned it round so that the figure was facing the room, and Tam Rell looked at it, and the woman in the chair looked at it, and about four people at the door looked at it.

"That is a figure of mine," said Ovid Merrow, "in my own hand, on a card, and it was given to a man with no office on the fifth of the seed month, and it is more than the hall pays, and it is not a bribe, and it has not been used, and I am entering it here because that shed on the other side of the water is going to have a line about my paper in it and this is the only line of mine in the world that anybody ought to have.

"It is not for a post. It is not for a vote. It was for one document, and one reading of one document, and it was refused in about four seconds on a causeway, and it has been in a coat since the fifth of the seed month, and he has known every day that it was in there, and I have known every day that he had it, and neither of us has said one word about it to anybody in two months.

"Here is what it bought, since I have to write it on a bench. I offered a man with no office money to be the second eye on the Share's instrument, and he said no, and I had to go to a clerk at a counter-desk who had never been to Rainmarket in his life and who did not know what a counterweight was. And he read the fourth clause out loud in a shed to about forty people, badly, on purpose, and told them he was doing it badly on purpose and why. And that is the whole of the risk in this basin this year, and there is a room at the Tallow Gate because a man with no office could not be paid, and I am not going to be thanked for any part of this, and I would like it minuted that I offered it and it was refused, and the refusal is the reason."

Nobody in the shed thanked him.

"You have one thing on that bench wrong and I am the only person here who can correct it," said Marek Vale. "There is a room at the Tallow Gate because a yard agreed four hours a day and nothing charged in forty minutes on the fourteenth, and then voted in six minutes on the nineteenth to put its own best paper into it and could not, and a rope-maker of Ropewalk lost a day of selling for the whole of it. I stand in it because a yard said so out loud at a tank on the ninth and refused to put it in ink. It is not yours. I would rather it stayed that way, and I would rather you wrote that down than the other one."

Ovid Merrow looked at him for a while and then wrote nothing down, which was the first time in nine years that a person he was selling to had told him to write something, and then he picked the card up off the bench and put it back into Marek Vale's hand, which took him about four seconds and which he did not explain either.

Marek Vale read his own card lying on a bench in a shed on the low-ground road, in a factor's handwriting and not his own, with a figure on it that was still more than the hall paid, and put his hand flat on the edge of the bench for a moment and did not say one word, and was not asked one word, and did not get a post and did not get a wage and did not get an office and did not get a vote and has never in this city been owed anything by anybody.

---

The boy went out into the rain and stood under the board for a moment with the tin under his arm, and then said the fourth thing, which was not a question about anybody's business.

"My father is not here," he said, "and he has not been since the flood year, and everybody in this lane knows that and nobody will say it to me, and I have come in here four times this month and I have had a plate of something twice off the woman in the chair, because she is not a house and she is not paid to be a house, and I have worked out that the reason she gives it away is that she does not know who I am and I would like to know whether she knows."

Signe Alve said no, she did not, and that she was sorry, and that it was a plate of nothing and it was not a kindness and it was not a price and there was not going to be one in her shed.

Ovid Merrow stood in the doorway of his own register with his hands behind his back and found that he had nothing at all, which had happened to him twice in nine years, and he said so, out loud, to a boy of fourteen in the rain, and did not improve it.
