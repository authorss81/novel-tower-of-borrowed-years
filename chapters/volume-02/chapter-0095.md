# Chapter 95 — It Was Fair

The register went live on the ninth day of the ninth month, at the fourth bell, at the Gate tank, by a decision of two bodies, in about twenty minutes, and neither of the two bodies was in a position to be thanked and both of them said so.

The Share carries the money. The yard gives the shed. That is the whole of the arrangement and it has not changed since the twenty-fourth of the harvest month when a board of five in Bell Stair found, in a Fund secretary's own words and under a term about dissent, that a levy is a figure a body puts on itself in a room on a day in the words of the body, without an office, without a seal and without anybody able to produce it afterwards against anybody.

The figure is one hundred and forty a season. It comes off sixty-one households on the low ground at a little over two pounds a head, and a hundred and forty on sixty-one is where it came from and not four a week, and the man who gave the figure has said out loud in a room and in a yard that the two do not agree with each other and that he did not get one out of the other.

The shed is eleven feet by nine and is built for a seed bin. It is open four hours a day on whatever days it opens. Nothing is charged. Nobody is asked to sign anything to come in.

And it goes live on the eighteenth of the ninth month, which is a date, and a date is the first thing this thing has ever had that Tallow Ward did not have to write on a nail.

Bessok Tan read the date back twice, in the yard, with about ninety people in it, and then said the thing that the yard had said on the nineteenth of the harvest month and the yard had not improved.

"It is a date," he said, "and I want it noticed what kind of thing a date is. A date is a day somebody else picks. We have gone four months without one. On the eighteenth it is a day somebody in this room has picked and the picking is the whole of what this room is, and I would like it in the bench book in that order."

Nobody wrote a total anywhere, and nobody was asked to, and the levy is capped: a member who cannot pay in money may pay in kind, and a member who has nothing pays nothing and is not entered.

Kern read the yard's three conditions off the back of the yard's own refusal, in the yard's own words, and put the bit of string back through the hole in it, and the third of them was the one that is the worst, and he did not soften it.

"The empty box stays empty," he said. "Not because we cannot fill it. Because we can, now, and that is worse. And our second person is a person of this yard and is to be asked the same four questions like anybody else, and she has been asked once, in the open, in front of about forty people, and nobody thanked her, and that is the arrangement and it is not going to change because the thing has a date on it."

And then he said the part about the hole, because it was the ninth month and it had to be said in a yard rather than in a corridor.

"There is a document in this basin that cannot be produced and cannot be entered," he said. "It is the refusal on this nail. It is the only one. We voted in six minutes on the nineteenth of the harvest month to put it in the register and it failed in ninety seconds on a rule we wrote ourselves at this tank, and the reason we are not fixing it is that we have been asked about it twice since and both times by a person who wanted it fixed, and I am going to say the rest of it and then I am going to stop.

"A hole in a thing that is a practice is a hole. A hole in a thing that is a law is an outrage. In about six months from now, when somebody in a hall writes down what this yard did in the ninth month of this year, they will write down that Tallow Ward's own method is not in it, and that will be true, and there will be a room in this city with a hole in it the exact shape of the best paper this yard has ever produced, and it will be there because of a rule we wrote at this tank on the twentieth of the rains month by somebody who is not in this yard. That is what it costs. That is what it has cost since the nineteenth. Read the refusal out twice and go home."

Teodor Lask read the refusal out twice and it is on the nail with a bit of string through it and there is no office behind it and nobody in this city can be made to produce it.

---

The posting desk on Bell Stair keeps a board, and the board is a list of houses licensed to take up an assignment of a rate arrears and sell the resulting claim, and the board is older than anybody who has looked at it in this building, and a house is put on it when a licence is issued and taken off it when a licence lapses.

A licence is a permission. It is not a judgement and it is not a reputation and there is nothing on that board about anybody's character. What it says is that a house may do a thing, on conditions, and the conditions are printed on the licence, and a house that does not meet them is not licensed to do it, which is a different sentence from a house that has done something wrong.

Sedge had the board out on the counter because the board comes down when a licence is issued or lapses and a licence had lapsed, and he had the instrument in front of him, and he had the licence in front of him, and Ovid Merrow was standing at the counter with Ilse Ferrow, who is thirty-five and has done the work for nine years and is not going to be described in a room as anybody's face.

"The third condition," said Sedge. "I am going to read it and I am not going to improve it, and I would like it noticed that I did not write it and that I have never seen it written twice.

"*That the claim be sold with its counterweight named upon the instrument sold, and the number of persons who will bear it stated upon the same.*"

He put a finger on the instrument, which was nine sheets and a schedule of fifty-two lines, with a stated rate on every one of them and a discount and a receiving party, and a counterseal in a calendar that nobody on this hill can read in a hand that nobody at this desk can check, and nobody at this desk said one word about the seal, because an audit in this city goes as far as the seal and stops.

"There is no counterweight on it," said Sedge. "Not in the box, not under it, not in a margin. There is a rate. There is a discount. There is a party at the end of every line and a date. There is nothing on this instrument that says what happens if the season it was made for does not come, or who it happens to."

"That is a question of form," said Ilse Ferrow.

"It is a question of a line on a form," said Sedge, "and I have got a fair bit of experience of the difference and I am not going to be the man who improves the difference in a room where a house is standing."

"Then the condition is not met," said Ovid Merrow.

"The condition is not met," said Sedge. "And I want to say the four things that are also true, because nobody in this room is going to have read them out and I am the man at the desk.

"Nothing has been found against you. No fraud, no misstatement, no figure that does not balance, and there is a man in Bell Stair who counts a house's own figures twice with his own left hand and has said out loud in a shed in Tallow Ward that this stack is the cleanest thing he has counted this year. Nobody has proved anything. You are not charged with anything and there is nothing to appeal, because there is no finding.

"Your licence has lapsed because compliance is a fact about an instrument and not a decision about a person, and if I put a licence in front of you tomorrow that names the counterweight and states the number of persons, this desk will put you back on that board at the fourth bell, and I will do it in about a minute, and nobody in this building will stop me, because I do not have the standing to stop anybody and there is a column with my name over it in a minute in a drawer and that is the whole of what I am."

Nobody thanked him and he did not ask to be thanked, and he said afterwards that a form has to be read by a man who does not know whether he is right, and that he had been in this building long enough that twice in it, and that he was not going to be moved off this counter by anybody's face.

Ferrow said the thing that was true and was not a defence, and she said it in the flat voice of a woman who has answered every question truthfully for nine years and has never once been thanked for it either.

"No," she said. "Not by the eighteenth. I have looked for a week and I will say that I have looked and I will say where I looked. A bundle cannot be made to comply in a week, and the reason it cannot be made to comply is that the answer is in it and the answer is a district, and a district is not a person and has never been a person and cannot be asked what it will bear, and that is not an excuse, it is the fact, and it has been the fact since the ninth day of the bad autumn when a cooperative of sixty-one people sealed a paper with a fourth clause on the back of the second leaf that names a district as the counterweight of a season.

"And I am going to say the commercial part once, because there is no reason for me not to and because this room is full of people who will be polite about it. A licensed house that will not sell a bundle can still sell a part. A part is a smaller thing with a smaller margin. This basin is about to have four prices for the same kind of thing instead of one, and four prices are worse for the people at the bottom of them than one price was, and they were already bad. I have been wrong about this for nine years and I am not going to be wrong about it gracefully."

---

The entry on the board is four lines and it is dated the ninth of the ninth month, and the fourth line is the one Merrow asked for and the third one is the one Sedge insisted on.

*That the Morrowfen assignment licence of the Sable Compact's house is lapsed under the third condition, the instrument produced being a bundled instrument sold as a single line and bearing no counterweight upon its face.*

*That no finding of fraud, misstatement or impropriety has been made against the house or against any person, and that the house has been heard and has not contested the fact of the instrument, and that the licence may be restored upon an instrument that complies.*

*That the officer at this desk writes what people say and has entered two things said to him in this room, one of them being that the loss was fair.*

*That the person who said it said it in the first person and meant it at the time he said it and did not know whether he would still say it on the way home.*

Ovid Merrow said it at the counter at about the fifth bell, in about nine seconds, in a good coat, to about eleven people, and the room went quiet in the way a shed goes quiet.

"It was fair," he said. "I want it in the entry and it is in the entry, and I am not going to soften it afterwards in a lane where somebody can hear me being bitter about it.

"And I am going to say why, in about six sentences, and then I am going to go down to Tallow Ward and find out whether I can put my own paper in a shed, and I expect that I cannot.

"The third condition has been in that licence for nine years. It has been the condition of a thing I sold every day of those nine years. I have never once met it, and I could not have met it, and the reason I could not is that the counterweight on that paper is a district and there is nobody in a district I can ask, and I have known that for nine years and I have never put it in writing and nobody ever asked me to.

"And the condition says: the number of persons who will bear it. Nine years of selling lines and I could not tell you how many people were inside a claim of mine, and I have said that out loud in a shed to a boy of fourteen and four adults, and I said it and I was not ashamed of it, and it is the only number in my trade that I have never had, and there is no form in this kingdom in which anybody may be given it.

"So a licence in this city has been requiring me, for nine years, to count the people in my own book. I did not count them. I am not going to stand here in a good coat and pretend that a rule I broke by not noticing is a rule I was crushed by. I did not count them because counting them is the work and the work is the part that does not get to be interesting, and I am very good at the part that does.

"That is fair," said Ovid Merrow. "That is what fair means, in a licence, and I have always said the word meant that and nobody in nine years has believed me."

---

He came down to the Gate shed at the sixth bell and asked to enter a line about his own paper, and Sedge was not there and Teodor Lask was reading and Sanne Droll was at the trestle and a yard's second person is a person of the yard, and Marek Vale was behind a board and could not enter one word of any of it.

They read him the rule. It took about ninety seconds and it was not unkind.

It goes in as its parts, with the sum, the date, the discount and the receiving party on each line, and the counterweight named to the people who will bear it and the number of people who will bear it, in the words those people used. And if a counterweight is named as a district, there is nobody to name it to, and the line does not go in at all.

"That is the rule you came to beat," said Ovid Merrow, after a while, "and it is my own paper, and I cannot enter it."

"Yes."

"Not because I will not."

"Because there is nobody to name it to. It is the first rule and it was the first consequence and it was the first thing said about it in a yard on the sixth of the harvest month by a rope-maker, and it has been the rule ever since, and it is going to be the rule when I am not here."

Ovid Merrow stood in a shed eleven feet by nine in a good coat and looked at a form with six ruled lines on it, and then he laughed, once, and it was not a bitter laugh and it was not a good one either.

"That is the whole year," he said. "That is the entire year and you have all worked it out between you in four months and I have been selling paper in the same basin for nine years. A yard's method with a name on it that is a tank and a date. You cannot buy that. I could not have bought it off a bench in front of me and I said so in this month in a shed to a boy and I was not wrong.

"And I am going to tell you the two things and then I am going to go home, because I have not been in a shed at the Gate before this evening and I have not got a better sentence than the two.

"The first is that the shed on the low-ground road is open and it is open four hours a day and Signe Alve is in it at four a week and it is the best office in this basin for this work and it is better than mine, and I have said so in public twice, and I am not going to be a martyr about any part of this, because I built the first one. In about a month the price of an explanation in this city is going to be nothing and there is no form on which anybody may be charged for asking, and the only thing I have lost this morning is that.

"The second is that I am not going to count them now," said Ovid Merrow, "and I want that in whatever record there is, and I know there is no record, because the only office in this basin that would take it is the one you cannot enter it in. I am not going to count the people in my own book, and the reason is that there is nothing left to sell them into, and if I am honest with myself in a shed in Tallow Ward at the sixth bell in the ninth month, that is the entire reason, and I have not got a better one, and I would have got a better one if I had been a worse man."

Ferrow said *no* to that, out loud, in two words, and would not give a reason, and he did not ask her for one, and neither of them thanked the other, and about eleven people in that shed watched two people who had worked together for nine years fail to say one true thing about each other and then go up the lane together.

They put a notice on the Gate shed door at about the seventh bell, in a hand that was not a clerk's and was cut in a copy room on Bell Stair and brought down the hill by a boy, and it had a tank and a date at the head of it and nothing above them.

*Tallow Ward. Gate yard. The twentieth day of the rains month.*

And under it, four hours a day on whatever days it opens, nothing charged, anybody may ask, and a bundle may not be entered as one line, and the eighteenth of the ninth month, which was the first date that thing had ever had and was nine days away.
