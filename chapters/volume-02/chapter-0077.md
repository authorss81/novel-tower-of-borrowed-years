# Chapter 77 — The Window in the Trees

He came up the road on the second of the month with a pair of borrowed boots in a bag and slept on a bench over the press and was up before the light, as he had been told to be, and Malnar was already out on the middle strip with a ladder and a grafting pot and did not say good morning.

The frost had gone off the terrace. What it had left was a soft brown mess on the ground between the rows where the windfalls had been lying when it came, and the smell of it by the eighth bell was the smell of a cellar, and it went up the hill with the sun on it.

"You are right about the boots," said Malnar, from about thirty feet up. "And you are wrong about the coat, and I have not got a coat to lend you, so put that in whatever you are keeping."

---

The press was worked by a woman of thirty-nine called Halda Brnec who had had it since her mother stopped, and there was nobody else on the holding doing anything to the fruit, and on the second of the month she was four days behind a frost that had taken a fortnight of pressing out of a season that is twelve weeks long, and she said so before anybody asked her, and she said it while she was doing it, and she did not stop.

"Eleven days of pressing is what I pledged in the spring," she said. "Eleven days out of my own hands, in the season, at the ordinary wage, and it falls due on the eleventh and I have known it since the spring. The screw went in the middle of the season and the heat is not where it should be and a crew came and went in the first month of it and I am doing it with two men who are also doing it on the holding two miles down. Eleven days is not enough and it was never going to be enough and the factor knew it when he took it, and he wrote it down twice, and I read that page."

She put her hands in a tub of water to the wrist, which is not a thing a presser does in the cold and is a thing a presser does when she is going to talk.

"Malnar has it as surety," she said. "He signed in the spring on the back of it, and I did not ask him to and he offered before I had finished asking, and he has not read the schedule and I have not read the schedule, and neither of us has read it, and I have been living on that since the seed month."

---

The instrument was in a tin box in the front room of the Under-Row and it was one sheet and it had a schedule annexed to it of fifty-two lines, and Marek read the schedule because he had been told there would be one and because he had come up a road a day to read one.

It was the Share's assignment schedule. Fifty-two lines, each one a household and a rate period and a sum and a discount and a date and a signature, total not stated in any column he could find, and the fourth line was a holding of forty-one acres in the Red Uplands called the Under-Row, and the line was dated the fourteenth of the second month, MR 412, and the receiving party was not a person but a house in Crown March, and the thing he had bought for four hundred and ten pounds in the bad spring was the receiving end of that line, or something in it, and Malnar had guaranteed a woman's advance on a schedule that said so in the fourteenth line from the top.

Nobody had lied to him. That was the part that stayed.

Marek read it twice and then sat down on the bench in the front room with it in his hands and worked out, on his own, in about four minutes, the whole of the rest of it. The claim he had bought was the security. It was unproducible. The district had restated the flood quarters on the eighteenth and then gone back to work on the twentieth and the low ground's ratepayers had assigned their collection rights to a house over a chandler's shop forty miles away, and the thing he was standing surety on was a piece of paper that had been sold twice since he put his name to it, and the paper was the reason there was anything on the sheet at all, and a house in Crown March had not told him any of that because there was no obligation on anybody to tell him, and the correct sentence is that nobody had any duty and that was the whole of the design.

He could not tell him. He had gone into that yard ten days ago and told him not to read the schedule, and the reason he had told him not to read it was that the schedule was harmless to a man who was not a surety, and it is the same document.

---

On the eleventh of the month the factor's man was due at the Under-Row, and Halda Brnec's eleven days of pressing fell due on the same morning, and Marek had been on the ground since the second and had been waiting for a day, and on the ninth he got one.

He did not decide to do it and he wants that said, because the day before this he did one thing on a cart and has thought about the difference every day since. There is no moment of decision. There is a morning, and a frost-bitten heap, and a woman four days behind, and eleven days that are the only days, and a man with no office who has been up and down this road twice in a week and has nothing to do and can do one thing, and puts his hand on the tin box, and holds the settlement — the eleven days, the pressing, the event on the morning of the eleventh — and holds it.

Three days. Which is the most he has ever held and about a third of what he wanted, and he knows the arithmetic on that now, because a hold is a store and the store is in him and there is a limit to it that is not a matter of courage, and the limit arrives without asking him anything and is not the same limit twice.

The window in the trees is ten days long. The last ten days of the growth month, and a graft goes in properly in the last ten days, and the fruit goes down and the press goes and both of them are in the same ten days, and there is no version of this where you get one of them back.

---

Nobody knew. That is the whole of what a hold is and he had learned it once properly in a cart yard and learned it twice as well in an orchard.

Halda Brnec pressed on the fourth and the fifth and the sixth. The ladder went up and down the middle strip. Malnar grafted, because the grafting is his trade and the crew had not come and would not now come, and he did the top rows himself and put his own graft in on the sixth at the top of the terrace with his own hands, and it took.

On the seventh Marek went down off the terrace with the borrowed boots in the bag, because there was a cart on the low-ground road that wanted doing at the second bell and the road up is a day's road and he had been told so twice before he believed it. He did that one thing, and came back up on the ninth, and the two of them are not the same and he has had two days to find out why and has not.

While he was down, two men came from the holding two miles down, and then three, and a cart came up the terrace road with sacking on it and no fruit on it, for the fruit, and a boy's mother came as far as the top of the lane and said that the fruit was not going to be pressed this year and that the animals were going to have it. Malnar told him all four of those things in about ninety words on the evening of the ninth and did not editorialise on any of them.

On the eleventh at the first bell, a Crown March factor's man came up the road with a book and did not find anybody to sign for anything, and went down again, and Marek watched him go down from the top of the terrace and he could have stopped him and did not, because stopping him was not what he had come to do and he had been on this ground since the second.

On the eleventh at the second bell the hold ended, and there was no noise and nothing to see and no mark anywhere, and a settlement came due on a holding forty-one acres up a terrace in the Red Uplands, and a woman who was nine days behind pressed fruit that was going to rot, and she had eleven days of her own hands owing and there were not eleven days left in the season, and the days had to be found in a different year.

And the chain walked, and it walked in the ordinary way.

The borrower first, and the borrower could not pay, not because she would not and not because she had anything, but because a pledge of days is a pledge of days and the days were in a season that had gone, and there was not one day in the season left to pledge and the advance stands against the value of the crop and the crop was not made. Then the surety, and the surety was a man of forty-nine on a ladder, and he came down it.

"There is a figure," said Halda Brnec, "and I will pay it, and I have been trying to pay it since the seventh, and I want to say one thing about it in front of a witness and I want the witness to be the man from the low ground because he has been on this holding since the second of the month and he has not once pretended to be asleep.

"I was four days behind on the eleventh and I would have been four days behind on the twelfth, and on the thirteenth, and there was no twelfth of the season. That is the whole of it. Not the frost. The frost is a bad thing and everybody knows it is a bad thing and the frost is not anybody's fault and the frost did not do it. The season has twelve weeks in it and the press is the second half of it and the screw went in in the middle of the season and the heat is not where it should be, and that is mine, and I did it, and I have a page of my own on it that I did read.

"And the frost killed the windfalls in the bottom of the strip and I did not get to them, because I was behind on the top, and the top is where the press is, and I have been behind on the top since the ninth of the month. And the reason I have been behind on the top is that the fruit on the top was the best on the holding and came down first and it is the fruit that pays the screw and the screw has to be paid before the rest goes down, and that is correct, and I would do it again, and it is the reason there is no eleventh day."

She wrung her hands out.

"Nobody made me do that. There is no rule that made me do that. I did it because I am a presser and that is the order fruit comes down in."

---

Marek had come up a road for one day and stayed nine, and there is a version of this in which he stayed three and went back down on the fourth, and he did not go back down on the fourth, and there is a version in which the hold came on the seventh instead of the ninth and he did not know on the seventh that it was going to be the ninth, and he has gone over both of those a great deal since and he is going to keep going over them, and it makes no difference at all, which is the finding.

What he bought was three days out of ten. What it cost was ten.

That is the lesson and it is the same lesson as the cart and it is only visible the second time, and it is this: a hold delays perfectly. It is the best instrument in this city. It has never once been late and it has never once been wrong and it did exactly what it was asked to do, on time, to the letter, for three days, and it could not touch one single legal consequence of a single thing that is any of this about.

Malnar's claim was gone on the twentieth of the seed month and the hold did not bring it back. The low ground's ratepayers had assigned their rights on the sixth and the hold did not unassign them. Halda Brnec's advance was a year old and lawful and the schedule was annexed to it in the spring and the hold did not take the schedule off the sheet. When the eleven days came due there was no room in the argument for a man from Bell Stair to have held them, because nobody had asked him to and nobody would have accepted it, and there is no form in the kingdom on which a man with no office enters that he has made a season late.

He got nothing. There is no other way to say it. The cart went on the sixth bell and four bells had bought one load of quicklime and a man's morning. The eleven days came on the second bell of the eleventh and three days had bought nothing at all, and the reason they bought nothing is that there was nothing on sale.

---

There is a vault book of marks on the second floor of the Chamber and it is kept by a man called Osk and there is no fee.

There would be two entries in it now, one made on the eighth of the growth month and one made on the ninth, and the first of them is four bells and the second of them is three days, and both of them would be in a shorthand that a clerk who has been hurt at work can read and a man who has not cannot, and neither of them would say what it was for, and he was not going to ask and he was not going to write anything down about it, and there was no rule about any of that and it was all of his own.

Malnar came down off the ladder at the fourth bell and stood in the lane between the strips and put his hands in his coat, and did not look at Marek, and did not ask him anything, and said:

"Now I am going to tell you what I think happened, and you are going to stand there and let me say it, because you have been on this holding since the second of the month and you have not once asked me a question about money, and a man who does not ask about money on a holding is either a fool or a man being careful with somebody.

"The fruit went soft in the top strip in a week and I did not press it because the press is mine and I have had it since my mother stopped and it is not good for this year, and it is good for every year after this one, and I chose the press. And that was the season. And a man out of the Crown March road came up here on the eleventh and did not find anybody, and that is the only reason it went on my side of the sheet instead of on hers, and I am aware of what I am saying, and I would say it to a yard, and I have said it to you, and I would like you to go down that road and not say anything."

Marek said nothing.

"That is right," said Josip Malnar. "And I would like to say the other thing as well, and I am aware that it is not fair. There is a factor on this holding who has been careful with me for three years and who has never once told me a false thing, and he is a hundred and forty miles up the Crown March road, and it is not possible that he did not know what a schedule says, and it is possible that he had no reason to think a man who bought a flood-quarters claim at a good price in the bad spring had ever read one, and that is not his fault, and if it is anybody's fault it is mine, and I have not read it, and I have told you twice that I have not read it, and I said it before you came."

"I know you have not," said Marek.

"You do not know what I have not read," said Malnar. "I have not read it because I did not want to know. And that is the last thing I am going to say about money on this holding this year."

He went up the ladder again, and got four rungs up, and stopped, and looked down at a man in borrowed boots standing in the middle of forty-one acres with a tin box under the arm that had cost him ten days of a year.

"You are welcome to stay the night," he said, "and I would take it as a kindness if you did not tell me anything you have come up here to find out, because I have found out everything and I have had it in my hands for ten months, and I am tired of it, and I have not got anybody to be tired of it with."
