# Chapter 100 — Nothing at the Head of It

The register went live on the eighteenth of the ninth month, and it is the first morning, and by the fourth bell there were about eighty people in the lane outside the Gate shed and the shed holds twenty-two.

The notice on the door had been up since the seventh bell on the ninth with a tank and a date at the head of it and nothing above them, and somebody had put a second notice under it in a different hand on the sixteenth, which said *the eighteenth*, and had got the date wrong by being right, and nobody has taken it down.

*Four hours a day on whatever days it opens. Nothing charged. Anybody may ask. A bundle may not be entered as one line.*

There was no total, and there is never going to be one, and the yard said out loud in the seed month why a total is a number of people who agreed with something, and about nine people in Tallow Ward have read that sentence off a nail and about forty have had it said to them at a trestle.

Teodor Lask is nineteen and he reads things out twice, because that is his office now and nobody appointed him to it, and on the first morning of a live register he said out loud to the lane, about eleven times in four hours, the only true thing anybody had.

"There is a line in here in a hand for every person who has come through that door," he said, "and a bundle is not one line, and a hand is the only kind of entry there is. There were forty-one lines in forty-one hands on the fourth morning of looking in the harvest month and there are more in there now and I cannot tell you how many and neither can the room, and I am not going to stand in this lane and give you a figure, because a figure about a room like this one is a number of people who agreed with something and none of you have agreed to anything. You have been asked. That is the whole of it and it is not nothing."

---

The trestle was out in the yard and not in the shed, because a levy is not a secret, and it was the third week of the collection, and there was a box and a slate with no total on it and two people of the Share behind it, and about forty people came over and put coin in, and seed in the bottom of sacks.

The levy is capped. A member who cannot pay in money may pay in kind. A member who has nothing pays nothing and is not entered.

The girl was at the trestle at about the fifth bell, alone, and she is sixteen, and she has her mother's coat, and this time she brought a slate.

She put the slate on the trestle face up, in her own hand, and it said her name, and under her name it said that she had nothing and that her mother could not do days and that the book says that is allowed.

Bessok Tan looked at the slate for about four seconds and then he did the only thing he has ever done in front of a person, which is to put the roll down on the trestle, flat, and leave it there.

"I am not going to write your name on this," he said, for the second time in a season. "Your mother is on the board and she is a member and the cap is hers and I would give it to her again this afternoon. And there is nothing at this trestle that can ask you anything and there is never going to be, and you have written it on a slate and brought it down here on your own, in your own hand, and that is worth more than anything I could put on a roll, and I am not going to pretend that it changes anything."

"Will somebody ask me," said Wenna Drel.

"No," said Bessok Tan. "Nobody is going to ask you and I am not going to and the yard is not going to, and I have said that out loud on a nail and in a room and at this trestle twice now, and if anybody ever does ask you then it will be a different thing and you can say no to it, and that is the whole of what I have got to give anybody in this yard today."

She took the slate back and went and stood in the shed and did not ask anybody anything, and read nothing, and stood about four feet off the counter for about a quarter of an hour with a room of strangers in it, and then went home. Nobody followed her and nobody wrote anything down, and the cap is still a cap, and it is still the correct cap, and the person it does not reach is still standing at a trestle in a yard.

---

There is a board on the wall inside the shed, and it went up in the second week, and it is a list of the lines in the register, and the clerk who made it was a boy of fifteen from Ropewalk who is not a clerk at all and did it in a morning because there was nothing else to write on.

He ruled a column on it at the right-hand end, for the kind of paper each line is about.

Nothing is at the head of that column.

There was nothing at the head of it when he ruled it and there has been nothing at the head of it since, and it is the third such place in this basin in one year — a sheet cut off a seed sack in a keeper's house in the growth month, a column on a board in a shed in Tallow Ward in the seed month, and this — and the fourth is a column on a bench in a shed on the low-ground road that has been bare at the head of it since the day two painters were sent home, and nothing in this city has ever proposed a heading for any of the four of them.

Somebody proposed one on the eighteenth of the ninth month at about the fifth bell, in the ordinary way, out loud, the way a person proposes a thing in a room. He proposed *rate* for a set of them, and then *loan* for the rest, and he said it in a voice and not in a bad voice and he was not being clever and he had thought about it and he had a reason.

The room went quiet in a way that a shed of twenty-two people goes quiet.

Because the second line of the form is *what the person behind it said, in their words*, and the word *person* was cut into that line by a clerk on a hill in about nine seconds who told a yard to its face that he was not sure of it. And because the fourth line of the form has to carry the counterweight named to the people who will bear it and the number of them, and the rule the room is built on is that a bundle may not be entered as one line, and a heading over a column of people's papers is a line, and it is the one kind of line that cannot be entered at all, because there is no person behind it.

A rate is not a loan. A rate is a cost put in a box with no person in it, and a class is not a person, and a district is not a person, and a kind is a district with a smaller hat on, and every person in this room has said some version of that sentence out loud in the last four months and not one of them said it in a yard.

Nineteen people declined to name it.

It took about half an hour and nobody counted it out loud and nothing was written down, and one of the nineteen was a woman of forty-six from the weighbridge who cannot read and whose husband says figures out loud for her at counters and works the north road and is away for weeks, and one of the nineteen was a boy of fifteen who had ruled the column in the first place and who said *it is not mine to name* and went back to the back wall. Nobody was persuaded and nobody was argued with and no name was proposed a second time.

Then the yard voted, from the step of the tank, with about a hundred and forty people in the yard, in about four minutes, and the yard voted both ways and it is the second vote that matters.

Not to fill it, and not to rule it out. Not to name the column and not to say on the wall that the column will never be named, which is a heading too, and a promise about a heading, and a thing a room can be held to in a year by somebody who was not there on the day.

"A heading is the one thing on a sheet that cannot be put in afterwards by anybody who was not there on the day," said Aldis Kern. "That is what this yard voted in four minutes in the rains month, on a different sheet, on the same principle, and I am not going to let anybody tell me it is a different principle because it is a different board. There is a boy who ruled it in and he does not know what to call it and he is fifteen, and in four years he will be nineteen and he will still not know, and a man who wants to name that column can come back in four years and read the nineteen hands in the mouth of about forty people who were standing here, and there is no form on which any of us is anybody's record.

"And I am going to say the other part, because it is the ninth month and this is the morning it is live and about nine of you did not come in the last fortnight and Pike has no idea which nine and neither do we, and that is the first thing this room has cost anybody and it has not paid it.

"None of this makes a person safe to lend to. I said that in a yard in this month and I am saying it at this tank on a live morning. There is a room where a man can find out what he signed, and there is a queue of about eighty outside it, and this morning at about the fourth bell one of the two people who carry the yard's water went up the lane past all of you with two pailfuls and we all stood in a queue and watched her go and nobody asked anybody to move. And the fifth line of that form is the hours and the hours are nobody's paper and it is empty and it is going to be empty, and the sixth line is the days a person loses by standing in a room and there is no box in this kingdom for the days, and a woman of fifty-one came down eleven switchbacks out of the Red Uplands a week ago and got four lines onto that wall and lost a day and a half of walking and there is nowhere in this kingdom to write the day down. That is what we have built. It is a room. It is not a seed."

---

The refusal is still on the nail in the yard and it is still not in the register, and there is a space on the wall inside the shed where it would go if it could be entered, and the space is under a column with nothing at the head of it, and so it cannot go in there either, and the yard did not know that until the eighteenth of the ninth month and has not found a way round it and is not going to.

Kern said it from the step in about forty words and nobody wrote it down.

---

The lane filled again at about the sixth bell with about thirty people who had been sent away by the queue, and the reader put the lamp on at the seventh because the shed is not a warm building, and about nine people who were in the lane at the fourth bell were not in it at the seventh, and nobody knew which nine, and it is the second week that has happened and the room has still not found a way to know.

At about the sixth bell a man came down the lane with a piece of paper in his hand and stopped about nine feet off the counter, and he was about forty, and he is a household in the Tanner's yard and the ward knows him, and he has nothing, and he did not have anything this morning either and had it at the fourth bell and did not at the fifth.

The paper in his hand was a rate arrears on a plate on a shed door between the Gate and the north road, and the sum on it was forty-three and some months, and the sum was not his and he had never been asked about it, and there was a house at the end of it that he has never heard of.

The person in the lane with the table was not a house. She had a form on the table, and a bundle of them under the table, and the form was the Compact's assignment form, and a form is a sheet of paper, and a form is not a house, and taking up an assignment of a rate arrears is a thing a licensed house may do and there has not been a licensed house in this basin since the ninth of this month.

She had been about eleven feet from the counter for about four minutes when Ovid Merrow came out of the shed on the low-ground road side, because he had been in there since the fifth bell with a bundle of about nine sheets and a schedule of fifty-two lines on a bench, and he came out in a good coat and stopped about six feet from the man.

"That is my house's form," he said, "and I am going to say four things in about fifteen seconds and none of them is going to help you and I would like you to have them in this order.

"The form is not my house. A sheet of paper is not a house and a bundle of them under a table is not a house, and I have no licence this morning, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person who is not a house may be stopped from using a sheet of paper, and there is no form on which a person may be stopped, and both of those are the same fact.

"That paper is a rate arrears on a shed and the box that takes it is the return box and it is the correct box and it has been the correct box for eight years, and it is correct this morning as well, and nobody in this city can say one word against it, including me, and I have been the only man in this basin who could explain a piece of paper for nine years and I cannot explain this one to you for free because you have not come to a room and asked.

"I am not going to witness it. I would be permitted to if I asked, and the reason I am not is that a witness has to understand the terms and must not be paid by the borrower, and the terms on that sheet are my house's terms and the form is my house's form, and I cannot stand on one side of a paper and hold the other side of it up. That is not a kindness. I want that understood. It is a rule and it is an old one and it has cost me money this morning before I have sold anything at all.

"And I am not going to stop you and I could not if I tried, because a man in a lane may sign anything and there is nobody in this basin who has the standing to tell him not to, and the reason there is nobody is that a ward is not a person and a shed is not a person and a district is not a person, and the three of those are the only three things that could have been asked."

The man looked at the table for about four seconds and then he signed.

Nobody shouted. There were about thirty people in the lane and about nine of them watched it and about twenty-one of them were in the shed being read to and did not see it, and Teodor Lask was reading a fourth line out loud about a rate on a member's ground and did not stop, and he heard it and read the fourth line again, twice, at the ordinary speed.

Merrow went back inside. He did not say anything else, and nobody thanked him, and he has not said since that it was the right thing to do, and he has not said since that it was a bad thing either, and a person who was in that lane at the fourth bell and was in it at the seventh has said, to about four people on the causeway, that the man who came out of that shed and stood next to a hungry man for a quarter of a minute and then went back in without stopping him is the reason the shed has a queue, and also that the hungry man is going to be poorer, and that both of those are the same morning, and that he does not have a way of being on one side of it and is not going to pretend otherwise.

Marek Vale was at the counter, and the counter is two trestles and a board with a form on it, and he is the reader of that register because a yard said so out loud at a tank on the ninth of the harvest month and refused to put it in ink.

He watched the whole of it. He had about nine feet of lane and about nine seconds in which he could have walked out and said one sentence, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be stopped, and the rule of the register is that anybody may enter a line about their own paper and nobody may enter a line about anybody else's, and the man in the lane is the person behind that paper and he was not in the room and is not going to be.

He did not go out. He wrote nothing down, because there is nothing to write it on, and the register keeps no total and has a form with six ruled lines of which four have ever had a hand on them, and the fifth is the hours and it is empty because the hours are nobody's paper, and the sixth is empty for ever because there is no box in the kingdom for the days a person loses by standing in a room, and this man is going to lose a day he did not stand, standing in a lane, on a wet morning in the ninth month, and there is nowhere on that form, or on any other in this basin, where that day can go.

Then he read the first line out again, twice, at the ordinary speed, which is what a room does.

---

The shutter went over the tank at the seventh bell and about eighty people in the lane went home, and a trestle with a box on it and a slate with no total on it stood in the yard, and there was no water at the Ropewalk standpipe, and a slate with a girl's name on it was lying on a trestle where she had left it and Bessok Tan had not put it in the box and had not thrown it away and has it still.

Inside the shed there were forty-one lines in it as of the fourth morning of looking in the harvest month and a great many more than that now and no figure at all, and a fifth line for the hours that is empty because the hours are nobody's paper, and a sixth line that is empty for ever, and a board on the wall with a column at the right-hand end of it and nothing whatever at the head of it, and nineteen hands that declined to name it, and a yard's vote in four minutes not to name it and not to rule it out, and a space underneath where a yard's own refusal would go if a yard could be entered, and it cannot.

And the ninth month went on, and the frame on the wall of the levy office in Bell Stair had one name under its last line, and the district account was held by nobody, and the recited carry was still unapplied, and forty-three and some months was still on a plate on a shed door at a discount with a house at the end of it, and a lamp in Rainmarket was out until the third month, and a man of fifty-eight was at home and had stopped, and a woman of thirty-four was working sixty-one trays twice a day in a cold shed in the dark, and a boy of nineteen was in a shed in Tallow Ward reading out a fourth line twice at the ordinary speed, and in a lane outside there was a piece of paper with a fresh signature on it, and it was not a bundle, and the rule that beats bundles had nothing to say about it, and a register is not a seed.
