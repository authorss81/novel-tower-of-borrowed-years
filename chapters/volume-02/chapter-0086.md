# Chapter 86 — The One They Cannot Enter

The yard was told on the evening before, in the ordinary way the yard is told things, which is that somebody said it out loud at the tank and about forty people heard it and about eleven of them said something before they went home.

Milan Vray had the objection and had it ready and it was the best thing anybody said that day, and the yard voted against it.

"Two months ago we agreed, in this yard, that a round which repeats is a schedule, and that a schedule is the third box, and that a schedule is a thing an office sends round and can send round again without asking anybody," he said. "And I have been turning this over since yesterday and I think we are now proposing to send one thing round, once, to a building, and to call it a register, and I would like the yard to notice that it is the same action with a nicer coat on and that the nicer coat is the part I do not trust.

"I am not against it. I am saying that if we do it we should do it knowing what it is, and that the boy should write down that we knew, and that we did it anyway, because I would rather we did it than not, and I would rather the yard's own minute in nineteen years said *we knew and we did it* than *nobody thought about it*."

"That is the first sensible thing anybody has said at this tank in four months," said Sanne Droll, "and it does not change my vote, and I am putting it in the yard's minute anyway, and the boy can have my words and not my hand, same as everything else."

---

The yard voted for it on the nineteenth day of the harvest month in about six minutes, and it was the first thing the yard had voted for since the twenty-nine answers went up on a nail, and nobody could afterwards agree on which of them had thought of it first, which is the yard's own condition and was applied to itself without anybody noticing.

"There is a document in this city that nobody in it can be shown to be the seller of," said Sanne Droll, "and it is hanging on a nail in a shed with a hole in the roof, and a man in a good coat has said in a lane that he has been trying nine years to get hold of a thing like that and has failed every time.

"And a register of risk that cannot hold it is a register with a hole in it exactly the shape of the best thing in this basin. So I am for putting it in, and I want to say before anybody says anything that I know the argument, and I have made it to myself twice on the way down here this morning, and I am still for it."

The yard carried it. Not a count written anywhere. A thing carried in a yard, which is what a yard is, and which is worth exactly as much as the yard it is carried in.

---

They did it properly, which is to say they did it in the open at the tank at the second bell with about eighty people in the yard, and the form was on a board on two upturned buckets, because the form is one sheet and one sheet does not go on the ground.

Sedge had brought it up the hill himself the day before and had not cut a second one, and the cutting of it had been done in the copy room and the six lines were ruled and the heading was the tank and the date, and the second line said: *what the person behind it said, in their words.*

Right. So somebody had to be the person behind it.

Marek Vale stood at the end of the board with the yard behind him and understood what was about to happen about nine seconds before it happened, and knew it, and stood there.

"Second line," said Sedge. "Who is the person behind the refusal on the nail."

"It is the yard," said Kern, from the step.

"Yes," said Sedge. "And I have to ask the yard what it said."

And then a yard did not answer, and the yard was not silent out of rudeness, and this is the part that took the whole of the next half hour and is the reason the register is going to live with a hole in it.

---

They tried everything, and every one of the things they tried was correct.

Somebody said the yard could name a keeper, the way a house names a person, and Droll said the yard has a captain and a captain is a person, and Sedge said then the entry is about Aldis Kern of the Gate yard, and the yard went quiet, and Droll said no before anybody had finished the sentence, and said it in a voice that stopped the discussion where it stood.

"If a man's name goes on that sheet," she said, "then that sheet can be produced at that man for the rest of his life. He is forty-four. He has got a plate on a door and a captain's job and eleven years of a tank and no more than that, and a man in a good coat has told this yard in a lane that he has never in nine years been able to get hold of a paper with nobody behind it, and we were the first, and this is how we lose it, in a yard, in nine minutes, by doing the sensible thing. No."

"Then I put the yard's name on it," said somebody. "Tallow Ward."

"And the second line wants a person," said Sedge, "and the entry wants somebody who can be asked whether the words are theirs, and a ward is not a person, and I have read your own rules this morning and the first one says *ask the household, not the yard*."

"Then nobody answers it," said Vray, "and the second line is empty, and the first line is the refusal and the second line is nothing, and that is an entry."

And a woman at the front said the thing that finished it, and she was not of the yard, and she had come down off the causeway that morning because somebody had told her there was a thing being entered, and her name was Marta Brel and she was forty-six and she could not read and had come because she wanted to know what her own line said and had been told it was not ready.

"That is the sack in Mirren's house," she said. "I have heard about it. I have not seen it and nobody showed me it, and I am told it is a piece of sack with eleven lines on it and nothing at the top of it and no column names and no total at the foot. Well. If you put that in here, then you have a piece of paper with nothing at the head of it in a building, and it is worse than it is in Fisk's house, because a piece of sack goes home with the woman who wrote it, and a piece of paper in a building is a thing somebody can come and ask about, and there will be a day when a man in a good coat asks this room for the entries with no name at the top of them, and the room will have to say yes, and there will be about nine of them by then."

Nobody said anything.

"And I have said my piece and I would like to go and find out about my own line," said Marta Brel, "and I have been here for twenty minutes, and I have not found out about my own line, and I would like it noted that nobody in this yard thought to tell me whether I was in it or not, and that I have been in this city thirty-one years, and that I am one of the fifty-two and I have known it since a woman came to my door in the seed month and said my own name out loud, and that I cannot read what it is I have signed, and that this room was supposed to be the place where I found that out, and it is a tank."

"Then it is," said Marek Vale.

"It is not, because there is nothing in it yet," said Marta Brel. "You have a form with six lines on it and a yard that cannot answer for itself, and you have had a day and a half, and I have walked down off the causeway to be told that the thing I came for has not been written down yet. I am not angry. I have been not angry about this since the seed month. I would like it written down in this yard that the woman at the weighbridge is on the list of fifty-two and cannot read, and that she came, and that she was sent away for a rule about a yard, and that she will come again because there is nowhere else in this basin to go."

---

Neris Kade had been at the back for the whole of it with her sheet under her arm and the sheet had nothing on it and it was not going to have anything on it, and when Droll turned round and said *somebody has to say this to him* she declined in four words.

"I am not going to."

"You are the only person here who is not a party to anything."

"That is exactly why," said Neris Kade, "and I have said the same shape of sentence in a Crown March record once this year in a room in Bell Stair and it is in a book four hundred miles from here and I cannot take it out of it, and I am not going to put it in a yard as well, in the same month, for the same reason, and be the woman who says the true thing twice in two places in one season and enjoys it."

Somebody said, mildly, from about the middle, that a man in a hall had said all of this once already.

"He has," said Neris Kade, to the yard, and not to him, and then stopped, and did not say anything else about it, and did not look at anybody.

Nobody thanked her, and Marek Vale did not thank her either, and she had not expected to be thanked, and the two of them would not refer to the rest of it either, in the yard or on the causeway or afterwards, for the rest of the year.

---

The entry was abandoned at the fifth bell and the sheet was not written on, and Sedge said so out loud, and ruled a line through the first of the six lines in a way that was not a cancellation, and Kern came out of the step and told the yard what that line meant, because the yard had voted at a tank in the fifth month of the year that a mark a yard invents for a thing it cannot ask about is a thing it will be doing in a year what it has spent a summer refusing to do.

Kern's meaning was: *asked once, and the answer stands, and the answer was that the register cannot hold it, and the answer is going to stand there and nobody is coming back.*

Then the yard did the thing it had been doing all summer, which was to be right out loud.

"Right, then," said Aldis Kern, to about eighty people in the open at a tank. "So the rule that has just stopped us is our own. Not the office's. Ours. Written on the back of a complaint sheet on the twentieth of the rains month by somebody at this tank who is not in this yard and whose name I have not said and am not going to say, and it is the first line, and it is four words, and it is *ask the household, not the yard*.

"And I said it. I have said it out loud at this tank twice this summer and I am not going to stand in front of eighty people and be found out having found it. I do not know who held the pen, this yard does not know who held the pen, and we are not going to find out, and that is the only reason the sentence is any good at all. It is mine and it is the best thing this yard has ever said, and it has cost us the only document in this basin that nobody can be traced to, and I would like that written on the nail next to it in my own words, and I would like it read out by the boy, and I would like it read out twice, and then I would like everybody to go home."

They read it out twice. Teodor Lask read it out twice. Nobody wrote a number on anything.

And the sheet stayed on the nail in the shed with a hole in the roof, with its two hands and a bit of string, readable by anybody who walked in and producible against nobody at all, which is the only kind of paper this basin has and the only kind that cannot go in the thing that was built to hold it.

---

She caught him at the shutter of the tank, at about the sixth bell, in the dark, and she had the sheet under her arm with nothing on it and she had been carrying it since the second of the seed month and had carried it up four terraces and down again twice, and she did not put it down.

"You are going to say that the rule was the yard's own," she said.

"I was."

"I know you were, because I have known it for an hour and a half." She was not unkind and she was not going to help him. "Do not do it in a room. Not tomorrow, not in the ninth month, not at a table with a name on it. You will get the argument into a record and the record will say that a rule about a yard stopped a thing, and in four years somebody will go looking for the rule and will find one that a yard wrote about itself in the rains, and the finding will be right and it will be useless, because the rule did not stop you. The room stopped you, and the room had a form in it with one word in the second line, and that word is yours, and a clerk cut it in nine seconds on the fourteenth of this month and told you himself that he was not sure of it."

Marek Vale stood at the shutter of a tank in Tallow Ward and did not say anything for long enough that the shutter came off its chain in the wind and hit the wall twice.

"Say that to me on a flat above a mill in the dark," she said, "or do not say it at all, but do not say it in a room, and do not put my name near it, because I am not going to be in it twice."

"I will not put your name near it."

"That is the first thing anybody has done for me this month and it is also the cheapest," said Neris Kade, "and I am not thanking you for either, and you are welcome, and I am going to walk down to the low ground now and look at the women I have examined this season, of whom there are a great many and not one of them has ever asked me a question, and I have told nobody in that yard that I have said this to you and I am not going to."

She went down the causeway at the pace of a woman who has a long day behind her and a longer one in front, and Marek Vale went up it, and neither of them said the rest of it, then or afterwards, and that was the end of that and it was the smallest of the four.

---

The register was going to live with a hole in it, and the hole was going to be the shape of the best piece of paper in the basin.

The yard's refusal was going to hang on a nail in a shed with two hands on it and a bit of string, available to anybody who walked in, and it was not going to be in the register, because a yard is not a person, and a ward cannot be asked, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody serves a notice on a yard, and the yard had written the first of those three things itself, on the back of a complaint sheet, at that tank, on the twentieth of the rains month, and had spent a month being proud of it.
