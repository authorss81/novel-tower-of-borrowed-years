# Chapter 97 — Under Her Own Name

She went up the upland road on the twelfth of the ninth month with a satchel and nothing in it, because there is a rule she enforces herself and has enforced for six years — a season examiner may not carry a lot she has examined — and the instrument in Hartlow is in her own district, and the instrument is a lot in the only sense that matters, and so she could not take it and would not have taken it if she could.

It is four terraces and a stone bridge and the road takes a day, and it goes out of the basin in eleven switchbacks and the frost took the middle out of it in three places years ago and nobody has ever had the money to put it back, and the cart goes at the speed of a cart.

The paper is in a room off the seed room in a stone building with a slate roof, on a table under a sheet of oiled cloth, and it has been there since a girl of fourteen found out at twenty-five that she had been on a roll her whole life and had never been shown it, and asked to see it, and was told no, and said *it was my life*, and was told *then whose thing is it for*, and the room was opened fourteen months ago.

The notice is nailed to the outside of the door and it has been read by four people in this basin.

*ANYBODY MAY COME IN AND SIT AND LOOK. NOTHING IS CHARGED. NOTHING IS SIGNED.*

Neris Kade read it standing in the snow on the twelfth of the ninth month and then went in and sat down, and the woman of fifty-one was already sitting in the room because she is always in the room, because the room is the only room in this valley where nobody has ever asked her a question.

---

Her name is Ruta Vrabec. She is fifty-one. She is on the roll of the Hartlow seed cooperative, which is a roll of six women, four of them over fifty and one of them a child, and the sixth line of that roll has an examiner's name on it and the examiner is not a member and has never been a member of anything.

She filled cones for nineteen years and then stopped and she has given no reason for it that anybody in this valley accepts, and the reason is the reason. She will not be a seed keeper. There are going to be four of them over fifty, and they are going to die, and after them there is a boy of nineteen who is better at it than any of them was at his age, and after him there is nobody, and the boy has been told twice this year that there is nothing after him and once too often.

"Read me the fourth clause again," she said. "Not the paper. The thing you said."

"The pledge is accepted as a class and not as an individual," said Neris Kade. "And the clause underneath it says: *and the seed-keepers of this village after us bear the keeping*."

"That is me and Tomek," said Ruta Vrabec, "and it has been me since I was thirty-two and I did not know it until about a minute ago, and I have not been asked, and nobody in this valley has ever asked me anything, and there are four women on that roll who would tell you the same thing about their own houses, and one of them is seventy-eight and I am not going to say her name to you in this room."

"I am not going to ask you a question," said Neris Kade.

"You are going to," said Ruta Vrabec. "You have been sitting in that chair for ten minutes with a satchel."

"I am going to tell you what I came for and then I am going to go away and let you decide, and I am not going to do it in a room, and there is a room in this basin four days away in a shed in Tallow Ward where anybody may enter a line about their own paper, in their own hand, and be read out in public, and it costs nothing, and there is a queue outside it from the second bell. And I have not told a single person in this village about it, and I am not going to tell you about it, and I am going to tell Janko about it, because he keeps the roll and he is the only one of us who ever goes to a bank in Bell Stair and that is two trips in a year and he does them out of duty and calls it the weather.

"That is the whole of what I have come for. It is a room. I am not asking you to be in it. I am telling you it is there."

Nobody was in it for about four minutes. There is a fire in it and a bench down one side and a table down the other, and the fire is the only warm thing for four miles.

"I will go down the road," said Ruta Vrabec.

---

Janko Vrabec came down on the cart with them, and he is fifty-eight and he is the last of the four terraces and he told them in the first minute in the growth month that he is going to die this winter, and he came down in the ninth month because the paper is on the cart under a board and he is not having it carried by a woman and a boy.

They came down the eleven switchbacks and across the flat part above the mill where the wind comes off the water, and they got to the basin on the evening of the thirteenth, and the road takes a day, and there is nowhere in this kingdom to put a day.

The notice on the shed door at the Tallow Gate had been up since the seventh bell on the ninth and had the tank and the date at the head of it and nothing above them, and it had a bucket holding one corner down, and about forty people had read it and about a third of them had come and stood in the lane.

*Four hours a day on whatever days it opens. Nothing charged. Anybody may ask. A bundle may not be entered as one line.*

The notice on the door of the room in Hartlow had been up for fourteen months and had been read by four people in this basin, and the one on the shed had been read by about nine hundred, and they are the same three rules, and one of them is nailed on with four nails by a man with a hammer and the other was cut in a copy room on Bell Stair by a man who says on the page that a form has to be cut by a person who does not know whether it is right.

Neris Kade read both, standing, and did not say anything about the difference, and nobody asked her to.

---

They were in the shed at the second bell on the fourteenth.

The register had forty-one lines in it in forty-one hands and a fifth line for the hours that is empty because the hours are nobody's paper and a sixth line that is empty because there is no box in the kingdom for the days a person loses by standing in a room. Teodor Lask was reading. Sanne Droll was at the trestle with the collector's box and a slate with no total on it. About twenty people were in it and there were about fifty in the lane and the queue was doing what a queue does, which is to take the road.

Marek Vale was behind the board and could enter one word of any of it.

Kade came into the shed and was named at the door.

"That is a season examiner of the Rainmarket and the upland season works and she is a second person and she is named here and she does not get a hand on anything," said Teodor Lask, to a room of about twenty people, because that is the rule and it is an old one and it is not the register's, and it is the second time in three days he has had to do it.

"I know the rule," said Neris Kade. "I have spent six years arguing that anybody can be told the rule out. I wrote one once, in a Crown March record, and it is not a good record and it is in it."

"You may come to the counter and you may not touch the form, and the fourth line is for anything anybody else has to say about it, and that is the only line in this thing that is yours," said Teodor Lask. "The rest of it is hers. I have the wall ready and I am going to put both of them up and I am not going to number them."

Then the room worked out who was going to enter the line, and it took about a minute, and Marta Brel was not there and nobody needed her.

Janko Vrabec is the keeper and the paper is his cooperative's paper and he wanted to enter it and could not, and he said so himself before anybody asked him, in about four words. A person may enter a line about their own paper. The counterweight on the paper is not the keeper. The keeper is the man who is left holding the drawers.

The second question is: is that your line, is that the person behind it, is it your words.

Ruta Vrabec said yes to the first and yes to the second, and then she stopped, and Teodor Lask, who is nineteen, waited, and did not read it twice, and did not prompt her.

"I have never in my life been asked a question about anything by anybody in this village or in a hall," she said. "I have been on a roll since I was nine. My name is on a paper I was not shown and a line I did not know about, and I found out about it at twenty-five because I was the one who asked, and it is me and the boy, and we are the two of us, and I am not going to be a seed keeper and he is going to be one and there is nobody after him and I would like that in the second line in my words because the second line is the one I am allowed to say things in.

"And I would like the number on it, because the form wants the number of the people who are going to bear it and the number is two, and one of the two is nineteen and did not choose it and the other one is fifty-one and is saying so in a shed in a ward she has never been inside."

---

She wrote it herself, on the counter, in the open, in a hand that is not a clerk's and not a lawyer's either, and the second line filled and the third line filled and she was not sure about the fourth so Lask read the second back to her from where she was standing and she corrected one word.

Kade's sentence went on the fourth line. It is the only line in the form that is hers, and it is in her own hand, and it has her name at the foot of it and the day, and nobody was asked for either and nobody could have been.

It says that the pledge was accepted as a class and not as an individual, and that the clause beneath it reads *and the seed-keepers of this village after us bear the keeping*, and that a class is not a person and she has known that for two years and it is the reason she wrote the sentence and the sentence is a confession and it is the only honest thing on the paper. That she certified the instrument at the second bell two years back in that room before the room was open, because there was no room. That the witnesses were real and were not paid by the cooperative and she checked them twice and one of them is dead and the other is eighty-one and does not live there any more and she has not written to him in a year and a half. That the paper is well made and the mechanism is lawful and every figure on it balances and nobody has to be punished for anything. That she saw what it was going to do, that it was going to leave one nineteen-year-old holding four terraces of seed drawers in ten years, and she saw it at the time and wrote a sentence about it and went on certifying.

And then the last two lines of it, which are: that she has had two years to write this sentence in a room and did not, and that she is not asking anybody for anything, and that if anybody in this basin ever says that an examiner was wrong about a class then somebody in this basin is going to have to read this entry first, and it will be on a wall in a shed in Tallow Ward with a name at the foot of it, and it cannot be taken off, because she asked for it to go on the same sheet as the entry and not on a paper of its own.

"I have asked for one thing," she said, "and it is not forgiveness and it is not for me. I have asked for it to be on the same sheet, in the open, with my name at the foot of it, because a correction on a paper of its own is a thing that can be lost and this one is going to be standing next to a woman's sentence in a room that anybody may walk into, and in a year somebody is going to read both of them and they are going to have to read them in the wrong order to make me look better than I am."

Nobody in that shed said thank you and she had not come to be thanked and she had said so on a road in the dark two months ago and she was not going to be thanked now either.

---

Marek Vale read the entry out.

He is the reader of that register because a yard said so out loud at a tank on the ninth of the harvest month in about a hundred and forty people's hearing and refused to put it in ink, and nobody can take the four hours or the road or the words off him, and there is no paper of it anywhere.

Every line in that register is read twice. The second time slower, which is a thing the room does because of a girl of sixteen who stood at a counter in the harvest month and a keeper on an island and a day in the growth month.

He read hers once, and stopped, in a room of about twenty people and fifty in a lane, and stood there.

"Read it again," said Neris Kade.

"It is read twice."

"Then read it twice," she said, "and do it the way you do it, and do not do it slower, and do not do anything else. I have had this sentence in a drawer in my own head for two years and it has never been said out loud in a room by anybody but me, and if you make a thing of reading it twice then it is a moment, and it is not a moment, and I would like it to be the ordinary reading, and I am asking you for the ordinary reading because I have not got a better claim than anybody else in this room and that is the whole of what I have."

He read it the second time at the ordinary speed and in the ordinary voice, and she stood at four feet off the counter and did not stop him, and neither of them looked at the other one, and it was smaller than the mill and it was the smallest thing either of them has ever asked for and neither of them will refer to it again.

---

The sixth line is the days a person loses by standing in a room, ruled, and empty, and it was empty on that morning and it is empty now, and there is a woman in Tallow Ward who has lost a day and a half of walking in the ninth month to stand in a shed and get four lines onto a form, and a boy of nineteen who has lost the same day and a half, and a man of fifty-eight who has lost the same day and a half and is going to die this winter, and the sixth line is the one place in this basin where any of those three things could be written down, and there is no box in the kingdom for the days.

"That is the part I am angry about," said Neris Kade, at the counter, to a room, "and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it again this year. Not the class and not the paper and not the four hundred times I have read that sentence in the dark. The days. A man in a copy room cut that line in about nine seconds and told a yard to his face that he was not sure of the form, and he was right, and it is the right shape, and it is the biggest hole in the thing and he said in a yard that every person in it would find it inside a week, and Ruta Vrabec has found it, and she came down eleven switchbacks to find it.

"So: it is a room, and it costs nothing, and it is the only place in this basin where a person can be asked what they are holding, and it is four miles and a day away from the only village in this basin that has been doing it for fourteen months, and there is no form on which anybody writes down the walking."

Janko Vrabec said the thing that ended the morning, and he said it to the room and not to anybody in it.

"I have a notice on a door in Hartlow," he said, "and it has been on it fourteen months, and it was the first thing anybody in this basin ever did like this and there are four people in this basin who have read it. I put it on because a woman of twenty-five asked to see a paper and I said no. That is the whole reason it exists and it took me a fortnight and a hammer and four nails and I would put it on again in the same week.

"You have taken four days to get one line out of a room that has been open eight days," he said, "and I am not doing the arithmetic out loud because there is a form in this shed and a rule on it about totals, and the rule is right. I am going to say one thing and then I am going back up the hill in the morning because I have a house to shut.

"You did not bring anybody up this road. You went up yourself and you came down yourself and you asked nobody to carry a thing. And in a yard in Tallow Ward there is a woman with a rope who has been losing a day of selling every week since the ninth of the harvest month, and there is a woman in Ropewalk who has never been thanked for standing at a counter, and there is a girl of sixteen in this basin that nobody has ever asked whether she minded. So we are not the only ones carrying it. We are just the only ones you could name, and I am not blaming you for that, and I am not letting it stand in this shed either, because if it stands in here it becomes the story of a woman who is fifty-one and came down a hill."

Nobody thanked anybody. The shutter went over the tank at the seventh bell, and about thirty people in the lane went home, and the register had forty-two lines in it in forty-two hands, and a name at the foot of the fourth line of the forty-second, and a day, and a bit of string on a nail four miles away with four nails in it, and a light in a stone building on the second terrace.
