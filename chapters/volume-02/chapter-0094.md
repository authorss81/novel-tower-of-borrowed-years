# Chapter 94 — Cannot Choose

The notice went up on the Gate tank on the morning of the seventh of the ninth month, wet, in a hand that was not a clerk's, because no person in this city can serve a notice on a yard.

A ward cannot petition, appeal, be sworn or be cross-examined. That was proved out in this basin in a room in the rains by a woman from the Fund and it has never been said otherwise since, and one of its consequences is that the finding of a board, whatever the board decides, reaches four hundred and six people as a sheet somebody's hand is holding, at a tank, in the weather, over three boards that cannot be carried into a room and have never been able to.

The three boards were the yard's roll of the pail water, the oiled sheet of the forty items of the fourteenth with the Underbank at the top of it, and the two sheets of the answers. The notice went on above them, and it was weighted with a stone, and it was still there in the evening, and the next morning somebody's grandmother had put a cup on the corner of it to hold it down, and nobody moved the cup for two days.

---

The second-floor hearing room in Bell Stair was built for thirty.

It held about ten. The rest of it was a corridor and a stair, and they were full, and they were full in the rain, and at about the second bell there were about four hundred people in that building and about nine in the room, and the room has a door that closes and the door was shut, and nobody in the corridor could hear anything that was said inside it, which is a fact about the day and not a decision anybody took.

The rope on the stair was Ilya Senn's, and he put it there himself at the fifth bell the night before, and he is a Lantern Warden, forty-one, twenty-two years, and he maintains the pumps and the locks and the emergency lamps of this basin and he has never in his life asked to be in a proceeding, and he said two sentences to a crowd of four hundred people on a landing, which were that the stair was narrow and that he would not carry anybody down it against their will, and which were received by about two hundred people as an insult and by the rest as fact.

Bel Harrow presided and was ineligible to be chair, which is written at the top of every minute he has served since the ninth of the seed month in his own hand. Sedge for the Chamber. Kern for the ward, in his own name, under a standing provision older than anybody in the room. Vendl for the Fund. And the Trust's seat empty, for the fifth time, and the Trust had not sent anybody, and the office's second refusal still used the words *in the ordinary course* and they were still in quotation marks in a minute in a drawer in this building.

Harrow had no clerk and could not get one, and served the minutes himself, in his own hand, and it takes him about four minutes a page.

Neris Kade had the second seat, which is not a membership and has never been a membership. Her sheet's one function was to hold four figures in the order they were called out, and it was blank when she came in, and by the sixth bell it had four figures on it, and they were these.

Forty thousand nine hundred. Forty-three and some months. One hundred and forty a season. Four hundred and six.

She wrote them in the order they were called and in the words nobody used, and she did not put a total at the foot, and she did not put a sign between the first and the second, and when the Chair asked her at about the fifth bell whether the sheet was an argument or a record she said it was a record and that she had been asked to hold figures and not to hold a view, and that if she had held a view it would be a view with an examiner's name at the top of it, and a column with an examiner's name over it becomes a thing an office says the examiner agreed to, and she said that in a room in Crown March this year already and it cannot be taken out.

---

What the room was asked to do was choose.

Not to pay. Not to refuse. Not to find a fault. Choose, between two things that are both charged against Tallow Ward's district account in the ninth month and are going to be charged in the same week, and one of them is on the frame on the wall in the levy office and the other of them is on a plate on a shed door between the Gate and the north road.

Harrow put them in the room himself, and he put them in flatly, and then he stopped and did not say a word for about four minutes, and the four minutes were the hardest four minutes of that day and nobody in the corridor knew they were happening.

"The first is the recited carry," he said. "Forty thousand nine hundred. Entered once a year against the district account since before the flood years and never applied to anything, because there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that asks whether a fund exists. The water office has asked for it to be applied in the ninth month and has asked for the district to be charged and the charge entered. Applying it requires the district account to be held to its presented figure. That is a cut. The water office is permitted one such order and holds no copy of it, and the officer who enters it is the officer of the levy office, and that officer has told this table nothing and has told me nothing, and I am not going to ask him in here.

"The second is a district claim. Forty-three and some months, at a discount, presented against the same account, with a receiving party at the end of it that is a house in Rainmarket, and the party at the head of it is a shed, and a shed is not a household, and a shed cannot be asked. It arrived on the plate on the sixth. The man whose plate it is offered in writing to pay it out of his own and the offer is in a book that no person may be shown, and I have been told about that book by a man with no office who cannot produce it either.

"I am going to ask the table to choose. And I am telling you now, before anybody speaks, that I do not know how it is going to end and that I have not got a preference, and that a Crown March representative is not a member of this board and cannot vote in a money matter, and that four of you can."

---

They could not do it, and it took about two hours, and what stopped them was not a disagreement.

The disagreement was there. Vendl wanted the first thing dealt with because a sum that has been standing for three hundred years is not a claim and ought to be written off, and Kern said he was not going to be in a room that wrote off a district's debt on the ground that it was old, because a debt that is old is exactly what a yard is. Sedge wanted the second one produced and was told by three people at once that a rate may be read and a copy of a rate is a return and the water office could not issue one out of that counter if there were a form for it, and there is not.

The thing that stopped them was a thing about the shape of the two.

"They are not two of a kind," said Sedge, in about the fourth minute, and he is a third-grade clerk with no service length anywhere in this basin and he was the first person in the room to see it and it took him four minutes. "One of them is a number and one of them is a sum. One of them has been standing for three hundred years and there is no party to it at either end, and if you apply it, it comes out of the account. The other one has got a party at both ends and a discount in the middle, and if you apply that, the house gets the money and the shed stops owing it.

"They are not two of a kind. They are two different instruments that have been put in the same place because there is one place for a rate to go in this basin and no others."

"Then the board is being asked for something it cannot do," said Neris Kade, from a seat that is not a membership, and it was not a motion, and nobody thanked her for it and she had not expected to be.

"Go on," said Bel Harrow.

"I am going to say one thing and it is the only thing I have got and I have had it since the sixth of the month and I have not put it in a room because a Crown March record already has the same shape of sentence in it this year and it cannot be taken out.

"This table is not being asked which of two charges the ward can bear. It is being asked to compare them, and a comparison needs them to be the same kind of thing, and they are not, and the room has worked that out in about four minutes and there are nine of us in here and there are four hundred people on that stair who are going to be told this morning that a board was asked to choose and declined to.

"And a declining, in nine months, is going to be read as a refusal to decide. It is not. It is a finding that two things that are not comparable have been put in front of a body with one seat standing empty. And I would like that in the finding in those words. And I am not a member and I cannot move it and I cannot vote and I have been told twice this year that a column with my name over it becomes a thing an office says I agreed to, and so I am going to say it to the table and not put my name on it, and if the Chair writes it down I would rather he wrote down that it came from a seat that is not a membership, and if he does not, I would rather he did not, and I am not going to say which."

Harrow wrote it down, and he wrote down where it came from, and it is the fourth time that has happened in a minute of a board of five and every one of the four times has cost somebody something.

---

The work of the morning was done by a term, and the term was term four, and it was about eleven minutes.

*A member who dissents may have the grounds written in her own words, in the finding, and the duty to write them is the board's and not the member's.*

Kern had his objection by eleven o'clock and he gave it in the first person, standing, at the end of a table, in about a room of ten, and it was five sentences long and it did not have one word in it that had been written down before.

"I am the captain of this ward's water and I have been refused nine hundred and forty pounds in the seed month and I have said no to it in this ward's own words and it is on a nail with a string through it, and I am not going to be asked again by a smaller thing.

"You have got two charges and you have asked which one this ward can carry. I am telling you that the question is not mine to answer and not yours either, and I will give you the reason in one sentence and then I am going to sit down, and the reason is that a yard cannot be assessed a rate at all, and that has been true in this basin since before I was captain, and a body that cannot be assessed cannot be asked what it can carry, and if you ask it anyway and get an answer, the answer is a man talking, and a man's mouth is not a ward.

"The second one is not ours at all. Forty-three and some months belongs to a shed, and the shed cannot be asked, and it is on our roll because a yard is the only account in this basin that can take a stranger's uncollectable private sum without anybody's name going anywhere. We did not do it and we cannot undo it and there is no form on which a yard may say no to a sum arriving on it. If you want the second one off this account, you do it with a form, and there is no form, and I have been in this city forty years and I have never once heard of one.

"And the first one, I do not know what it is, and I have asked nobody, and I would like it written down that I have asked nobody, because in a year somebody will say that a yard captain in the ninth month knew."

Harrow read it back to him twice, the way he reads everything, and Kern corrected one word and did not correct anything else, and then Harrow did the thing term four requires of him, which is to write it in the finding in the member's own words with the duty to write it being his and not the member's.

And then the Chair did the other thing, and it is the reason this chapter is the chapter it is.

---

He took the paragraph out of the room.

He said he was going to write it again on the back of a complaint sheet off a trestle at the Gate tank, in the yard, in longhand, in front of the people it is about, and Kern said no before Harrow had got to the end of the sentence, and then took the no back, and said why.

"Take it out," he said. "Not because I want my words on a yard wall. Because a finding of this board is a Crown March record, and four hundred and six people in this city have never once been inside one and would not go if they were asked, and my objection goes in a building on a hill in a hand that a Crown March representative is lending me, and that hand is worth precisely nothing to anybody in Ropewalk and I am not going to be a man who says so in a yard and means it.

"And it goes on the nail," said Kern, "because the refusal is on the nail, and a yard's refusal and a ward captain's objection on the same nail, with a bit of string, is the only place in this city where somebody in the ninth month could stand in front of a thing they are actually affected by and be in the same place as it. That is not a procedure. There is no form for it. There is a tank."

It took about twenty minutes in the rain, at the fourth bell, with about a hundred and forty people in the yard and nobody in it holding anything over the Chair's head, and Harrow wrote on the back of a complaint sheet off a trestle with a carpenter's pencil that one of the tank-keepers found him, in longhand, at about a line to the page, and Kern stood beside him the whole time and did not read it upside down, which was a thing he could have done and did not, and which cost him something and which is not in any record.

It was read out twice, by the yard's reader, who was nineteen.

It went on the nail under the refusal and beside the sentence from the nineteenth of the harvest month, with a bit of string through it, and it is on that nail now, and anybody may lift it off, and there is no office behind it, and no clerk can be asked to produce it, and a Crown March representative wrote it, and that is the only thing in the world about it that anybody in this basin can check.

---

The finding was read out before the corridor went down the stair, and it said that the two charges were not comparable and that the board had no standing to adjudicate between them, and that neither had been paid and neither had been refused, and that the recited carry remained unapplied and the district claim remained on the account, and that the Trust's seat was empty for the fifth time.

Harrow asked, before he read it, whether anybody in the room wanted to say anything, which is a thing a Chair does and not a thing a Chair has to do, and a man with no office stood at the back of a room built for thirty and was asked and said one sentence.

"There is no office in this city that can produce the steps between that sum and this account," he said, "and that is not a mystery and it is not anybody's fault. It is a form. Somebody in this basin would have to cut one, and the last time anybody in this basin cut a form for a thing that was not a levy, a schedule, a posting or a return, it was a man on Bell Stair who told a yard that he was not sure of it, and they used it anyway."

Bel Harrow wrote it into the minute in his own hand, and there was no name at the foot of the paragraph, and he did not say why, and nobody asked, and the Chair looked at it for a while afterwards and then put the pen down.

Nobody thanked anybody. The corridor went down the stair in the rain, and four hundred and six people went home with nothing, and the lamp on the low-ground road was out, and the plate on the third shed had a house at the end of it, and the nail at the Gate tank had three things on it and no office behind any of them.
