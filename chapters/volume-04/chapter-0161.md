# Chapter 161 — What It Would Take

Marek Vale went up the river on the first of the first month of MR 416, and the basket at the records office at Verrow stands on a landing with a rail round it, and the rail is chest height to a man of forty-nine and waist height to a man of five, and on the morning of the second Damir Pribis had to move a chair to get at a drawer that is above the reach of anybody who has not moved a chair.

"There is one thing I am going to do and then you are going up the river again," he said, "and I am doing it because a man with no office stood at my window in the eighth month and looked at a shelf, and I opened a basket, and I have carried something out of that for four months, and I have not had anybody to put it to, and I would rather say it out loud than carry it about the building."

"You did not ask me a question."

"No," said Pribis. "That is the whole of it. I have had four months of a question I was never asked, and the reason I have not got rid of it is that nobody in this building has ever asked me one, and I have been here eleven years."

He got the drawer open. It took him a while, and he did it the way a man does a thing he has done a great many times and has never had to hurry, and the chair went across the boards and came back and stood in the middle of the landing with its back to the rail.

---

The drawer had a standing instruction in it in a hand that was not his and had not been in the building since before he started, and it was a single leaf folded in three, and he unfolded it on the shelf because a table in that room is for entries and a leaf does not go on a table.

"You want a room in which instrument is witnessed," he said. "Say that again and then I am going to tell you that I have written down that you want it, because if I do not, you will tell somebody at the bottom of the river that I said it and I will not have said it."

"I want a room that no office owns, in which a thing witnessed is good outside the room."

"That is a different sentence and it is a great deal more work." He put a finger on the leaf. "Read the fourth one. Fourth, not third, and I will wait while you find it, because everybody reads the third."

The third was about the keeping of entries and the sequence of a shelf. The fourth said that a room in which instrument is witnessed is recognised by the region, for the purposes of instrument sealed elsewhere, where a responsible party is named; and that a responsible party may be a person, an office, a share, a ward, a guild, a market, or a company; and that where the responsible party is a person, the form requires an employer.

"That is the whole of your difficulty and it is in one word and I am going to say the word out loud because you will find it in about four minutes otherwise." He said it. "Employer. Not resident. Not keeper. Employer.

"And there is a minute on the wording, and the minute is in the same drawer, and the minute does not say why, and I have read it and I am not going to tell you what I think it means, because I have been opening this basket for eleven years and I have opened about nine thousand entries and I have never once been asked what I think."

---

"You are going to ask me what a responsible party is for," he said, before Marek had got the question out. "A responsible party is the thing that is answerable for the paper. Not the person who did the work and not the person who is standing in the room. The room can be anybody's and the room can be moved, and the employer cannot, because an employer has a place you can write a letter to and an employer is still there when the person dies.

"And that is the whole of the answer and it took me four minutes to say it and I have been saying it to myself since the eighth month."

"Then a room with no employer cannot be recognised."

"A room with no employer cannot be recognised, and therefore a paper witnessed in it is good in it and nowhere else. Not refused. Not bad. Good there, and worth nothing the moment it is carried out of the door, and the moment it is carried out of the door somebody has to write on it where it was witnessed, and that is a clerk's hand, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a clerk writes the address of a room that is not in a book."

"And there is no book of rooms in a district that is not in any other book."

"There is no book of rooms," said Damir Pribis, "and I have looked, and I have been eleven years in a building that keeps everything anybody has ever entered, and there is not one of them."

---

He went and got a second thing out of a press behind the landing, which is where a regional records office keeps the forms it has stopped using, and they are kept because a form that is in use cannot be produced as evidence and a form that is not in use can.

"There is one form for a witnessing," he said, "and I am going to tell you what it is before you spend nine days working it out, because I would have spent nine days on it and I am being kind to you and I am not going to pretend that is not a reason.

"It is the same form as the attestation. Four lines and a month. Name, line, the sentence in type, the date. And I am going to say the thing that will stop you sleeping, and I am saying it because you have been up a river before and you will go again.

"It does not name a paper. It names a line. A ditch. A roof. A bin. And the sentence it prints is a promise to speak to any paper that touches that line while the signer lives, and a room is not a ditch, and a room is not a roof, and there is no way in the world to write a room on the second line of that form, and I have tried, in the sense that I have held a pen over a piece of paper three or four times in eleven years and put it down again."

"So the room has to invent a form."

"The room has to invent a form," said Pribis, "and I am going to say the rest of that in one sentence and then I am never going to say it again to anybody.

"A form invented in a room that no office owns is a form that a man in a good coat can produce at anybody he likes in about four years, with nothing on it to say where it came from, and there is nobody in the world that a sheet can point at and say *that man made this and he will say so again tomorrow*."

He squared the leaf on the shelf with the edge of the drawer and put the drawer back and moved the chair back to the wall, and the whole of the errand took about as long as it takes a man to read a notice.

---

"You have not asked me the thing you came up here to ask," said Damir Pribis.

"I have not asked it because I worked out in the eighth month that the answer is what you have just said, and I came to see whether the answer is written down anywhere, and it is, and I have about an hour of the boat back."

"Then the hour is yours and I am going to wash my hands before it, and I am going to say one thing at the window on the way past and it is the only thing in eleven years I have said to a man who came up here with nothing in his hands.

"You will need somebody standing in that room whose word a person in another room will take.

"That is not an office. It is not on a form. There is no entry in that drawer and there is no entry in any other drawer in this building and I have looked in all of them, twice, and I have looked since the eighth month and I found nothing, and that is the one part of your room I cannot get you from a shelf. Everything else you can buy or borrow or cut out of a sheet. That, you cannot."

---

The boat down was flat and cold in the same way it had been flat and cold the last time he came down it, and Marek Vale sat on the after board with his hands round the rail and let about a quarter of it go by before he understood what he had come up the river to find out.

He had come up to find out whether a room with no employer existed as a thing in a drawer, and it existed, and it was four lines long and it wanted an address, and the address could not be written, and the second line of the form could not hold a room, and a form invented in an unowned room was a form with nobody's name on it.

And the one part that could not be got off a shelf was a person.

There were about nine people's attestations of record in this basin and every one of them had been produced once. The ones who had been produced could not be produced again. And the ones who had not been produced were people nobody had ever asked, and asking them was four years of work, and there was no office in this kingdom whose business it was to ask, and there was no form on which anybody could be entered as having been asked.

So the whole of a room that no office owns is a room with a rail round three sides of it and a table and a lamp and a keeper, and a form with nobody's name on it, and one person in it whose word a person in another room will take.

And the only person in this basin who had ever had that was him, and he had spent it in a room eight feet by eleven on the thirtieth of the ninth month, in about nine seconds, with a good pen, and the certification had taken and the document was good and nobody in this kingdom would ever take his word for anything again for as long as that paper stood.

He got off the boat at a stair on the second of the month with a wet sleeve and nothing in his coat, and the hearing was on the fourth, and he had nothing to put in front of it but the fact that he had been up a river and come back.

And there was one more thing on the table in his head that he had not taken off it, and it was this. There is one form in this kingdom whose price may be entered as a class of people's willingness, and it is the record loan, and Damir Pribis entered one in the third month of MR 415 and cannot unwrite it, and he told him that on a landing in the eighth month in the ordinary voice of a man reading an entry.

If a room like that was ever to have a price put on it — and it would, and it would not be the price of the table or the lamp or the paint, it would be the price of the standing behind it — then the price would be entered the same way, in the last of five boxes, in words, as a class. And a filing clerk would read it out and write it down and not ask what it was, because there is a great deal on a landing in an ordinary week and nobody has ever yet asked a man to stop for a class.

And the second time it is done, it will be done faster, because the first time took a board above and a man from that board and three months.

And a woman of forty-nine would read it out and a man with no office would hear it, and neither of them would be able to say no, because there is no form in this kingdom on which a filing clerk may refuse a price that has been read out to him by a person with standing, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with no office may refuse to hear one.

He had that on the after board for about a quarter of an hour and then he got up off the board and went and found the man who sells fried seed-cake at the foot of the stair.

At the foot of the stair a man selling fried seed-cake asked whether he wanted one, and Marek Vale said that he did not, and the man said that nobody in this city buys one off him in the seed month and never has, and went back to his tray.
