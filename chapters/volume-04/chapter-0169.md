# Chapter 169 — The Fourth Line

The shed at the Gate in Tallow Ward was shut, and there was a notice on it, and the notice said what the room was and did not say a Crown March office, and it had been on that door since about the harvest month of last year, and it had been put up in a week when the yard voted four minutes on something and would not say afterwards what.

The woman who let him in on the ninth of the second month of MR 416 was sixty-one and had kept the wall list for as long as there had been a wall to read and had a key in a lockbox on a nail and did not ask who was at the door, because there was nobody in that yard at that hour who was going to be anybody in particular.

Ruzena Kvas put the lamp on the bench and stood aside and let him look at it, and the list was on the wall at the far end in about forty lines, and the hand had got worse going down the way a hand does, and at the end of the fourth line there was a column with nothing at the head of it.

He looked at it for a moment and did not say anything about it and would not, and there was not one person in that shed in the whole of the morning who was going to be asked about it, and that is the way it has been since the second week of the seed month of MR 414.

"You will want to know how often it is read," said Ruzena Kvas.

"I have not said anything."

"You have not said anything and you have been looking at the fourth line for a moment, and nobody looks at the fourth line." She took a folded sheet off the bench and squared it. "Twice at the ordinary speed, once a week, and it is read out and nobody writes and nobody answers and that is the whole of the practice, and it is not a hearing and it is not a roll and it does not go anywhere.

"And I do it and I have done it for as long as there has been a wall to read, and eleven or twelve people in this yard have stood in this room and watched me do it, and not one of them has ever said thank you to me, and I am not standing here to be thanked and I would like it noticed that I have not asked for it either, and if a clerk in Bell Stair asks you what I am I am a woman who reads a wall out loud."

---

"I read other people's papers out loud in that room four hours a day on the days the shed is open," said Marek Vale, "and I have been doing it about a year and a half, and I never once knew what the wall list was for, and I worked out in the first four seconds that I was not going to get to the bottom of that column today, and neither were you, and I am not going to ask."

"Then stand at the bench and do not touch it."

She read it.

She read the whole wall list from the top, at the ordinary speed, in the voice of a woman reading out a price list, and the fourth line came out of it in the same voice as the other thirty-nine, and it came out between a line about a boiler in the second month of last year and a line about a handcart in the third month of the year before that, and it took a moment.

*Neris Kade. A roof over a counting floor in Bell Stair. The growth month of MR 412. Eleven weeks.*

Then she read all forty again at the ordinary speed, and this time she read the fourth line a second time when she came to it, and stopped for the length of a breath afterwards, and went on with the rest, and the silence after the fourth line was about the length of a line.

---

Neris Kade had come with him and had not been asked and had not been called, in the way that a field examiner is neither of those things and turns up anyway, and she stood at the rail side of the shed with her hands behind her back, about nine feet from the wall, and she had not moved once during either reading.

Nobody asked her anything. Not Ruzena Kvas, who had been reading that list since the wall went up and could have said *is that you* in four words and did not, and not a yard-man who came in at about the fifth bell and stood at the door for a while and then went out again, and not a boy who came in halfway through the second reading to fetch a pail and stopped at the wall for a moment because everybody stops at that wall.

Marek Vale had his hand in his coat and took it out again, and it was the fifth time in two months, and both of the last two had been in front of other people, and Neris Kade saw him do it and did not use it, which is a thing she has never once done for him in three years and did that morning.

That is a fact and it is not an invitation. Her name has been read out twice at the ordinary speed once a week for four years and she has never been asked one question about it, and there is no form on which she could have been asked, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which anybody may be entered as saying they were free when they signed anything, and she has never once been asked that either, and she is the only person in this shed who could say that sentence accurately and she was not going to.

---

"You are not going to ask me what the line is," she said, to Marek Vale, when the second reading was finished and Ruzena Kvas had gone out to the tank.

"No."

"That is correct and I want to say why it is correct, because I have been in this yard four times in three years and I have worked it out.

"If you ask me what the line is, then it becomes a conversation between you and me about a paper, and there is a moment in which I would answer, and the answer would be true, and the answer would be about a roof over a counting floor and about eleven weeks of work in the growth month of MR 412, and it would not be about the paper on the wall, and then in about four years a man in a good coat could come to this shed and ask me about the paper on the wall, and I would have to say *we spoke about it once on the ninth of the second month* and there would be nine words on a sheet in a file in Bell Stair that I had never seen, and it would be the only document in this business that I had made myself.

"And I am not going to be made a document. I have not been asked that about anything in four years, and I have noticed, and I noticed it in about the fourth year and I decided then that I was going to let it keep happening, and I have let it keep happening, and it is the correct decision, and it has cost me a reading of my own name a week for four years."

She looked at the wall.

"I want to say the other half out loud in this shed, because you have been reading that list for a year and a half and I have never once watched you do it, and I have watched about a dozen people do it, and not one of them knew what they were reading.

"You did not know what the fourth line was. You knew there was a fourth line. And you have been reading this yard's papers out loud for a year and a half and a woman of twenty-two who carries papers about this city at night has heard you do it, and nobody in this yard has ever asked you what the wall is, and I am telling you now that I know what the fourth line is and that I have never been asked, and I am not going to be asked, and if anybody asks me in this room today I am going to walk out of the door and go and stand at the tank, and you may all watch me do that."

Nobody asked her anything in that room for the rest of the morning.

---

Drazen Topic was at the market rail at about the fifth bell of the ninth of the second month and took a count there every morning of his life, four times a day, and wrote it on a slate with nothing at the top of it, and Marek Vale found him at the rail on the way out because it is on the way.

He did not begin well. He said the first four sentences badly and had to start the fifth one over, and what he said in the end was that there was a sheet in a drawer in a building a night and a day up this river with his name in the third of five boxes on it, and a date that was a month and no day, and that he had been told he was a witness about a boundary between two market pitches in the sixth month of MR 413, and that he had signed about a boundary, and that he had not been asked, and that he had been counting heads at a rail every morning for eleven years and had never once been told to put anything at the top of a slate.

"That is the whole of it," said Marek Vale. "There is no form on which anybody may be entered as saying they were free, and I have looked, and there is a woman of fifty-one at a counter who says the same thing and has been saying it for nine years, and I have found out this month that there are nine people in this basin who have been produced, and that five of the nine can be found and four of them cannot, and that you are the second I have found and the first I have found since the fourth of this month, and that one of the four is dead."

Drazen Topic put the slate down on the rail.

"Eleven years," he said. "I have been counting at this rail for eleven years and I have counted about nine hundred people a morning and I have never once had anybody tell me a number I did not put there myself."

"No."

"My name went on a paper about a boundary in the sixth month of the year before last and I signed it in a queue because a man said it was a formality and I had a pitch, and I am not going to say I did not agree with it, because I did."

"You did."

"Then say the thing that is going to happen to me," said Drazen Topic, "because I am not going to spend a morning working it out on my own and I have nine hundred people to count in an hour."

"Nothing is going to happen to you," said Marek Vale. "That is the finding of the fourth of this month and I was in the room and it is written down and it says that a room cannot enter a finding that a person did or did not consent to anything, and there is none to enter, and they looked. And I am not going to soften it, and here is the other half: nothing is going to happen to you because nobody is going to be able to do anything to you either, and you have been used once and you cannot be used again and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may say so, and I have been telling people that for about a fortnight and I have not found one thing to do about it."

The rail was busy by then. Drazen Topic picked the slate up.

"I have been counting at this rail for eleven years," he said, "and I have never once been asked to be there and I have never once been asked anything, and it has never once occurred to me to say thank you to the man in the good coat at the third window, and I am not going to start now on the strength of nine words on a market road." He put the number up. "Nine hundred and about forty. Move along."

---

The yard did not thank anybody and the register did not gain a line and the rule was not extended and nobody put a head on anything, and the wall list has about forty lines on it and a column at the end of the fourth with nothing at the head of it, and that is the way it is going to be.

And on the ninth of the second month of MR 416 a man who has read other people's papers out loud in that room four hours a day on the days the shed is open read a woman's willingness out loud twice at the ordinary speed in a shed, and she was standing in the shed, and he was not asked, and he did not ask, and the reading will be done again on the sixteenth, and on the twenty-third, and on the thirtieth, and there are people in this basin who are spent and cannot be found and one of them is dead, and none of that is fixed by a wall and none of it is going to be.
