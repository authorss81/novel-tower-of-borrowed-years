# Chapter 194 — Three Rooms, and Nobody Who Called Them

He read the four documents out loud in three rooms on three days, and he stopped in each of them at a different place, and none of the three people who stopped him had told the other two that they were going to stop him.

There was no arrangement. There is no minute and nobody drew one up and nobody sent a message. A man with no office went up a ladder, then climbed a stair over a counting floor, then walked down a lane to a room with a bench in it and a bin in the corner, on three days of the fifth month of MR 416, with the same four sheets in a flat case, because that is the only job he has left and it is not a witnessing and it does not have to be.

What there is, is three findings, made on three days, in three rooms, in three sets of words, by three people who had not appointed each other and who did not know the other two were doing it and who still do not.

That is the whole of the quorum. It is not a court. There is no instrument in this kingdom that could make it one, and there is no form on which a person may be brought before it, and there is no list of its members anywhere, and it has no seat, no stipend, no minute, no seal and no name.

"It is three men in three rooms who said no to the same four papers in three different ways and would not let each other do it any other way," said Zlata Hruska, on the first day, from the side of the rail she has never once stood on. "That is what it is. And I have thought about it since the eighth and I am going to say the part that makes it a quorum and the part that makes it worth nothing, and the two parts are the same length.

"It is a quorum because we did not appoint each other. Nobody chose these three rooms. There is a lane and there is a counting floor and there is a man who sells candles, and that is a thing that happened, and the day somebody picks the three of us it is an office and the day somebody reads out a list of us it is a roll, and a roll is a return and a return is made about a person.

"And it is worth nothing, and I want that said before anybody gets pleased about it. Three findings that no office can be shown and that any person in this basin may ignore, and will. A man in a good coat will not be slow about it. There is no form anywhere on which a person may be compelled to do anything at all because of anything any of the three of us has said, and there is nothing whatever in this basin that will make a district do a thing on the strength of a woman who cannot write saying a thing in a room over a dye house."

---

"The first day," said Marek Vale, "was the eighth. I read the first of the four and she let me get nine sentences into it and then she stopped me, and she did it by putting her hand on the table, which she has never done in that room in nine years, and everybody in it went quiet and she said what she wanted said.

"Here is the sentence. *The four documents are all about lines, and a line is a thing in the ground, and all four of the documents are well made, and there is not one form in this kingdom on which a person may be entered as saying they did not agree to a line.*

"And then she said the part that is hers and not the finding, and she said it to the room and not to me. *I have said no to a copy, and no to a head, and no to a form, and no to a reach, in one morning, in the third month of last year, and I was right four times, and I have not said it out loud since and I am not going to now. But I have been in nine rooms in five months and the only thing in all nine of them that nobody has ever done is stop a man reading out loud halfway.*

"And then she said: *you may go on, and it will take four more minutes, and I would like to tell you that I do not want the four minutes and I am not going to have you stop, because a reading that a person stops is a reading somebody chose.*"

---

"The second day was the ninth," he said, "and it was the room over a counting floor in Rainmarket, and the man who keeps the floor was in it and about eleven other people were, and he stopped me in a different place, and his stopping place was earlier, and his reason was about the fourth document and about a boy.

"He let me get the first three right through and into the fourth, which is the indentures, the one about a boy who was fourteen and is eighteen and has two years to run on him. And then he stood up off his bench, which took him four seconds and which he did not hurry, and he said the thing that is the best thing anybody said to me in four months and he said it in about nine seconds.

*You have published four documents with the reason each one is dangerous, and every one of the reasons is right, and you have not put a name on any of them, and that is correct, and I have got one question and I am going to ask it in this room and I do not want an answer.*

*The reason the first three of these hurt anybody is that a person was already spent when they were used. The reason the fourth one hurts is that a person was fourteen. And the only thing your list does about either is not say a name. And not saying a name is right and I am not arguing with it.*

*So here is my question and it is not about the boy and it is not about a form. How many people in this basin have been found out.*

*And I am not asking for a figure, because I have been in this room over a counting floor for four days now and I know there is no figure and I would like it entered that I know there is no figure and I am asking anyway.*

*Because on the second of the month I stood at that rail and I read out four documents to nine hundred people a day, and in nineteen days since, how many people do you think have worked out that they are on one of them. Not the four. The other thing. How many people in this quarter have been in a queue in the last nineteen days and worked out that they were already used, and worked out that nobody is going to come and ask them about it, because there is no form on which anybody may be asked.*

*You have found out a second time. Every one of them you have found out a second time by making it public, and nobody asked them for that either.*"

---

Marek Vale stopped there, and did not go on for four minutes, and said so in three rooms.

"That is what was said on the ninth and it has not been answered and I am not going to answer it, because he did not want an answer and because he is right, and because the answer would be a number and there is no number.

"And I want to say one thing about the two of them together, because the two of them are the entire reason those three findings are worth the paper they are not written on.

"The first woman said: a reading that a person stops is a reading somebody chose.

"The second man said: I asked a question and I do not want an answer, and I know there is no figure.

"Neither of those two things is a finding and neither of them is a rule. And a person can only do one of them in a room that has no office in it, because in an office you stop a reading and the stopping is the office's, and in an office you ask a question and the answer is entered whether the person wanted it or not.

"That is all a quorum is. Three rooms in which a man could be stopped and not be charged for stopping him, and a person could ask a question and not be made to answer it. I do not know what else a quorum is and I have not got a second meaning and I am not going to go and look for one, and I would like it entered that I did not look."

---

"The third day was the eleventh," he said, "and it was the room at the bottom of the lane, the one with the bench in it and the bin in the corner and a chair nobody has ever sat in.

"And nobody in that room stopped me. I want that on the page before anything else about it, because in nineteen minutes on the eleventh of the fifth month I read those four documents out loud twice at the ordinary speed in a room with a bench and a bin in it and nine people in it, and nobody put a hand on the table, and I read the whole of it, and that is the only day of the three on which I was not stopped.

"What happened instead is that the man who sells candles stood in his own street door for about a quarter of an hour while it was going on and did not come in, and when I had finished he came about two steps into the room and asked for one thing and it was not about the four documents.

"He said: *I have got a notice on my front door since the fifteenth of the second month and nobody may take it up and nobody may take it down, and there is a board outside my back door with a notice on it that this table of five paid for, and I have not asked what is on it and I am not going to.

"And the thing I want said is this. There are three rooms in this basin now and I am in one of them, and there is a room over a dye house, and there is a room over a counting floor, and I sell candles and I have a back room with a floor and a street door and a bench in it, and in nineteen years I have never had a name on my shop for the road I am on, and I have asked twice in four years and been told twice there is no name, and both men were correct.*

*So you have got three rooms and none of them is in a book. And I would like it said in whatever it is you are making that they are the room by the dye house, and the room over the counting floor, and the room behind a man who sells candles, and that nobody has given any of them a name.*

*And I am going to say the rest of it because I am fifty-eight and I would rather be asked for something than be left standing in a street door.* There is no office in this city that can be shown a door in this city. *That is not modesty. I have been at this four days and I have worked out that it is geography. And I would like somebody in these three rooms to write that down, and if it is put in a book in Bell Stair in about four years I would like there to be nothing on it about me.*"

---

Nobody wrote it down. He had asked them not to and they did it in a way that took about nine seconds.

"It is the third finding," said Neris Kade, and she was not in that room, and she was in all three of them, and she was in none of them on a day anybody chose. "And it is the only one of the three that is a fact about a place and not a fact about a person, and it is the one that will still be true in about four years, and the other two are about things people did and people will have moved on from by then.

"I have been in three rooms in five months and I am a field examiner and I do not put a number on anything. And I have got a sentence and I am going to say it once.

*There is no where for the three of them. A room is recognised where a responsible party is named. A room with no name is not a place, it is a set of people standing in a floor, and it cannot be sent to, produced at, or given an area, because it has not got a point on any line a clerk in Bell Stair can look at, and the reason it has not got one is that there is nothing to put it on.*

*And that is not a cruelty and there is nobody in Bell Stair who has refused anybody anything on the ground that this basin has no map of it. It is simply that nobody has ever been able to write it down, and a thing nobody can write down does not exist, and the four documents on the causeway are all of them about a line of accounts that has two ends, and those two ends are the only two ends of anything in this basin that anybody has ever been able to write on.*

"So: three rooms, and nobody who called them, and a quorum of three people who did not appoint each other, and no seat, no name, no list, no fund, no minute, no form, and nothing that any office in this kingdom can be shown.

"And a man is not going to go and find a fourth one, and I want that said too, because I have been in three rooms in five months and I have watched what happens to a room the day somebody goes looking for another one, and it is a copy, and after it is a head, and after it is a reach, and a reach is an area and an area is a district and a district is a line in a book, and the man is standing in the middle of it with a book in his hand wondering how it happened."

Marek Vale did not write it down either, and said why, and the reason was not humility and was not the queue and was that nobody in the three rooms had asked him to and a man who reads papers out loud four hours a day on the days a rota says is a man whose hand is on a thing.

And nobody in any of the three rooms thanked him, and the reason they believed him about the other thing is that he stood at the back of all three of them, on three days, and told the truth about himself in the first minute of each, out loud, in the plainest words he had, which is that no room in this city will take his word as evidence of anything for as long as a paper he certified on the thirtieth of the ninth month of last year stands in a book in a building he has never been inside.

A man who will do that is not a man who has come to get something out of the room, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be entered as saying they did not agree to a quorum of three, and the lane is still a lane that has no name on any map a clerk in this city can look at, and the bin is still in the corner of the room behind the man who sells candles, and nobody has swept it and nobody has thrown it out.
