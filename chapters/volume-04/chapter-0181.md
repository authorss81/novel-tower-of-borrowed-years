# Chapter 181 — A Man Who Wants a Room Named

The low ground went under on the night of the twenty-eighth of the third month and it went under the way the marshal said it would go under, which is to say without any drama in it at all, and by the first bell the next morning a mile and a bit of the Rainmarket low ground was a foot and a half of water with a mile and a bit of new clay standing in the middle of it like a spine.

The bank held. The foot and a half of good clay that eleven men had put up since the first month stood up in the water and did not move, and that is the whole of what a marshal's instrument buys and it is not nothing.

The four sledges went back into the store on the low-ground road at about the seventh bell on the twenty-eighth and the key went back on the nail inside the door, and Milos Havel, who keeps the district's tools and the key to the store they go back into, stood in the doorway of his own store and looked at the nail.

"There is a works in force," he said, to nobody, in a flat voice. "I want that written down somewhere by somebody. I have said the word *none* once in nine years, on the nineteenth of the second month, in front of a settlement marshal and a man with no office, and I said it about a works board, and the board was the only thing wrong. I am not going to say it again and I have not got a way of saying the other thing, and a man who cannot say the other thing is going to be taken for a man who is saying the first one.

"And there is no ground," he said. "That is the whole of it. You cannot put clay on a field that is under a foot and a half of water and I am not going to stand on a bank in the fourth month and be told by nine men that the clay will hold, because the clay is not the question. The question is there is nowhere to put it."

---

There is a room above a counting floor in Rainmarket that a man had cleared out on the twenty-ninth of the third month and had a bench put in and a table and a lamp and one chair, and on the second of the fourth month of MR 416 there were eleven people in it and there was a sheet of paper face up on the table with a name at the head of it.

The name at the head of it was *Danko Pehlin*.

He was forty-four and he had kept the counting floor for nine years and nobody had ever said a word to him about why a floor where seed is counted against a board should want a room over it. He did not say the reason first. He let the paper lie there for four minutes while eleven people looked at it, which is a thing a man does when he has been holding a piece of paper for a day and a night and wants the room to arrive at it by itself.

"This is a form," he said. "It is not a good one and I did not have it written. I had it written in the second month of last year for a lease on the floor itself and the man in the office said it would do, and it has been in a drawer since then, and it has got my name at the top of it and a line for a date, and there is nothing else on it."

"What is it for," said Zlata Hruska.

"It is for a room."

"Where did you get that."

"It is the form they give you." He said it without heat, the way a man says a thing he has said to himself a hundred times and has not once said to anybody. "A room in which instrument is witnessed is recognised by the region where a responsible party is named. And the responsible party can be a person, an office, a share, a ward, a guild, a market, or a company. I have read that on the back of a form twice and I have never had a reason to read it until the twenty-eighth of the third month, and I read it on the twenty-eighth of the third month, and the reason is that the water came and the sledges went back on a nail and eleven men are on the lanes and not on the reach, and I thought: there has got to be somewhere in this quarter a person can be asked a question."

He put his thumb on the name at the head of it.

"I want it called the Counting Floor. Not my name. The floor's name. So that a paper in that room is good in that room and good outside it, and good outside it is the only part of it that is worth anything to anybody."

---

Nobody said anything for about the length of two pulls of a press, which is a comparison that came to Marek Vale and not to anybody else in the room, and he did not make it out loud.

Zvonko Maras said it first and he said it standing, because he had not sat down and had not sat down every time he had been in a room since the first month of this year.

"Then put the floor's name on it," he said, "and the floor is a market, and the market is a line in a book in Bell Stair, and in about four years a man in a good coat comes down here with the floor's minute and reads out that the market agreed."

"I have not got a minute," said Danko Pehlin. "That is rather the point of it. There is no minute. There is a bench and a table and a lamp and me, and if there is no minute then there is nothing to produce, and if there is nothing to produce then the room has not said anything, and a room that has not said anything cannot be held to anything."

"You have just described a room that does not exist," said Zlata Hruska, "and you have described it correctly, and I have been in one for nine years and I am not going to pretend to a man of forty-four that it is the same thing. It is not the same thing. Yours is better than mine and that is the difficulty."

"Then let it be better than yours," said Pehlin.

"No." She did not say it loudly. "Because of your name at the top of that piece of paper, and I am going to tell you why in nine sentences and then I am going to go back to the ladder and I am not going to be asked a second time.

"Your name is a person. A person is a responsible party and a person requires an employer. You have got no employer. You keep a floor for a man in an office and that man is not your employer and if he were it would be worse. So the moment your name is at the head of that form, this room is a person's room, and a person's room cannot be recognised by anybody, not by me, not by a man in a good coat, not by the region, and it is a worse dead room than a nameless one, because a nameless room at least has not tried.

"And if it is the floor's name then the floor is a market, and a market is a line in a book in this city, and the day a market is a responsible party for a room the market has a minute about it, and the minute says what the room said, and in four years a man in a good coat produces the minute at a clerk in a good coat and the clerk writes down that the market agreed. And nobody in that room ever agreed to anything, and the minute will not be a lie, because he will not have said anything in it either. That is the whole trick and you have just described it to me without knowing you were describing it."

---

"I know I am describing it," said Danko Pehlin. He did not put the paper down. "I have known since the second month of last year. I am not a fool and I am not a man in a good coat and I have not come up here to be talked round. I have come up here to be told no by somebody, and I would like it done quickly, and I have a bench and a table and a lamp and I have had them since the twenty-ninth of the third month and there is nothing at all in this room that is worth anything to anybody and I know that better than anybody in it, including you, and I would like to know what I have got."

That stopped the room.

Margit Sova, forty-one, twenty-six years in the trade, four days of writing left in a hand that has got worse over twenty-six years, said it, and she said it as a woman who sells chaff and has counted a hundred and forty pounds of it in a morning and knows what a thing is worth for nothing.

"You have got a floor with a bench in it and a lamp and a door that shuts," she said. "And on the fourth of last year I had a pitch with a lease and eleven weeks of a season and a list in a box under my counter, and in the sixth month of last year a man in a good coat told me at a window that I was a witness to something, and I have not been able to hold my pitch since the growth month and there is no form in this kingdom on which a lease is renewed.

"So I am going to tell you what you have got and I am not being kind. You have got a room in which a person can be asked a question and can answer it, in a building anybody in this quarter can get into, on a ground nobody has a hold on, and there is not one paper in it. That is worth something. It is worth exactly this: it is worth a person being asked."

"And what is that worth," said Danko Pehlin, "in a room I could have named in four seconds with a piece of paper that is already written."

Margit Sova did not answer that, and she did not have to, and the not-answering was the answer, and eleven people in a room over a counting floor in Rainmarket heard it happen.

---

Neris Kade was in it and had been for about half an hour and had not been asked anything, which is what she is for.

"You have got one more thing," she said, "and it is small and you will not want it, and I am going to say it because I am a field examiner and this is what a field examiner is for.

"You have got a man who counts. You have got nine or ten people in this quarter who cannot read a form and can read a day, and if you put a day against a name in this room the way it is done on a wall at the end of a lane, then a person can come up here and find out whether the room is worth anything by looking at whether anybody came. And nobody is required to come. And there is no word at the top of it."

"Then somebody could read it off," said Pehlin.

"Then somebody could read it off," said Neris Kade, "and so could a man with a list in a good coat, and so could anybody who walks down a lane and can read, and there is no argument I am able to make with you this morning that would make that not true. It is the price. I am not going to sell you a day as though it were safe."

---

At about the third bell he picked the paper up off the table.

He did not make a thing of it. He turned it over once, which is what a man does with a piece of paper he has decided not to use, and then he folded it in four, the way a thing that has been in a drawer since the second month of last year is folded, and put it in the breast of his coat, and buttoned the coat.

"Right," he said. "Then it is a room with nothing at the top of it and a man who keeps it who is not on it. I want that said in this room while there are eleven people in it, and I want it said by somebody who is not me, and I do not want it written down, and I know that those three things do not go together and I am having them anyway."

"I will say it," said Zlata Hruska, "and I will not write it, and you will not be thanked for it and I will not be thanked for saying it, and here it is in the air in a room with a bench and a lamp in it: this room has no name and no keeper and no form and no reach and nothing on the door, and it is a room a person can be asked a question in, and that is the only thing it is, and I have said the same four sentences in four rooms in six months and this is the first one of the four that has cost somebody something."

---

Milos Havel came up at about the fifth bell, and he came up the stairs of the counting floor one at a time with a hand on the rail, and he had his coat on, and he had the key to the district's store in his pocket and he had said the word *none* in front of a settlement marshal on the nineteenth of the second month and had not said it since.

He stood in the room with his hat in his hands and did not sit down, and there were about nine people in it by then.

"I have got a question," he said. "I have never had one in this basin and I am not going to pretend I have not wanted one for about four months."

"Ask it," said Danko Pehlin.

"I have got a class on a piece of paper a night and a day up this river with a day on it and my thumb on the mark, and it says the willingness of the people of this ground, and it is true, and it does not say how many of us there are, and nobody will tell me, and I have stopped asking that." He turned the hat over. "And in the fourth month a season comes off my ground. Not a rate. A season, on a line of accounts, and there is no figure for it and there is no figure for the ditch on the same line either.

"And I have been going round for about a fortnight trying to find out what a person does about that. Not whether it is going to come. It is going to come. Whether anybody is told."

He stopped, and looked at the floor, and then at the woman who had come up a ladder in a lane with nothing written at the top of it.

"Is there a form in this kingdom," said Milos Havel, "on which anybody may be told that a bill is coming."

There was nobody in that room who could have answered it faster.

"No," said Zlata Hruska. "There is not. I have been asked at a door for nine years and I have never once been able to say *yes, and here is what to do*, and I have been told once, by a man at a counter with a book, that there is no form on which a person may be told that they have been spent, and I believed him, and I have not found one since, and I have been looking."

"Then that is the answer," said Milos Havel.

"That is the answer," she said.

He put his hat on and went down the stairs one at a time, and at the bottom of them he stopped and came half of them back up, which is a thing a man of fifty does when his knees are not what they were in the second month.

"I have known that since the twenty-seventh of the third month," he said. "A man in a good coat told me in a store and I asked him the same question and he told me the same thing you have told me, and I have carried it about for a fortnight.

"And I have come up here anyway," he said, "and I would like that noted by nobody, and I am going to come up here again when the fourth month is out, and I am not going to stop coming, and I am not going to be able to do a single thing about it, and that is the arrangement."

He went down.

---

Danko Pehlin stood in the middle of his own room with his coat buttoned over a piece of paper he was not going to use.

"That is a room," he said.

"That is a room," said Zlata Hruska.

"I have had a bench in it since the twenty-ninth of the third month and I did not know what it was for until about ten minutes ago, and I have had it for four days and I did not know, and I want to say that out loud in a room I have not got a name for, because in about four years a man in a good coat is going to ask this man what his reasons were, and I would like the answer to have a thing in it that is true."

"What is the thing."

"That eleven people came up a stair today," said Danko Pehlin, "and a man of fifty came up it twice."

Nobody thanked anybody. The lamp wanted trimming and somebody trimmed it. And on the wall at the end of a lane with no name on any map a clerk in this city can look at, there is a day in chalk against ten names and about six days, and no word at the top of it and no eleventh name, and this batch of years is a reckoning year and has a good deal of it still to run, and there is nothing whatever in a room over a counting floor in Rainmarket that a man in a good coat cannot walk into and read.
