# Chapter 168 — A Finding in a Room That Does Not Exist

There were four chairs in that room and none of them matched, and they came from four different kitchens, and on the second of the second month of MR 416 at about the fourth bell they were set in a rough square with the rail round three sides of it, and the lamp was in the middle, and the fifth person sat on the bed because there was no fifth chair and nobody pretended otherwise.

There is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which two or more people may sit in a room and make a finding. There is no office to sit in front of, and no chair, and no minute, and no seal, and no stipend, and no member list, and no rule about how long anybody has to be there before they count, and no form on which anybody may be entered as having been there.

There is also no name for the lane, and no roll, and no book anywhere in this city that anybody in that room has ever been inside, and therefore there is no place on earth that a finding made in that room could be sent to, and there is nobody it could be produced at.

Nim Rook said that last part out loud, before anything else, in one breath, and then said she was sorry, and Zlata Hruska said she was not to be, because a person who says a thing out loud in a room before the room starts is doing the room a service and not apologising to it.

---

"There is one piece of business and then I am going to say a thing I have been carrying up a ladder for a fortnight," said Zlata Hruska.

She put both hands flat on the table.

"Ask me whether I have been produced."

Nobody asked it for a moment, and then Neris Kade asked it, and the asking was done in the ordinary voice because that is what Zlata Hruska had asked for.

"You have been produced twice," said Zlata Hruska. "Say the rest of it, because you have been in four rooms this month and I want the whole thing in one go."

"Twice. A sheet in the sixth month of MR 414, about a bin behind a dyeworks, on the low ground. And a sheet in the second month of last year, about the same bin, in front of nine or ten people. Both of them in a fair small hand and both of them signed by you, and neither of them asked you anything, and you were not told that either of them was about anything. And the second one was produced in a room with about nine people in it and you were one of the nine and you were not the one being asked anything."

"Thank you," said Zlata Hruska, which nobody in that room expected, and she said it in a completely flat voice, and she got up and took the lamp to the window and did something to the wick with a piece of cloth, and came back and sat down.

"I have been asking one question at that door since I was twenty-five years old," she said, "and it has taken somebody else nine years to ask it of me, and I am not going to say anything about that in front of you, and I am not going to be grateful, and I want it written down that I said I was not going to be grateful, because I have been in a lane for nine years and I know exactly what gets said afterwards in a lane when a person has been grateful.

"Right. That is the business. Go on."

---

Nobody wrote it down, and then a man of about forty got up off the wall side of the room and said that there ought to be something written, and Zlata Hruska gave him a carpenter's pencil and the flat side of the door-shutter and said that was the paper in that establishment and he was welcome.

The finding went on the inside of the door in four lines, in a hand that was not good and was not meant to be.

It found that any person may come to that room and ask whether their name is on the wall, and that the answer will be given.

It found that the answer cannot be made to hold, and that the room has nothing behind it, and that the room knows that, and is going to go on giving the answer anyway.

It found that the person who keeps the room has been produced twice, on two sheets, about a bin, and was not told, and that she is the first of the nine in this basin to be told anywhere at all, in that room or in any other.

And it found, in a fourth line that Neris Kade made her put down and read out again, that this room has no power to do anything whatever with any of the three findings above it, and that the room is not going to pretend to have power it has not got, and that anybody in this city is free to disregard all of it, and there is nothing in this kingdom that can be done about that, and the room is not a court and will not be called one.

---

After that a fish came up the ladder, which is not the way a fish comes, and Lovro Prazak, forty-nine, who carries fish up from the low ground and sleeps in a doorway at the end of that lane, came up it with his hands wet and asked whether there was anybody who could tell him one thing.

"Say the thing," said Nim Rook.

"Whether my name is on that wall."

There was silence in that room for the length of a breath and every person in it went to the gap where the rail did not meet the wall and looked, and Zlata Hruska went down the ladder about eight rungs and came back up with her sleeve to her elbow.

"It is," she said. "It is on the twenty-ninth. It is not in my hand and it is not in the hand of anybody who is in this lane now and it is not in my hand in chalk either, it is in pencil, under the chalk, and it has been under the chalk since somebody rubbed the twenty-ninth out on the day it went up, and there is a person's name on the twenty-ninth in chalk about a foot above yours, and I do not know the person's name either and I have not asked in six days because I have been busy with a lamp."

Lovro Prazak said that he had never given his name to anybody in that lane, and that he had slept in the doorway at the end of it for nine years, and that he had never been asked for anything, and that this was the first time in nine years that anything in that lane had wanted something from him and that he wanted very little.

"Take it off," said Zlata Hruska. "It is going to take a wet sleeve and a moment and it is the only thing in this room that has ever actually been done in it."

She did it herself, on the twenty-ninth, and it took a moment.

---

"And now the other half of it," said Lovro Prazak, "and I am going to say it because there is no point in a name."

"Go on."

"A name is a name against a thing. That is all a name is. My name is not a thing anybody can use. It is worth exactly as much as the wall you have written it on and about a foot of brick at the end of a lane, and if you had put my name in a book in Bell Stair with a street on it, then I would be a person, and there would be a person to do that to."

Zlata Hruska went and got the piece of chalk and wrote on the wall where the pencil had been, low down, in small letters: *a doorway at the top of this lane, and no other address, and no name on any map.*

"That is a where," she said. "It is the only where anybody in this city can give for themselves without being invented, and all of us do it this way, and it is why the lane has no name and it is not because anybody has been cruel about it. There is nothing to be cruel about. There is no where."

"Then the finding has a where in it," said Lovro Prazak. "And a where that is a doorway at the top of a lane. And that is not anywhere. And you are not going to be able to send that."

"No," said Zlata Hruska. "And I am not going to try, and I am not going to be ashamed of it in front of you either, and I am going to say the whole of it in one sentence because you are the first person to ask me a question in that room that was not about me.

"Any person may ask whether their name is on the wall, and the answer is given, and the answer is not worth anything outside this room, and that is the entire finding, and it is the whole of what this room can do, and I have known it since the ninth month of MR 414 when I put a rail up, and I have not told a soul about it in all that time, and a man in a good coat came up this ladder in the fourth month of last year and I let him stand on the other side of it while he told me what a room was for."

---

The man with the carpenter's pencil wrote the fourth finding on the outside of the door as well, and it took a long time because the brick was cold and the pencil was not the right length, and it is the reason that finding exists twice.

It is not a figure of speech. The room did that deliberately, on the evening of the second of the second month, and it did it because the inside of a door in this city is the side nobody looks at, and because in about two months the door is going to be painted, and the painting is not going to be done by anybody in that lane and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be asked to paint over a finding or not to.

And when it is painted, that finding is gone.

There is no copy. There is no book and no office and no roll and no seal and no form on which two people in a room with a rail round three sides of it may enter a finding and send it anywhere, and the finding on that door is the only finding in the history of this basin that is made by people who cannot make one, and the only copy of it is a second coat of pencil on the other side of a board about four feet by two.

And that is not nothing, and it is not a remedy, and it is not a court, and the first thing that happened in that room, before the pencil, was a man of forty-nine come up a ladder with wet hands to ask whether his name was on a wall.

Nim Rook came down the ladder first and sat on the two steps in the wet for a while and then said the thing she had said she was going to say, which was that she had told them all the truth about a person on a column with nothing at the head of it, on the fourth of this month, in a room in a building, to a Deputy with a book, and that the Deputy had told her that the room was not going to join her two nines together, and that she had thought about that on a roof since the fourth of this month and that the Deputy was right and that it was the worst thing anybody had ever done to her and that she was going to go on being angry about it for about a year.
