# Chapter 166 — Two People Who Need One

There was nowhere in that part of the low ground to put a piece of paper, and that turned out to be the whole of the argument for about two minutes: a witness has to be told a thing standing up, and a person standing up in the open on a market road is a person in a queue, and a person in a queue is a person a man in a good coat can produce at them later.

So Marek Vale and Neris Kade carried a table out.

It was an ordinary deal table with four legs and it came from a room behind a chandler's and it went down the road between them, one at each end, on the twenty-third of the first month of MR 416, and they set it down outside a shut pitch on the low-ground road with a canopy frame on it and no canvas, and a stone edge about fourteen feet long, and a lockable box under the only counter that still had a top on it.

Margit Sova had a broom in her hand because a man with a barrow on a market road will sweep a pitch whether or not he is holding it.

"You have brought a table," she said.

"We have brought a table."

"You have brought a table to a pitch that cannot be held, on a road where about nine hundred people go past a week, and there is a woman standing at the far end of it with a spade, and I have been trying to work out for about a minute what the table is for and I have got to it and I would rather you did not explain it, because if you explain it I will have to think about it."

Odile Marn came down the road and stood at the other end of the table and put the spade down against the leg.

---

"I have a board in a seed house off this road," she said. "It has nine lines cut in it and it was made by the district and it belongs to no office and line four is the Counters' Walk ditch. And a season works in force on a board has to be witnessed to be a works, and it is not going to be cut this month and it is not going to be cut in the second month either, and I would like somebody to explain to me the two things I do not understand.

"The first is that the ditch has not been cut since the rains of the year before last, and I asked for it in the ninth month of last year and I got a fortnight on the ground behind my wall and no ditch, and I said then, in a building, that a fortnight is not a ditch.

"The second is that a woman may not cut a ditch on her own boundary with a witness who is not spent, and I have been to nine counters in about a month and every one of them was correct."

"You are spent," said Neris Kade.

"I am," said Odile Marn. "I was produced in the sixth month of last year. I found out about it in a rail queue because somebody counted the queue and wrote a number on a slate, and I have not been able to do one thing with it since, and I am telling you now so that you do not spend the next ten minutes finding out."

---

"I have a lease," said Margit Sova. "I had a lease and it is finished and I cannot renew it and I cannot sell it and I cannot give it away, and that is an arithmetic and it is not a hardship, and I said all of that to this man on the market road in the ninth month of last year and he wrote none of it down, which was the correct thing to do and which I have thought about perhaps six times since.

"And what I would like said out loud on this road, in front of four people, is that I am not asking for a witness for the lease. A lease is witnessed. Everybody knows a lease is witnessed. I am asking for a witness for the *holding of the pitch*, which is a different thing, and there is no form for the holding of a pitch, and a woman who cannot be a witness cannot hold a pitch, and a woman who cannot hold a pitch has no way at all of saying so that anybody has to do anything about.

"And I have been to four men in good coats since the sixth of the month last year and every one of them has told me the law and not one of them has told me the other half."

---

"Which one of you can be the other one's," said Marek Vale.

Neither of them answered for a while, and a cart went past at the far end of the pitch and did not slow, and it was the whole of the answer.

"I have a pitch that has been shut four days running since the harvest of the year before last," said Margit Sova, "and you have a boundary ditch, and if I stood in your ditch and said that I had seen a woman put a spade into a line of wet grass, then a man in a good coat could produce that sentence at me in about four years and use it to take a stall off a woman of forty-one who has been in the trade twenty-six years. And you have two children and a wall and a season's sowing behind you and I am not going to do that to you and you are not going to ask me to."

"I would not ask you," said Odile Marn. "And the same the other way, and I have thought about it, and I am going to say the true reason and it is not a compliment to either of us.

"It is that I have been spent and I know precisely what it is, because a man in a good coat told me on the sixth of the month last year and asked me if I would like to hear the terms and I said no and he went away, and there was not a second question in it for either of us, and I have not been able to get over that. And I am not going to stand in a room and say a thing about your lease and be spent for it, and neither are you, and if you stand in a room and say a thing about my ditch then you will be spent for it and there is nothing whatever anybody can do to either of us afterwards."

---

"I will do it," said Neris Kade.

She said it before either of them had finished and she said it to both of them at once, in the ordinary voice of a person reading a term off a form, and Marek Vale did not stop her, and she went on.

"I am a season examiner for Rainmarket and the uplands. I have not been produced. I am not on a board of anything. There is a sheet in a flat case in Bell Stair with my name at the top of one of two columns and his name at the top of the other and a rule on it that neither of us may write in the other's column, and on that sheet it says, in his own hand, that he is not a witness and not a party and not a surety. I have read it four times. I was not going to quote it at you and I have now quoted it at you, and you can both do what you like with it.

"So say the day. The ditch. Odile, and the day you want it. And Margit, and whatever it is you need said about a pitch, and say it as one sentence, because I am not going to be a second person in a room, I am going to be a person who has stood in a room once, and after today I am exactly the same as you are and none of us will ever know which of the three of us was standing here in the first place."

The two women looked at each other across a deal table on a market road.

"No," said Odile Marn.

"No," said Margit Sova.

And then Margit Sova said the rest of it, and she said it to Neris Kade and not to Marek Vale.

"You are the first person in four years who has offered me that, and I want you to understand that I am not refusing it out of politeness and I am not refusing it because I think you would be careless with it. I am refusing it because I know what it is to be used, and I would be doing it to you, and I would be doing it on a market road in front of four people, and you would be standing in a room in the second month for the rest of your life saying *I said it once*."

And Odile Marn said the other half of it, and she said it to Marek Vale.

"And you are a man who reads papers out loud, and I want you to hear the difference between the two offers you have just made me. Hers was a body in a room. Yours was a form with a box in it. And the box would get filled in, and a man in a good coat told me that on a bank on the sixteenth and I have not been able to get it out of my head, and I have known since the sixth of the month last year that the reason nobody asks me anything is a queue, and there is no box in a queue."

---

Marek Vale put his hand into his coat and there was nothing in it, and he took it out again, and neither of the two women said anything about it, and it was the fourth time in about seven weeks.

"What you both actually need," he said, "is not a witness."

"Yes," said Margit Sova. "Say the rest of it, because I have been waiting since the sixth of the month last year for a person to say the rest of it and you are six or seven seconds late."

"You need a form that says no. You need a piece of paper with your name on it and one word on it, and the word is *not*, and it means: this lease is ended, and I do not hold this pitch, and I do not owe anybody on this ground, and nobody may produce anything against me about this ground. Not a challenge. Not a refusal that goes into a queue. A thing that exists on its own.

"And there is no such form. There is not one in this kingdom. I have been three years in this work and I have never seen one and I have never been told of one and I have asked a woman of fifty-one at a counter in the ninth month of last year and a clerk a night and a day up this river on the second of this month, and neither of them was lying to me, and both of them would have known."

---

Margit Sova put the broom against the counter and went under it and came back with the box.

She put the box on the deal table, which nobody had asked her to do, and the hasp was stiff, and she worked it with the heel of her hand, and there were nine sheets in it and they were thin and they had been folded and unfolded so many times that they had gone soft along the middle crease.

"I have never read this out," she said. "Not to anybody. Twenty-six years of what a pitch owes and what it was given and in what order, and there is not one name in the whole of it, and there is no head at the top of any page, and there is no total anywhere in it and I could not add it up if I tried, because a total is a figure I would be putting on a table in my own hand about nine hundred people.

"I am going to read nine lines of it. On a table. In the open. And I am not doing it because it is evidence and it is not evidence, there is nobody here to enter anything and there is nobody in this city who could produce it at me in four years and I do not care.

"I am doing it because you have brought a table, and because a table is the first thing anybody has put in front of me since the sixth of the month last year that was not a form, and because it is about time."

She read nine lines of it in a voice that got worse as it went, the way a hand gets worse, and nobody wrote any of it down, and when she had finished she shut the hasp and put the key on the nail inside.

Nobody asked her what the column at the top of the page was for. Nobody offered to head it. It went back under the counter with the padlock on and the key on the nail, and it is there now, and it has never had anything at the top of it and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may put one.

---

They carried the table back at about the fourth bell. It went down the road between them, one at each end, and a man with a barrow made way for it without being asked, and neither of the two women came to watch it go, because neither of the two women had come to watch it arrive.

Margit Sova's pitch was not held that month and the ditch was not cut in the first month or the second, and it is not going to be cut in the third, and there is nothing whatever in this basin that can cut a ditch.

And two women who had never spoken to each other before the twenty-third of the first month of MR 416 knew each other's names by the fourth bell, which is four hours, and neither of them said a word about it in the lane, and there was a man in a good coat nowhere in either of those four hours.
