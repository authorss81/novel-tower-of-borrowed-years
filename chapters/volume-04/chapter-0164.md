# Chapter 164 — The Basin Is Short

The bank on the Rainmarket low ground had been raised a foot in six days and it had about a mile and a bit to go, and the clay came out of a lane at the top of the reach in barrow-loads, and the ground was still hard enough to take a barrow wheel, and it would not be for much longer.

Marek Vale went down it at the second bell on the sixteenth of the first month of MR 416 and found eleven gangs on it and a board on a trestle at the top with eleven names on it, and the eleven names were the eleven names of the witnesses, and the district had put eleven on the board because a works board takes eleven, and the number of people whose attestation was of record in this basin and had been produced was not eleven.

There were about nine people's attestations of record in this basin. He had come to the number himself coming down the river in the eighth month of last year and had stopped at it because nobody would confirm a figure about a number of people, and the board on the trestle wanted eleven.

"You have got the same eleven I have got," said Neris Kade. She had a coat on and a stick and had been on the bank since the first bell. "And the district has known since the fourth of this month and the district has not said anything, and that is the same as a vote, and it is a vote that has been taken."

---

The woman who came down the face of the bank at about the third bell had a coat that had been in a hedge, and no gloves, and she took one off and put her bare hand flat on the clay and left it there while she looked at her own hand.

"The clay is good," she said. "I want that said first, because I have read a great many reports and they all begin with what is wrong. The clay is good and the ground is right and there are about nine barrows on the top of the reach and there is nobody to load them."

Her name was Seren Calder and she was forty and she was a settlement marshal, and she said the word *marshal* the way a person says their own trade in somebody else's yard: without weight and without apology.

"I have been in this basin four days. I have been up the low ground and down it and I have been in two rooms in Bell Stair and I have read a file, and I am going to tell you what is wrong with the file, and then I am going to tell you what is wrong with the bank, and the bank is the one that matters.

"Everything about the bank is right. Eleven gangs, a mile and a bit, six days while the ground takes the wheel, and the ground will not after that, and there will be a fortnight after that in which it cannot be done at all, and in the third month the low ground goes under. That is not a fear and it is not a figure anybody has made up. It is what the water does. I have been doing this nineteen years and I have never once known it to do otherwise.

"And the bank cannot be raised, because a works board has to be witnessed, and I cannot witness a board and neither can a district, because witnessing is a person of record standing in a place on a day, and the people of record in this basin are nine, and the nine have been produced once, and there is no form on which a person may be produced twice."

---

"Somebody said a word about the ground being short," said Marek Vale.

"They said the basin is short. They were correct and they were being polite." Calder put the glove back on. "Here is the whole of it and I am going to say it in the order I say it and I am not going to soften anything, because softening it is what got nine people into this and I would rather be the one who says it.

"A settlement in a public emergency may be sealed on a surety. That is the oldest instrument there is and it is the only one that works in a fortnight. A surety is a person, and the person is named, and the naming is done by an office, and the office gets the name out of a book, and the book is a book of attestations, and every attestation in that book in this basin has been produced once, and a person who has been produced once has said the word already and there is nothing left to say it with.

"So I have nine names in a book and I need eleven on a board, and there are nine people on this bank who are not short of labour and are short of something they cannot give me, and every office in this city that I have asked in four days has said the same nine words, which are *that is not a question this office puts*.

"And I have not come here to argue about the nine words. I have come here because I have been in four rooms in four days and I have decided to do the thing that nobody in those four rooms would do, and I am going to say it and then I am going to stand on this bank while you answer it.

"I am going to treat a refusal in a public emergency as endangerment. If a person of record is asked to stand in a place on a day and says no, and the consequence of their no is that a bank does not go up on this ground, then that person is endangering the district, and the district may act on that, and I have the instrument, and it is short, and I have used it twice in nineteen years, and both times I was right about the water."

---

Neris Kade did not say anything for the length of a breath, and then she said the thing that was true, in the voice she used for a thing she had checked.

"You are being told to sign something you have not read," she said. "Not by a man in a good coat. By a person who will fill in a box. And when the nine of them sign, on the ninth morning, in a queue, in your voice — which you will have given them, because you always do, because you are good at this and it is a kindness — then you will have nine witnesses, none of whom agreed to anything, and you will not know which of the nine was willing, and you will not be able to find out afterwards, because there is no form on which anybody may be entered as having been asked.

"So the bank goes up and the account is false on the ninth morning, and in about four years a person with a good coat produces it, and the finding is that the name behind the paper is the name in the witness box, and that a man cannot stand on both sides of a line, and that nobody was asked — and that is a true finding, and it will be the whole of the finding, and you will have put your own name in the middle of it, and I do not believe you want that."

"I do not want it," said Calder. "That is correct and I have not got an answer to it and I am not going to manufacture one on a bank. It is also true that if we do not do it, this ground is under water in the third month. I have said both of those and I am not going to choose between them here, because choosing between them is what this basin has been doing for three years and I am not here for that either."

---

"There is a thing I have," said Marek Vale, "and I am going to say it in the correct order, which is the thing itself first and then why it is worth nothing."

"In the third month of MR 415 a record loan was sealed at a records office a night and a day up the river, on a standing authority, correctly, to stop a district account being struck out at the end of the season. Its price was entered in the last of five boxes and the price was not a figure and it was not a number of months. It was *fourteen attestations*. And that is why there is no rate on this low ground this season, and that is the only reason, and the rate was going to be on this ground in the fifth month of last year.

"And the fourteen are in that book with the nine. And the nine on your board and the nine in that book are the same nine and I have been to the office and there is no form in it on which a filing clerk may strike an entry out of a regional record, and the man who entered it is a man of forty-nine who tells me things before I ask him and who has not been able to make himself ask for a quarter of an hour at a time since the third month."

He put his hand flat on the trestle and left it there.

"I have said that and I know exactly what it is worth, and I am telling you the figure on it before you ask, because you are the first person in four days who has asked me a question instead of telling me a thing.

"I have no paper. I have been up the river twice and both times I came down with nothing in my coat. And I have not got a standing, and I have not got an office, and I am a man who on the thirtieth of the ninth month of last year stood behind a comparison in a room eight feet by eleven and put his name in a box, and a chain walked to me, and what came off me was not a year and not a season and it was that no room in this kingdom will take my word as evidence of anything for as long as that paper stands.

"So you have it. It is true. It cannot go in a file, and I would not know what to tell you to do with it, and if you use it you will be using the word of a man whose word you have been told by three people in four days does not count, and if you do not use it then I have just said a true thing out loud on a bank and got nothing, which is what happens to me most days in a month."

---

Calder looked at him for longer than is comfortable.

"I am not going to act on it," she said. "I want to be exact about that, because you have been exact with me and I am not going to have you telling people afterwards that I did. I have not got the record loan in front of me and I am not going to seal anything on a sentence a man said on a bank. That would be my version of what has happened to nine people in this basin and I have read every one of those nine files in four days.

"And I want to say one more thing, and it is the last thing I am going to say on this bank that is not about the bank.

"There was a loan sealed at Verrow in the third month of last year and I have known about it since about the fourth, and I did not go and read the price box, and I did not go and read it last week either when I came down here, and I am telling you that on a bank in front of three witnesses so that it is said. I am the settlement marshal for this reach and I seal things, and I did not read it, and I am not going to be able to tell you why, and I have not got a good reason and I have had a week to find one."

She took the glove off again and looked at the clay.

"Now. What would you have instead. Somebody always has something instead, and I have got six days and a mile and a bit of bank, and I will listen to it, and I am telling you in advance that I am not going to be pleasant about it if it is a form."

---

"A room," said Marek Vale. "A room with no employer and no surety and nothing on the door, in a place this city cannot name, and in it a person is asked whether they were free, and the answer is written down, and the writing down is what makes it count, because a thing nobody wrote down did not happen, and that is true in every room in this city including yours."

"That is a form," said Seren Calder. "I am not being clever. Look at it. A box, a question, a hand that fills it in. You have described a form, and I have read nine thousand of them, and I am not going to tell you that they are all the same, because they are not, but I am going to tell you the one thing about all of them.

"The box gets filled in.

"Not by a villain. By the person at the window with the list, at the eighth bell, in a good voice, on a morning when there are nine or ten people in front of the window and the day is long. And the box will say *free* nine times in a morning, in your hand, in the hand of a man who has been told that the form requires an answer and not that a person requires one. And the entry will be true. It will be a true entry in a true box in a true form, and it will be false, and there will be nothing whatever in the world to show anybody which of the two it was.

"That is not an argument against your room. That is a description of what is already in it, and you have been in this basin three years and you can have that for nothing, because you have seen the fourth line on every one of them."

---

Neris Kade had a sheet in her coat and a flat case, and she took the sheet out on the top of the trestle and wrote in her own column, and she did not write in the other one, and Marek Vale's column had nothing in it at all since the last night of the ninth month and he did not have a pencil out and there was nothing in his coat to write with anyway.

"Send for the four," said Calder. "Not to me. To whoever is keeping them. And before you send, work out what happens on the morning they come, and work out what you are going to say to the ninth of them when they understand what they have been asked to do, because I have watched four people in four days arrive at that moment and I have watched two of them walk out of a room, and I am telling you that the moment is not the asking. The moment is the ninth of the month, in a lane, in the rain, with the ground going soft and a list in your hand.

"And I am going to ask you one thing and it is not part of the marshal's business and I am asking it because I would rather ask it than have it come up on a day I cannot answer it.

"I am going to be on this bank at the sixth bell on the eighteenth, and again on the nineteenth, and again on the twentieth, and on each of those days I am going to be standing where I am standing now with a list of eleven names on a board. If you are somewhere I can find on one of those days, then I am not going to have to come and look for you, and if you are not, then I am going to have to write a line about that too, and it will be a very short line, and it will be true, and I have never yet written a line that was not true."

She went up the face of the bank in her stocking feet because the clay took the boot, and she did not look back, and the gang on the fourth rank kept working for about a quarter of an hour after a marshal had been on the bank and then stopped in ones and twos, the way a bank does.
