# Chapter 185 — Nine Hundred, and a Drawer

The press on Ropewalk was running at about the second bell on the sixteenth of the fourth month of MR 416, and it had been running since the second of the second month, and about nine hundred sheets of a four-line form were going out of that shop in the ninth week of a run that nobody in this kingdom had any power to stop.

Iveta Roslak took the sheets off the delivery board in twelves and stacked them, and she did not stop working when two people came up the shop and stood at the end of the stone, because that is what a printer does.

One of them was a man with a wet sleeve. The other was a woman of twenty-two with a satchel and the walk of a person who has spent a year and a half in a copy room in this city and has not spent a day of it since.

"You have come about the form," said Iveta Roslak, and put the stick down, "and before anybody says anything I am going to say four things and you will not get an answer out of me to any of them, and I have been asked three times since the growth month and two of the three were men and one was a woman and all three were told the same four things, and I would like it noticed that I told them to a man in a good coat the same as I am telling them to you."

---

"One. I cannot stop it and neither can either of you. There is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be told to stop a printer. There is no office whose business it is to be told. The person who would have stopped it would have to be a person with a name at the top of something and there is not one in this basin, and the seat that used to be one is empty and has been empty seven times and I have stopped counting."

"Seven," said Nim Rook.

"Eight, this month," said Iveta Roslak. "I am told. And I have got a shop and a press and I am going to keep printing. I have a compositor and I have got a boy who inks and I have got a woman of about thirty who does the delivery on a cart and she is going to be paid on the ninth, and there are nine hundred of these and I am going to finish them, and I would like that said at me in a shop so that I have heard it said."

"Nobody is going to stop you," said Marek Vale. "I have come up the hill to say it to you and I have not been on that hill a long time and I have got a notice on the back of a printed thing that says a man whose word no room will take, and I have got a finding in a book that says the same thing in nineteen words, and I could not stop a printer if I had a form in my hand and a man in a good coat to give it to."

"Two," said Iveta Roslak. "I do not know who asked for them and I am not going to find out and I am not hiding anything. I have been paid in advance. A clerk came into this shop in the second month of this year, put down an order on a pad in a fair small hand, and gave me a name, and the name is the name of an office and not the name of a man, and the order said nine hundred of the four-line and the same paper and the same price, and I have printed what I was asked to print and I am not going to invent a party in a shop to make the morning easier for two people standing at the end of my stone."

"A fair small hand," said Nim Rook.

"A fair small hand," said Iveta Roslak, "and I have set type off a fair small hand every week for nineteen years and I could not tell you one thing about the person who wrote it, and neither can you, and that is not a mystery anybody has to solve. It is a fair small hand."

---

Nim Rook walked the length of the shop and read the rack, and she did it the way a person reads a rack, which is backwards and with her hands behind her, and she read four proofs and put one back in the wrong place and did not apologise for it.

"You have got one of everything in a tray," she said.

"Every shop has."

"You have got one of everything in date order and the ninth item in the tray is the first thing you ever set and it has five lines on it."

Iveta Roslak went into the back room and came out with the flat wooden tray and put it on the bench the way she had put it down for a man with a wet sleeve in the eighth month of last year, and went through it, and the ninth item in date order was the same form with a fifth line under a rule that nothing in the world was ever going to be under.

"That is nineteen years old this month," she said. "I set it in the third month of that year with a stick and a galley and no compositor, and I have set that forme eleven times in nineteen years, and nobody in this kingdom has ever asked me why, and I am not going to be asked it today either, and I am not going to be asked it by somebody who has read about it."

"You told him," said Nim Rook.

"I told a man who came in with a folded leaf of cheap paper and wanted to know what was on the third line," said Iveta Roslak, "and I have told a compositor's boy and a woman of about thirty who does the delivery and a man from a paper house in the third month, and that is four people, and you are not going to be the fifth and I have not got anything left for it."

---

"Three," she said, and she squared the sheet on the bench with her knuckle, "and this is the one I have not said to anybody and I am going to say it to two people who have not asked for it.

"I am going to cut the fifth line."

The shop was not quiet. The press was running. She waited for a gap between two pulls anyway, which is a thing printers do and which is a kind of politeness that costs money.

"Not into the forme I am printing. Into a separate forme, for the tray, dated, in order, in this shop, where it will be in a drawer in a building on a street that a man in a good coat can walk to in four seconds. That is what a printer does with a thing she has cut. I keep one of everything. That is not an archive and it is not a secret and I have told that to a man with a wet sleeve and I am telling it to you now because the two of you have come up here for the same reason he did and I would like there to be two of you."

She went and got the type out of a case and put five pieces of it on the stone in a row and squared them, and she did not have to measure them, which told Nim Rook more about the shop than any of the rest of the morning had.

"Four," said Iveta Roslak, when the five pieces were set and spaced and lying loose on the stone under a weight, "and this is the last thing and I am going to be exact about it, and then I am going to go back to the press.

"I am not going to tell you whether it is right."

---

"You are going to have to," said Nim Rook.

"No," said Iveta Roslak. "And I want to be exact about the shape of the no, because the shape of it is the whole of it and you have both come up a hill for it.

"It is not that I do not know. I have had five pieces of type in a case for nineteen years and I have known what they say since the day I cut them out of a forme and put the forme in a tray, and I have never once been asked and I have never once had to say.

"And it is not that I am frightened of the answer, because a woman of forty-four who has had a shop for nineteen years is not frightened of a piece of type. I have been frightened of two men in good coats in a year and I have printed for both of them and I would print for them again.

"It is that the moment a printer says a form is right, there is a printer's name at the top of it." She put two fingers on the stone beside the type. "I have watched this business for nineteen years. A form is a piece of paper that a person signs, and a form is a thing because somebody stands behind it and says it stands. Not the person signing. The one behind. The one whose word the signing person goes home and tells her husband about. And the whole of the trouble in this kingdom is that there is a name at the top of every form and there is not one box anywhere under it for the thing that matters, and I have known that since I was twenty-four and I have known it in my hands and not in my head.

"So if I say the five lines are right, then a printer has said a form is right, and the printer is a person with a name at the top of a thing, and in four years a man in a good coat can produce a piece of paper at anybody in this kingdom and it will be a form that a printer stood behind. And I am not going to be the first person in this kingdom to put my name at the top of a thing about a witness. There are about nine people in this basin whose names are at the top of things and every one of the nine has been produced once and there is no form on which a person may be produced twice.

"And I would like it noticed that I did not say it is right and I did not say it is wrong, and I cut the type, and the type is in a tray in date order, and I have said no vote."

---

"She will not say," said Nim Rook, at the end of the lane down on the low ground, at about the sixth bell when the copy room shuts and there is nothing in the hill worth waiting for.

"I am not going to," said Marek Vale.

"You believed her."

"I have had four months of believing people and I have been wrong about none of it and I have not been thanked for one of them, and I have got four things left that I could say to her and every one of them is a question and she has told me the truth four times and the truth is that a person who cannot be asked is a person who cannot be answered for, and that is not her being clever. That is her being the only kind of careful person there is."

"You could have asked her whether it is right."

"And if she had said yes I would have been a man with a printer's word in his mouth and a form with five lines on it and no office and no standing and nothing to do with either of them," said Marek Vale. "And in about four years a man in a good coat could produce the two of us together, a printer and a man with no office agreeing, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which either of us could say that we were asked."

---

The run finished on the twenty-first of the fourth month, and nine hundred four-line forms went out of a shop on Ropewalk on carts and on backs and were carried up a hill to a copy room and were put in a drawer and were signed at a counter in the ordinary way by people who were not asked anything, and every one of them was well made and correctly filled in and in type and not one of them has a defect in it.

And in a drawer in the same shop, in a flat wooden tray, in date order, at the end, there is a forme of five lines and it has a day on the wrapper and it is not being used and it will not be used by anybody without an act by somebody else, and no act has happened, and there is no form in this kingdom on which any person may be asked to bring one about.
