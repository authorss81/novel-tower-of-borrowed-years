# Chapter 173 — A Season and Not a Person

The Counters' Walk ditch is four hundred yards of wet grass between two seed yards with a hedge on both sides of it, and on the twenty-sixth of the second month of MR 416 there was a woman standing in the middle of it with a spade that had a worn socket and a straight handle and a piece of label tape round the shaft, and she was not digging.

"You are going to tell me I need a machine," she said.

"You need a machine," said Marek Vale.

"I have wanted a machine since the rains of the year before last," said Odile Marn. "I have a ditch that was cut in the growth month of MR 411 by four men and a boy and has silted up since the rains month of MR 414, and a wall at the head of it with a course in it that does not run, and I have been to nine counters in about a month and every one of them was correct."

"There is a board in a seed house off this road," said Neris Kade, "and I am going to say the whole of what is on it out loud, and then I am going to say what may be done with it, and then I am going to stop talking, because I have been in this basin three years watching a man describe a mechanism in a ditch and call it a morning."

---

The board had nine lines cut in it and it was on a trestle in the seed house and it had been made by the district and it belonged to no office. Line four was the Counters' Walk ditch. Line eight had a name against it. Line nine was a well, and line nine was empty.

"There is a season works in force on this board," said Neris Kade. "It is in force while the district does not withdraw it and the district has not withdrawn it and there is no rule anywhere that says a board stops being a board because nobody has looked at it. It was witnessed in the ninth month of last year, in the ordinary way, by a person standing in this building on a day.

"That person was you," said Odile Marn.

"That person was you, and you did it in the rain because a man from the water office said it would be doing the district a favour, and you have not thought about it since, and I have thought about it about nine times in five months."

"I have thought about it every day for five months," said Odile Marn. "I have not thought about anything else. I was produced in the sixth month of last year and I found out about it in a rail queue, and since then I have known exactly what I am, and what I am is a person who stood in this building once and cannot stand in it again.

"So there is a works on a board and there is no witness for it, and the two things are the same size."

"They are not the same size," said Neris Kade, "and the difference is the whole of what I have come to say, so I am going to say it now and then I am going to stand at the trestle.

"The works is in force. That is a fact about a board in a building and it does not need a person to be true. A witness is a person standing in a place on a day, and that is a different fact, and a person cannot do the first of them and a piece of wood cannot do the second of them, and the two have been sitting in one another's mouths in this basin for three years and that is the reason nothing has ever been read correctly.

"Which means the works can be pointed. Once. At a holding or at a line, because a holding is part of a line and a line is on the board, and pointing it at more of the line is not a bigger power, it is the same power with a longer reach, and I have checked that twice in the last four days with a book and I would like it noted that I checked it.

"And the cost comes out of the ground it is put on. Not a person. A ground. And I am not going to soften that and Marek is not going to soften it either, because the two of you have spent five months being careful in ditches and the ground does not care how careful anybody is."

---

Milo Trban, fifty-two, who had worked the ground above Odile Marn's for thirty years and who had come in the seventh month of MR 411 and signed a piece of paper about a wall because she had asked him to, was standing at the door of the seed house with his coat off and had not come in.

He had a ground of his own above hers and a sowing on it and no hands, and the second sowing had been late everywhere on the low ground since the ninth month of last year and it was late on his.

Marek Vale asked him what he needed.

Trban looked at the board for a while.

"I have got a fortnight on that ground and no hands to work it," he said, "and I am fifty-two and I have two of my own in the town and a girl coming in the fourth month, and I do not want fuel and I do not want money and I have said that to four people in five months.

"What I want is that ground to come up the way hers came up behind her wall in the ninth month of last year, and I want it to be a fortnight and not a season, and I want it to be this month and not in the next one, and I have known for five months that the only way that happens is a man puts his hand on a board, and I have been standing at this door for about a quarter of an hour deciding whether I was going to be the sort of person who asks for it."

"Who asked you?"

"Nobody," said Milo Trban. "That is what I have been standing here for. Nobody has ever asked me anything about that afternoon. I signed about a wall in the seventh month of MR 411 because a woman I have worked beside for thirty years asked me to, and I have not been asked a single question about it since, and I have built five months out of that and I would like it to go on."

Marek Vale put his hand flat on the board and slid it about four inches along line eight until it was against the holding above hers, and did not say anything, and the ground came up.

A man of fifty-two who had worked it for thirty years looked at it for a moment and then went and sat down on a seed bin and did not say anything for about a minute, and then said that it was early, and then said nothing else about it for a week.

---

"You have done it once," said Odile Marn. She had not taken her eyes off the board. "You did it once in the ninth month and I have heard about it from four people who were not in the room, and a fortnight is not a ditch, and you have not cut my ditch."

"No."

"And you have just done it a second time and that one was a fortnight and not a season and it was a fortnight because I asked for a season and I did not get one and I decided to want the smaller thing, which I want to be honest about, because I have been in a ditch for five months and I have got good at wanting the smaller thing."

"I asked him first," said Marek Vale. "That is the whole of the difference and it is not a discovery and I did not work it out in a room. I asked him first and then I pointed it. In the ninth month I asked you and then I pointed it. That is twice."

"Then do mine," said Odile Marn. "Not a holding. I want to be exact about it, because I have had a fortnight put in me in a way I did not ask for and I am not having another one. Do the line. Do all four hundred yards of it. And put the cost where it goes, which is on the ground, and I want to know now what the ground is."

---

He pointed it at line four.

Not at a holding. At the line, all four hundred yards of wet grass, the whole of it, and the same hand went along the board a foot at a time and the trench came out of it and the clay came out of it and the water in the bottom of it went along to a place where there was somewhere for it to go.

It took a day and a half. A machine came down the Counters' Walk at about the seventh bell on the twenty-sixth and went back up it on the twenty-eighth, and the four hundred yards of the Counters' Walk ditch is a ditch, and it is a good one, and a man of fifty-six who has worked that ground for thirty years came down it, walked the whole length of it, walked back up it, and said that whoever laid it wanted the falls the other way for a low ground, and then went to work somewhere else.

"The cost comes out of the ground it is put on," said Marek Vale, "and I am going to say it before you ask and I am going to say it in the same words I said in the ninth month, because the words have not changed and what has changed is how much of the line there is.

"Not a person. A ground, and a season on that ground, and not this season. This season is now. Next season, on the line, and on the line and not on the holding behind your wall, because I pointed at the line and I pointed at it on purpose.

"And I do not know what a season weighs. I have been in this work three years and I have never once known until about nine months afterwards, and there is no form on which I could tell you today, and I am telling you so that in nine months you will not be told it by a stranger."

---

Odile Marn stood in a ditch and looked along four hundred yards of it.

"The other side," she said.

"Whose?"

"Zdenka Hrab's. Sixty-two. She has held that yard since her mother's and she has never signed anything in her life that I know of, and she came out of her door on the twenty-eighth and looked at this and went back in, and she has not said a word to me about it, and she is not going to, and she does not know that there is a cost and neither do I.

"So that is where it goes. A season on a line, and a line is not a piece of ground, it is a border between two pieces of ground, and a border has two addresses, and the cost does not have one, and it will come to both of us at about the same time and neither of us will know to say no.

"And I have been waiting five months for a person to come up this road and cut this ditch and I have imagined it about nine hundred times, and in every one of the nine hundred it was a man with a machine and a board and a thing he was allowed to do, and I never once imagined the bit after, and I would like it noted that I was not able to imagine the bit after because nobody told me there was one, and that is the last time anybody is going to be able to say that about me."

---

Milo Trban had not gone home. He was still at the door of the seed house with his coat on.

"You did not ask me," he said, to Marek Vale, and not to her.

"No."

"I want to say the thing about it and then I am going to go to my ground," he said. "I have stood at this door for about an hour and worked out that you have known for five months that the only man in this basin who could put a hand on that board in a way anybody would believe is a spent witness with no office who has a sheet in a flat case with two names at the head of it, and I have worked out that in the ninth month of last year you did that behind my neighbour's wall and did not ask me, and that she looked at my wall afterwards and decided something and has never told me what.

"And I am not saying you were wrong. I am saying I would have said yes, and I would have said yes in about four seconds, and I have had five months of not knowing, and I would rather have had the four seconds.

"That is all. I am not going to be thanked and I am not asking for anything and I have a sowing to go and see to."

He went up the lane.

---

And on the twenty-eighth of the second month of MR 416 the Counters' Walk ditch was a ditch, four hundred yards of it, cut in a day and a half by a machine and a man with no office who asked two people what they needed before he pointed anything at either of them, and the cost of it was a season on a line, and a line has two addresses, and one of them belongs to a woman of sixty-two who came out of her door on the twenty-eighth, looked at four hundred yards of new trench, and went back in without saying anything, and she does not know yet that she is going to be sent a bill for a ditch she did not ask for, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be told that a bill is coming.
