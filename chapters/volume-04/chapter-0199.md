# Chapter 199 — No Living Counterpart

Nim Rook brought a challenge on the twenty-first of the fifth month of MR 416, and she had never brought one in her life, and she had thought about it for five weeks, and she did not have a form for it and did not need one, because a challenge is free and there is no form in this kingdom on which a challenge may be refused.

She had the sheet in four folds in the inside of her coat. She took it out in the second of the four rooms behind the Threshold, which is ten feet by eight, at about the third bell on the twenty-first, and put it on the desk, and Deputy Tolek did not touch it for about four seconds.

"You have made that yourself," she said.

"I made that on the thirteenth of the fourth month in a copy room in Bell Stair in about a quarter of an hour," said Nim Rook, "out of a page that was on a table in front of me for a reason to do with my work. It is a true copy. It is in my hand. It is in four folds because I have been carrying it since the thirteenth of the fourth month and I have not shown it to anybody, and I want that entered before I say anything else.

"And you are not to look at it for about a minute, and I am going to tell you why, and then you can look at it."

---

"A challenge requires a sheet," said Deputy Tolek. "I have said that to a settlement marshal on this floor and I have said it to a man in a good coat and I have said it to a man with no office. There is no sheet in this room. I have no instrument for what is in front of me, and I am not going to pretend otherwise."

"You have got one," said Nim Rook. "It is on the desk and you have not touched it and I have put it there. It is not a form. It is a page and it is a true copy of a page and I made it out of a page that was on a table in front of me for a reason connected with my work, which I am not going to explain, and it is a true copy and that is the whole of it and it is the only thing in this business I have ever been sure of.

"And I am not here about whether the page is true. The page is true. I have had it since the thirteenth of the fourth month and I have not been able to find out one thing about it, and there are three people in this city who have told me no about it in three different trades, and I am not here to ask them again.

"I am here about the other half."

---

The Deputy read the sheet out. It is a page of a ward roll. The far-right column of it has nothing at the head of it and there are about nine names in that column and the hand on all nine is a fair small hand, and on the fourteenth line down of that column there is a name.

**Ilen Rook.**

Nobody in that room had said that name out loud in this city in five months, and the woman saying it was twenty-two years old and had a satchel and had been out on the road at about the seventh bell three nights a week, and she said it once, in the ordinary way, as though she were reading a thing off a page, because she had practised it in a lane four hundred times.

"I have not said that name in this city for more than two years," she said, "and I have not said it to him and he has not asked me, and I am going to say it in a room because a room with a hearing officer in it is the only kind of room there is where a person is allowed to say a thing they have not said.

"And here is what I have not been able to find out, and what I have come with a true copy about, and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have got.

"I do not know whether there is a living person with that name on it. That is the whole of it. That is five weeks of a road and two rooms and a woman of fifty-one whom I have never asked for anything and a man with no office who has not read it. I have a name on a page and a page that is true, and the page does not say whether there is a person."

"It is a ward roll," said Deputy Tolek. "A roll of a ward is a page of people in a ward. That is a fact about a ward and not a fact about a person, and there is a finding of the fourth of the first month of this year in a book in a building on the causeway side that says a court cannot enter a finding that any person did or did not consent to anything, and it has looked, and there is none to enter.

"And I am not going to give you an answer that is not an answer. There is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be entered as having been on a page, or as not having been on a page, or on which you may be told that a name in a column is a person. I am not aware of one, and I have been nineteen years in this work and I have looked."

---

"I am not asking you about the page," said Nim Rook. "I am asking you about a legal copy, and I am going to say the whole of why I know to ask that, and it is nine sentences and I have been carrying it since the thirteenth of the fourth month and I have not said any of it to anybody.

"A true copy is true of a page. A legal copy is not that, and I have carried one on a road twice a month for six years and I know the difference, and the difference is a counterpart. A legal copy is made for a court and it comes in counterparts, one for each party, and every counterpart is entered in an issue book with the name of the person it went to, and the party holds it and the party's name is on the line. That is what a legal copy is. It is two halves and one half is a person.

"And there is one in this city, in a book a night and a day up this river and in a building on the causeway side, made in the fourth month of last year, of a return of persons of record, and it has counterparts, and the issue book for them is a book a hearing officer may open.

"And I am not asking you to guess. I am asking you to open it, and to look at the fourteenth line of the counterpart column, and I am telling you the name on it before you do, so that you cannot be a person who opened a book and found a name and went pale, and so that there is nothing in your face for anybody in this room to have seen afterwards."

Deputy Tolek read the sheet out again to be sure of the line, and then she went and got a book out of a drawer, and it took her nine minutes to find the right one because there is no head at the head of the issue book either, and she said so, twice, out loud, to nobody.

---

She had it open on the fourteenth line and she read the name and then she read the line under it, which is the other half of a counterpart, and the other half is not a name.

It is an address.

"There are about nine of them on this page and I am not going to read them out, and there is a rule about that which you know and which I have said in this room on a different morning," said Deputy Tolek. "Every one of them is a name in the first half and an address in the second half. That is what a counterpart is. The name is who it went to and the address is where they are, and a counterpart is delivered to the address and not to the person, because a person is not a place a court can send anything to.

"And I can read the name on the fourteenth line. I can enter that it is a name in a counterpart column of a legal copy of a return of persons of record, that the legal copy was made in the fourth month of last year, that the copy is good, and that the name on the fourteenth line is the name on the fourteenth line of a ward roll page with nothing at the head of the column.

"And I cannot enter one other thing, and I am going to enter that I cannot, in the same sentence, and here it is.

"The address under the name cannot be produced at this court. It is an address and I can read it and it is a place, and there is no office in this kingdom that can be shown that place. There is no form on which an office may be entered as having sent a counterpart there, because a form for that requires a door, and a door requires a name, and there is no name on any map a clerk in this city can look at.

"So: I can enter that a counterpart was issued. I cannot enter that it was delivered, or that anybody received it, or that the person on the fourteenth line is alive, or that she is dead, and there is no question I can put to a person about any of those four, and I have looked, and there is none to enter.

"And that is what no living counterpart means. It does not mean there is nobody. It means there is no half of this document in the hands of a person, and no office in this kingdom can make one, and in about four years a man in a good coat is going to produce this page at somebody and it is going to be a true page about a true name, and there is going to be nothing whatever on it to say where the other half went."

---

Nim Rook stood in a room ten feet by eight and did not say anything for about the length of a minute, and then she said the thing she had come for and she said it to the Deputy and not to the man at the rail of the side with no rail.

"Thank you for looking," she said, "and I am not going to be thanked and I would like it entered that I said that about a thank you, because I have watched this basin for six years and everybody in it says some version of that sentence and it is the only sentence I have ever been sure of.

"And now I am going to ask the question I have not been able to ask anybody for five weeks and I am going to ask it in this room, and I know who in this room can answer it and I know why he has not, and I am going to let him not answer it, and I want that entered first."

"Nim," said Marek Vale.

"The moment you find out anything at all about a name of mine, you tell me," said Nim Rook. "I set that on a stair in the flood year, and you have kept it for three years through five months in which there have been nine things you could have found out about a name of mine, and you have not asked me one question in five months, not one, and there has been a certified copy with a column on it sitting in three rooms and you have never asked me whether I had seen it.

"You were right on the twenty-ninth of the fourth month in about a second and a half and I have thought about that second and a half every day since and I have not said so to you because there has been nothing to say. I am saying it now because there is going to be something to say in four seconds and I want the price paid before it is late and not after.

"You have the page. It is on the desk. There is a column on it and there are about nine names in it and the fourteenth line down is a name and it is the name of a person I have not said out loud in this city in two years.

"It is not a good page," she said. "It is a true page. It is a true page and it is a true copy and I have carried it since the thirteenth of the fourth month and it is the only thing I have ever been sure of. And I have just been told by a hearing officer in a room ten feet by eight that the other half of it went to an address that no office in this kingdom can be shown.

"That is the answer. I asked whether there is a living person with that name on it and I have got the answer and the answer is that nobody can produce the other half, and I am going to sit down, and I am not going to be told what it means, and if anybody in this room tells me what it means I will stand up and go out."

---

Marek Vale looked at the page on the desk for four seconds, and then he said it, and he said it in under a second, and the room heard it.

"Ilen Rook is not one of the nine people in this basin whose attestations have been produced," he said. "She is not a signer. She has not signed anything at a counter in this city in the five months I have been in it, and she is not a party to any of the four documents, and I have been through every page I have been handed since the growth month of last year and her name is not on one of them, and I want that said in a room and I want it said now.

"And I have found out about a name of yours. I have known it since the thirteenth of the fourth month, because you told me you had a copy of a page with a column on it and you had told nobody and you had not told me, and that was the price being kept, and I did not look and I was right not to look.

"And I am telling you now, and I am telling you before the fifth bell, and I did not make a copy of anything or put a thing in a book or write a word down anywhere, and I never will, and I want the Deputy to enter that I have said it in a room with a hearing officer in it and not in a corridor.

"That is the price. It is three years old and it has been paid and I am not going to be thanked for it because I have not done anything. I have not read a thing I was not asked to read. That is the whole of my work this year."

"Then it is paid," said Nim Rook, "and I have been waiting five weeks and it took four seconds, and I am not going to say anything about that either because I have a thing of my own to say and I have been carrying it since the thirteenth of the fourth month and I have not said it in any room in this city and I am not going to say it in four rooms.

"A certified copy is true of a page. A true copy of a page that was never true is still a true copy."

She put her hand flat on the desk beside the sheet and did not pick it up for a while.

"That is a sentence I have had since the flood year without knowing I had it," she said. "And it is the only sentence in this business that I have ever needed, and I have never said it out loud anywhere before this afternoon, and I am not going to say it again.

"And the thing it is about is a page. Not a person. A page of a roll of a ward with a column on it and nothing at the head of the column and about nine names in it in a fair small hand, and the roll is true, and the page is true, and I have had a certified copy of it since the sixth month of last year and the copy is good, and I have been unable to get one single person out of it in two years.

"And the reason is not that there is nobody on it. The reason is that nobody can be got out of a page, because a page does not have a person in it. It has a name, in a column, in a hand, and the hand is a fair small hand, and I have never seen that hand write anything and I am not going to see it write anything and I would not know it if I did."

She picked the sheet up off the desk and folded it in four and put it inside her coat, in the place it had been since the thirteenth of the fourth month.

---

"And I am not going," she said, to the room, before anybody had said anything about it. "I want to say that out loud, because there are four people in this room and one of you is going to work it out inside a minute, and I have thought about it for five weeks and I have a road and I have done it before.

"It is four hours. There is a charter and the charter needs a witness and there is a woman at the end of it, and if I set off this afternoon I would be back before the seventh bell and there is nobody in this basin who could tell me I had not been, and no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be told not to go somewhere, and none on which anybody may be told not to find out either.

"And I am not going, and the reason is not courage and it is not a rule and it is not the price.

"A room that sends a man has a reach. A reach is an area. An area is a district. A district is a line in a book in this city, and the day that line gets written down this room is an office and the ladder is a door of an office. That was said on the twenty-first of the third month last year by a woman of about thirty-four and it is the reason there are three rooms instead of one, and if I go up that coast road on my own initiative on the strength of a hearing officer's finding then the three of them have sent a woman and the three of them are an office, and there is no form anywhere on which a person may be asked to un-send her.

"So I am going to go out on the road at the seventh bell and carry a piece of paper in this coat, and I am going to do the work that is actually in front of me, and a woman of twenty-two is a courier and the door of my trade is a road and it is not on any map either, and the whole of my trade is that a thing that is not on a map can still be carried from one end of this city to the other and nobody has to have a district to do it."

Deputy Tolek entered the finding and read it out twice at the ordinary speed, and it says that a challenge was brought on the twenty-first of the fifth month of MR 416 by a night courier of twenty-two out of a true copy made by her own hand, and that a name appears on the fourteenth line of a ward roll page and on the fourteenth line of a counterpart column of a legal copy of a return of persons of record, and that the address under the name cannot be produced at this court, and that the court has no question it can put to a person about it and has looked, and that there is none to enter.

And it says that the name in question is not a person of record in this basin, that she is not a signer, and that no document in this basin in the last year has been produced against her.

And it does not say she is dead, and it does not say when, and there is no form in this kingdom on which either of those may be entered, and the page is true, and the copy is true, and the other half of it is on an address no office in this city can be shown, and nobody in that room was thanked for anything.
