# Chapter 180 — The First Person Asked to Accept a Class

The boy of about nine came down the low-ground road at about the sixth bell on the twenty-seventh of the third month of MR 416 with a sentence from a man who keeps the district's tools, and the sentence was seven words long and gave no reason, and the boy was paid in kind for it and was not asked what it was about.

The store is on the low-ground road with a door about seven feet high and a key on a nail inside it, and there were four sledges in it and the smell of clay that has been in a store for a month, and Milos Havel was standing at the counter with his coat on and a sheet of paper in front of him, and his thumb was on the paper and he had not put it on the mark.

Marek Vale walked down from the copy room, which shuts at the six, and the young man who had the form stood up off a stool when he came in.

---

The young man was twenty-six and had been three years in the office on this road and he had a coat that had come from a good shop and a form that had come out of a drawer, and he was called Luka Mesic and he said his own name without being asked, which is a thing a man does when he has read that the man he is talking to has spent a witness and has no standing.

"Mr Vale. I have got a list of eleven on this and you are not on it, and I am not here about anything you have done," said Mesic. "I am here because this man has not put his thumb on a piece of paper and I have got to be able to say that I put it in front of him, and you came down the road and I would rather have you in the room."

Havel did not look up.

"I asked for him," he said. "I have known since the second bell that there is a form and I have not been able to work out what it is, and a man who keeps a key is a man a person can ask, and you are the only person in this basin a man can ask."

---

"This is not a new loan," said Mesic.

He said it in the order it goes on the paper, and he was not in a hurry in the way of a man who has been told to be quick, and Marek Vale did not interrupt once, and afterwards said it was the only time in three years anybody had explained one of these to him without asking him two questions first.

"There is a line of district accounts," said Mesic, "and the low ground is on it, because a record was sealed on it in the third month of last year at the office a night and a day up this river, and it is a record loan, and it is lawful, and it is correct, and it has been upheld twice.

"And the account is alive. That is the thing you have to understand before anything else, and it is the only thing on the paper. The account was not struck, and a rate that was going to come on this ground in the fifth month of last year did not come, and the ground has not gone under. The works stopped about a month ago because of a board and not because of money. I know about the board and I am not here about the board.

"The loan carried its price in the last of five boxes as attestations, and there are fourteen of them, and the whole of that season was carried in the ninth month of last year, and everybody on this ground knows it and nobody can prove it.

"And all fourteen have been produced. Every one. There is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be produced twice, so the price cannot be carried again in attestations. It has to be carried forward, and on a record loan it may be carried forward as a class, because a counterweight on a record may be named as a district or as a class and neither is a person. It is the only instrument in this kingdom that will take one, and it takes it because a record does not test how it was obtained."

---

"And a class cannot consent," said Marek Vale.

"No," said Mesic. "It cannot, and that is the reason the box is there and not a name. If a class had to consent then the box would need names in it, and nine names is a figure about a district and a district is a line in a book, and we would be back where we started and there would be nothing on the paper in the fourth month."

"So the class is the ground. It is going to say the willingness of the people of this ground, and it is going to be true, and it is not going to say how many of them there are, and I am not going to be able to tell you that either, and nobody in the office above me is going to be able to tell me. I have asked. I asked in the second month and the answer was that it is a class."

He put the pen down on the counter, which he had not had in his hand until then.

"And then the last box needs a person," he said, "because an instrument needs a hand, and the hand is not the class. The hand is a person who has been shown the class and takes it. I have got eleven names on this list, you are not one of them, and I have not had a conversation with any of the other ten, because a man in my office told me in the second month that if I stand in a lane and explain the box then the entry is mine instead of the office's. He is right."

---

Havel put his thumb off the paper and looked at Marek Vale.

"You tell me," he said.

And Marek Vale stood in a store on a low-ground road at about a quarter past the sixth bell on the twenty-seventh of the third month of MR 416 and could not tell him, and said so, and it took him four sentences to say why.

"I have a hand," he said. "I have had a hand for three years that can make a thing come up out of a board, and I used it twice in the second month of this year and both times I asked the person first, and one of those times it was a ditch and one of those times it was a fortnight on a man's ground, and neither of them was a person.

"And I cannot do a thing to a piece of paper. There is no office in this kingdom that would call what I have a thing you can put on a form, and I have not got a paper, and I have not got an office, and on the eighteenth of the first month a settlement marshal wrote on the back of a printed notice about a standpipe that I am a spent witness and that the standing is refused, with a day in the head of it, and I asked her for it.

"So if I tell you not to sign it you are a man who has taken the word of a spent witness, and the only person in this basin who would act on what I have just said is a man of twenty-six with a form, and he is right."

Havel said the word *right* back at him twice, which is a thing men do.

"You have never once said a thing in this basin that I have checked and found wrong," he said, "and you are telling me you are not a person I can use, and I have been holding this paper for two hours."

---

"What do you get," said Marek Vale, to Mesic, "when he signs it."

"The works," said Mesic. "The account stays on the line through the fourth month, the sledges go out of the store tomorrow at the seventh, the eleven go back on at the eighth, and a foot and a half more goes on the reach before the water is where the water is going to be. That is the whole of it and it is true, and I have checked the water myself twice this month, and I am not a marshal and do not need to be one.

"And what he pays is a season on his own ground. Not a rate. A season, taken off at the end of it, on the line of accounts, on the same line the ditch is on, and there is no figure for it and there is no figure for the ditch either, and I have not got one."

"So he knows," said Havel.

"He knows because I have told him," said Mesic, "and I am required to tell him and I would have told him if I had not been required to, and I want that in whatever room this ends up in. And he is going to say yes in four seconds, and I have watched eleven people and four have said yes, and I have never been able to stop one and I have stopped trying."

---

Havel said yes in about four seconds, and he said it out loud, and he said the reason before the pen was anywhere near the paper, and Marek Vale heard the four seconds go by and could not have stopped them if he had been a different man with a better standing.

"Because the ground goes under in the fourth month," said Havel, "and I put four sledges in that store on the nineteenth of the second month because I had no works to put them on, and I told you then that I was not right to come and I knew it when I was coming and I came anyway. And the eleven have been on the lanes and not on the reach, and I have a man of fifty in the second gang who has done every day of it for five months and nobody has asked him to sit down.

"And the man in the good coat has told me the ground goes under, and he is right, and I have been right about the word *none* and wrong about everything else, and the only difference between those two things is a piece of paper with a season on it, and it comes off my ground and not off his, and I would like it written down that I knew that when I said it, and nobody is going to, and that is correct."

Mesic wrote the class in the last of the boxes. It took him the length of one line and it was true, and it said the willingness of the people of this ground, and it did not say how many of them there were.

---

"Then be in the room," said Havel.

And Marek Vale said the thing he has said in four rooms in this basin in five months, which is that he cannot.

"I cannot be your witness. I am spent, and a spent witness cannot be a second time, and if I stood in a room and watched you take that then I would be a second entry against your name and there is nowhere in this kingdom to put a second entry, and both of us would be standing in a room and neither of us would be in it.

"And I have a day on a paper in a building on the causeway side that says the standing is refused, and you have just signed a paper that says a class accepted, and in four years a man in a good coat can produce both of them and he will not need a third thing from either of us."

"Then be in the room," Havel said, "and do the thing you do."

So Marek Vale read the whole of it out loud twice at the ordinary speed, which is a thing he has done for four months in a room up a ladder where nothing is being proved. Being read is not a witnessing and is not evidence and does not have to be, and Havel said after the second reading that the words were the same, and that was the end of what Marek Vale could do.

---

They went out of the store at about the seventh bell and the sledges came out behind them with eleven men, four of them off that cart two days a week in the fifth month of last year, and Havel walked at the front and did not look up the reach once.

There are eleven names on the board on the trestle at the top of the reach in a fair hand that has never been asked anything, and they were on it on the nineteenth of the second month when there was no works in force and they are on it now that there is one, and this works is in force on a paper with a class in the last of five boxes and a day on it and a man's thumb on it, and no office has ever asked the district why there are eleven names on a works board when the number of people in this basin whose attestation is of record is about nine, and there is no form on which it may be asked.

And on the twenty-eighth of the third month of MR 416 there were eleven men on a bank a mile and a bit long, and a foot and a half of good clay on it, and a settlement marshal who had been right about it since the sixteenth of the first month and had never once been wrong about it, and a season on a line, and a line has two addresses, and one of them belongs to a woman of sixty-two who came out of her door on the twenty-eighth of the second month and looked at four hundred yards of new trench and went back in without saying anything, and she has still not been told that a bill is coming.

And a boy of about nine carried seven words down that road and was paid in kind and was not asked what any of it was about, and the form went into a book a night and a day up the river, and in about four years a man in a good coat can produce it at a man who keeps a key, and it will say that a class of the people of that ground accepted a winter, and the class will not be able to answer.
