# Chapter 193 — The First One Put Down

Margit Sova brought the lease up the ladder in a cloth and brought the two seasons' accounts in her apron and put the accounts down first, because the accounts were the reason she had come and the lease was only how it had happened.

"They are in this hand," she said. "That is my hand, and it is worse than it was in the growth month of the year before last, and I have four days of writing in it and then I am finished, and I have known that since the fourth month and I have been not saying anything about it for four months.

"Nine lines. One season a line, a hundred and forty pounds of chaff in the season, and what came in and what went out, and what I owed and what was owed to me, and the ninth line is the season that ended at the harvest of last year and there is nothing after it because the lease went up at the end of this month and there is no tenth season to write.

"Read that out," she said, "in front of these people, and then read the lease, and then somebody in this room is going to say that the lease is void, and I have known it is going to be said since the second of the fourth month and I have not come up here to be talked out of it."

---

Nobody read it out, and that was the first thing that went wrong with the morning, and Sova did not let it lie there.

"You are all sitting there," she said, "waiting for the man with no office to read it, and he cannot. He said it himself on the ninth of this month at the bottom of this ladder, in front of nine people, and he is right and I asked him here anyway, which is the fourth time this year I have asked a man to do the one thing he has told me he cannot do and I would like somebody in this room to notice that about me as well as about him."

"The man who reads a thing out loud is not a witness and it does not have to be," said Zlata Hruska. "I have said that in three rooms. Reading a thing out loud in this room is not evidence and it is not entered, and there is nobody in this lane anybody can come back to me about, and that is the whole advantage of the room and it is also the reason the room cannot be used for this. Read them out, Margit. They are your nine lines and you know what they say."

---

She read them out.

Nobody in the room had heard a woman do arithmetic about a pitch before, and it was not what anybody expected, which was the arithmetic. She read the quantities first, because quantities are the part nobody argues with. Then what each of them had fetched to that pitch and from it. Then, line by line, what she was owed by three people and what she owed to two, and she did it without a page and without a pause, and when she came to the ninth line she stopped for about the length of a breath and then read it anyway.

Then she put the accounts face down on the table and took the lease out of the cloth and put it on top of them.

"Two seasons," she said. "That is the lease. I took it in the growth month of the year before last because a hard standing and a slope off it are worth having in the season and there is no other pitch on that road with a slope, and I had eleven weeks of a season and a list in a box and I wanted the two seasons in front of me.

"And it was witnessed. In this room. On the fourteenth of the growth month, by a woman who came up this ladder with me because she was asked, and not by anybody an office sent, and not because anybody was paid, and not because she was standing in a queue of nine.

"Which is the whole of it. That is why it is void, and it is void for the reason you all said out loud in this lane in the third month of last year and again in the fourth, and I have not come up here to be told it by a clerk."

---

The lease was a good piece of paper. That is the thing to say first and it was said first, by the man in a wet sleeve, out loud, before anybody said a word about void, and it is the reason nobody in that room could enjoy the morning.

"There is nothing wrong with it," he said. "It is well made. It is in type. It is filled in properly, the pitch is described so that a man could find it with a cart, the term is two seasons and it says two seasons, and the rent is on it and the dates are on it, and the date is a month and not a day, which is the form and not the office.

"And the witnessing on it is not a second reading of a first one. Nobody was produced twice to get that lease. A woman stood in a room on a day and said she saw it, and she was not asked whether she was free, and there is no box on the paper on which she could have been asked, and there never has been one.

"So the lease is not false. The lease is a true paper with a true name on it and a true witnessing behind it, and it is void, and those are two different things and I am going to be careful about which one I am saying out loud in this room because the second one is the one that is going to cost a woman of forty-one a pitch."

"It is void because there is no employer in this room," said Zlata Hruska. "That is the whole mechanism and I have said it in three rooms and I am not going to say it a fourth time as though it were new. A room in which instrument is witnessed is recognised by the region only where a responsible party is named, and where the responsible party is a person the form requires an employer. There is no employer here. There has never been an employer here.

"So the paper is good in this room and good nowhere else. And a lease is a piece of paper about a pitch on a public road, and the moment anybody in this kingdom wants that piece of paper to be good in a district office, this is not the room it happened in."

---

That was the first voiding, and it was made at about the third bell on the sixth of the fifth month of MR 416 in a room with a rail round three sides of it, and there is no office in this kingdom in which it is entered, and there never will be, because a room with no employer cannot be recognised and a finding made in one cannot be sent anywhere.

So it was made in a different place, by a different person, and it took the shape of a finding and it went into a book in a building on the causeway side.

Orren Vaas, forty-one, a factor in Rainmarket and in the trade nineteen years, had brought the lease up to the counter on the causeway side on the fourth of the month and had been told, in the ordinary way, by a young man in a coat from a good shop, that it was four lines and a month.

He had then gone and found out from a man who sells candles what was in the room that had witnessed it, and had gone and found out from a woman who cannot write and from a man who keeps the district's tools, and on the sixth of the fifth month he came up the hill to the second of the four rooms behind the Threshold, which is ten feet by eight and is the room a challenge is heard in, and he brought no sheet, because a challenge is free and anybody may bring one and there is no form on which one may be refused.

"I want it said that I have brought nothing," said Orren Vaas, "and I have been told there is no fee and no clock, and I have been told by two people on the way up here this morning that the room is not what it looks like, and I have come to it anyway, because in nineteen years I have never once gone round a thing like this and I am not going to start."

Deputy Tolek counted four sentences and told him so.

"You are right that you have brought nothing," she said. "I can enter that a challenge was brought on this date by a factor in Rainmarket. I cannot enter that the room is the room, because the room is not the room. I can enter that a lease exists, that it was witnessed in a place with no responsible party named in it, that the witnessing is therefore good in that place and nowhere else, and that the instrument is void on its face and void has to be sealed again.

"And I am going to say the other half before anybody in this corridor hears it from somebody else, and I am saying it to you and not to him.

"Void does not put anybody off a pitch. There is no office in this kingdom that can move a person off ground on an instrument that is void, because the void is in the instrument and not in the person, and a person is not a party to the voidness of a paper she did not sign. And the landlord holds a lease of his own off somebody else, and that lease is not void and he has not come here, and he does not have to.

"So there is a pitch on the low-ground road with a good lease in the landlord's hand and nothing valid under it, and there is no form in this kingdom on which either of those two men may be put on it or either of them put off it, and that is what the first voiding looks like, and it is going to look like that every time, and nobody is to come to me afterwards and tell me they thought it would at least be tidy."

---

Orren Vaas stood in the middle of a room ten feet by eight and was owed something and was right about it.

"I have paid for two seasons of that pitch and I have never seen the money again," he said. "I have not seen the money again because I got the goods. I took a hundred and forty pounds of chaff a week off that pitch for a whole season and I sold all of it, and I have a name and a trade and nineteen years and every sack of it came across a hard standing that a woman held on a paper from a room with a rail round three sides of it.

"And I want to be exact about the thing I am owed, because I have had four days to work out what it is and it is not money and I would like it in a finding if it can go in one.

"I am not owed the chaff. I got the chaff. I am owed the right to put my own cart on that pitch and sell off it, and I had that for a season, and I do not have it now, and I am not going to get it, and the person who took it from me did not take it, she signed a paper about a slope in the wrong kind of room, and she is forty-one and she cannot write down her own accounts in a hand any more.

"And here is the part that I want written down and I am not going to pretend it is noble. There is nothing on that pitch now. Not a pitch for me, not a pitch for her, not a pitch for anybody, and the landlord is going to let it to somebody in the sixth month and that somebody is going to be right and I am not going to have a word against them, because I have been the somebody in the ninth month of a year and I did not ask the woman one question and I would have been told to go to hell.

"So I have been paid in chaff and I am owed a pitch and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be told that a piece of ground they have had the use of for a season is not there any more. And I have been in this basin five months and I have read a notice on a wall that says four things are true, and one of the four is that any person may come to a counter and ask whether their attestation has been produced.

"I have not signed anything about that pitch in my life. I signed nothing and I have not been produced and I am not one of the people that notice is for, and I want that said in this room, and I want it said because in about four years a man in a good coat is going to produce a document at somebody and I would like one fact about me on a page before that happens, and the fact is that I asked for no witness and got none and lost a season of trade on somebody else's paper."

---

Nobody put the pitch back and nobody put anybody on it and nobody took the chaff back.

Marek Vale came down the hill at about the fifth bell and stood at the rail of the side with no rail and read the finding out twice at the ordinary speed because that is what a man does before he says anything about a page, and then he said the thing and it was the only thing he had come for.

"I want to say one sentence about the man who sold the chaff," he said, "because he has not asked for it and he will be recorded as having asked for the other one.

"He is owed something and he is right about what it is, and it is not a chaff debt and it is not a pitch that anybody in this kingdom can hand back, and this room is not going to be the place where a factor of forty-one is told his loss is part of the argument.

"He came up a hill with nothing, and the finding he has got is about a lease, and the lease is not his and the loss is not the one this business has been spending people on for five months. He lost a season. A woman of forty-one lost a pitch. There is a woman in this basin who has been produced and cannot be asked again, and there is a woman at a coast who was told by a carter's mouth, and neither of them is in this room, and neither of them lost a season of trade.

"So I am going to say the true thing and I am going to say it in his mouth, and I have no standing to say it in my own and Deputy Tolek will put it in a book whether I say it or not.

"He is owed a pitch. That is a fact about a person and a piece of ground and it is the same shape as the finding this morning and neither of them is in the same thing. And I am not going to put them in the same thing, and he has not asked me to, and that is the whole of what I have come down the hill for."

Orren Vaas looked at him for a moment.

"You are the man with no office," he said. "The one the woman of thirty-four said cannot be a witness. I have heard four things about you this morning and I would like to say one thing back and then I am going down the hill to my own business, which is worse than it was this morning and which nobody is going to pay me for.

"You have got four documents and reasons on a causeway, and a form cut in a copy room with a cost chalked on the wrapper, and a lease that is void. And in about four years a man in a good coat is going to produce all three at me, at a clerk, at somebody, and there is not one of them with my name on it, and that is not a mercy. That is a thing that will be true about me for as long as the paper lasts, and nobody will ever be able to establish that I was in the room or not in the room, and I am going to be a man in a file with no entry in it at all, and I have never in nineteen years been harder to find."

He went down the hill. Nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him, and the man who reads papers out loud is still a man whose word no room will take, and the pitch on the low-ground road sat there with a good lease in the landlord's hand and nothing under it, and the low ground was dry for the first time since the twenty-eighth of the third month and there was a mile and a bit of new clay on it that a settlement marshal had said would hold and that had held, and nobody went up and told her so.
