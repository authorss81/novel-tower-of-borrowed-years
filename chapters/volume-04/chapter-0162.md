# Chapter 162 — Nine Sentences

The second of the four rooms behind the Threshold is ten feet by eight and it is the room a challenge is heard in, and it has a desk in it instead of a table, and the desk came from a counting house in Rainmarket in the flood year and has a shelf on the far side of it, nine inches deep, and the shelf is where a thing goes when it has been given in.

Nim Rook came in at the second bell on the fourth of the first month of MR 416 with the flat case under her arm, and she put the case on the desk and took the copy out of it and gave the two pieces of card to the woman at the shelf, and she did not look round the room when she did it, and she did not look round the room afterwards, and she had said on the last night of the ninth month of last year that she was not going to and she had not.

Perpetua Ohl had entered the challenge at the first bell and could not refuse it, and had brought it up herself, and stood at the rail with her hands behind her back for the whole of it in the way of a person who has delivered nine hundred things at a counter and has never once stayed to watch.

The hearing officer was Deputy Vera Tolek, forty-four, who had a book and a pen and three sheets of foolscap and who wrote down the name of the person and the thing challenged and then looked up and said:

"Before you begin. There is a rule in this room that a person may say as much as they can hold in their mouth and I am not going to interrupt you for the length of it, and if you want me to stop you, say so. And there is no fee and there is no time on the clock. Is that understood."

"It is," said Nim Rook.

"Then stand up and say it, and I am going to count the sentences, and I want you to know before you start that I am going to count them out loud afterwards and that the count is not going to be used against you."

---

She said nine sentences, and here they are, and the hearing officer wrote them down in nine lines and did not improve any of them.

"My name is Nim Rook and I am twenty-two years old and I am a night courier in this city.

"On the second of the eighth month of last year a man in a good coat told me at a window that I was a witness to a paper, and he did not say which paper, and he did not ask me anything.

"I have not signed anything this year.

"I have signed things in my life and I am not going to pretend to you that I have not.

"I have never been asked whether I was free when I signed anything, and I have never been asked that about anything at all.

"There is no form in this kingdom on which a person can be entered as saying they did not agree to it, and I have had three years to look for one.

"I am not here to be told that I am not a witness, because I do not believe anybody in this room can tell me that, and I have read the paper.

"I am here to have it entered somewhere that I was told on the second of the eighth month of last year by a man in a good coat at a window, and that he did not ask me.

"This is the copy, and it is a true copy, and it is good in a room, and somebody stood behind the comparison, and I am not going to say who, and I am not going to look round."

She sat down.

---

"That is nine," said Deputy Tolek, "and I counted them as they came, and I am going to say one thing about the count and then I am going to stop talking about the count, and the thing is that nine sentences is a very small number of sentences and it is the first time in this room that a person has needed nine.

"Nine is not a fault. It means you were not performing. A person performing needs a great many more, and I have heard a great many more, and I have written down a great many more, and none of them were findings."

She put the pen flat and turned the copy round so it faced her and read all of it, which took her about a minute, and she read it the way a person reads a thing that has no surprises in it, because there were not going to be any, and then she read the foot of it.

"It is a certified copy of a page of a ward roll of this city. The seal is a ward seal. It is true of the page, and a certified copy is true of the page, and I am going to say the next sentence because it is the sentence that this hearing is standing on and nobody has said it out loud in this room for nine years.

"A true copy of a page that was never true is still a true copy."

---

Neris Kade was on a bench against the wall that had been put there when the desk came from Rainmarket, and she had come because a challenge may call a field examiner and nobody had called her, and she said so in four words to the Deputy before she sat down, and the Deputy wrote *field examiner, not called* on the first sheet of foolscap and ruled a line under it and did not ask her a single question for the whole of the morning, which is the only thing about a field examiner that is worth having and the reason she had come.

Marek Vale was standing at the back, by the door, on the side of the room with no rail, because a man who has come to a hearing without being called stands where a man stands when he is not wanted to be looked at.

The Deputy read the foot of the copy twice more and then said:

"There is a name in a box at the foot of this. It is in the box that a clerk puts a name in when he stands behind a comparison, and the box has nothing at the head of it, and the rule in this room is that the name in that box is read aloud so that the person it belongs to knows it is there.

"The name is Marek Vale. He is not a party. Nobody has called him. He is standing at the back of the room by the door, and he put the name there, and the date is a month, because that is the form and there is no other date in this kingdom a person can put in that box."

She looked up.

"Marek Vale. Will you stand where I can see you and confirm that you are the person in that box."

"I am," said Marek Vale. "I stood behind the comparison of that copy against a page in a room in this city on the thirtieth of the ninth month of MR 415."

"And is that copy a true copy of that page."

"It is."

"Thank you." She wrote it down on the second sheet of foolscap, and then she stopped writing, and looked at him, and said:

"I am going to say the next thing carefully, because I have been a hearing officer for nineteen years and I have never had to say it and I have thought about how to say it since about the third of this month.

"Mr Vale, I have your statement and I am going to enter it, and I want you to understand what entering it is. I am entering the words of a man standing in a room. That is all. I am not entering a person whose word can be used again, because I do not have a box for that and neither does this room and neither does the building behind you.

"I have your name on a paper. I do not have you."

He said nothing, and the Deputy Tolek wrote *name on paper, person not before the court* on the second sheet of foolscap, and it is the coldest line in that finding and it is the only line in it that is about a rule rather than a fact, and the rule is the one that put him in that room, and it is not anybody's fault, and it is very old.

---

"Now the two nines," said Deputy Tolek, and Nim Rook said, "There is one nine."

"There are two," said the Deputy, "and I am going to say them out loud, and I am not doing it to be cruel, and I am doing it because you put them next to each other and a person who puts two things next to each other in a room has a right to know whether the room is being asked to treat them as one thing, and it is not going to.

"The first nine is on the paper in front of me. It is about nine names in the far-right column of a ward roll page in this city, and the column has nothing at the head of it, and one of the names is on the fourteenth line down. That is a fact. It is a true copy and I can prove it and I do not need to, because it is a copy.

"The second nine is in what you said at the first bell. Nine people have been told in the last four months that they are witnesses, and you are one of them.

"Those are not the same nine. I cannot show you that they are not the same nine, because the second nine is not a set of names, it is a thing that four men in good coats have said at four windows, and I have not got those four men and you have not got them either. But I have got the first nine and it is a page, and about nine is a number a person says when a person has looked at a column and counted to the end of what is filled in.

"So this room is not going to join them. Not because I think you are lying, and I want that said before anything else, because I have been in this room four hours over two years and I have never once thought a person who stood in front of me was lying about a paper. It is because a room cannot make a finding on a conjunction that nobody can test, and if it does, then in four years the finding is the thing that gets produced, and the finding will be a true copy of a mistake."

She put the pen down.

"And I want to say that this is the first time in as long as I have been sitting in this room that a person has stood in it and been told that the two halves of her own argument do not fit together, and I have not enjoyed it, and I am not going to pretend to have enjoyed it, and it was correct."

---

"Then ask me the other thing," said Nim Rook. "You have not asked me the other thing and I have been waiting for you to since the second bell and I would rather you asked it now than that you found out afterwards that you should have."

Deputy Tolek took her hand off the book.

"I am not going to ask you whether you were free," she said. "And I want that written down, and I am going to dictate it. Write: *The court did not ask the person named in the fourth column whether she was free when she signed, the court has no finding to enter on the question, and the court has looked for one and it is not there.*

"There is no such finding. I have been a hearing officer for nineteen years and I have entered findings of fact, findings of arithmetic, findings of conduct and findings of record, and there is no finding in this building that says a person was free or was not free on a day in the past, and there is not one in any other building in this city, and there is not one in the paper in front of me, and there is not one in the book I have kept since I started, which is in a hand that is worse than it was.

"So I am not going to ask a question I cannot enter the answer to. A court that asks a question it cannot answer is not a court, it is a queue, and I have been the queue twice in my life and I did not enjoy it either."

---

The finding was entered at about the fifth bell and it took three quarters of an hour and Vera Tolek read it out before she signed it.

That a certified copy of a page of a ward roll of this city has been produced and entered, and the copy is true of the page.

That the far-right column of that page has nothing at the head of it, and about nine names are in it, and nothing was produced to this court to show what the column is for.

That the person named in the fourth column of that copy is not a party to any paper before this court, and is not named in any paper entered before this court.

That the person named in the fourth column of that copy has been told, once, on the second of the eighth month of MR 415, at a window, by a person in a good coat, that she was a witness to a paper, and that the name of that person was not given to the court and cannot be obtained.

That this court has no question it is able to put to a person about a paper other than a question about that person's name.

And then the last line, which Deputy Tolek wrote last and read out twice.

That this court cannot enter a finding that any person did or did not consent to anything, and the court has looked, and there is no such finding available to it, and the court is not going to invent one, because a court that invents one is a place where a finding is made up, and this room is not one of those, and it is the first time in my service that I have been able to say a thing like that and mean it.

---

They made her sign it and she read it and she signed it, and the Deputy put the date in the head of the page in full, with the day, because a court in a building has a day, and then she gave the finding to the woman at the shelf to be copied, and the copy would go into a book on the second floor of a building on the causeway side, and there it would stay.

"That is the whole of it," said Deputy Tolek, "and I am going to say the last thing now while it is fresh, and then I am going to go and eat, because I have been in this room since the first bell.

"Mr Vale, I entered your statement. It is in the finding. It says a man stood behind a comparison on the thirtieth of the ninth month and that the copy is true of the page. If anybody produces that paper at me in about four years, I will be able to read your own words back to them off a page with a day on it, and there is not one thing in this kingdom they can do to stop that, and you did that in about nine seconds and you were not careful and I am not going to pretend that you did not know.

"And they will not be able to do one thing to stop it, and it will not help you, and you knew that too, and I have read enough papers in my life to know what a man looks like when he has signed one and cannot remember eating."

Neris Kade wrote one line in her own column of a sheet in a flat case in her coat and did not write in the other one, and Marek Vale's column on that sheet had nothing in it at all since the last night of the ninth month, and neither of them said one word about that in a corridor, and it was not going to be the kind of thing they said one word about.

---

They came out on the causeway side at about half past the fifth bell, and the light was going and the counter was shut and there was a lamp burning under the arch, and Nim Rook went to the rail and stood at it with both hands flat on it, and she did not look round, and she was not going to, and that had been said on the last night of the ninth month and had not been withdrawn.

"You are going to be in a book," said Marek Vale.

"I am going to be in a book in a building on this side of the causeway for as long as that building is standing," said Nim Rook, "because a finding about a form that cannot name a person is a finding about nothing, and that Deputy does not put a person's name on a page for nothing, and she is the only officer in this city I have heard of who has ever entered a finding that admitted there was nothing in it, and I sat here and worked it out in about a second while she was reading the last line out.

"I have been a woman having a bad morning in this city for three years and it has never once been a finding. It is one now. I would have paid for it and I did not have to pay for it, and the price was my name in a book, and I knew the price before I said the nine sentences, and I said them anyway, and I am not going to be told by you or by anybody on this causeway that I was brave."

"I was not going to say it."

"I know. That is the second time this month."

---

Perpetua Ohl was still there, and she had not gone home, and she had been standing under the arch for the whole of it and she had not gone into the room, and she came over to the rail now and put the book she had been carrying on the stone and did not open it.

"You are going to want four names," she said.

"I am not going to want anything. I have had a day."

"You are going to want four names," said Perpetua Ohl, "because you have found out that a room needs a person in it whose word another room will take, and you have found out that you are not that person any more, and I watched you work that out on a stair in the ninth month of last year and there has not been one working day since on which you did one thing about it, and it is two days.

"Four names," she said. "That is all I am going to say. I am not going to tell you anything else about them, and I am not going to write them down, and I have not written them down, and I have had them in my head since the first of the fourth month and I have said them out loud to nobody, not even to myself with any care, and there is a reason and I am going to give you the reason and then I am going to go home.

"The only way anybody in this basin finds out whether a person will stand in a room and say what they saw, is to produce their attestation and stand them in the room and find out.

"And the people whose attestations are of record in this city are the people who have been produced, because that is the only way a record of that kind gets made.

"So the four names I have are the four names in this basin that a room could use, and every one of the four is a person who has been produced once, and there is not one of the four who has not been harmed by it, and there is not one of the four who is not the reason they were produced, and I have known that since the fourth month and I have been at that counter for thirty years and I have never said it out loud in front of a person and I am saying it now at about the sixth bell on the fourth of the month because you have spent yourself and I am not going to be the one who waits for the right morning."

She did not open the book.

"They are in here," said Perpetua Ohl, "in the order I found them, and the order is not alphabetical and the order is not by street, and I am not going to tell you what the order is, because the order is that I heard about them. You may have the four on the seventh of this month and you will not have them before, and you will not have the book."
