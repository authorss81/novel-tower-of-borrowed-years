# Chapter 177 — Eleven Answers

The Deputy sent for Marek Vale at the second bell on the thirteenth of the third month of MR 416 and sent for him by a boy of about nine who carried messages for anybody in that part of the city and was paid in kind and was not asked about anything, and the sentence the boy carried was eleven words long and gave no reason.

She had four more questions written on a sheet of foolscap and she had struck three of them out with a pen before Marek Vale came into the room, and she told him so before she asked the first of the one that was left, and she told him the other three as well, and he said nothing at all about any of them, which she noted and did not remark on.

---

"I am going to begin by saying the thing that is wrong with this," said Deputy Tolek, "because if I do not say it in the first minute then I have spent nineteen years being careful about the wrong half of it.

"You are not a party. Nobody has called you. You have no standing, no office, no pay, no vote, and no right of entry, and you told me all of that in nine seconds in the doorway and it is correct, and I am going to sit here and take your answers and then do nothing whatever with them, and I have told you that in advance because I would rather you heard it from the chair than worked it out over two months.

"And I am going to do it anyway, and the reason I am going to do it is that you are the only person in this basin who has seen any of it happen, and there is a document in a book that says an instrument cannot do what it is for, and it is in a book because a marshal asked a room to write something down, and it is going to be produced at somebody in four years, and there is not one fact in it that anybody can stand behind.

"Answer what is put. Short answers. Do not explain the mechanism. There are nineteen people in this building who have the mechanism and nine of them have had it wrong on purpose and one of them had it wrong for nineteen years because she was twenty-four and it saved a quarter of a sheet, and none of that is in this room and none of it is wanted in this room."

---

The first answer was his name and that he had no office.

The second was that on the thirtieth of the ninth month of MR 415 he stood behind a comparison of a copy against a page in a room eight feet by eleven behind the hall, and the copy is a true copy, and the certification is good, and it is in a finding of the fourth of the first month with a day on it, and he entered it himself as a clerk.

The third was that he did not read the paper and that he has never been able to find out what it was about, and that a person who certifies a thing whose subject they do not know has not witnessed anything, and that he has had three months to think of one way to be kinder about it and has not found one.

The fourth was that on the eighteenth of the first month a settlement marshal wrote on the back of a printed notice about a standpipe that he is a spent witness and that the standing is refused, with a day in the head of it, and that he asked her for it, and that he would have got it in a year with a name on it that somebody else had chosen.

---

"There," said Deputy Tolek. "That is a document with a day on it about you and it is the only one in this kingdom, and I am going to put it in the finding, and I want you to understand that I am doing you a service and that the service is going to be worth nothing to you in about four years and I am doing it anyway."

"I do not need it in the finding."

"I know you do not need it. That is precisely why it goes in. A marshal's book is not a book anybody in this city reads, and a book in this building is, and in four years the difference between the two is going to be the difference between a man who was refused a standing and a man who was never asked for one.

"I am a clerk and it is the only verb I have got and I am going to use it. Next."

---

The fifth answer was that he has been to the records office at Verrow twice, a night and a day up the river, and that on the first occasion a filing clerk of forty-nine had to move a chair to get at a drawer, and that on the second occasion he was given the whole of the standing instruction and a minute in the same drawer, and that the minute has never been acted on by anybody and that the man who keeps it will not say what he thinks it means.

"Can the court enter a minute that has never been acted on."

"The court can enter that a minute exists," said Deputy Tolek. "The court cannot enter what it means and I am not going to try, because in four years a man will produce it against a filing clerk and he will not produce it against me, and the difference is that a filing clerk answers for what he wrote and I answer for what I entered."

---

The sixth answer was a period, and it was the only answer in the morning that was entered without any difficulty at all.

He had gone to the shed at the Gate in Tallow Ward. The shed had been a register of risk since the eighteenth of the ninth month of MR 413, and he had a woman of sixty-one who keeps the wall list and reads it twice at the ordinary speed once a week and is not thanked and has never asked to be, and a list of about forty lines, and a rota of hours on the days the rota says, four hours a day, and none of it is a statute and none of it is a law and it is a practice and it has no head on it and no office has been told it is either.

"That is the easiest thing anybody has put in front of me in two months," said Deputy Tolek, "and I am going to say why and then I am going to stop enjoying it.

"A period is a fact about a shed. A shed can be shown to a person. I can put a period in a book and a person can walk to Tallow Ward and stand in front of the door and see that it is a shed and that it is shut, and that is what a fact is, and that is why I can enter one.

"It took you three years to work that out and it took me four seconds to enter it, and I am not going to pretend those are the same kind of work, and I am not going to be gracious about it either, because you have not once in three years come into a room and given me the easy thing first."

---

The seventh was the column, and there the Deputy stopped.

"You have described a shed with about forty lines on a wall and a column at the end of the fourth line with nothing at the head of it."

"Yes."

"I am not going to ask you when it stopped having anything at the head of it, and I want the reason on the record, and the reason is that I have looked and there is no such finding available to me, and I have looked in a building in this city and in a building a night and a day up this river and there is no box in either of them on which a person may be entered as saying when a column stopped being headed.

"And I am aware that I have just declined to ask the question everybody in this room wants asked, including you, and I am aware of what that costs, and I am not going to enter a finding about a thing I have declined to hear, and a room that enters findings about things it did not hear is a room that is making them up."

Marek Vale said nothing at all, which was the correct thing and cost him something.

---

The eighth was a number, and Deputy Tolek had the certified copy in front of her and it was the same copy and she had read it in the first month and she turned it round so that the far-right column was away from him and away from the door.

"State what you know of the number of people in this basin whose attestation has been produced."

"About nine."

"Struck. I cannot enter a figure about a number of people and you know why and I read the reason out of a drawer a night and a day up this river and you did not. Give me the list."

"There is no list."

"There is a list. A woman of fifty-one at the counter on the causeway has one and has had it since the first of the fourth month and she read four names out of it to you on the seventh of the first month and would not lend it to you and has not written them down.

"State the four names."

"No," said Marek Vale.

The Deputy Tolek did not press it, and did not look up, and she wrote *the person named stated that a list exists, that the list is not his, and that he will not give it* on the fourth sheet of foolscap, and then she said:

"I am going to write one more sentence under that and it is the only sentence in this morning that I have thought about before the sitting, and it is this.

"This court holds a certified copy of a page with about nine names in a column at the far right of it, and the column has nothing at the head of it, and I have not asked you for one of those names, and I am not going to, and there is no form on which this court may ask you for a name it would then have to enter. A court that asks a person for a name in order to have it on a page is a queue, and I have been a queue twice in my life and I did not enjoy it either.

"That is not an oversight and I am not going to be thanked for it."

---

The ninth answer was the room over a dye house, a rail round three sides of it made out of a bed frame and three bought brackets and one shoe-tongue of iron, four unmatched chairs, a lamp, and a finding in carpenter's pencil on the inside and the outside of a door-shutter, and the door is going to be painted, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be asked to paint over a finding or not to, and there is no copy of it anywhere and the only copy is the second coat of pencil on the other side of a board about four feet by two.

"Can that finding be produced at anybody."

"No. It cannot be produced at anybody at all, which is why it is the only document in this basin that nobody can make anything of, and it is also the only one of mine that is going to be painted over in about nine days."

---

The tenth answer was the thing the Deputy had wanted and had not known she wanted until he said it.

"I have been in a room with a rail round three sides of it on eleven evenings since the first of this month and I read papers out loud in it, and reading a thing out loud in that room is not a witnessing and is not evidence and does not have to be, and believed means heard and does not mean entered, and I have got the only job anybody in this basin has offered me in four months and it is in a room where nothing is being proved, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to pretend it is anything else."

Neris Kade was in the room and had not been called and had not been asked, in the way that a field examiner is neither of those things and turns up anyway, and she stood against the wall where the bench is and she said one sentence and it was this:

"He reads better than I do and I have never once asked him to stop."

And then she said nothing else at all for the rest of the morning, which Deputy Tolek noted and did not remark on, and which was the correct decision of the only person in the room who had one to make.

---

The eleventh was not a question. He had asked for it and she had let him, and that is the whole of what happened.

"There is nothing I want out of this room," said Marek Vale, "and I am not going to ask for anything and I am not going to be thanked for standing in it, and if it is entered anywhere that I was helpful I would like it struck, and if it cannot be struck then I would like it entered that I asked for it to be struck."

"Noted," said Deputy Tolek, "and it is not going to be struck, because you did help, in the way that a man who has been in a room and said what happened in it helps, and I am not going to let a man who has spent himself turn it into a favour afterwards because a favour can be returned and that cannot."

She read the whole of it out loud twice at the ordinary speed before she signed it, and then she read the last paragraph out loud a third time, on her own, and then she put the pen down.

---

"That is the finding of the thirteenth of the third month of MR 416, and I am going to say the last thing about it and I am going to say it to the room and not to you, because you have heard all of it and it is in your hand and it is in a book, and what is left is for the people who are not going to read either.

"That a person was examined, and that he answered eleven things, and that his answers are entered.

"That the court has found a use for one of them, being a period, and that the court has not found a use for any of the other ten.

"That no answer of his is available to this court as proof of any fact whatever, for the reason entered on the fourth of the first month, which is that a court cannot enter a finding that a person did or did not consent to anything, and this court has looked for another way and has not found one and is not going to invent one to fill the place of one.

"And that the reason is not that his answers are untrue. The reason is that this court has no way of telling one man's answer from another man's answer, and that is a property of this court and not of him, and it is the only sentence in nineteen years of my own hand that I have wanted to be wrong about and have not been able to make wrong."

---

They made him sign it, and he read it, and he signed it, and the Deputy put the day in the head of the page in full with the day, and gave the finding to the woman at the shelf to be copied, and the copy would go into a book on the second floor of a building on the causeway side and there it would stay.

Neris Kade wrote one line in her own column of a sheet in a flat case in her coat and did not write in the other one, and Marek Vale's column on that sheet had had nothing in it at all since the last night of the ninth month of MR 415 and he did not have a pencil and there was nothing in his coat to write with anyway, and neither of them said one word about that in a corridor, and it was not going to be the kind of thing they said one word about.

He walked down the causeway at about the fifth bell with a man of forty-nine's statement in his own hand in his head and a woman's statement in his ear about reading better, and a man of forty had said on the eighteenth of the first month that the standing is refused, and the standing was refused on the thirteenth of the third month as well, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be told that a standing has been refused, and there was nobody on the causeway to tell.
