# Chapter 170 — The Coercion Is Not in the Signature

The room's standing business, which Zlata Hruska had put on the table herself on the fourteenth and twice since, was a question with one word in it and the word was *instead*.

*What do we do instead.*

The deal table came up that ladder on the fourteenth of the second month, borrowed off a man behind a chandler's for the evening and carried up by two men and a boy of nine, and it went up the outside wall about half sideways, and it took four of them and it took about a quarter of an hour, and it is not in any book and it went back down the same way.

On the evening of the sixteenth they put four sheets on it.

"They have to be the four," said Odile Marn. "Or it is not a finding. It is a conversation about a thing in general and half this city has had that this month and I am not having it in this room."

The fourth one came up the ladder in the middle of the second lamp, brought by a carter who had it inside his coat. Jelka Sondor was on the coast and had gone on the second of the month and had not come back, and there was a carter at the counting house on the low ground who said that a woman at the coast had given him a folded sheet for a woman with a rail and no name, and had not said a word about it and had not wanted a receipt, and the carter had brought it up a ladder and had been given a cup of something and had gone straight back down.

---

They put them down in the order they had been signed, which is the only order there is.

Zvonko Maras, a roof over a coal bay on the Rainmarket low ground, in the second month of MR 410, in a fair small hand, signed in a bay doorway with a boy in the household.

Odile Marn, a boundary ditch between two seed yards on the Counters' Walk, in the growth month of MR 411, in a fair small hand, signed on a bank in the rain because a man from the water office said it would be doing the district a favour.

Margit Sova, a bin in a courtyard, in the second month of MR 412, in a fair small hand, signed for a man from the water office who wanted to know who put the bins out on the Counters' Walk.

Jelka Sondor, a bin behind a chandler's on the Counters' Walk, in the growth month of MR 412, in a fair small hand, brought down from a coast by a man with a cart.

Four sheets, four ruled lines apiece, no boxes, and every one of them a good sheet.

---

"I will say the first thing," said Zvonko Maras, "because I have been in a room with a rail round three sides of it twice in my life and both times I was the one being asked something, and I have got opinions.

"That is a good piece of paper. That is a well-made thing. I have handled paper in a coal bay for thirty-six years and I know what bad paper is, and that is not bad paper, and whoever printed it knew what he was doing, and there is not one mark on that sheet that a man could point at and say he forged it, and I have been waiting about four years for a man to point at something."

"That is the first half of it," said Zlata Hruska. "And I have been saying the first half of it at a door for nine years and nobody has ever written the second half down, because the second half is that it does not matter, and nobody wants a document that says it does not matter.

"So. Look at them. Not what is on them. What is not on them."

---

They looked at them for about a quarter of an hour and it went in four steps, and it went fast, and it went in the order it went in because everybody in the room had been doing it separately, in three different places, for four months, and had nothing to add to each other.

First: none of them names a paper. A roof. A ditch. A bin. A bin.

Second: every one of them carries a month and not a day, and there is a month on a ditch from the growth month of MR 411 and the ditch is still a ditch and the bin is still a bin and a roof is a roof, and the whole of the difference between a sheet about a thing and a sheet about a moment is a line of type that is not on the form.

Third: there is nothing at all, anywhere, on any of the four, for whether the person was free.

And then the room stopped, because the man who read papers out loud said the thing that stopped it, and he said it in the wrong order and had to go back.

---

"There is a fifth line," he said. "On the original printing. It says *the paper*, and it was cut out of the forme in the third month of a year nineteen years gone by a woman of twenty-four in a print shop on Ropewalk, to save a quarter of a sheet of paper on about four hundred copies, and she set the forme herself and consulted nobody because a line is a line.

"And I have been to that shop. She keeps a tray of one of everything she has ever set, in date order, because you cannot set type you have never seen a proof of, and the first one is in the tray and it has five lines on it, and she has thought about that afternoon every working day for nineteen years, and there is nobody in this kingdom to be angry with, and I have said that already and I am not going to say it again.

"So when I say there is nothing on these four about whether the person was free, I have to say it properly, and this is the whole of what this room has found and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be able to say it again in the same way.

"The fifth line was the paper. The freedom was never on the form at all. Not on the four-line printing and not on the five-line printing and not on any printing anybody in this kingdom has ever had, and there is no forme in a drawer in a shop on Ropewalk with a box in it for it, and there is no other forme in this city, and if there were one it would be a different form and a different form is a form that nobody in this basin has ever seen.

"A cut line can be put back. She had it in a tray for nineteen years and anybody could have asked her for it and nobody ever did and now I have, in a way, because I have stood in her shop and read it. A line that was never in type cannot be put back by anybody. It has to be printed for the first time, and printing it for the first time means a new form, and a new form is a thing a man in a good coat can produce at anybody in about four years with nothing on it to say where it came from.

"That is the difference between the two, and it is the whole of what a room with a rail round three sides of it found on the evening of the sixteenth of the second month of MR 416."

---

Nobody said anything for a while, and the lamp wanted trimming and nobody trimmed it.

"So put a box in it," said Zvonko Maras. "A new form. I have just told you that is the answer and I have been in this basin thirty-six years and I can tell you that is the answer and it is the answer I would have given a clerk in the fourth year. A box, and the question, and a hand that fills it in, and there it is."

"Yes," said Zlata Hruska. "And I have been saying that at my door since I was twenty-five years old, and I stopped saying it about six years ago, and I am going to say why this room can work it out in a moment and I could not in nine years.

"The person who would fill it in.

"Not a villain. I want that said in this room, because I have been angry about this for nine years and I have not got anybody to be angry at and I am not going to invent one at the last. A man at a window. With a list. At the eighth bell, on a morning when there are nine or ten people in front of the window and the day is long and he has been saying the same five words since the fourth month.

"And he would fill it in *free*. Nine times in a morning. In the voice he uses. And the entry would be true, because he would have asked, because the form would require him to ask, and he would ask.

"And it would be false, because he did not ask, and there is not one thing in the world that anybody could ever show which of the two it was. A form is a page. A page does not know the difference between a man who asks because he wants to know and a man who asks because there is a line on a sheet in front of him.

"That is what happened to nine people. That is what happened to me twice, in the sixth month of MR 414 and in the second month of last year, about a bin. And nobody put a hand on my paper. Nobody ever has to. I have signed a great many things and every one of them was free, and I was not asked about any of the other ones, and none of the other ones existed yet when I signed the first."

---

The finding was entered on the door that night, in a third coat of pencil, on the inside, and Neris Kade read it out twice at the ordinary speed because it was a long one and because somebody in that room had learned in about a fortnight that you read a thing out loud twice or you have not read it.

*That there is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be entered as saying they were free when they signed anything, and that there has never been one, and that a box for it would be filled in by the person whose not asking has done the harm.*

*That therefore the harm is not in the signatures and never was, and that the coercion is a queue with a man at it, and that a queue is not on any form, and that there is no box for a queue, and there is not going to be one.*

*That this finding changes nothing, and that the four sheets on the table are four good sheets and are going to remain four good sheets, and that the ditch is not cut, and that the pitch is not held, and that there are people in this basin who are spent, and that four of them cannot be found, and that one of the four is dead, and that this room has told two of them and cannot tell the rest.*

And that is the whole of what was found, and it is in four lines of carpenter's pencil on the inside of a door that is going to be painted in about two months, and there is a second copy on the outside of the same door, and there is no third copy anywhere in the kingdom, and nobody has any power over the paint.

---

"Which means the wall," said Zlata Hruska.

Nobody answered her for a moment.

"Which means the wall and nine days and a piece of chalk, and a woman of forty-one who has got to the end of her hand, and a question asked out loud by somebody who is not an institution, in a room where nothing is proved and nothing has to be, and a woman of thirty-four who asks it at her door and gives people a cup of something afterwards because it is that time of the day.

"That is what is left, and it is not a remedy, and it does not un-spend anybody, and it is the only thing anybody in this basin has that is not in a queue."

---

A boy of nine came up the ladder at about the third of the seventh bell with a message from the end of the lane, which was that a carter had said that a man in a lane off the copper road had said that a four-line form was being set in a shop down there, and that there were nine hundred of them going out, and that the shop was not on Ropewalk, and that nobody in that shop would say who had asked for them.

Nobody in that room could do one single thing about it. There is no form on which anybody may be told to stop a printer, and there is no office in this kingdom whose business it is to be told, and the Trust's seat on the board of five has been empty seven times, and the person who was set to stop it would have to be a person with a name at the top of something and there is not one in this basin.

"A form with a line on it," said Zlata Hruska, "is a thing a printer sets."

It was not a question and it was in the flat voice she used at her door, and nobody answered it, and she did not ask it twice, and she had been not asking it twice since she was twenty-five years old.

The lamp went out at the seventh bell, which is what the lamp does, and nine people went down a ladder one at a time into a lane with no name, and a man with no office was the last one on it, and he came down with both hands on the rungs because there was nothing in either of them.

And on the brick by the end of the lane there were ten names in chalk, in a hand that had got worse over twenty-six years, and the tenth of them was the worst one on the wall, and there is no eleventh, and about nine people in this city are spent, and a quarter of a sheet of paper on about four hundred copies is the whole of the reason, and there is nobody in this kingdom to be angry with, and the queue is still at the window, and the window is still open, and the wall is the only thing in the basin that nobody owns.
