# Chapter 175 — What the Ninth Month Bought

Roko Bregar had carted clay off the low-ground bank for five months and he came down the lane at the first bell on the sixth of the third month of MR 416 with the cart behind him and one wheel making a noise it had been making since the second of the second month, and he had a board on the near side of the cart with notches cut in it and a date against every notch.

The board is about four feet long and it is the only instrument in this basin with a day against every line on it.

---

"You have got a date on that," said Marek Vale.

"I have got a date on that since the second week of the first month," said Roko Bregar, "when my own man started cutting them, and I have never once had a piece of paper with a day on it in my life, and I did not know until about the fourth of this month that a date on a thing is unusual, and then I heard it from a man with no office standing in my cart road."

"I am sorry about that."

"Do not be. I have been putting days on notches for five months because if I do not I cannot tell whether a man has carted a load or looked at it, and that is the entire reason and it has nothing to do with anybody's trouble."

He put a hand on the board and did not turn it over.

"Eleven of those are still in the district's carting and there are two hundred and some notches on this board and the rest of them are in the district's store yard where a clerk puts them once a month, and neither of those places has ever sent me a figure, and I have stopped asking for one in the last fortnight because I can work out what is happening without one."

---

"What is happening," said Marek Vale, "is that you have carted for five months for a district that is not paying you, and that the reason it is not paying you is on a line of district accounts a night and a day up this river, and that the reason the line is alive is that in the third month of MR 415 a record loan was sealed on it, correctly, on a standing authority, by a man from the board above that office, and that its price was entered in the last of five boxes and it was not a figure."

"Fourteen attestations," said Roko Bregar.

Marek Vale did not say anything for about four seconds.

"You have got that from a carter."

"I have got that from a carter and a carter and a woman on a causeway and a man who reads papers out loud in a shed, and it is the fourth time in five months that I have heard it said by somebody who was not in a room when it was entered, and every one of the four was in a good coat at a window at the time, and I would like somebody in this city to tell me which of us is the liar."

"Nobody is a liar. That is the difficulty and it is the whole of it, and I have been up that river twice and I have never seen a page that says anybody stole anything, and what I have seen is a price that says fourteen of something, and there is no return on it."

"Then I am not owed a figure," said Roko Bregar. "I am owed a load. I have done a little over two hundred loads of clay and the district has had a foot of bank out of it and a hole nine feet long in the face of it, and the ground is going and the works stopped a month ago, and the four men who were stood off were on my cart two days a week and are not now."

---

They went up the causeway at about the third bell because that is where the count was done, and the count was not being done.

The boy who had done it was at the end of the rail with nothing in his hands. He was the son of the woman who does the tower's laundry and he was about twelve now, and there was a slate under his arm with nothing at the top of it, and he had been paid four coppers an hour in the ninth month of MR 415 for four days on four days of that month, and nobody had asked him to count anything since.

"Has somebody stopped it?" said Marek Vale.

"Nothing has been stopped," said the boy. "There is no slate of mine and nobody's. I have brought the slate because I have to bring it, and I have not been asked to write anything on it, and I am not going to write anything on it, and my mother says I am not to write anything on it unless I am asked to by somebody whose name I could say if somebody asked me."

He was not asked. He went and stood at the end of the rail with his hands in his sleeves, and nine or ten people stood at the rail, and nobody counted them, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which anybody may be entered as having counted them.

"He did it on his own," said Marek Vale, to the counter. "On the second, the third, the fourth and the fifth of the ninth month of last year, and she told him to, and she had never had it done before and she has not had it done since, and a woman in a seed house had asked her nine days earlier whether it was a thing people did and said *somebody ought*."

Perpetua Ohl had the book open on the counter and she did not close it, which she has never done for anybody in thirty years.

"No," she said.

---

"No," said Perpetua Ohl, "and I am going to say the whole of it in four sentences and then I am going to go back to my work, and I would like the four sentences to be remembered by somebody, because the four sentences are the only thing in this city that has ever been said to me on this counter that I have not said back to myself about nine times in thirty years.

"The man from a carting road is owed a figure and I cannot give him one. A figure about a number of people is a figure about a district. A district is a line in a book. A book that has to balance in the year it is balancing gets one number first, and the true one in beside it with two initials, and the first one stays on the page.

"That is the reason, and it has been the reason for as long as I have stood here, and I am not going to improve it for a carter, because a carter is a person and this is the only counter in this basin where a person can come and be told the truth about a figure for nothing."

"And the figure is fourteen," said Roko Bregar.

"The figure is fourteen attestations," said Perpetua Ohl, "and I have entered two of them in this book in my own hand in thirty years and I have never been asked to say what an attestation is."

---

The boy and the slate and the queue went out of the chapter and into the middle of the day, and Marek Vale stood at the rail afterwards with a thing in his head that he had been carrying since the second month of last year and had got good at not saying, and it was this, and he said it out loud to a counter because there was nobody else in the city he could say it to.

"There are about nine of them in this basin," he said, "and the price says fourteen, and the difference is five, and I have never once said what the five is and I have never once been told what the five is, and I have known both of those figures since the eighth month of last year and I have said them out loud in two rooms and on a bank and on a counter, and I have never once put them next to each other in my own head on purpose."

Perpetua Ohl put the pencil down flat.

"Then you are going to leave them where they are," she said, "and I am going to tell you why, and it is the same four sentences with the numbers taken out.

"The moment you subtract one from the other you have a figure about five people, and a figure about five people is a figure about a district, and you would have made it in four seconds in a lane and it would be true, and it would be the first time in this basin that anybody had put a number on the people whose willingness is the price of a winter.

"And a number on five people is a list, and a list is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper is a thing a man in a good coat can produce at you in about four years, and in four years he will produce it and there will be nothing on the page to say how anybody worked it out. Not one word. Not my hand. Not yours.

"So the five stays as two numbers that do not touch. And I have kept them apart for about five months and it is the only piece of housekeeping in this city that I have ever been proud of, and I have never told anybody that either."

---

Roko Bregar took the cart back down the low ground at about the fifth bell and the wheel made its noise the whole way, and on the low-ground road there was a foot of bank with a hole in the face of it and four sledges in a store and eleven men in a lane and a rate that had not come, and a board on a cart with two hundred and some notches on it and a date against every one.

"What did the ninth month buy," said Marek Vale to him, at the trestle, and he said it in the plain voice of a man asking a question out loud so that the answer would be in the air, and he did not care who heard it, and about four people heard it.

The carter thought about it.

"A winter," he said. "That is what it bought, and you have got the month wrong and I am not going to be unkind about it because you have been decent about my cart. The loan was sealed in the third month and the ninth month is when it was paid out in people, and I know that because I was on this road in the ninth month and the queue at the counter was about forty deep at the fourth bell and I carted for three men who came off that queue in one afternoon and could not stand up straight.

"The ninth month is the month this ground stopped being a thing that had to be paid for. Eleven weeks of works, a mile and a bit of bank, a foot of clay, and a district that answered for itself on somebody else's paper. And a rate that was going to come on about nine names in the fifth month did not come, and the fifth month is when it would have come, and I remember the fifth month because there was a week in the fifth month when a man came round the pitches in a good coat asking people to put money on a list, and the list went away in the middle of the fifth month, and I have never found out who took it off him.

"That is what it bought. And every one of the loads on that board is a piece of it, and there are two hundred and some, and the ground has got them and the district has got a foot of bank, and I have got a wheel that makes a noise and a board, and I am not going to be told a figure about a number of people by a woman on a causeway, and I would like somebody in this city to explain to me what happens to the clay if the account is struck out after all."

---

Nobody explained it to him, because there was nobody there, and the wheel made its noise down the lane.

And that evening the board went on the wall of a cart shed with a nail through the top of it, the way a carter puts a tally board where his own men can see it, and there is a day against every notch on it, and it is the only record in this basin of what the ninth month of MR 415 bought, and no office has asked for it, and it cannot be produced at anybody for about four years because it is in a cart shed on the low ground and there is no office on the low ground that has any business knowing what a carter's board is.
