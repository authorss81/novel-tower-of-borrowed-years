# Chapter 174 — A Physician's Winter

Sera Holt was at the clinic at the eighth bell on the second of the third month of MR 416 with a bag on the table and a tray of sheets out of it, and the tray had come off the low ground that morning with nine sheets in it and a man from the district office had come with it and had put a paper on the desk face down and had not said anything for about a minute.

"There is no form on this," he said.

"I have had it about a minute and I have not read it."

"You do not have to read it to know what it is."

"You may go and find somebody who does," said Sera Holt, and he did not go, and he did not read it either, and after a while he said that the district needed eleven certificates by the fourth bell and that the works would not go forward without them, and she said that the works would not go forward with them either and that both of those were true, and that the second was the reason she did not need to read it.

---

She was fifty-two and she had been a physician for twenty-six years and a senior witness for nine of them, which is a thing that gets left behind in a few years because a person stops being the sort of person who can be asked, and she had been the sort of person who could be asked until a year in which she stopped being willing, and nobody in this city has ever worked out which of the two happened.

"You are going to ask me what a certificate is for," she said, "and I am going to answer it, and I am going to answer it the way I answered a man in a seed house a year ago, and I am not going to be quick about it.

"A certificate is a statement that a body can do something. Not that a body ought to. Not that a body has agreed. A body can. And a certificate goes to the board of this clinic, and the board writes it into the roll as a cost, because a thing that is done and not counted is not a thing that was done, and I have been on this board for nine years and they are right about that, and I do not know a clinic that is not right about that.

"And the clinic's costs go on the same account as everything else in this city. And a district's answer to a cost is a rate.

"So here is the winter, and it is my trade and not my opinion. I have found a way of making the cost smaller twice in twenty-six years and both times the cost was still there and I moved it one desk to the left. In the growth month of a year two gone I certified a woman out of four of eleven days, and it was the correct instrument, and the cost went on the roll, and the district's answer to it was a rate on a woman's ground.

"And in the third month the low ground goes under, and in the third month a body has got about nine weeks left in it, and nine weeks is not a season, and nobody has ever put the two on the same sheet in this kingdom, and the moment you do you have a field instrument asking a question about a body, and there is no box on a field instrument on which anybody is asked whether she is well."

---

"The paper," said Marek Vale, "is from the district office."

"It is."

"And it is a form."

"It is a form and I have not read it and I am going to tell you what is on it, and I am telling you because I have worked out that nobody in this basin can get a physician to put her hand to a piece of paper this month, and I would like that known before the fourth bell rather than after it."

She put two fingers on the face-down sheet.

"It asks a physician to certify that eleven people on the low ground are fit to be on a bank in the third month. It has a date on it. It has a line for the name of the person certified, and it has a line at the foot of each for a mark, and there is nothing on it anywhere that says the person was asked, because there is nowhere on it to put that, and because there has never been anywhere.

"You can have it or you can leave it. If you leave it, I will certify nobody, and the eleven go out on that reach tomorrow uncertified, and two of them will be on a bank in the third month who cannot be on a bank in any month, and I will know which two before the fourth bell and I have known since I read the sheets in that tray, and I have not read the paper and I have not needed to, and that is the only advantage I have got this morning and I am not going to give it up to be helpful."

---

"You are refusing eleven people a thing they did not ask you for."

"I am refusing a district a form," said Sera Holt, "and if you want the true shape of it, a district in the third month is not asking eleven people anything, because a district has not got eleven people. A district has got a works. And the works has got nine lines on it on one board and eleven on it on another, and neither of those numbers is a number of bodies, and I have spent my life on the difference and there is not one instrument in this kingdom that has it in it.

"And I will tell you the thing underneath it, because you are going to get to it on your own in about nine days and you can have it now.

"The account of that ground is on a line of district accounts. In the third month of last year a record loan was sealed at a records office a night and a day up this river, correctly, on a standing authority, and its scope names that line, and that is the only reason there is no rate on the low ground this season, and that is what the winter was bought with.

"And every cost I certify out of a day goes onto that account, and that account is alive, and a cost on an account that is alive does not go down to the ground. It goes into a box, a night and a day up that river, in the last of five boxes, and it is not a figure when it gets there and it never has been.

"So the price of this ground's winter is being put in as a class of people's willingness, and I am a physician, and I am not going to be the one who signs the class. I have done nothing else for twenty-six years except not be that."

---

"You have a bag on your table," said Marek Vale.

"I have a bag on my table."

"I am not going to ask what is in it. I have been up a river and down again and I have not asked anybody one question this month that I could not answer the answer to, and I would like it noticed that that is the only method I have at the moment."

"You are going to come down to the low ground with me," said Sera Holt, "because I am going to the low ground, and because I have got to count eleven people in the open and I am not going to do it in a room with a tray on it, and because a physician who has certified nothing in the third month has no business staying in a building in the third month."

---

The ground had gone off completely. A barrow could not have been got up the top of the reach and there were two men on it with barrows proving that at about the fourth bell, and the clay in the face had the sheen on it that clay has when the water is behind it and looking for somewhere to come out.

They got down to the top of the reach and Holt put the bag on a stone that was dry and opened it, and there were sheets in it, and on the top of the sheets there was one that had a mark in the left-hand margin opposite a line, a small hooked stroke with a short bar under it, made in two seconds by a hand that was not either of theirs.

Marek Vale looked at the brickwork above the bag and she looked at the low ground and neither of them said one word about it, and it was not spoken of again, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which anybody may be entered as having seen it or not seen it.

"Stand up," said Sera Holt, to the eleven.

Nine of them stood up straight. Two of them got a hand on the bank, one at each end of the reach, and one of those two was about nineteen and the other one was about fifty and neither of them said anything.

"That is not a number," said Sera Holt. "I want to say what that was so that nobody in this basin can put it on anything. That was a morning, and it was a cold morning, and it was nine people who stood up and two people who put a hand on a bank, and it tells me that two people in the third month cannot be on a bank in the third month.

"It does not tell me anything else. It does not tell me that those two cannot dig. It does not tell me what they can do, and it is not a rate, and I am not going to enter it in that tray, and if one of the nine district men in this city comes up here tomorrow and asks me for a figure I am going to read him the sentence I have just said and then close the bag."

---

The two men with the barrows had stopped. One of them said that they had a man of fifty in the second gang and that the gang had been told to move a hundred yards of cut a day and that a hundred yards was a good figure when the ground took a wheel and a foolish one now, and Sera Holt said that it was both, and that both of those were ground facts and not body facts, and that she was not going to help anybody with the difference because there is no form for it.

"You have a man of fifty in the second gang."

"I have a man of fifty in the second gang and I have had a man of fifty in the second gang since the ninth month of last year and he has done every day of it and he has not been ill once."

"Then you have a man of fifty who has done every day of it for about five months and nobody has asked him to sit down," said Sera Holt, "and I am going to say the true thing about that and then I am going to stop.

"A body's calendar is what a person can do in a week, and when, and how many weeks of a year are any use to a body. A field's calendar is a year, and the district can be named in it, and when the district can be named in it the year is a fine thing to charge against. Those are two calendars and they have never once lined up and there is no office in this city that has both of them in it, and a field's calendar does not have a place in it where a person is asked how he is.

"So the low ground is going to be worked in a field's calendar, in the third month, by people who are in a body's, and there is no box on either for that, and in nine months there will be a great many people on this ground who cannot do what they did in the first, and the ground will have a figure on it, and the figure will be the district's, and there will be no person in it anywhere."

---

"One question," said Marek Vale. "And I am going to ask it badly and I am telling you in advance that I am going to ask it badly."

"You have been telling me that for three years."

"The clinic borrowed," he said. "In some year. On some terms. And a physician is a person in this city who has signed things about a roof or a floor or a building, and there is a bag on a stone, and if anybody in this basin ever needs to stand behind a sheet of yours then I am the only person here they can ask and I am not going to be able to do it."

Sera Holt closed the bag and put her hand flat on it.

"I am going to give you one of about six things I could give you," she said, "and I gave one to a man in a seed house a year ago, and I am not going to be quicker about it this time and I am not going to be nicer.

"It was not necessary. There is no loan. I am not going to tell you why there is no loan or what a figure was on a line once, and you are never going to ask me either, and if you do I will answer you the way I answered a man in a seed house.

"And that is the one thing. And you are welcome to it, and it is worth about as much as it sounds like, and you are still not a person a room will take, and I would like the two of those to be in the same sentence in your head for the rest of the day."

---

They went back up the lane at about the sixth bell and the reach behind them had nine men on it and two men on it not quite, and the man of fifty in the second gang was one of the nine and did not know that anybody had counted him and would not have cared.

On the desk in the clinic the district's form was still lying face down where she had left it, and she had not read it and she was not going to, and at the fourth bell a boy of about nine who carried messages in that part of the city and was paid in kind and was not asked about anything took it back down to the district office without knowing what it was, and no certificate went out of that clinic on the second of the third month of MR 416, and the ground was not stopped by it, and the eleven went out at the seventh bell and two of them put a hand on a bank at about the fourth hour after that, and nobody wrote any of it down, and the tray of nine sheets out of the low ground went back into the bag, and the bag went on a shelf in a clinic in a city where there is no form on which anybody may be entered as having been in the third month at all.
