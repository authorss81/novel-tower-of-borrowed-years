# Chapter 155 — The Price Is Not Money

A night and a day up the river is not a journey. It is an overnight in a room over an inn, a breakfast that costs about nine times what a breakfast costs in Rainmarket, and then a morning on the water in flat light, and the reason people say it is a journey is that nothing that happens on the trip is available afterwards, and the reason nothing is available afterwards is that the boat puts you down at a stair and goes.

Damir Pribis had been opening the basket at the records office at Verrow for eleven years and the basket is a set of shelves on a landing with a rail round it and a lock on a hasp, and the lock is a padlock and the key is on his person because there is no second key and never has been, and on the sixth of the eighth month of MR 415 he opened it at about the third bell for a man he had met once, at a counter, six weeks before, in a conversation about a bundle of about three hundred sheets.

"You are not on anything," he said, before Marek had got his coat off. "I looked. You have no standing, no office and no form, and if you had come with a form I would have had to write your name in the day book and there is a day book."

"Then I will stand here and look at the basket."

"You can stand there and look at the basket," said Pribis, "and I will tell you what the basket is, because I am going to have to tell somebody in about four years anyway and I would rather it was a filing clerk who asked me than a man in a good coat who did not.

"The basket is every entry into every regional record of instrument for this region for eleven years. Every. Not a selection. It is on the landing because a regional record does not live in a room, it lives in a place you can get to, and a fire in a room is a fire in a record and a record that has to be carried down a stair by two men in a hurry is a record that gets carried down a stair by two men in a hurry."

---

He went up the landing twice and came down with two cases and put them on a table by the window.

"Case one is this month. Case two is the third month. I do not remember either. I am not going to pretend to a man with no office that I remember, because I have been asked to remember by about four people in eleven years and the only one of them who got anything out of me was a man who did not want anything, and that was a granary clerk and he wanted to know where a number was kept and I told him the room, and he did not go, and that is the only time in eleven years anybody has used anything I said for anything."

He opened case two.

The entries were in a hand that was not his and not the same throughout, and they were ruled into columns, and every entry had a number and a district and a kind and five small boxes at the foot of it, and four of the five were filled in on most of them and the third one — the witness — was filled in on most of them too.

They went through for about forty minutes. And on the eleventh of the third month, at about the fortieth entry down, Damir Pribis stopped with his finger on a line and said:

"Here."

---

The entry was longer than the others and it had a head over it that none of the rest of them had, because it was not a district entry.

It was in the third month of MR 415 and it was sealed at this office on a standing authority and it named a district account that was going to be struck out at the end of the season, and it said in the ordinary language of a form that the account would not be struck out, and the district would go on answering for itself, and the season's works in the district would go on in the ordinary way.

Its scope named a line of district accounts. Its price was entered in the last of the five boxes, and the last of the five boxes is the box for what a person gives, and what was entered in it was not a figure and it was not a number of months.

It said: *fourteen attestations*.

"I did that," said Pribis. "I entered it. I am going to say the rest of it before you ask me anything, and I have said it in this order to myself about nine times in three months and I have never said it out loud, so I may get it wrong, and if I get it wrong you are not to put it down.

"It is a record loan. It is the only one of its kind I have entered in eleven years and I have entered about nine thousand entries and there are four kinds in this basket and this is not one of the four. A record loan changes what an office will accept as evidence. That is what the word means. It does not change what is true and it does not change what a person saw, and I have never had to enter one because nobody has ever wanted one, because a record loan costs a person something and the offices that could seal one are offices that do not like paying for things in people.

"And it was sealed here on the eleventh of the third month by a man from the board above this office, and he did it on a standing authority, and he did it correctly, and he read the price box out to me and I wrote down what he read out, and I did not ask him a question about it, and I want to know what you would have done."

"I would have asked what fourteen attestations are."

"That is what I did not do," said Damir Pribis, "and I have had three months with it and I have not been able to make myself ask it in a way that makes me better, so I have had to go somewhere else with it, which is that I write in a hand and I cannot unwrite it, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a filing clerk may strike an entry out of a regional record, and I have looked, and I did not look hard, and I looked hard enough to be able to say that sentence out loud to a man in a stair in the third month and I did not say it then."

---

"You are going to ask me why nobody in this basin can be told how many there are," said Pribis, before Marek asked, "and I am going to answer that too, and then I am going to go and open case one and you can watch me do a piece of work, because you have been standing at a window for an hour and I would like to be doing something while I say this.

"There is no office in this kingdom that will read you a sheet of names and give you a figure. Not this one. Not the one a night and a day up the river. Not the one on the island, and I will tell you the reason once and then never again: the reason is that a figure about a number of people is a figure about a district, and a district is a line in a book, and a book that has to balance in the year it is balancing gets one number first and the true one in beside it with two initials, and the first one stays on the page.

"So the number on that entry is a district figure. I entered fourteen because I was told fourteen and I have no way whatever of telling you how many of them have been produced, or where, or against what, and neither has anybody in this office, and I have looked in the basket for a return and there is no return, and the reason there is no return is that a return is a thing you make about a person and there is not a person on that entry, there is a class of people's willingness."

He stopped there, and went and opened case one, and began to go through it.

---

The boat down was flat and cold and Marek Vale sat on the after board with a sheet of nothing in his coat, because there was nothing to put in a coat.

He had gone up a night and a day with three questions and he came back down with the third one the only one that had an answer to it, and the answer was that a form with a line cut off it had been in type in this city for longer than the Trust had been able to do anything about it, and that a records office had found a way to pay for it in a box that nobody had ever tried to fill in before, and that the price had been fourteen and the number in this basin was about nine and there was no form on which he could be told either of those things in a way that would hold up in a room.

Nobody had forged anything. Every one of the people whose names were in the box had signed a form, in type, at a counter, in person, about a ditch or a roof or a stall or a boundary, and had been free when they did it, and had not been asked about any of the other thirteen things, and the thirteenth thing had not existed yet when they signed the first.

And a man in a good coat had said to about nine of them, one at a time, in about four months, in the ordinary voice of a man doing an ordinary job, *it is only a formality* — and had not asked them one question, and there was no box for it, and there had never been a box for it, and the box that did exist had been cut out of a forme in an afternoon in a print shop on Ropewalk to save a quarter of a sheet of paper on four hundred copies.

He had been in rooms for three years looking for a hand. The hand had been a compositor's, and it was nineteen years old, and it was not in anybody's.

And in a cold room above a chandler's yard on the low-ground road, at about the ninth bell, with a sheet of nothing in his coat, he worked out that there was no document in this kingdom that would answer any of it, and that he had a sheet in a flat case in Bell Stair with two names at the head of it and a rule under them, and about eleven lines on it about four people, and one of the four was a man of twenty-two whose name was not on either column because he was not in the room.
