# Chapter 151 — Twenty-Two in Four Months

The public witness counter on the causeway side of the tower had a rail in front of it that was not for leaning on, and a brass plate on the wall above it that said what the counter was for, and a second plate under the first one that said what the counter was not for, and the second plate was put up in the flood year by a clerk who is not named anywhere in this manuscript and who was right.

Perpetua Ohl had been at that counter for thirty years and had entered other people's words every working day since she was twenty-one, and on the fourth of the sixth month of MR 415 she had a book open on the counter in front of her that was not a register and was not a return and had no head at the top of the page, and she was doing what she does, which is counting, and she would not do it for a man.

"You will want the number and I am not going to give you the number," she said, before he had asked.

"I did not ask for the number."

"You came up the causeway at the second bell with a face on you that says *number*, and I have been on this counter for thirty years and I know that face, and it is the same face as the Tower's own, which is a face that wants a figure and then wants to know what the figure is a figure of."

"Then I will wait for you to want to say it."

She went on counting. She counted with the end of a pencil on the board under the brass plate, not on the page, because a figure that is made on the page in front of you is a figure somebody can be shown afterwards, and she had learned that in about the fourth year and had never had to be told it twice.

"There is a thing you should know before you stand at my counter any longer than you have to," she said. "A challenge is free. Anybody may bring one. There is a fee for a settlement and there is no fee for a challenge and there is no form on which a challenge may be refused, and I have never refused one, and I am not going to, and the reason I am telling you is that in about four minutes you are going to ask me whether I can turn one away and the answer is no and I have not had to think about it."

"I am not going to ask you to turn one away."

"You were going to ask me to turn one *down*, which is a different sentence, and it means the same thing, and a man who is careful with words will use that one on purpose."

---

There were two people in the queue at the second bell and about nine at the fourth and a woman at the third window with a sheet in a cloth who had been there since the first bell and was not going to leave.

Marek Vale stood at the end of the rail and read the whole of the cloth out of her hands, which took about a minute, and then read it again, and the second time was not for him.

The sheet was a single leaf of a printed form with a head at the top of it in type. It named a district water account, it named a season, it named a rate that had not come, and in the fourth of five boxes at the foot it named a man as a witness, and the man's name was in a fair small hand and the shape of the hand was the shape of every hand in this city that has ever been taught to write by the same master.

"Was that the first time you have brought that," said Marek.

"No."

"How many times."

"That is not a thing I am asked and it is not a thing I keep," said the woman. "I have been told about a man being a witness to a thing about water and I have come to find out whether that is a thing anybody can look at, and the man at the third window has told me that it is a thing anybody can look at, and I would like it looked at."

"You will be told what you are told."

"I have been told four things in a month by four men who were not the same man and I would like one of them looked at."

She took the cloth back over the sheet and went and stood at the third window again, and she was not at the front of the queue when it moved, which is a thing about queues that nobody in this kingdom has ever written a rule about.

---

"The nine that are the same," said Perpetua Ohl.

"There are nine?"

"There are nine in my book and I have not counted them out and I am not going to count them out, and the count is twenty-two and that is a different number and it is the only one I will give you, and I will give you the two as well because the two is worse than the one. Two in the whole of last year. Twenty-two since the first of the fourth month, and the first of the fourth month was about nine weeks ago."

"Say that again the way you would say it to somebody who has to write it down."

"I will not say it again the way you would say it to somebody who has to write it down, and if you write it down I will not have said it, and you will have said it, and that is the arrangement and it is not a new one and I have been at this counter for thirty years." She put the pencil down flat. "And I will tell you what I think you came up here for, and then I will go and do my work, and then you may stand there as long as you like.

"You came up here for a hand. You have wanted a hand since you were about twenty-two. You are a man who reads sheets and you have spent three years looking for a person who wrote something wrong, and the reason you have spent three years looking for one is that a person who writes something wrong can be shown a thing, and a person who is made to write something wrong cannot."

"There is no forger," said Marek.

"I did not say that."

"You did not have to. You have had nine of these in a book for four months and every one of them is a name in the witness box and a person at this counter says the name is not theirs for this paper, and if there were a forger you would have found the hand in about a fortnight, because a forged hand is a different thing to look at and you would know it in nine seconds and so would I."

"Then say what is in it, because I have not got here in nine weeks for a lecture about hands."

---

She got up and went behind the counter and came back with something under her arm, and it was a bundle of paper tied with a piece of string in three turns, and it went on the counter with a sound, and the counter is stone and the tower is old and the sound went along it.

"Everything that has ever come through this counter that was not a settlement is in here," she said. "There are about three hundred sheets and they are not a record. They are what people brought and I could not enter and could not refuse, and I have kept them for thirty years in a bundle under a counter, which is not a thing anybody taught me."

"Why keep them."

"Because in about the ninth year a man came and asked me whether his mother's name had ever been on anything at this counter, and I could not tell him, and he went away and I found out nine years later that she had, and I have kept them ever since." She turned the bundle over. "And that is the reason I am going to untie this and I am going to be slow about it, and the reason I am going to retie it afterwards is that if I do not retie it properly then this bundle is a thing that was opened, and a thing that was opened is a thing a man in a good coat can produce at me in about four years."

She cut the string in three places and did not use a knife, because a knife is a thing you do not use on a bundle in a room with a brass plate on the wall.

There were about three hundred sheets in it and they were not all the same. That is the first thing and it is the thing that takes a man about nine minutes to see and about nine seconds to understand: a bundle of forms that is mostly one kind is a stock and a stock is a printer's business, and a bundle that is a mixture is a record of what people actually brought, and what people actually brought in thirty years is a mixture, because a counter is a place where people bring the thing they have and not the thing that is tidy.

But about nine in ten of them were the same.

---

Four ruled lines and no boxes. The name at the top. Then a line, and in the line a description in a person's own hand of a thing that is not a paper — a ditch, a roof, a stall, a boundary, a bin, a boat. Then a third line, and the third line was in type and was the same on every one of them, and it said:

*I will speak to any paper touching this line while I live.*

And then a fourth line, and in the fourth line there were two words and one of them was a month.

Marek Vale read the third line nine times, which is not a figure of speech, and then he put his hand flat on the counter and left it there.

"Perpetua."

"I have been here thirty years and you have been on this island about nine times and you have not used my name once."

"I am using it now. When was this printed."

"That is the fourth line question and I can only answer it about the fourth line: the month is there and the day is not, and there has never been a day on one of these in the time of this counter, and I have never once entered one and thought about why there is no day on it, and I have entered about nine hundred."

"And the third line is type. It is not written. Nobody writes that sentence. It is on the form."

"It is on the form."

"So it was printed on purpose, by somebody, for a purpose, and the purpose is in the sentence and not in the form."

"Yes."

"And the second line is what the person is going to speak to."

"Yes."

"And it is not a paper."

"No," said Perpetua Ohl. "It is not a paper. I have had thirty years to notice that and I am telling you that I did not, and I am not going to be praised for it and I am not going to be told that I should have, because I have entered about nine hundred of these and I have read the name and I have read the line and I have copied the month, and those are three things, and the three things are what is on the sheet, and a counter keeper reads what is on the sheet."

She untwound the string off the bundle and then she retied it, and she did it wrong the first time because she was thinking, and she undid it and did it again properly in about nine seconds.

"And here is the other thing and it is not mine and I have never said it out loud to a person at this counter and I am saying it now because you have read nine lines of type in nine seconds and you are the first person in three years who has looked at one of these the way you just looked at one.

"Nobody has ever asked me whether the people in that book were free when they signed."

---

She said it in the ordinary voice of a woman who has said numbers out loud for thirty years, and she put the bundle down on the counter on top of the cloth on the woman's sheet, which she should not have done, and Marek did not say so, and neither did she.

Then she said:

"There is a room behind the third window and it is eight feet by eleven and it is not a hearing room and it is not a witness room and it is the room people are put in when they have to stand and wait, and there is a rail round three sides of it and a window in it with a wire across it, and on the fourth of the ninth month of last year a woman stood in that room for two days and a night because there was nowhere else for her to stand, and there is no form on which anybody may be given a bed."

"And the third of the five boxes on the sheet at the third window names a witness."

"The third of the five boxes names a witness."

"So a person is put in a box, and the box does not say what the person is a witness to, and the paper goes on without the box knowing."

"The paper goes on without the box knowing," said Perpetua Ohl, "and the box is me, and I have never known either, and I would like it noticed that this is the first time in thirty years that a man has read the third line out loud in this room and it has taken him nine seconds and I have had three hundred sheets in a bundle under a counter for thirty years."

She picked the pencil up off the board.

"You can stand at that rail as long as you want," she said. "I am going to be here until the sixth bell and I am not going to say the count again and I am not going to say the nine, and if you go up that causeway to a room a night and a day up the river and come back with a figure, do not come back here with it, because I will not enter it and I will not refuse it and that is the whole of what this counter is."

There were about nine people in the queue at the sixth bell and a woman at the third window with a sheet in a cloth, and a bundle of about three hundred sheets tied with string in three turns under a counter on the causeway side of a tower, and the third line of every one of them in type said the same twelve words, and not one of the nine hundred sheets in that bundle in thirty years had ever had a line on it that named the paper.
