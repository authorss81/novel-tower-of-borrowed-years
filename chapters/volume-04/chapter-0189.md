# Chapter 189 — The Room With a Street Door

The chandler told the lane on the morning of the twenty-seventh of the fourth month of MR 416, and he told it standing at his own front door with the notice still on it, and the people in the lane heard him, and the notice on that door had been there since the fifteenth of the second month and had not come off and was not going to come off.

"A man came to me on the twenty-fourth with a paper," he said, "and he was not unkind about it and he was not drunk and he was not in a hurry, and he asked me what stands on the ground behind my shop.

"And I have got a bin back there that has been there since the second of the first month of this year and belongs to nobody and I have asked twice who owns it and I have never been told, and I have decided to go on not knowing and I told that to nine people in this lane in the fifth month and I have thought about it every day since, which is a sentence I have never had to say in my life.

"And on the twenty-fourth I could not go on not knowing, because there is a piece of paper and it is in his hand and he is going to come back, and it stopped being true the moment I saw four people go into that back room and I have got a shop and a front door and forty years of a trade and I did not sleep on the twenty-fourth, and I am standing here telling the lane because I would rather they heard it from me than from him."

"What did you say to him," said Drazen Topic.

"I said I did not know," said the chandler. "And that was true on the twenty-fourth and it stopped being true the moment I saw four people go into that back room, and I can name three of them."

---

They were in the room at the bottom of the lane by the second bell, and the room had a bench in it and a bin in the corner and a chair that nobody sat in, and there were four of them, and the reason there were four of them was not a rota and had never been a rota and was four people who had come down a lane because the chandler had asked them to, and they were not asked again after that, and nobody in the lane said the word rotation and if anybody had said it somebody would have said the word back.

Zvonko Maras, fifty-eight, a coal cart, who had refused a wall in the first month of this year and had his name on nothing. Margit Sova, forty-one, twenty-six years in the trade, four days of writing left in a hand that has got worse over twenty-six years, whose lease runs out at the end of this month. Drazen Topic, thirty-four, who counted at a market rail four times a day and had a slate under his arm that was blank side up.

And Borka, about sixty-one, who had been in the lane nine years and had been in the place before there was a lane, and who could not write, and whose name was on a wall at the end of the lane in chalk in a child's hand, and who had never asked the child to take it off.

"You have four people in here," said the man in the good coat, at the eighth bell, from the street door, with a form and no list, "and I have three names off a man who sells candles, and I would like the fourth before I go up the street with this."

He did not come in. He stood on the step, which is a thing a person does who has been in a room before and knows about the door.

---

"There is no clerk in this room," said Borka.

There was nothing else in it for about four seconds, and the four seconds were the four seconds.

"That is the whole of what I have," said Borka. "I cannot write. That is all I have got and I have been not saying it for a month because the last time I said it I was asked a question about it and I am not going to be asked a question about it today, and I am telling you first so that you cannot come in here and find out and put it on your paper with the other three.

"You have got three. You have not got me. I am not going to tell you. And I could not write it if I tried, and that is not a thing I am saying so that you will think better of me, it is a thing I am saying because it is the only advantage I have ever had in my life and I am tired of it going to waste."

The man in a good coat looked at the form for a while.

"You understand what that is," he said. "I am not threatening you. I am telling you that a person in a good coat can walk down a lane and read a wall, and there is a wall at the end of this lane with names on it in chalk, and I have not read it and I am not going to, and I am telling you that I could."

"I know you could," said Borka. "I have known that since the twenty-seventh of the first month. And you are welcome to."

---

Drazen Topic put the slate down on the bench face down and then turned it over so that the blank side was up, which is the gesture of a man who has been asked one question too many in a lane and has worked out in advance what the answer is.

"There is nothing on it," he said. "I have not got a slate of my own with a head on it. There is one in this lane and it has eleven lines on it and a head that I wrote at a rail on the twenty-second of the second month and washed off and wrote again, and the four names on it were washed off at the bottom of a ladder in the seventeenth of the third and are in four heads and that is the correct arrangement.

"And I am not going to tell you that four heads cannot be got at. Four heads can be got at. I am telling you what is on a slate and there is nothing on a slate and the reason there is nothing on a slate is that there is nothing to be on it, and a man in a good coat can produce a piece of paper at anybody in about four years and I have not got one and I have not signed one in about five months and I am not going to start because somebody has come up a lane with a form."

"Have you been produced," said the man in a good coat.

"Twice," said Margit Sova, from the bench. "Both times by a different man and neither of them knew about the other, and I have not been told one thing about it except that I was a witness, and I have not got a pitch because of it, and I have not got a pitch because of nine or ten other things as well, and I am not going to be one of the reasons a piece of paper gets fuller in this quarter."

---

"You were at the room behind the Threshold on the twenty-fifth," said Zvonko Maras.

"I was," said the man in the good coat.

"I heard about it. I am not interested in that and I want to say so before anybody in this lane starts putting the two together, because you have come up here about a notice and not about a room, and there is a difference and I have got a head of my own for it even if I do not use it." Maras did not move off the wall. "I want to say the one thing I have come down here to say and then you can write what you like on your paper.

"That notice is nailed to a board about nine feet from where I am standing. I have read it. It has four things on it and every one of the four is true and not one of the four is any use to me.

"My lease ran out on a roof in the second month of a year six years ago and it has been running on a page ever since. I asked for a thing read out on the seventeenth of the third month in a room up a ladder and on the twentieth of this month I got it read out and a man put his name at the foot of it in pencil, and he did it because a boy of about twenty-nine is entitled to ask me a question, and I did not want it and I said yes anyway.

"So there are four true things on a board and I have been able to act on one of them, and the one I could act on was not on the notice and it cost a man with no office a thing he is never getting back. That is what a notice does. It tells you a thing is true and then you have to find out what it costs, and the finding out is the whole of the work and there is no board anywhere in this kingdom that pays anybody for the finding out."

He put his hands behind his back.

"You have got three names," he said. "Write them. I have been on a coal bay for thirty-six years and my name is not worth three minutes of anybody's morning and I have not been on a wall in five months and I am not going to start."

---

Then somebody asked the question that was in the room and had been in it since the second bell, and it was Zlata Hruska, and she had come down the ladder and had not gone up it again.

"Go and read the wall," she said, to the man in the good coat. "You have not read it and you have told her you have not read it, and it is at the end of this lane and you are a person who can read, and I am not going to stop you and I am not going to ask you not to, because if I ask you not to then somebody has asked you, and a thing somebody is protecting is a thing somebody owns."

"That is a very great deal of rope," said the man in the good coat.

"There is a wet sleeve and about four minutes," said Zlata Hruska. "I have done it once, for a man who wanted his name off it, and it came off in about a minute and the name went with it, and the day I did that was the only power this room has ever had, and I have thought about nothing else for a month.

"And here is why it is not going to happen again, and I am going to say it once and then we are all going back to what we were doing.

"A wall in chalk has no head on it. A man can rub a name off a wall and a man can leave a wall alone, and neither of those two things is a fact about the world, and the man in a good coat who comes up here in four years is going to have a piece of paper and not a sleeve. A wall cannot be protected and a wall cannot be lost. It is the only thing in this basin in that position and that is exactly why it is in that position, and the day I start protecting it I have made it a thing that needs protecting, and a thing that needs protecting has a person on it who is doing the protecting.

"You may read it," she said. "It is chalk. It is in the open. It has been in the open since the twenty-seventh of the first month and a good many people have stood in front of it and worked out that a day is against every single line on it, and there is no word at the top of it and there is no eleventh name, and anybody may walk along it and say *that is the twenty-ninth* and be right or be wrong in front of the other ten."

He did not go and read it. He stood on the step for about another minute with the form, and then he said:

"I have three names and a fourth that is not on here, and I am going to write the three, and I am going to put *a person present, not named* against the fourth, because I am not going to put a word on a form for something I have not got, and I have watched a good many men do that and every one of them was pleased with himself afterwards."

"Write that," said Zlata Hruska. "Write it exactly like that. And I want somebody in this room to notice what has just happened, which is that for the first time in this basin a form has got something right on it by accident, and that nobody who did it was trying to."

---

He wrote the three names at the bottom of the step and put the form inside his coat, and the three names are on a piece of paper in a good coat's hand in a quarter of this city and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be told what is on it or be asked to have it taken off, and in about four years it can be produced at any of the three of them, and it will say that three people were in a room at the bottom of a lane on the twenty-seventh of the fourth month of this year and a man in a good coat asked them three questions, and it will say nothing whatever about what any of them said, and that is the correct arrangement and it is not enough.

The man in a good coat went up the lane and did not stop at the wall at the end of it, and the four people in the back room did not go up it either, and the chandler came back at about the fourth bell and stood in his own street door and said that the room was still his and that he had not signed anything and that he did not know who the bin belonged to and was going to go on not knowing for as long as there was a bin in the corner of it.

And nobody thanked anybody, and the chair in the room at the bottom of the lane was empty the whole of the following week, and a room with a street door in a lane that has no name on any map a clerk in this city can look at is not a court and has no office and cannot be produced at anybody, and on the day the man in a good coat went up the lane four people were in it and not one of them was a clerk and not one of them had said a word about the form except a woman of about thirty-four who told a stranger on a step that he was welcome to read a wall.
