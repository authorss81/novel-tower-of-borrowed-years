# Chapter 190 — Three People Who Will Not Look

Nim Rook had made a copy, and she made it in a quarter of an hour in a copy room in Bell Stair on the thirteenth of the fourth month of MR 416 out of a page that had been on a table in front of her for a reason connected with her work, and it was a true copy, and it was in her own hand, and it was folded in four in the inside of a coat, and she had carried it since the thirteenth of the fourth month and had not told anybody in this kingdom that she had made it.

What was on it was a page of a ward roll, and the page was not remarkable, and the far-right column of it had nothing at the head of it, and there were about nine names in that column, and the fourteenth line down of the far-right column had a name on it in a fair small hand, and Nim Rook had not said that name out loud in this city for more than two years and was not going to say it on the twenty-ninth of the fourth month to a man in a wet sleeve or to anybody else.

She wanted one thing out of it and had said so to exactly nobody: she wanted to know whether there was a living person with that name on it.

---

"You are not going to look at it," said Nim Rook.

She had it out of her coat and had it half unfolded and she stopped with it half unfolded, which is not a thing a person who is going to look does.

"No," said Marek Vale.

"That is the first time in four months."

"Then it is the first time in four months and I would like you to notice that you have said so, because you notice everything and I have got four things left that I am any good at and refusing to look at a piece of paper is not one of them and I have just done it and I did it in about a second and a half and I would have got through the whole thing in about nine."

She folded it again.

"You have read every single thing anybody in this basin has handed you since the growth month of last year," she said. "You have read four things out loud in that room and you read a sheet of a man's roof twice and you read a form in a shop and a notice in a street. You have never once not read one. And you have been in a bad way since the thirtieth of the ninth month of last year and you have made a career out of being the man who will still do the job, and I have watched you do it four hundred times."

"You have a price," he said.

"I have a price," said Nim Rook, "and I set it on a stair in the flood year, and it is one sentence, and I have never had it out of my head once and I have not said it to you in three years because there has been nothing to say, and here it is and it is the whole of why you were right a second and a half ago.

"The moment you find out anything at all about a name of mine, you tell me."

"That is not what that has got to do with."

"It has got everything to do with with it and I am going to say the rest and then I am going to stop, and you are going to let me, because you have not asked me a question about this in three years and that is the arrangement and the arrangement is the only thing either of us has ever kept.

"You have found out nothing about a name of mine. Not one. In five months I have sat in three rooms with you and you have not asked me one question about anything to do with me, and there has been a certified copy on a desk in one of them with about nine names on it and you have not asked me whether I have ever seen it, and I set that price in the flood year because I have been in this city for six years and everybody in it has a price and mine is not money and it is the only one anybody has ever tried to keep with me.

"And you were not going to look at that paper because if you looked at it and I showed you the column then you would have found out something about a name of mine, and you would have had to tell me inside a minute, and I have got a copy of a page in my coat and it is the only one in this kingdom that I made, and I have never asked you for anything, and I am not going to start tonight by taking the only thing I have got away from you in one second and a half."

She put it back in her coat.

"I am not angry," she said. "I want to say that and I have had three days to be angry and I am not. You did it right. I want it written down that I said you did it right, and there is nothing to write it down on, so you will have to do it."

---

The first of the three was a woman of thirty-four at the top of a ladder in a lane with no name on any map a clerk in this city can look at, and Zlata Hruska did not take it out of her coat and did not unfold it and did not ask to.

"No," she said.

"You have not looked at it."

"I have not looked at it and I am not going to and I am not going to ask what is on it, and I have had a fortnight to decide whether I would and I decided on the first day and it has not changed since.

"The room said no to a copy on a lane in the third month of this year and I gave four reasons and the first of them was that a copy is a piece of paper and paper outlives what it was copied from and does not know it is a copy. And that is true and it is not why, and I have not got anybody to say the rest of it to, so I am going to say it to you.

"I cannot unsee it. That is the whole of it. I have been at a door for nine years asking people one question and I have never written one answer down and that is not a virtue, it is a position, and the position is that there is nothing in this lane anybody can come back to me about. And if I have seen that page then there is one thing in this lane that somebody can come back to me about, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody can be held to what they have seen, and so the day a man in a good coat comes up this ladder and asks me whether I have seen a page, I have got exactly two answers, and one of them is a lie and the other one is the first copy of that page in this city and it will be in my hand and in my house.

"And I would rather be a person who has to think for a minute about a question than a person who has to think for a minute about a lie. That is all of it and I have got no better reason than that and I am not going to be given credit for it."

---

"Are these the nine who were told," said Nim Rook, in the lane, to nobody, and answered herself, which she does.

"No," she said. "And I want to say why in one sentence and then I want to leave it alone for the rest of my life.

"There are about nine names in a column on a page with nothing at the head of it, and there are nine people in five months who have been told at a window that they are witnesses, and those are two lists and a Deputy of the Chamber took them apart in a room in the fourth month of this year and she was right and I have been angry about it since the first month and I am not angry about it now.

"A room cannot make a finding on a conjunction nobody can test. And if it does, then in about four years somebody produces the finding, and there will be nothing in this kingdom that can tell anybody which of the two things we had made, and that is the whole of what I have got against a Deputy of the Chamber who was right.

"So the two lists do not touch. I am not going to put them together for a man with a form on a step and I am not going to put them together for anybody, and I do not want it said anywhere in this quarter that I have put them together, and I have not, and I will not."

---

The second of the three was a settlement marshal, forty years old, in a room over a chandler's yard with a board against the wall and a list on the board, and she was working on a settlement of her own and had been working on it for most of this month, and the woman of twenty-two came up the outside stairs with a folded sheet and did not get further than the doorway.

"Seren Calder."

"I have something I am not going to show you," said Nim Rook, "and I have come up your stairs anyway, and I want to say why I have come up them, and then I want you to say the thing you are going to say, and I am not going to argue with it, and if you are going to say nothing I would rather you did that on a staircase than in a room."

"There is no verb for that in this kingdom," said Seren Calder, "and I have asked for one and nobody gave it to me, and I am not going to start inventing them for a courier at eight bells on the fourth month. What do you have."

"A copy of a page. Made by me, in a copy room, in a quarter of an hour, out of a page that was on a table in front of me for a reason to do with my work. It is a true copy. It is in four folds. It has about nine names in a column with nothing at the head of it and I am not going to tell you the names and I am not going to tell you whether the names on it are people anybody in this city has ever met."

---

"No," said Seren Calder.

"You have not asked what it is."

"I do not need to know what it is. I have worked it out in four seconds and I am going to say it in one sentence and you are going to go down the stairs.

"I have stood in that room twice. In the fourth month of this year I went up a ladder in a lane with a person I was arguing with and I stood on the floor of it, and there is a standing authority in this kingdom that lets me seal a settlement on a witnessing held in a room the marshal has been in, and it is old and it is narrow and it is the only door in this business, and I have said in four rooms in two months that I will seal a settlement on it and I will not read the price box and I will not read it whatever is in it.

"And that is exactly why I am not going to look at that paper.

"Because if I look at it, then I am a settlement marshal who has read a page, and I will have to say whether I can do anything with it. And I cannot. There is no surety a person can be named out of for any of it, and about nine names on a page is not a surety, and a class is not a person, and I have a board against that wall with a list on it and every name on the list is a name this city can produce and none of them is on your paper in front of me.

"And a marshal who has read a paper and cannot act on it is the most dangerous object in this city, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be required to put one of me down. You go down my stairs."

---

Nim Rook went down the stairs.

At the bottom of them she stood in a yard for about as long as it takes to change her mind, and then she went up two of them again, which was a thing she did and did not mention afterwards.

"I asked a woman in a lane to say the thing she was going to say and she said it, and I asked you and you said it, and I have got one more and she is at the end of a road and it is a night," she said, "and I have been thinking since the fifth bell whether I am going to ask her the same question or a different one."

"You will ask her the same question," said Seren Calder, "and I will tell you now, and you may carry it up a road in the dark and it is worth nothing and I am not charging you for it.

"I asked for the finding I made in a room on the tenth of the third month to be produced at me in four years, when the man in the good coat comes, and I said I would be in the room and I would let him read it out and I would not have read the price box then either, and he can put that in a file as well.

"I have not read the price box in the fourth month of last year and I have not read it now. And I am not going to start in the fourth month of this year either, and it does not matter whose sister it is or whose name it is or how few of them there are. I have not read it because I would not be able to put it down, and I would rather be a marshal who has not read it than a marshal who has read it and has a reason, and I have a reason, and I have had it since the second month, and I have said it out loud four times in four rooms and I am saying it a fifth time to a woman of twenty-two on my own stairs because you asked me to and not because it does me any good."

---

The third of the three was in Tallow Ward, at the shed at the Gate, at about the sixth bell on the twenty-ninth of the fourth month, and the rota said the shed was open and it was open four hours a day on the days the rota said and nothing whatever in this kingdom knows it is a shed.

Ruzena Kvas was sixty-one and she kept the wall list, and she read it twice at the ordinary speed once a week, and about forty lines went up the wall, and there was a column at the end of the fourth that had nothing at the head of it and that she did not read and did not talk about and had never been asked about and was not going to be asked about by anybody in this basin.

"You are the one who was at the window in the fifth month of last year," said Nim Rook.

"I was at a window in the fifth month of last year," said Ruzena Kvas, "and so were three other people, and none of us knew about the other three, and I have never worked out whether that is arranged or whether that is what a city is."

"I have a piece of paper and I am not going to show it to you and I have not come up the road to ask you a question about it. I have come because I have asked two people already this week and both of them said no for a reason that is their own, and I would like to know whether the third one is the same, and I would like to know before the sixth month."

"No," said Ruzena Kvas.

"You have not asked me what is on it," said Nim Rook, "and you are not going to now, and I have come up a road to a shed in Tallow Ward to be told no by a woman of sixty-one who has never been thanked for anything, and I would like that noted, because I have been told no twice this week already and one of them was on a staircase."

"I have not asked what is on it and I am not going to. And I will tell you my reason, and then I am going back to the wall, because the wall does not get read while I am talking to a courier.

"I keep a list. That is my whole trade and I have done it for longer than you have been alive and nobody in this yard has ever thanked me for it and I did not begin to want to be thanked and I have not started.

"And a list is a thing a person keeps. A piece of paper in a coat is not a thing a person keeps, it is a thing a person carries, and there is a difference and the difference is years. A person carries a thing and forgets which pocket. A person keeps a thing, and a person who keeps a thing is a keeper, and a keeper is a person with a name at the top of it, and about nine people in this basin have been produced once and there is no form on which a person may be produced twice.

"So if I have seen that page then it is a second list, and it is in a lane with no name on it and not in this shed, and there are two lists and one of them has got a woman of sixty-one behind it, and you cannot have two of those. I have one. I have had one for a very long time and I would rather have one that nobody can produce at me than two that somebody can.

"And there is the wall," she said, "and I will say the last thing about it and then I am going to go and read it.

"I do not read the end of the fourth. I have never read it, not aloud and not to myself, and I have not counted what is under it and I am not going to start in the fourth month because a courier has come up a road. And a wall is the only thing in this basin that a person can stand in front of and say *I do not know what that is* and be believed, and if I put a page in my head then I will have started keeping something I cannot say the name of, and I have got years of that left in me and I am not going to spend one of them on a piece of paper in a lane."

---

Nim Rook walked back down the road in the dark with the copy in her coat and the rest of a night to think in, and she had been told no three times by three people in three trades who had never met each other, and by a man with no office who had never refused a thing anybody handed him in five months and had refused this one in a second and a half.

She had wanted one thing out of it, and she had not got it, and the three people who would not look at it had not cost her anything except the thing she wanted, and none of them had asked her for a copy, and none of them had asked her for the name, and not one of them had said thank you.

And a page in a copy room in Bell Stair was a page, and a copy is a piece of paper and paper outlives what it was copied from and does not know it is a copy, and there is nothing in this kingdom that can look at one and tell anybody where it came from, and Nim Rook was a night courier of twenty-two with a satchel and a coat and a road, and she went out on the road at about the seventh bell with a thing in four folds that she had made in a quarter of an hour, and the rest of the night to think in, and the door of her trade is a road and it is not on any map either, and she had not told anybody her sister's name and was not going to, and it is not a name anybody in this basin has said out loud this year.
