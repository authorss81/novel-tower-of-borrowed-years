# Chapter 200 — The Door Is Open

The wall at the end of the lane had a day in chalk against every line of it on the twenty-fourth of the fifth month of MR 416, and about six days had run since the rota was last written, and a day goes off a chalk wall about a week before a name does, and the name is the part that is any use, and that is not a fault in anybody's work.

Nim Rook came down the lane at about the second bell with the chalk and did the whole of it in about eleven minutes and did not correct one hand.

That is the rule. It is not on the wall and it is in four heads and it has never been written down anywhere, and it is that the hand does not correct anybody's hand. A name in a tradeswoman's chalk from the twenty-seventh of the first month is left as the tradeswoman left it. A day in a copy-room hand from the twenty-first of the third is left as the copy-room hand left it. A week goes on it, and nobody rubs anything out, and if two hands have said the same thing in two different ways then the wall has two answers to that day and anybody who walks along it can say which one they think is the better one and be wrong in front of the other nine.

Ten names. No word at the top of it. There has never been an eleventh name and nobody has ever asked for one.

Zvonko Maras's no is on the wall in the only way a no ever gets on a wall, which is by not being on it.

---

He was on the bay at the first bell on the twenty-fourth of the fifth month, and a man of about twenty-nine pulled a truck out of it, and the truck was out and the light was up and the man stood at the mouth of the bay for a while and then said:

"Two days ago a woman came up a ladder in a lane and told me a clerk had put his name at the foot of it."

"She did not tell me," said Marek Vale. "She was not in the room when I did it and she has not asked me about it and I have not told her."

"I did not ask who," said Zvonko Maras, "and I have not looked at it, and I have had it in the inside of my coat on the left for two months and I have decided today to have it out, so you are going to read it out to me and I am going to stand here while you do it and then I am going to ask you one question and you are going to answer it, and neither of us is going to be thanked."

There was nobody else in the bay. The coal was in and the tools were on the wall where a man of fifty-eight has kept tools for thirty-six years.

Marek Vale read it out loud, twice at the ordinary speed, because that is what a clerk does before a name goes on a page, and it is the only qualification he has got, and both times it was a good sheet with a name in the fourth line and a month and not a day and a printed third line and a roof over a coal bay and not a paper, and there is no defect in it and there never has been.

"Here is the cost and I have said all of it in this basin before and I am going to say it again because a man who does not repeat a cost is a man who has decided somebody else has heard it," he said. "A counter-signature is the institution's act and it is not mine. When a chain is false-witnessed it walks to the living clerk who countersigned in good faith, and I did, in good faith, and there was nothing wrong with my hand or my eye or my honesty that afternoon, and I have had five months to find a way of making that not matter and there is not one.

"And that sheet is a true page with a clerk's name behind it, and it is good in this kingdom in a way nothing in that lane has ever been good. And I am still a man whose word no room will take, and I will be that tomorrow, and being a clerk this morning has not changed it and it is not going to change it the next time either, and I am not going to get a third letter from anybody saying so, and I have had two.

"And the paper is not false. That is the whole of it. It names a roof and not a paper, and a man of fifty-eight signed it at a counter in the second month of a year six years ago and was not asked one question about it, and there is no box on it anywhere in this kingdom on which anybody could have asked him, and there never has been one."

Zvonko Maras took it out of the inside of his coat and held it in both hands for a while.

"I did not want that," he said. "I said so in the room on the twentieth of the fourth month and I am saying it again on my own coal, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be told I was brave, and I would like somebody in this bay to be angry with me about it and there is nobody, and that is the correct arrangement and it is not a hardship.

"Ask it now. I have been standing here for two months building up to a question I am not going to be able to answer."

"What do you want to know," said Marek Vale.

"I want to know what I am," said Zvonko Maras.

He said it in the voice of a man asking for a figure.

"I have been in a coal bay thirty-six years and I have had a roof since I was twenty-two, and I signed a sheet in the second month of a year six years ago because a man at a counter said the district was doing me a favour and I wanted a thing mended, and I have never been asked one question about it until a man with no office put a clerk's name at the foot of it so that a boy in this house could ask me.

"And you have read it out twice and you have told me four costs and not one of them is the answer.

"So here is the question and I am going to say it in the words I have been saying in my own head for two months and I have not got it right and I am not going to get it right in a bay at the first bell on a day when I have eleven loads to do.

"Am I a witness, and if I am, then to what, and for how long, and does it stop when I die, and if it does not stop then what is it called when a dead man is holding a thing up."

Nobody in that bay answered him, and he did not ask again, and he put the sheet back inside his coat on the left over the heart, which is where a man puts a thing he intends to be asked about, and he went to his eleven loads.

---

About the fourth bell on the same day, a woman of about thirty-four read out loud in a room about nine feet by eleven, over a dye house, on the side of the rail she has never once stood on.

She had a sheet in a flat case that Neris Kade had carried and opened, and Kade was in the room and did not ask to be asked, and Marek Vale was in the room and was at the back by the step, and there were about eleven people in it and one of them was a man who keeps a counting floor and one of them was a man who sells candles, who had come down his own street and gone up the ladder and could not climb the last three rungs and had not asked anybody to carry him and had not been asked.

The sheet is a line and not a page, and it is ruled in two columns and gone over in ink, and it has **two names at the head of them**, in the same hand, at the same size, and a rule under them that no person may write in the column with the other person's name at the head of it, and a line at the foot, cut, and blank.

Both columns have lines in them.

"This is the sheet," said Zlata Hruska. "I am reading it because there is a woman in this room whose name is at the head of one of these columns and a man whose name is at the head of the other, and neither of them is going to read it and both of them have said so, and I have been asked one question at a door for nine years and this is what I am for.

"I am going to read all of it, including the parts that are not filled in, because a sheet that is read up to the empty parts is a sheet that has been read.

"Neris Kade. A field examiner. Four lines. A line about a boundary that has two ends and eleven holdings on it and no number on the sheet. A line about a bank that came off a reach in the second month and went back on the twenty-eighth of the third. A line about the water coming off the low ground in the fifth month and nobody knowing the day."

She turned the sheet about a foot and kept hold of it so that it lay flat.

"Marek Vale. No office. One line, written on the twelfth of the fourth month on a bank of new clay with a pencil that was not his, and it is a period and not a person, and it is about a store on the low-ground road, a key on a nail, and a doorway a man can stand in. And a second line, about a Deputy of the Chamber and a finding of the thirteenth of the third month and eleven answers, in which he says she was right and was not being gracious about it, and I have read that line out three times in three rooms and it is the only line on this sheet that anybody in this basin could check.

"And the rule under the two names is that no person may write in the column with the other person's name at the head of it. I am not going to say anything about that rule. I have been asked by three people in five months what it is for and I have not asked them, and it is their rule and it is the only thing on the sheet that anybody put there on purpose.

"And there is a line at the foot, and it is cut, and there is nothing on it."

She held the sheet up so that the eleven people could see the bottom of it.

"There is nothing on it and I am not going to guess at it, and I am not going to leave it off, and I have decided today that I am not going to read a sheet in this room ever again without saying that there is a rule here and an empty line under it, and that is the end of the sheet, and those are all of the words on it."

---

And then a woman of about thirty-four picked up a different piece of paper off the rail, and it was a five-line form, and there were nine people in a room and nobody had asked her to read it and she read it.

"The two names at the head of that one are the same as the two names at the head of this one, and that is a coincidence and it is a bad one, and nobody is going to find it for about four years and I would rather say it now.

"It is a form with five lines on it. Four ruled lines and no boxes, and a name, and a line, and a printed third line in type, and a date that is a month and not a day. That is the whole of a sheet that about nine people in this basin have signed in five years and there is not one mark on any of them that a man could point at and say he did it wrong.

"And there is a fifth line. It is a blank with two words in type over it, and a space the length of two fingers, and the space is empty, and there is a day on the wrapper of the forme it was cut in and a price chalked on the same wrapper in the same clerk's hand and no name at the top of either.

"The two words are the paper."

Nobody said anything for a moment and a boy of about nine came up the ladder with eleven words and no reason and was paid in kind and was not asked about anything, and a woman of thirty-four read out two words of type in a room about nine feet by eleven, and those were the only new words anybody in that lane has heard in five months.

"Those two words were cut out of that form nineteen years ago to save a quarter of a sheet of paper on about four hundred copies, by a woman who was twenty-four and in her first year and consulted nobody, and she was not hiding anything and she will not say whether it is right. And they went back into the form in a copy room in Bell Stair on the fourth of the fifth month of this year at ten minutes to the sixth bell, by a man who has said nothing whatever about whether it is right and has said nothing whatever since, and who put the price of the fifth line on the wrapper in chalk because there is nowhere else in this kingdom to put it.

"And the price is on that wrapper and I am going to read it, because it is in a clerk's hand and a clerk's hand is a public thing, and I have read it twice and I am not sure of one word of it, and I would rather get it wrong out loud than not read it.

"*Five lines. The fifth names the paper. The paper does not exist when the paper is signed, so the sheet is signed twice, and a person signed twice is spent, and the cost of the fifth line is that every witness in this basin is spent on the day they stand up. No form on which a person may be asked to stand up twice.*"

She put it down on the rail.

"That is the price and it is the true one and it is not paid by anybody yet, and every person in this basin who stands up on that form from today is spent, and the only instrument this business has ever had for asking somebody whether they are free is a thing that costs them the last thing they have got, and a woman of thirty-four said in the second month of this year that a form a woman is handed is a form a woman signs, and she was right, and the fifth line is that form, and it is not an improvement.

"And I want that said in a room with a rail round three sides of it, and I want it said by a person who reads papers out loud four hours a day on the days a rota says, and I want him to hear it in his own room."

---

Marek Vale was at the back by the step and had not said anything for about twenty minutes and did not say anything then, and the eleven people in the room did not turn round to look at him, and that was arranged before anybody came up the ladder by a woman who cannot write who stood at the top of it and told about eleven people so, and nobody had asked her to and she had not been thanked for it.

And a man whose word no room will take stood at the back of a room in a place that has no name on any map a clerk in this city can look at, and he read out a clerk's handwriting in another person's mouth, and about nine feet by eleven, and a rail round three sides of it made out of a bed frame and three bought brackets and one shoe-tongue of iron made in a lane, and a gap on the fourth side where the rail does not meet the wall, and a fourth chair on that side that has been empty since the first month and is a woman at a coast and is going to stay empty.

The rotation that afternoon was a practice and not a statute and nobody could compel anybody to it and there was no minute and no fund and no list of members and no name.

There are three rooms. The room by a dye house, reached by a ladder on the outside wall, with the rungs at the top of it bad. The room over a counting floor in Rainmarket, with a bench and a table and a lamp and a man who keeps it who is not on it. The room behind a man who sells candles, with a bench in it, a street door, a bin in the corner that belongs to nobody and has not been swept and has not been thrown out, and a chair nobody sits in, and a notice on the man's front door since the fifteenth of the second month that nobody may take up and nobody may take down.

And a wall, nine feet of brick, ten names, about six days, a day in chalk against every single line, no word at the top of it, and no eleventh name.

---

The other things went on, and none of them was repaired.

There are four hundred yards of new trench on the Counters' Walk cut since the twenty-sixth of the second month, and a woman of sixty-two at one end of the line and a man who keeps a key at the other, and eleven holdings on it, and the number is in the head of one person and in the column of neither, and she has not been told anything, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody may be told that a bill is coming.

A woman of forty-one is selling a hundred and forty pounds of chaff a week off a pitch with a good lease in a landlord's hand and nothing valid under it, and the pitch is not hers and is not anybody's, and she wanted the pitch and has not wanted a witness once in her life.

Three names are on a piece of paper in a good coat's hand off a man who sells candles, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be told what is on it or have it taken off, and in four years it can be produced at any of the three of them, and it will say nothing whatever about what any of them said.

A woman of twenty-two has a piece of paper in four folds in the inside of a coat and will not go up the coast road, because a room that sends a woman has a reach, and a reach is an area, and an area is a district, and a district is a line in a book in this city, and the day that line is written down there is an office and the ladder is a door of an office. The search is not finished. A page is true, and a copy is true, and the other half of it is on an address no office in this city can be shown.

---

And at about the seventh bell, at the end of the day, a door stood open.

It is a door on a lane that has no name on any map a clerk in this city can look at, and there is no name on the door, and there is no employer behind it, and no surety, and no certifying institution, and no minute, and no seal, and no stipend, and no membership list, and no area, and nothing that anybody in Bell Stair can be shown.

The room asked a question and gave the answer and stood in it. That is the whole of what it can do, and it is not a remedy, and it does not make anybody safe, and a man in a lane can still sign a thing on an ordinary morning, and a form in a good coat can still be produced at anybody in four years, and there is no office in this kingdom that can stop a man at a window with a list.

And a man with a wet sleeve is standing at the back of it who is a person whose word no room will take, and a woman who cannot write is standing at the top of a wall with a day in chalk against her line and a child's hand under her name, and a form is cut in Bell Stair with five lines on it and the fifth one says the paper, and nobody in this basin has ever said the name of this place out loud, and there is nobody to say it to.

And the door is open.
