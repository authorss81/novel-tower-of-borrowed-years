# Chapter 182 — The First Refusal of a Seat

The chandler had a back room, and the back room had a floor and a street door and a bench in it, and on the fifth of the fourth month of MR 416 he came down the lane at about the second bell and told the lane about it in the flat voice of a man offering a thing he has thought about for three days and has not been thanked for once.

"It is not a room with anything in it," he said. "There is a bench and there is a bin in the corner that is not mine and I have asked twice about the bin and nobody has told me who owns it. I have not swept it. I am not going to sweep it and I am not going to throw it out, and I would like somebody to notice that those are two decisions and I have made both of them and neither of them is about you."

Nobody noticed either decision and he went on.

"You cannot get up that ladder," he said. "There are three bad rungs at the top and there have been three bad rungs at the top since the first month and I have watched four of you go up it in a week and one of you is sixty-one. I have not asked her name and I am not going to and if one of you tells me I will not use it."

"His name is Borka," said Drazen Topic, "and she has been in this lane nine years, and her name is on the wall in chalk, and if you are not going to use it then you are in the same position as everybody else and I would like you to stay in it."

---

Nobody had sat in the back room in the three days it had existed, and the reason took about half an hour to say and it took four people to say it and every one of them was right.

A room needs a person in it. That is not a rule, it is what a room is, and a room with a floor and a door and a bench in it and nobody in it is a shed, and a shed on a street anybody can walk into is not a room and cannot be asked anything in.

And the person in it has to be the same person every time, or it is not a room, it is a queue, and everybody in this lane knows what a queue is and what a queue does to a person and what a queue does to a piece of paper.

And a person who is the same person every time in a room like that is, by the time anybody has said it out loud, a keeper.

"You have got a room," said Zvonko Maras, from the doorway, "and the only thing standing between it and being a shed is somebody's arse in a chair, and the only thing standing between somebody's arse in a chair and a keeper is a name, and the day his name is on it you have a man in a good coat's office asking who keeps the room on the Counters' Walk, and you will have to say."

"Nobody will have to say anything," said Zlata Hruska. "That is the part none of you have got to yet and it is the part that decides it. Nobody will be named. That is not a promise, it is a description. There is nothing in this room for a person to be named against, and I said in the second month of this year that a keeper is a person with a name at the top of a thing, and I am not going to be one in a lane as well as in a room, and that is still the answer and I have given it in three rooms this month."

Maras put his head on one side.

"You are not the one who is going to be in it," he said.

"I am not the one who is going to be in it."

"Then who is."

And the lane stopped, because everybody in it had been waiting since the twenty-eighth of the third month for somebody to be the one who is in it, and everybody in it had an answer for that question, and every one of the answers was a person who could not do it.

---

Margit Sova said it first and she said it as arithmetic and she was not being brave.

"I have four days of writing in this hand and then I am finished, and I have said that out loud on this lane and I am not going to take it back. If I sit in a chair in that room and anybody comes up and says a thing, and I write it down, then on the fifth day I cannot, and there is a room in this quarter with a woman in it who cannot write down what was said in it. And that room will be full by the second month and it will be full of things nobody can check."

"You would not write them down," said Zlata Hruska.

"I would want to. That is the whole of my objection and it took me three days to work out and I am putting it in the air. A woman with four days of writing in her who is asked to be the one who writes is a woman who will be asked whether the writing is right, and the day she is asked that the chair has a person in charge of the writing. I have kept a list in a box under a counter for twenty-six years and I have a column in it with nothing at the head of it and no total, and I know exactly what that box is. I am not being careful. I am frightened and I am also right."

Somebody laughed at the end of it and she did not stop, which is harder.

---

Drazen Topic said the other thing and he said it with the slate in his hand, blank side up.

"A man who counts at a rail four times a day," he said, "is the worst person in this city to put in a chair. I have been at that rail eleven years. I have counted nine hundred people in a morning. If I stand in a chair in a room with a street door then the first thing that happens is that people stop coming past the rail and start coming up the street, and then I have counted something else and it is not a count any more, it is a queue with me at the end of it, and I have seen what a queue does and I do not want to be the man who counts one.

"I will stand in a room on a day. That is on the wall and it is in my own hand and a day is against it. I will not sit in a chair anybody comes to."

---

Zvonko Maras did not offer and nobody asked him twice, and the reason he gave was one sentence and it was the reason he gave for the wall in the first month and it is not the same reason.

"I have got a roof over a coal bay and I sleep under it," he said. "If I sit in that chair then that chair is a thing I am in, and I have spent six years under a name on a sheet about a roof and I have not been asked one question about it, and I am not going to go and sit in a chair in a street and start being a person somebody asks about. I will stand. That is the whole of what I have got and I would like it recorded that it is the whole of what I have got."

---

And then it was Borka, and nobody had meant for it to be Borka, and she had been in the lane long enough that everybody had stopped organising things around her, which is the only advantage a person of sixty-one who cannot write and is in no book has ever had.

She came up the lane from the end where the wall is, at about the third bell, and she was not asked anything, and she said it anyway, and it took her four seconds and it was the shortest thing anybody said in that lane in a month.

"No," she said.

"Have you understood what they are asking you," said Zlata Hruska. "I am going to ask you that and then I am going to leave it alone for as long as you want it left alone."

"I have understood it and no."

And then she stood in the middle of the lane while a good many people looked at a woman of about sixty-one who had been in that lane since before it was a lane, and she waited, and nobody told her to go on, and she went on when she was ready and not before, which is a thing you only learn from people who have been talked over.

"I cannot write," she said. "That is all of it and I have been not saying it for four days.

"Everybody in this lane has said a reason and every one of your reasons is about the paper. Sova's is about the writing running out. The man at the rail is about being a queue. The man with the coal cart is about a roof. All of you are frightened of a piece of paper, and the piece of paper has not arrived yet, and you are all refusing a chair on its account.

"I cannot write, and that is a thing I have, and it is the only thing I have, and I am not giving it to a chair in a street. If I sit in that chair and a woman comes up and says a thing, and I take it, and I hold it, then I am the paper. There is no other shape for it. A room that cannot write anything down has to have somebody in it who is not writing, and the somebody has to be a person, and the person has to be the same one, and the day the same one is the same one for four months then that person is a piece of paper in a room, and she cannot read herself back, and nobody in the lane can read it for her.

"And then one day somebody comes up that street with a list in a good coat, and he does not have to be cruel, and he does not even have to be in a hurry. He asks the woman in the chair whether a thing was said in that room on a day, and she has to say yes or no, and either answer is a document about a person, and there is nothing whatever in the world that can take it back.

"I have never been in a book in my life. I have been in this lane nine years and in this place before there was a lane, and I have been on a wall once in chalk by a child of about nine and I have never asked him to take it off and I would not take it off if I could ask. That is all I have and I am not being brave and I am not being clever and I am not going to be one of the reasons somebody gives a talk in a room about a woman who cannot write."

She stopped, and looked at the ground, and picked a thread off her sleeve.

"You do not need me in it," she said. "You need a room. A room does not need a person in it to be a room. A room needs somebody in it who is not keeping anything."

---

Nobody said anything for a while, and the bin sat in the corner of the back room where the chandler had said he was not going to sweep it and was not going to throw out, and the four women who carried it up the lane on their backs were not there and were not needed.

"Then the chair stays empty," said Zlata Hruska.

"That is the first time in nine years I have heard anybody say that about a chair," said Borka, "and I have been in nine years' worth of rooms."

"It is the fourth chair in this business that is empty," said Margit Sova, "and I am not going to be the one who counts them out loud in a lane, and I have said the arithmetic in my own head twice this morning and it is a number about a room and a room is not a district and that is the whole difference between me and a man with a list."

Nobody thanked her for that either.

---

The chandler stood in the street door with his arms folded for about an hour while they worked it out, and at about the fifth bell he said the thing he had come down the lane to say, and he said it without any of them having asked.

"I want one thing out of it and I am going to say it now so that nobody can say afterwards that I did not say it in the open," he said. "I have got a notice on my front door. It is in the ordinary language of a form and it says the district is not collecting and it does not say why, and it has been on there since the fifteenth of the second month, and it is on a door people come to when they have nowhere to put a thing, and I would like it off my door."

He waited.

"Can it come off," said Zlata Hruska.

"No," said the chandler. "I have been to a counter with a person on the sixth of the third month and a man in a good coat told me it is a district notice and it is not mine to take down and it is not the district's to take up, and he was correct, and I have thought about that for a month. Nobody may take that notice off that door. Not him, not you, not a settlement marshal, not me, not anybody, and there is no form on which anybody may be asked to take it up or not to.

"And that is not what I am asking for. I am asking for a room where a person comes up a street and says a thing out loud to somebody who is not going to write it down, and then goes away, and that the bin in the corner stays where it is."

He looked at the corner.

"I do not know who that bin belongs to," he said, "and it is not mine, and I have asked twice and nobody told me and I have decided that I am going to keep not knowing, and that decision is going to cost me something at some point and I have made it anyway, and I am telling you that now because I am fifty-eight and I would rather be asked for something than be left standing in a street door."

---

So the room at the bottom of the lane is a room with a bench in it and a bin in the corner that belongs to nobody and a chair that nobody sits in, and the door is on a street that has no name on it, and there is no form for the room and no minute for it and no name at the top of it, and on the morning of the sixth of the fourth month of MR 416 a woman of about thirty-four stood in it and said out loud, in front of nine people, what the room was and was not, and then went up a ladder to a different room and said the same thing in a different set of words, and nobody thanked her, and the two rooms are two rooms and neither of them is the other one and there is no list of them anywhere.

And a woman of about sixty-one who cannot write went down the lane to the end where the wall is, and stopped at it, and did not ask a child of about nine to rub anything off, and there is a day in chalk against ten names and about six days and no word at the top of it and no eleventh name and nobody has ever asked for one.
