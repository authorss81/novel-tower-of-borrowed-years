# Chapter 46 — Fourteen Places

The board sat in the Gate yard on the seventeenth at the sixth bell, because a board that cannot serve a ward in a room has to be taken to the ward, and a yard is not a room and everybody at that table knew it and said so before anybody else could.

Harrow had it on two trestle tables carried down the hill at the fourth bell by four men the Chamber had supplied, because the Chamber is a party and parties carry furniture. Grale's frame had come off the wall of the levy office for the first time in nineteen years and stood against the tank facing the yard, and Grale had not carried it and had asked that somebody else did, and nobody had asked him why.

There were four hundred and six people in the yard and in the lane behind them, and the rain was not doing anything, and the Gate tank was full, and somebody at the back said the word *full* out loud and about thirty people turned round to look at the tank as though it had personally done it.

"Before the Chair begins," said Renata Vole, standing. "This office certified a settlement on the fifteenth of the rains and did not strike it and is not striking it now. The finding stands. The cause is entered. The collection was unblocked on the twelfth of this month and has been held four days by a term invented in a yard by a Crown March representative, and I did not object to that term because I could not find a reason better than the reason to agree, and I have said that once already and I am saying it again here, because about four hundred people are standing in front of me and not one of them has heard me say it in the open.

"I have said in a room that the ward drank four hundred and forty against a lawful hundred and sixty and that the excess is a lawful settlement and stands. That is still true, the ward is still going to pay, and the bill is not going away because of anything that happens at this table. I have not been wrong about that and I am not going to pretend in a yard that I have."

She sat down. Nobody told Renata Vole she had been right, and she had not asked, and the yard worked out inside about nine seconds that this was a thing about yards and not about offices.

---

The examiner was at the second seat with a sheet, and she was not a member of anything and did not vote, and the finding of the seventeenth is where she is on the page.

"Chair," said Neris Kade, before the first thing was taken. "The board's third term. A person may decline to answer and the declining is written down as a declining and no inference is drawn. I have watched a woman decline three things in two days and I would like the Chair to read the three declines, in her words, in front of four hundred and six people, because a term that is going to survive this month will survive by being read out loud once and not by being true."

Harrow read them. Three lines, in a hand of his own, in the first person, in the words of the households: *we are not being difficult, we are being somewhere*; *we will take the work, we do not want the days*; and *I am not saying no to the work, I am saying no to the days, and I am saying it because you cannot ask a woman for a day out of an account that has already taken her child.*

"Term four as well," said Neris Kade. "Captain Kern's grounds are in the finding in his words and in the Chair's hand and he has not been asked to pay for them, and I would like it noticed that a term invented in a room has now been used to stop the Chair and to hold three refusals in a yard, and that I cannot vote on any of it, and that I have been in the second seat since the thirteenth for the reason the Chair gave then, which was that a person who puts the bodies in has to be in the room."

"You are," said Harrow. "You have been all of it. I am not going to thank you for it in a yard, because a board that thanks its own non-members in public is a board that has started confusing itself with a favour, and you would be the first person to tell me so."

"I would," said Neris Kade, "and I did not expect it to, and that is the entire of what I have learned this month and I have written it nowhere."

---

The finding was made in the open, in sentences, one at a time, over two hours, and nobody held a page.

The first was about the entry. The board could not strike anything out of a desk's book and had said so on the ninth. What it could do was publish a correction. The correction was going on the public register in Bell Stair, and in the margin of the entry of the ninth of the rains in the water office's own book there was going to be written *struck from the public copy by the emergency appeals board, seventeenth of next month, and not replaced* — and the words *and not replaced* had been put there by a supervisor of the settlement desk in a corridor at the fifth bell on the evening of the sixteenth, on his way out of a room he had been made to leave by two men, and the Deputy had agreed them inside a minute and neither of them had made anything of it, and Grale wrote the marginal note in about four seconds without being asked to think about it.

"That is the whole of the institution," said Harrow. "The entry stays in the book and everybody can go and look at it, and on the next page there is a line in a district accountant's hand saying it has been struck and that nothing has been put in its place. In nine months somebody will ask what is in that place, and the answer is going to be on the page, in a hand, with a date."

The second was the board itself. The emergency appeals board was confirmed to continue and its four terms were confirmed with it. Harrow did not read them out. He asked each of the four by name whether they stood, and each said *stands* in the yard, and Zorka Vendl said it in the first person and asked that it be noticed she had been asked twice to take money for a sitting and had declined twice and was still in the room and was still declining.

Then he stopped, looked at Aldis Kern, and said, "Captain, I have a term in front of me that I wrote in a room on the ninth and I am about to use it against myself, and I would like the yard to hear it happen rather than hear about it."

---

"Term four," said Bel Harrow. "A member who does not concur with a finding may have the grounds of the non-concurrence written down in their own words, in the finding, and kept with it. The duty to write them down is the board's. It is not the member's, and the member may not be asked to pay for it, and no member's grounds may be left out of the finding because the board does not agree with them.

"I wrote that in about four minutes on the ninth because I was frightened of a document surviving four people. I would like the yard to know that the Chair is now in the minority on his own instrument, in his own yard, and that he has to write it down himself because there is no clerk, and that if the Deputy's office had won this on the ninth then this finding would be one document saying that five people agreed, and that in nine years one of us will be gone and the finding will have been the only account of the day."

He put the pen on the trestle table.

"Captain Kern. The bill is to go onto four hundred and six named people as supervised work at the ordinary wage, and a household that will not do it is not to have another household's share put on it. Say your grounds."

Kern stood up in the yard, and it took him a while.

"I will not have it, and I want it written that I said no. I am going to say why in about a minute and then the board can do what it likes, and I will carry the finding out on a board in this yard afterwards and I will not argue with it.

"The yard asked at forty-one doors. That is the consent there is. Twenty-nine yes, three no, the nine who were not at home, and the hundred and sixty-odd nobody has been to, because forty-one doors is what four hundred people could do in three days with a stick of charcoal and a bit of string, and every one of them knew it at the time and said *we have not asked everybody* more than once at the tank.

"This board is going to put a levy on four hundred and six names out of twenty-nine answers, and I told this board in a room that I would not have that treated as a formality, and I have been heard, and I am still saying it, and I am not going to be talked out of it in a yard by a man in a good coat with a bad hand.

"So. One. That the finding names a thing the ward has not agreed to. Two. That the only way to make it true is for the yard to go out again, properly, with a schedule on a board and the households told what is on it. And I do not know how many days that is and neither does anybody at this table. That is a real objection and not a procedural one, and I will not have it written as a procedural one."

He sat down.

Harrow wrote it down. He wrote for about four minutes in the open, with four hundred and six people watching a man from Crown March put a ward captain's objection into a finding in longhand because there was no clerk. He read it back and Aldis Kern said *that is my words* and Harrow said *it is your words*, and then said, to the yard:

"That is the term working. I invented it in a room and it has just been used to keep me from doing a thing I wanted to do in about ninety seconds, and I would like it understood that the Chair thinks that is exactly what it is for, and that if it only ever came out in my favour it would not be worth the nine minutes it took to write."

---

The third thing was the money, and it took an hour, and about two hundred of the four hundred and six stayed for it.

The bill is two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints at the ordinary wage, and there is no longer any argument in the yard or in the room about what shape it takes, because the Chair proposed the shape on the afternoon of the sixteenth and it went into a yard then and it is a yard's business now and not a board's. What was still open on the seventeenth was the sentence itself, and Kern took it apart in public before the board had finished writing it.

"That is a minute, not a finding," he said, from the step, to about four hundred people. "The Chair wrote a proposal in a room on Thursday and read it out in a yard on Friday, and I have been on the receiving end of both, and a thing that a man thought of in a room on Thursday is not the same object as a thing that four hundred people agreed to on Friday. If the board writes what the Chair wrote, then the board has not decided anything and the yard has, and the yard did not have a meeting about it."

Harrow let that stand, and the finding was rewritten around it over the following hour: **not to be paid in days drawn out of the district account, because the Deputy put that reason in a room on the sixteenth in her own mouth; not to be levied on four hundred and six people who have not been asked, because the yard asked at forty-one doors and got twenty-nine answers; and therefore to be paid in supervised work, named, in the ward's own yards, on a schedule written out and hung on the board at the Gate tank where anybody can read it, at the ordinary wage, with every household asked again one door at a time in the yard's own way, and with the days the finding gives them to do it in.**

And it is to be paid out of the reserve carry.

That sentence was the one the yard argued about, and not about whether it was lawful, because nobody at that table knew. The argument was about what happens to a line in an account when it is drawn on for the first time in three hundred years.

"It is not money that has been sitting in a box," said Grale, from behind the frame, to nobody in particular and then, when four hundred people turned round, to all of them. "Forty thousand nine hundred has been entered against the water office every year since the flood years and never applied, because a carry is a line the office has entered against itself and cannot name a district to apply. It is not a fund. It is a hole with a number on it, and this board has just written the word *Tallow* beside it, and now the hole is a debt, and in about nine months this yard will be back at this tank and the office will want it back and there will be nothing on the line to give it.

"I am not saying no. I am saying that every person in this yard who has been told the ward is off is going to be told in about nine months that the ward is on, and I will be the one holding the account, and I am fifty-one and I have a bad chest, and I would like somebody to write that down as well and not as a reason."

Nobody wrote it down. Harrow wrote that the district accountant had said it, and that the Chair had read it back, and that the accountant had said *that is my words*.

And Renata Vole said, standing, that a carry applied for the first time in three hundred years was a thing her office would have to certify within the season or explain to a ratepayer, and that she had signed that line forward once a year for nine years without once asking what it was, and that if a board can name a district then a board can un-name it, and that she was not going to pretend in front of four hundred people that she knew which of those was going to happen.

---

The fourth thing was the school, and the fifth was a sentence a schoolmistress had been saying in a yard for eight days, and they were taken together because they are the same thing.

Pella Rusk stood up on the step of the tank and said the number once, in the open, in front of the people it is about.

"Fourteen places, against ninety-one children, and I am not going to soften it, and I am not going to thank anybody for it, because the number has not changed this week and the board has not put one child back in my room. I would like that said in the yard and not in a room, because the room is where people are polite.

"And this is what I came for. I have said a sentence in a yard for eight days and I said it was not a procedure, and somebody has finally been angry enough to write it down, and I would like to hear it read and then I am finished."

Harrow read it, because he had put it in the finding himself and the finding was in his hand, and it is in the record of the seventeenth of next month in those words: *A thing that cannot be given a copy is a thing that can be changed with nobody present.*

And then the answer to it, which took four minutes and was not a name and not a date and not a story about anybody, and which is the whole of what a month of this did to the city:

"Any order by which a district account is held to its presented figure shall be read, in the words of the office, to every party it names, before it is entered. It shall not be entered at the close of a month. It shall carry the day and not the month. And it shall carry the name of the officer who enters it. The office is permitted one such order and is not permitted a second, and no party may be given a copy, and that stands, and it is not the copy that makes it dangerous. It is that nobody was in the room."

"And who enters it," said Rusk.

"The district accountant," said Harrow, "because that is the office, and the office is one person, and I have looked, and it is not the Deputy and it is not the supervisor of the settlement desk, and the supervisor of the settlement desk is in a cell in Crown March at this moment."

Somebody at the back of the yard said *Grale*.

"No," said Grale. "It is not me either, because I have entered four of them in nineteen years and I did not put my name on one of them, and I have put my name on this one."

He signed it on the trestle table with a district accountant's pen, in front of four hundred and six people, on a piece of paper the water office is permitted to have, and his hand went bad on the last letter and he had to do it twice, and nobody helped him, and about eleven people in the yard understood what was happening and about three hundred and ninety-five did not.

And the finding carried one more line about the school, and Aldis Kern read it out, because he had asked to read the finding's fourth clause in a yard on the thirtieth of the rains and had been refused then and had not forgotten.

"The ward's school is named in this order as a party. The parties are read to before an order is entered. The order names it. That is the whole of the protection and I am obliged to say that it is not a sum and it is not a place. It is the first time in nine years that anybody cutting a line off that account has had to say out loud, in front of the ward, which line they are cutting, and in about nine months the school is going to find out before the north gardens' crew does, and the north gardens' crew is ninth on that page and my school is last, and a yard which knows a line is coming will spend the quarter before it lands.

"My school is worth fourteen places and it has been worth fourteen places since before I came here, and this week it is still worth fourteen places, and Wren Ostrow is still off my roll, and the four children off since the twenty-fourth are still off my roll, and nobody in this yard has put one of them back. I want that said at this table and in this yard and I do not want it said kindly."

Nadja Ostrow said it. She came up out of the yard to the step with nothing prepared, and what she said was four sentences, and she said them to about four hundred people.

"I asked for the days to be refused and they have been refused and nobody is going to offer me days again in any month. That is what I wanted and I have got it, and I want to be careful about the shape of what I have got, because it is the shape of a thing that is gone and not the shape of a seat coming back.

"My daughter is off the roll. She is still off the roll. I have been cooking for two hundred children for a month and I have got a wage coming out of a line nobody has ever drawn, and that line is going to have a name on it by the end of the season, and in nine months somebody is going to be standing at this tank telling me I have had a gift.

"I have not had a gift. I have had a bill that changed instruments, and I know exactly what it cost, because I was the one who made them put it in the open. And I am not going to stand in this yard and thank a man who is in a cell, and I am not going to thank the Crown March, and the woman from the Examiner's office read this finding out in the rain on the eighteenth morning to about two hundred of us and did it properly, and I did not thank her then and I am not going to now, and I want that in the yard's memory and not in a document, because I know what a document is this week."

And the yard, which had been extremely good for two hours, was extremely good for about four seconds, in the way that yards are, and then somebody at the back said that the lamps were lit along the Tallow Gate and could somebody be told, and the evening of the seventeenth of next month went on being an evening.
