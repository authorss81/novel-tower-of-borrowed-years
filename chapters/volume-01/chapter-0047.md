# Chapter 47 — Both, in One Paragraph

The account of the Third Sluice was closed on the twentieth of next month at the second bell, in the second-floor hearing room, which was built for thirty and had nine in it, and one of the nine was a man who had been in custody for three days and had asked to be present and had been brought.

The notice named a piece of apparatus and not a person, as it had on the twenty-eighth of the rains. *The Third Sluice, ward line, at the low kitchens, and the maintenance account of the Crown Terrace intake above it, from the second day of the rains.* The party was the senior Lantern Warden, Ilya Senn. A Warden may be a party to a maintenance account. A Warden may not be a party to a settlement, because a sluice is not a loan, and the hearing officer said so in procedure in one sentence, in the voice of a man reading a chair's rule, and the room went on.

"Nobody in this room is sworn," said Bel Harrow, at the top, because he said it at the top of everything and because the room he was in on the seventeenth had established it and he was not going to have nine people spend a morning working out from scratch that the ward cannot be sworn and therefore the room cannot swear anybody. "There is no form in this city on which a warden is sworn. A ward is a district. A district cannot speak. A person who cannot be sworn cannot be sworn to anything, and I am not going to have this table do by hand what the city cannot do by form.

"So everything said in this room today is said by a person, in their own name, on their own liability, and a person may answer or may decline to answer, and the declining is written down as a declining and nothing is made of it. And a statement a person makes in this room can be asked for on a sheet, and if it is given on a sheet it is kept with the finding, and the finding goes on a nail in a yard, and that is the whole of the weight this room has."

---

"What I want," said Aldis Kern, "is to write it myself, and I want it sworn, and I am going to be honest with the room about the second of those things, which is that I cannot be sworn, because a ward cannot be sworn, and I have found out in the last three days that this is a ridiculous thing to be the reason for.

"My wife is not in this room and does not come to these. She has a word for it and I am going to use her word, because I have been in three rooms this week where the whole of what happened to Tallow Ward was that nobody could be asked to say anything under anything, and I am tired of it, and if a Crown March representative will put the word *sworn* at the top of a piece of paper with my name under it in a yard in front of the people it is about then I will have it sworn and nobody need ever know it is not."

Harrow looked at Renata Vole, who was not in the room and had sent a message instead, and the message said that the Deputy's office had no objection to a word on a sheet and no opinion about it.

"Then it is sworn," said Bel Harrow, "and I am going to tell you, Captain, that I have written a word on a sheet in a yard that is not true, and that I have done it because the alternative is that the only account of the fourteenth of the rains that anybody in Morrowfen will ever read is written by a man who was not in it, and that this room is going to be wrong about a small thing in public so that it can be right about a large thing in a record. Write your sheet."

Kern wrote it at the trestle table in the second-floor room with a carpenter's pencil, because he could not hold a pen, and it took him most of an hour, and Harrow did not hurry him, and Vendl read it out afterwards to the room because he asked her to and because a sheet a man cannot read is not much of a record.

The first item on it was not the sluice.

"Vasil Ferun, thirteen, of the Underbank, who went into a cellar on the night of the fourteenth for a neighbour's goods and came out with a shin in two pieces. I have put the boy's own fault on this sheet as well as the water, because an account that leaves out the boy's fault is a handbill. The Underbank is item one. It has been item one on every sheet in this yard for six weeks and it is going to be item one on whatever else gets written down about that night."

Item two was Milan Vray's storehouse and the condemned tar in the cellar, valued three ways by three people and not at all by anybody with a seal. Item three was the hundred and forty people who had carried water off the basin by hand for twelve days and whose names had never been taken by anybody, including Ilya Senn. Item four was the tank, the standpipe, and the two hundred children.

Item five was a sentence: *The ward will pay for the water it took at the ordinary wage. The ward does not name the man who held the valve. The ward does not forgive anybody and the ward does not blame anybody, and neither of those is a decision, it is a fact about a yard.*

And then a sixth item, added afterwards in the same pencil: *This sheet was sworn at the second bell of the twentieth of next month and the word is not true and the sheet is mine.*

---

Ilya Senn stood up at the back of the room, where he had been for an hour with his hand flat on the coat and four letters in it, and asked to say one thing and not to be asked a question.

"You have heard a sheet," he said. "I have heard it and it is right and it is the only account of the fourteenth of the rains I have ever heard that is not a handbill, and I am not going to say thank you for it, because a man who wrote the Underbank first is not being kind to me, he is being accurate, and there is a difference and this yard knows it.

"Three corrections, and the first is a small one and the second and third are not.

"The first. It will be in the finding that I held the valve from the second to the twenty-fourth against a sealed order of the seventh, and that is two breaches and not one. Seven days against the order. Twelve days of my own authority, which is the worse one, because a man who holds a valve open against a written order is disobeying and a man who holds one open with no order at all is deciding, and I have been deciding since the second, and I would like the room to have that in the worst place it will go.

"The second. I am not the first name on that sheet and I want the finding to say so, and it is not modesty. Every account of that night for six weeks has had my name at the top of it and a boy's name in the middle of it, and the boy's name is on the sheet the Captain has just written, at the top, because he put it there and because he is right. A thing that has no witness on it is a thing the room believes the person standing in front of it about. That is what the eleven blank cells on the schedule mean. And I have been standing in front of rooms for twenty-two years, and this office has believed me about the water every hour since the second, and I have never been wrong about the water, and I have been wrong about everything else on that platform, and the day the finding gives the room a reason to stop believing me is the day a sluice is shut on a rising river with nobody holding a lamp."

"The third," he said, "is the one I would like the board to write down, and I am aware it is none of my business and I am saying it anyway, because a Warden's competence taught me a long time ago that I should make the terrible decision before anybody else can get to it, and this is the last time I am going to be in a room with a door in it.

"The account of that valve was heard on the twenty-eighth of the rains and it is open, and the terms this office offered me lapsed at the fourth bell of the fourth of this month and were not re-offered, and I asked twice and was told twice and the second time it was in a corridor. That was correct and I want it said. And the account is going to be read at the year end against whoever is standing in the room, and a year-end reading is a finding, and a finding can be made against a person as well as a plant, and there are four of those in this year in this office and one of them took a hand.

"Four days ago this board named a district against a carry that has been sitting unapplied for three hundred years, and protected a line at the bottom of a page, and put a word *sworn* on a piece of paper that is not sworn. So this finding is not being made in the same city the account was opened in, and I would like somebody in this room to say out loud that the year-end reading of an open account is now a question about a board and not a question about a man, and that if it is not, then somebody in this room is going to be very pleasantly surprised in about eleven months, and it will not be me."

Nobody in that room had thought of it. Marek Vale, who was at the back of a room built for thirty with a coat on and no standing, watched three of the five people at a table work it out at about the same moment and in the same order, and it was Renata Vole's office that got there first, which was what he had expected and what he had been dreading for a month.

---

The offer was made for the second time in the record, and the room had been built for thirty, and there were nine in it.

"A yard and a half a year," said Ilya Senn, "twenty-two years, and this hand. I put it in this file on the twenty-eighth of the rains as an offer made and not accepted, and it is on the record, and I am putting it there again because a man who has held a valve open for twenty-two days ought to be the first name on any account of it, and the only reason my name is not at the top of the Captain's sheet is that a yard is not a person and cannot keep a roll with anybody's name on it.

"I am not going to have it read in a year as a man who gave something. It is not a sacrifice. It is an offer, and it was refused, and it is going to be refused again, and I would like the finding to say *an offer made and not accepted* for the second time, because that sentence has been sitting in this file for twenty-three days and somebody put it there carefully and I would like it to be there twice."

"It will be twice," said Harrow, and wrote it, and Hesper Rann, who had asked to be present and was sitting against the wall in a coat he had not been in for three days, said nothing at all.

"It is not going to be accepted," said Nadja Ostrow, from a chair by the door, "and the Captain is going to tell you the same thing and tell it better, and I would rather you heard it from the Captain because I have said it once in a yard and I did not enjoy it and I am not going to say it twice for anybody."

"The ward pays for it and does not thank you for it," said Aldis Kern, "and those are two sentences and they are going to stay two sentences, and Captain Kern is a person and I am a person and this yard is a person and none of the three of us can be thanked and none of the three of us wants to be, and that is the answer and it is the only one there is going to be."

Then the Chair asked him to read the finding's paragraph, because the Chair had written it in ten words and it was wrong and he knew it was wrong and he wanted a man who was not a Crown March representative to tell him so.

"It is one paragraph," said Bel Harrow, "and it is the only thing in three days that I have wanted anybody to stop me writing, and I am reading it, and I have had it wrong since Tuesday, and I would like it said whether it is right.

"*From the second to the twenty-fourth day of the rains the party held open the Third Sluice, ward line, without authority against a sealed order of the seventh, and the supply so made reached two hundred and some hundreds of pints a night to the low kitchens and to the yards of a ward of about four hundred people, of whom two hundred at the low kitchens were children; and the water so supplied was not lawfully the ward's, and the ward has paid and will pay for it at the ordinary wage, and the party is a competent keeper of water and has never been wrong about it; and on the fourteenth the same breach put nine feet of water into the Underbank, where a boy of thirteen was hurt in the leg, and drowned the lower floor of a condemned storehouse of a rope-maker in the Tanner's yard, and the party has offered the whole of his wages for twenty-two years and this hand, and the offer was made twice and refused twice; and the office takes the second half of this paragraph to be the whole of the damage and not the first half, and enters the make-good as a charge against the district account, which will be cut at the close of this month in the ordinary course by an order that names its parties, and its author, and the day, and the office records that this is the first such order that has ever named them, and that the party did not ask for it and had no standing to ask for it.*"

"That is four sentences longer than it needs to be," said Ilya Senn.

"It is."

"It is correct."

"It is correct and it is one paragraph, and the room can take it or leave it, and I am going to say that I have never in nineteen years of being a clerk of one kind or another written a sentence that was the whole of an account of anything, and that a man in a good coat is not a man who knows how to do that, and that I got it out of a yard in about an hour on Tuesday and the yard did the rest."

The account was closed on a finding of fact with no penalty and no settlement. The finding was read in the Gate yard on the twenty-first by Zorka Vendl, who is a secretary and not a clerk and who has never read anything aloud in a yard in her life and who did it properly, and about a hundred and forty people stood in the rain for it and nobody thanked her either, because the yard had decided by the eighteenth of next month that thanking people for reading things out had gone out of fashion in Tallow Ward and nobody could say who had started it.

And on the twenty-first at the fourth bell a senior Lantern Warden went back up onto the platform of the Third Sluice with a lamp hooked to the rail and stood beside a shut valve doing nothing, at ease, and put his hand on the wheel out of pure habit every twenty minutes or so, and it did nothing, because a shut valve has nothing to do with a man at the last of the light, and the town main above it was still thinner than the yard had been told, and the Ropewalk standpipe was short again by the Saturday, and they boiled on the ration, and nobody thanked anybody, and that was correct.
