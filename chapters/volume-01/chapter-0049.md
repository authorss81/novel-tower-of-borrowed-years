# Chapter 49 — Four Rooms

The Fund's room was eleven feet by nine over a chandler's shop and it took four people and a chest, and Zorka Vendl had the discharge book open on a board before anybody came in, because she had been in the room since the first bell and she is a woman who is not comfortable with a room when there is nothing in the room.

Ondra Kelb came with his mother and sat down and put a hand-book on the table and then took his hand off it, and the hand-book is the fourth one Vendl has bought out of the Fund in nineteen years, and it does what it is advertised to do, and it is on the wrong hand, and both of those facts are the Fund's and neither of them is the Fund's fault.

"In nineteen years," said Vendl, "the Fund has paid a clinic, a cot, medicine, and a hand-book. That is the whole of what the Fund does, and the rule that says it is in the second leaf and it says the Fund pays such claims upon the injury of a clerk as the Fund has money for, and money is the reason I have said no twice to paying a recorder, and I am not going to be asked about it again this season.

"This year there is a finding. A finding is a different thing from a claim and I have been trying for three days to work out how to pay out of one, and I have got it, and it is not clever.

"The Fund will pay the clinic for the remainder of the year, the cot, the medicine, and the maker on Ropewalk by name, which is a rule this office adopted on the twentieth of the rains on the say-so of a man who has since been arrested, and I would like that entered because it was correct and it cost him nothing. The Chamber will pay a clerk's injury at its rate and has told me in writing that it will come out of a levy on the hall and not out of the district account, which took nine years to arrange, and which is the only good thing in this account.

"And there is a sum. It is in the finding of the seventeenth and it came out of a carry that has not been drawn on in three hundred years, and it is a sum of money, and it is the only part of the year that is new."

"How much," said Milena Kelb.

"Enough," said Zorka Vendl, "and I am going to say four things about *enough* in the first person.

"It buys a cot and a clinic and a maker and a rate, and the rate is at the ordinary figure for a junior clerk in his fourth week, and the figure was set in the flood years and it is about half what a man at a counter-desk earns in a good month, and I know that and I have written it in the margin of the fund's own book where nobody looks.

"It does not buy a hand. He will lift a cup at sixty. He will not hold a pen again, and the apparatus on the table is a good apparatus, and the maker on Ropewalk is the best in the city and she charges by the finger and she is not a fool.

"It does not buy the years, and there is no sum in Morrowfen that buys the years, and the physician has said so in writing and I have the letter, and I am not going to read it out because it will not help him and I have read it four times.

"And the fourth is that I do not know what the fourth thing is, and I have wanted to say that in this room for three days, and I am sixty-one and I have kept this book for nineteen years and there are four marks in it that mean a clerk never came back to the hall, and I have been asked twice to find one of them and could not, and I am not going to open that in a room with a mother in it."

She opened the discharge book, looked at the page, and shut it.

Kelb did not say thank you. He put his hand on the table, the wrong hand, and turned it over, and looked at the fine pale writing on it that was a nine-year-old's and was better than the hand-book and was not his, and he said:

"My mother wants to know whether the finding says the right name. Ondra Kelb, junior counter-clerk, in his fourth week. Not *careless*. Not *the clerk at the counter-desk*."

"The finding says *Ondra Kelb, junior counter-clerk, of the counter-desk of this office, who countersigned the totals on the ninth of the rains with a pencil, correctly*," said Zorka Vendl, "and the word *careless* is in it, because it is in the certification of cause of the twelfth of next month and that document is in the record and cannot be taken out of the record, and the finding says beside it that the word is not supported by any evidence before the board and that no witness said it. Both. On one page. In that order."

He nodded and did not say anything else, and his mother put her hand on his good arm, and Vendl said — to the room, not to either of them, and in the first person — that she had been a secretary for nineteen years and had never once written a word into a finding and did not know how, and that she was not going to learn in a morning, and that the Fund's contribution to the finding of the seventeenth of next month was a sum and nothing else.

Crown March, a room with one window and a table, the third of next month, and a man in custody who had asked for a hearing and been given one because he had offered four dates.

He gave them in the order they came, and the four dates are not in this record because three of them are the office's and the fourth is not, and a man in custody is entitled to one thing from a room, and it is that his list is read before it is discussed.

"You will ask me about the gate," said Hesper Rann, "and I am going to tell you that I have not changed a word of it since the twenty-fifth of the rains and I am not going to change it for you, and that if I had it again I would write it in a better hand, and that is a thing I said to a clerk in a room in the rains and it is the only sentence I have said in a month that I would say again.

"What I can give you is four dates on which I made an entry in the account desk at Bell Stair that was not a levy, a schedule, a posting or a return, and there is no box for the fourth kind and I used the box for the third kind.

"They are the third of the flood years, the eleventh of the flood years, the nineteenth of the flood years, and the twenty-second of the rains."

Grale, who had been sent for and had not wanted to come, stood up at the fourth date.

"I have known about three of those for two years and I have never been asked and I have never said, and I am not going to be asked now, because the two of us are the only people in this room who would know what the fourth one is, and if I say the date out loud in a room in Crown March then it is in a record in Crown March, and there is a line in that account that has been standing since before the flood years and it is not mine and it was not entered by me and I have not been able to find out who enters it, and I have been a district accountant for eleven years.

"The day I prove it, I have to stop. I said that in a yard on the tenth of this month and I am not going to unsay it in a room. You will have the three."

Hesper Rann looked at him. "You have had a horror of it for two years and you have said nothing and you have gone on cutting the order four times, and I am not going to moralise at you in a cell, and I would like it minuted that I have not."

"It is minuted," said the officer, "and Mr Grale has said the Chair's words, and I am obliged to say that the Chair has not said them and I am obliged to say it, and the Chair does not object."

The notice was on the Deputy Steward's desk on the fourth of next month and it had been signed by a man three floors up whom Renata Vole had met twice, and it moved her to the coastal returns, which is nine years at a desk facing a window with an audit of what the city had paid out over four hundred years, and it took effect on the first of the month after, which was a thing nobody had arranged on purpose.

"Two things," said Renata Vole, to the room, "and I am going to say both of them in the first person, and then you may go.

"One. I do not accept the characterisation in the notice. I do not accept that the position of Deputy Settlement Steward was held improperly, and I do not accept that the finding of the seventeenth of next month contains an error, because it does not, and I have read it four times in four days looking for one so that I could say so here with a document.

"And I do not concede that this ward was under water because of anything I did or did not do, or that the Tallow file was certified lawfully when it was not, or that the levy looks deserved. Two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints at the ordinary wage against four named households is not deserved, and I have said so in a yard on the seventeenth of next month in the first person, and I said it because a board had made me, and I would like that noted, because in about four years the only version of this month will be a version in which I was argued out of my own opinion.

"Two. And this is the one I have thought about for three days. Four terms were agreed in a room on the ninth of next month and one of them is a member's grounds written in their own words in a finding, and the duty to write them is the board's, and I refused it twice, on the record, in the same words.

"The term is now the term. It is in the finding of the seventeenth. And since the seventeenth this office has had to record the grounds of a member of the board twice, in a board's finding, in that member's own words, unasked and unpaid, because a Crown March representative took a sentence off the back of a complaint sheet in a yard and put it in a room and four people voted for it three to one, and I was the one vote against. In one case the grounds were that the finding was soft on a man. In the other case the grounds were that the finding was hard. Both are in the record and both are in my building and this office did them, and I did not ask for either.

"I did not concede that I was wrong," said Renata Vole. "I have never conceded that. I want it in whatever gets written about me. But that term is the only thing this month produced that a person in an office can use without asking anybody's permission, and it is in my building, and it was put there by a man I refused for nine years on a point of form, and I do not know what to do with that and I have not decided."

She picked up the notice and gave it back.

"Do not write to me about the yard," she said. "I have read what is in the finding and the yard's account is in it and it is in your words and not mine, and I would rather you left it there."

The sealed notice was nailed to the Tally Hall door on the evening of the fourth of next month, and it was four lines, and it was sealed, and the seal was the Trust's, and the standing order of the Trust on the sealed founding page says that no clerk may copy the page and no clerk may erase the signature, and does not say that a clerk may not know what a notice on a door says.

*The public copy of the first page of the oldest reference volume is to be replaced.*

*In the public copy the founding hand is not to be reproduced.*

*The original is not to be removed from the case.*

*This is to be carried out by the officer of the record and by no other hand.*

The duty clerk on the posting desk read it out twice because her practice is to read a notice out twice, and about thirty people were on the steps, and nobody would say afterwards that they had read it, and the woman at the chandler's shop two doors down said to a man in a lane that she had not read it, and a man on the third floor of the fund building said to his sister that he had not seen it, and a young examiner who was not a member of anything and had a sheet in her bag said to nobody in particular that she had not been in the building since the second.

Nim Rook, who was in the lane and who is a night courier and who carried anything at any hour for nothing, read it standing in the rain and said it out loud twice, to a lamp post, because the yard's rule about a number is a number in a room, and a notice on a door is not a number in a room, and the yard had found that out on the seventeenth of next month and had not found it out for the benefit of anybody who was not here.

Marek Vale was not in the building. He had been suspended since the second and the hearing had been set for the seventeenth and the hearing did not sit, because the instrument it was grounded in had gone into a finding on the sixteenth, and the Chamber's slip came down off the clerk's column on the morning of the eighteenth with eight words at the foot of it, and one of the eight was *grounded in a paper produced to the board*, and Marek read it standing at the bottom of the hill on his way up to work and did not go into the hall, because he was not clerk of the day for anything, and the letter *V.* was not put back on the assignment column, and nobody in the building explained this to him and he did not ask.

The woman on the posting desk let him in, because it was raining and he had been in the building every day that week, and she did not ask him for anything, and that is the whole of what the Chamber of Accounts was able to offer a man whose name had been on a slip for sixteen days and then had not been on it.
