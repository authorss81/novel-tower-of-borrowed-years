# Chapter 39 — Whose Pencil

The recount was taken in the second-floor room at the eighth bell on the twelfth of next month, and the room holds thirty, and there were about three hundred and forty people in the corridor and on the stair outside it, because the Gate yard had spent four days telling anybody who would listen that there was a date on a board.

Nineteen of them were let in. That was a matter of procedure and not of favour: a recount is a supervisor's step, and a ward cannot be a party to a supervisor's step, and a ward cannot be sworn and cannot be cross-examined, so what came into the room was nine people who were in it in their own names and could be sent out of it in their own names, and Aldis Kern, and Marek Vale, who had no standing in the room at all and was let in at the door by a hearing officer who had read a slip on a board on the second and worked out that a suspended clerk was nobody's clerk and therefore nobody's obstacle.

They were: Bohdan Rezek, of the fuel basin. Nadja Ostrow, of the low kitchens. Milan Vray, rope-maker. Pella Rusk. Milena Kelb, laundress. A young hand of the Tanner's yard, who asked for it and was allowed because he is nineteen. And Ondra Kelb, in a good coat, with a hand-book in the bag at his feet and a folded schedule in his inside pocket that he had brought and would not produce.

At the table: Hesper Rann, standing, because there was one chair and it was the chair with the leg. A third-grade clerk with a fresh desk's form and a pen. Levy clerk Ostrowsky, with the desk's book open at *recounts entered*. The hearing officer, in his own chair. And Renata Vole, at the back, on her own, without a seal and without papers, who had come because a cause entered in a room is a thing her finding rests on and she is not a woman who lets that happen in a corridor.

The other four hundred stood on a stair in the rain and were not wrong to be there and could not be in the room, and had been told so in exactly the words the hearing officer had used at the door of the Threshold on the twenty-second.

---

The procedure took eleven minutes and it was entirely correct.

Hesper Rann announced that a recount is the fourth clause of the settlement desk's standing practice, that any supervisor may require a re-addition of a certified total, that it requires a third-grade clerk present, and that it is entered in the desk's book with the hour, the clerk's name and the reason. He said all of it in the flat voice of a man reading a chair's rule, and one clause of it is the only public duty the Chamber of Accounts has ever performed, which is to record what a clerk has to say about his own work.

"The cause goes on the form before the arithmetic," he said. "Not because of anything in the file. Because the arithmetic is either going to agree or it is not, and if it agrees then a man who has written a sentence explaining why he did a lawful thing will want to know what he wrote. I have done them this way for twenty-two years and I am not going to explain myself about it in a room."

Marek Vale sat on the end of a bench in the ninth seat of a gallery with his hands on his knees and understood every word of it and could not have stopped it if the room had been made of paper.

The form went round. The third-grade clerk signed where it says to sign. Ostrowsky entered the hour in the desk's book. And Hesper Rann read the cause line into the record, in the ordinary voice of a man reading a sentence he has written himself and knows to be true.

Not one of the nine people in the gallery understood a word of it.

And that was the whole of the afternoon, and it lasted about forty minutes, and nothing in it was cruel and nothing in it was shouted, and it destroyed a man of twenty in his fifth week at a counter-desk.

---

The first thing that went wrong was not a person. It was a piece of procedure, and the hearing officer stated it in about nine words, standing up, with his back to the window.

"The cause must be capable of being acted on," he said. "That is what a cause is for. Clerk, is it."

"It is, ma'am," said the clerk of the day.

"Then state what the collection on this assessment was waiting for."

"It was waiting for the cause to be entered, ma'am. The fourth clause of the Deputy's finding of the thirtieth says the collection may not be entered before the cause is entered in the box at the head of the form."

"Thank you." The officer turned to Renata Vole. "Deputy. Does the cause satisfy the fourth clause as it stands."

Vole did not look at the table. "It is entered. The clause is satisfied. The collection may proceed in the ordinary course, at the ordinary wage, in the sums on the roll, and four of the eleven are payable this season, being the fuel basin, Ropewalk, the Tanner's yard and the Gate yard, of which two carry a household name in the margin." She closed her hands. "Nothing in this room disturbs my finding."

"Then the cause is entered, and the collection is unblocked, and the only question left in this room is what the cause is *of*."

Nobody in the room had asked, because every person in it was waiting for a person to be named and a cause is a sentence about a column.

"It is a sentence about where a number came from," said Hesper Rann. "The totals of the Tallow assessment were added at the counter-desk from a schedule that the ward was never served with. That is the whole of it, and every word of it is true, and the arithmetic is about to be done and it will agree, because it is a correct column."

The hearing officer said, "And where a false-witnessed instrument's collection goes, I said on the fifteenth and I will say it once more because this is the room it matters in. It follows the instrument. It does not follow the folder. An entry that carries a material term the parties were not served with sends the collection to the living clerk whose counter-signature is the institution's act, and that clerk is the only person in the city the collection can land on, and he is at the counter-desk of this hall."

The nine people in the gallery looked at a table.

"Who countersigned the Tallow entry," said the hearing officer.

The third-grade clerk said, "The foot of the entry, ma'am. I can read it."

"Read it."

He read it. A supervisor's signature, and a date, and beneath it in pencil, on the totals, and a name.

And the room said the name before the man who had put a pencil to a column on the afternoon of the ninth of the rains because the pen had not come.

---

Rezek got there first, from the front bench, without standing up, in the voice of a man who has been explaining his hands to strangers for a month.

"Pencil," he said. "Nobody has said the word careless yet. I want it on the record that nobody has said it. He was at the desk. That is the whole of what is being said, and I have been a foreman for twenty-two years, and I have had sixteen young men at a counter-desk in my life and I could not tell you one of them from the other, and if a man is at the desk then he is at the desk."

"Mr Rezek, the counter-desk is not in issue—"

"Then say what is in issue." Rezek put his hands out on his knees and did not open them, which was a thing he had not been able to do since the ninth of the rains and which he had stopped attempting in a yard. "Say that a boy was careless, or say that a boy was at a desk, and then say it to him, because he is in this room and nobody has looked at him."

The nine people in the gallery looked at Ondra Kelb.

He was in the ninth seat of the back bench with a good coat on and a hand-book in the bag at his feet, and he had understood the whole of it at about the second sentence, because he was a clerk and he knew what the entry was and he had added the column up twice with a pencil on the afternoon of the ninth of the rains while the ward's Gate tank was dry.

"It was me," he said. "I counted it. I counted it twice."

He put his hand on the bag.

"And I should like to say the one thing about it that is true, and then I will sit down, because I have had a practice since the fifteenth and the practice is that I say one true thing and then I stop." He did not put his hand out; the hand stayed on the bag. "Two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints. That is the excess on the ward's own copy at the foot of the roll, and I added the column up on the ninth with a pencil, and the column is right, and the total is right, and it has been right since the ninth. It is in Renata Vole's seal on a record and it is in the file and it will be there after every one of us is dead."

"Which is the ward's own figure," said the hearing officer.

"Yes, ma'am."

"And the corrected figure in the finding is two hundred and eighty."

"Yes, ma'am. Sixty pints apart in nine days, and I have not been able to account for the sixty pints and I have stopped trying, because I am the last person anybody should ask about sixty pints."

He sat down.

And then Milena Kelb stood up in the fourth row of a gallery, in a laundress's coat that had been washed the night before, and said her son's name, twice, in a voice nobody in that room had heard before and that three hundred and forty people on a stair heard perfectly well.

"Ondra," she said. "Ondra Kelb. That is my son, and he has been in this hall three weeks, and you have had his name read out in a room with a window in it and you have not once looked at the boy."

---

What happened after that was procedure, and Renata Vole conducted all of it, and she conducted it well.

The arithmetic was done. The column agreed with itself, as it had agreed on the ninth and as it would have agreed on any day since, and the officer entered the hour, and the clerk's name, and the cause, and the desk's book of recounts gained its thirty-second entry in nineteen years. Hesper Rann was kind to the boy twice, in the voice of a man asking after a colleague's family, once about the hand-book and once about his mother, and he meant both, and Marek saw him mean both, and it was the same man who had sat in a room on the twenty-first of the rains and asked after a cut arm in the middle of writing a sentence that had not been written yet.

The suspension of the ninth, which had been struck in the same breath, was not disturbed. The collection was unblocked. The four households named in the fourth clause were read out again by the Deputy herself, in the same order, and two of them carried a household name in the margin, and the yard roll on the board in the Gate said the ward will pay and does not forgive and does not blame, and the room was not the yard.

Hesper Rann said, to the whole table and not to one person in it:

"That is a lawful step and it is now complete, and I want it entered that the cause is true, and that the person it names is not careless and has never been careless, and that the arithmetic is correct, and that neither of those facts makes the entry any the less false-witnessed, because a material term was not served on the parties and that happened before any of us got here. I am not asking for anybody to be blamed. I am asking for the account of a ward to be taken from four named households and put against a schedule nobody was ever given. I have wanted that since the ninth of the rains and I have not put it in a drawer for a month because it is not a matter of a man's private arithmetic. It is a matter of what a ward owes and to whom."

Renata Vole said, "That will be entered in the terms of the settlement and not otherwise, and the collection will be entered on the ordinary course in the ordinary course, and this office does not intend to be delayed by a board on the sixteenth."

And Marek Vale sat in the ninth seat of a gallery of a room that held thirty and understood the whole of the afternoon at about the fifth bell, in about nine seconds, the way he had understood the third clause of a finding standing at a tank on the thirtieth.

There had been no forum for the six bells. There was still no forum for them. There was no office in the city that could hold a delay held without authority against an instrument that did not exist, and there was no box anywhere for a claim by a man against one, and the finding of the thirtieth had said so in eight words and had been right.

What had changed on the twelfth of next month was not that. What had changed was that the ward's bill was no longer waiting for a cause, because there was a cause, and the cause was a boy at a counter-desk, and the four households named in the fourth clause were going to be asked for two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints of water at the ordinary wage by people doing their work properly.

And the board sat on the sixteenth.

He had known that since the twenty-first of the rains, and he had known the sentence, and he had had twenty-two days, and he had not been able to put it in a room, and this morning he had been let into a room as a person with no standing in it and had sat on an end of a bench with his hands on his knees for forty minutes while a boy of twenty stood up and defended himself with the only number he had, in front of three hundred and forty people who had come up the hill in the rain to be in a room they could not be in.
