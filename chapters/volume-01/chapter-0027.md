# Chapter 27 — The Maintenance Account

The second-floor hearing room has wood in it and no window that looks at anything but the water, and it was built for thirty and there were forty-one in the gallery and nine at the table, and it was a Monday, and the eighth bell was called on time.

The notice named a piece of apparatus and not a man. *The Third Sluice, ward line, at the low kitchens, and the maintenance account of the Crown Terrace intake above it, from the second to the twenty-fourth day of the rains.* The party was the senior Lantern Warden, Ilya Senn. A Warden may be a party to a maintenance account. A Warden may not be a party to a settlement, because a sluice is not a loan, and the hearing officer said so in procedure at the top of the morning, in one sentence, in the voice of a man reading a chair's rule, and then the room went on.

The officer read the account first, and he read it well.

"From the second day of the rains, at the fourth bell of the night, the intake at the head of the ward channel drew short. It did not fail and it was not shut. From the second onward its supply was being taken down the channel to the ward, by a valve standing open, and the draw was such that by the eighth day the intake was short by more than it had been short at any time in the record. In the same period the ward trunk main at the second junction, above the ward meter, delivered to four yard counters four hundred and forty hundreds of pints in the peak night, and the account certified the junction at six hundred and forty, and the difference of two hundred was carried upon the district's irrigation account 4-Crown as make-good upon the intake. The draw of two hundred and some hundreds of pints a night is entered in that account from the second of the rains and was not entered in it in the month before."

He looked up.

"The office's position is that the intake was drawn down by an unauthorised act, that the draw was a diversion, and that the diversion is a maintenance matter and not a settlement, and that this room has no standing to inquire into anybody's character. The party will be heard on the account."

He read the ward's part next, and he read it in one paragraph, and Marek marked the paragraph afterwards in a notebook and did not know yet why he had marked it.

"From the second night of the rains the low kitchens at the Tallow Gate were supplied from the ward channel and from the basin by hand. The ward was not asked for the supply and was not notified of it. The ward's captain, a person, states that the ward will pay for the water it took at the ordinary wage and that the ward accepts that the supply was not lawfully delivered to it. The ward's own account of the night of the fourteenth is not produced and cannot be, a ward having no standing to produce."

That was the paragraph. Rescue and damage and the broken order, in a single paragraph, in the order the account happened to be kept in, because the account is a machine for moving water and it does not know what it is carrying. Marek had read four hundred pages of settlements and he had never once seen a document arrange a night into its own shape. It had not been done by anybody.

Then it went to Ilya Senn.

He stood up and put a folded paper on the table, and it was an order, sealed, dated the seventh, and beside it four letters, and he put them down in a stack and did not open any of them.

"This is the order of the seventh and I have broken it every hour since the seventh, and I have broken my own authority to keep the valve open since the second, and those are two different breaches and the second one began five days before the first, and I would like both in the room and I would like the room to notice that the second is the worse one, because a man who holds a valve open against a written order is disobeying and a man who holds a valve open with no order at all is deciding, and I have been deciding since the second and I would like somebody to write that down in the worse place."

"Will you tell the room why," said the officer.

"Because if I close it on the seventh at the seventh bell the low kitchens have no water at all, and there are three hundred people in them, and two hundred of them are children, and I have a count of them in my coat, and it is the count I made on the first night and it is a count of heads and I have kept it." He put his hand on the coat. "And because on the eighth day the Crown Terrace intake was short and the gardens made it up on our main, and that is two hundred a night, and I have known it since the sixth and I have said it to nobody, because a Warden who puts a number on a Crown Terrace account is a Warden who has been somewhere he had no business being, and because I am the man with the open valve and I could not stand in a room and be believed about anything."

The officer said, "You have put it in the room now."

"I have put it in the room now because the room has a clerk of the day in it who has been asking the right question for three weeks," said Ilya Senn. "That is not a compliment. It is a report."

---

Marek sat at the end of the table with his notebook and heard a room go quiet in a way he had heard once before, on the fifteenth, when Renata Vole had said four hundred and forty out loud on purpose.

It had gone quiet because the officer had been reading a column of 4-Crown and had come to the date, and the date was the second, and on the same leaf of the account the ward's own certified delivery stood at four hundred and forty for the ninth of the rains against four hundred and ten or thereabouts for a month before it, and a room of nine people and forty-one people in a gallery had just been shown that the ward's excess and a broken valve on a Crown Terrace intake were the same two hundred hundreds of pints of water, seen from two ends, and that neither man in this room had ever been in the same room.

Hesper Rann was at the table on the Trust's side, because a settlement valve is a settlement instrument and the settlement desk's supervisor was a proper witness to it, and Marek had watched him for two hours and had not seen him do anything at all except be kind to Ilya Senn twice, once about the coat and once about the count, in the voice of a man asking after a colleague's family.

Marek had also watched Hesper Rann listen to the intake's date.

The Deputy Steward came in at the fourth bell with the terms.

"Mr Senn. This office will record a maintenance irregularity and close the account, on a statement from you in these terms: that the ward channel was supplied by the party without authority between the second and the twenty-fourth, that the make-good water has been carried to the district account and is now the district's charge, and that no further action arises." She put the paper down. "You would lose no wage and no office. The irregularity goes on the plant's record and not on any settlement, and there is no proceeding here about the fourteenth of the rains, and your sister is not named in anything."

Ilya Senn read it twice.

"The make-good is now the district's charge," he said.

"It is now the district's charge. It has to be somebody's charge. It cannot be the water office's, because the water office drew it; and it cannot be yours, because you are not an account, you are a man; and so it is the district's, and the district's is the account that pays for the ward school and the ward's water and the nine men who water the gardens, and it is two hundred and forty per cent over, and the office cuts that account in a written order on a wall, and I am not going to read you the order, because you would not be able to un-know it."

"So the sentence tidies the file," said Ilya Senn, "and the next time that account is cut the school at the bottom of it goes first, and every person in this room knows what that means and not one of us can put a figure on it."

"Yes."

"And the alternative is that you hold the account open."

"The alternative is that the account is open and the charge is not entered, and the open account is read at the year end against whoever is standing in the room, and I have four of those in this year, Mr Senn, and one of them is a Warden who lost a hand."

He put the pen down and looked at the paper for a long while.

"Two things," he said. "And then I will sit down, and I would be obliged if the room did not stop me, because it has been nineteen days since anybody has let me finish a sentence.

"The first is a price and I will pay it. The make-good is now the district's charge. It does not say the district has been made whole. There is a boy in Tallow Ward with a shin in two pieces and a storehouse of condemned tar in a cellar and a hundred and forty people who carried pails for nothing and got nothing, and if I put my name to a sentence that says no further action arises, then that is the last instrument in Morrowfen that will ever mention them, and it will be an instrument about a valve.

"The second is an offer, and the room can laugh at it if it likes, because I know what it is worth. I am the man whose breach put the water there. Twenty-two years on this line, a yard and a half a year, and this hand. I cannot pay two hundred hundreds of pints a night for twelve days and I say that out loud so that nobody can think I did not know. I am putting it on the table anyway, because a man who has held a valve open for twenty-six days ought to be the first name on any account of it, and I am the first name, and I have been the first name since the second, and the only reason my name is not at the top of that sheet in the ward is that a ward is not a person and cannot keep a roll with anybody's name on it."

He sat down.

"I am not going to answer the terms this morning," he said. "I want them read again in a room, and I want the person who reads them to be a person and not an office, and I want to be asked in front of the party. Ask me again tomorrow and I will say what I have to say, and it will not be this."

"It is not this," said Renata Vole.

"No, ma'am. It is not." He looked at the paper on the table. "It is a sentence with three parts and two of them are true, and the third one is a hand-wave at a piece of the bill, and I have signed enough of those in twenty-two years to know what I would be agreeing to, and the worst of it is that the sentence would be true. I would be agreeing to something true, in order to be left alone, and that is a thing a man does at the end of his life and not at forty-one, and you would both of you be letting me."

The terms were read and were not accepted, and the hearing was adjourned to the eighth bell of the twenty-ninth, and the terms were held open until the fourth bell of that sitting, and Renata Vole looked across the table at Marek Vale, and it was the first time she had looked at him in a room since the twenty-second.

"That is my account," she said. "Clerk of the day, do you have anything for the room, and if it is a figure I will take the figure and I will not argue with it."

"The ward's roll, of which you have a copy, carries the excess at two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints against a lawful hundred and sixty," said Marek. "The corrected account is two hundred and eighty. That is the whole of it and it is sixty pints in nine days and I am not going to argue with that either. And the two hundred and some hundreds of pints of make-good has been entered in the account of the ninth without a single line against a person or a place, and the account is the room's account and the room is the only body in this basin that could put a name in that line, and it has had nine days to do it and it has not."

"Clerk," said Neris Kade, from the third seat, in the voice she used for a column heading. "There is a fourth figure and I am not going to sit on it, because in this building things die when the only person holding them stops breathing, and I am the only person holding this one. Four hundred and forty-one. I counted the Ropewalk standpipe by the hour for a fortnight of flood and the yards counted it by the day, and a hundred hundreds of pints is roughly what is left over when two people count the same water on purpose. The yard men will call that hundred my arithmetic when it is read out, and I will not argue with them, because they will not be wrong, and I will still be in this room afterwards. It has no document, and nothing in this tower will ever close it. Put the corrected four hundred and forty in and put mine under it, and the room will then hold two figures for the same night, and the room can decide which of us to be tired of."

The officer entered the corrected figure, and then entered hers under it in a hand that was not slow, and read both out, and nobody in the gallery argued with either of them, because they were figures, and one of them was right, and there is not very much that can be done in a room to figures that are right.

At the last of the light the second-floor room emptied into the causeway and Marek stood in the corridor with Hesper Rann going past him to the third floor at a pace that did not slow down, and understood two things at once: that Hesper Rann had wanted those terms taken in a room in under an hour, and that he had just told the Deputy Steward to hold an account open on a Monday afternoon.

The terms had been refused for the day. The account stayed open. The charge was not entered.

And the ward's bill of two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints of excess at the ordinary wage, with four of the eleven households payable this season, stood exactly where Aldis Kern had said in the Gate yard last night that it would stand if nobody in this building put a name in a line.
