# Chapter 44 — The Second Certification

"What is this document for," said Bel Harrow.

It was on the table in front of him where the bundle had been, one page, with a clerk's signature at the top of a box and nothing else on it a person could see.

"A recount's cause," said Hesper Rann. "It is a one-page certification of cause, which is what a cause is written on. A recount is the fourth clause of the settlement desk's standing practice: any supervisor may require a re-addition of a certified total, with a third-grade clerk present, entered in the desk's book with the hour, the clerk's name and the reason. It was sat at the eighth bell on the twelfth at this room's floor, and the clerk was present, and the hour and the name and the reason are in the desk's book, and that is the thirty-second entry in nineteen years. A cause has to be capable of being acted on. That is what a cause is for."

"Acted on by whom."

"That is the question," said Hesper Rann, "and I have been waiting two days for somebody in a room with a door in it to ask it, and I would like it said before I am asked anything else that every word of the sentence is true and I have never had cause to doubt it."

Harrow turned the page round so the seven along the two side walls could see it, and asked the hearing officer — not a member, in the room because a cause entered in a room is a thing a Deputy's finding rests on — to state the form of the question. A cause is a sentence saying why a lawful step was taken, and it has to be capable of being acted on by somebody with standing, and if it cannot it is a remark.

"Then tell me what it says," said Harrow, "and not the words. The words are in the file, the file is the office's, and I have read the sentence myself, and I would like what happens in this room today to be a board's finding and not a thing a Crown March representative said out loud on the sixteenth of next month. Shape only."

"It says that the totals of the Tallow entry were added at the counter-desk," said Hesper Rann, "and it says what they were added from, and what they were added from is a schedule."

"Who added them."

"The counter-desk. A third-grade clerk of the day, with a pencil, on the afternoon of the ninth, in about four minutes."

"And where was the schedule."

"At the foot of the fourth clause of the entry, cited there, in the file, in five pages." Hesper Rann did not move. "The ward's copy runs one, two, four and five. The schedule is page three."

"Whose hand added the totals."

"Nobody's," said Hesper Rann. "Mine. I am a supervisor and I do not go to a counter-desk. The clerk of the day at the counter-desk checked my column and put a pencil to the foot of it, and I have said in another room this month what a counter-signature certifies, and it is two things, and neither of them is a figure."

Nobody in the room said anything.

Hesper went on, in the same flat voice. "So the cause names the counter-desk, and the schedule it names is one the four named households were never served with, and the clerk who countersigned that column is the living officer of this institution whose act it was. Deputy, is there an action this office could take on that cause."

"Yes," said Renata Vole. "The collection on the Tallow assessment. Two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints at the ordinary wage, against four named households, being the fuel basin, Ropewalk, the Tanner's yard and the Gate yard, of which two carry a household name in the margin. The cause is the sentence that says why a ward's bill is charged where it is charged, and it points at the counter-desk, and the counter-desk is the only place in this city that collection can land."

The room did not make a noise. Two hundred and eighty people in a corridor did not make a noise, and that has been described by people who were in it as the loudest thing about the day.

"Oh," said Ondra Kelb.

He said it from the fourth chair along the left wall, in a young man's voice with nothing in it, and he did not stand up, and he had understood all of it at about the second sentence on the twelfth of next month and had understood none of it this afternoon until this one.

---

The Deputy dissented, her grounds went in in her own words, and the Chair wrote them, and it took two minutes, and it was the first time the board's own term was used and the first time anybody in Morrowfen found out that it worked.

"I dissent," said Renata Vole, "and I would like my grounds in the finding in my own words, because a member of this board proposed it, it was carried three to one, I did not concur, and I gave no grounds at the time and I am giving them now.

"The cause is true. I certified against that reserve four times in nine years and I have said in this room that I did not know what I was doing, and I have been believed, and I am not going to un-say it. The collection is lawful. The finding of the thirtieth stands, its fourth clause is satisfied, the cause has been entered, and nothing this board does disturbs it.

"What I dissent from is the word *aimed*. No instrument in my office's practice uses it. A cause line which is true, and capable of being acted on, and filed in the ordinary course, is not aimed at anybody. It is at a place. The place happens to have a boy in it. I have been in this building nine years and I have seen four causes entered against people and I have not seen one aimed, and I would like this board's finding to use a word its Chair can defend, and if the Chair cannot defend it then the word goes and my objection goes with it and I shall be glad of both."

Bel Harrow wrote all of it down. It took about ninety seconds and he did not abbreviate it and he read it back and she corrected one word, which was *aimed*, which she had said correctly.

"Noted," he said. "The Chair uses the word and the Deputy does not, and both positions are now in the finding, and that is the fourth term doing what it was bought to do, and it is being used against the man who proposed it, and I said in a room on the ninth that I would rather have a finding I could not act on than one I could act on and could not account for, and there it is at about the seventh bell of the afternoon of the second day of a board that has sat twice."

"Write it in your own hand," said Renata Vole.

"It is in my own hand," said Harrow. "It is in nothing else."

---

Then a man at the back of a room built for thirty said a thing that had been in him since the twenty-first of the rains, and he said it with no standing and no paper and no office.

"I wrote nine words on the twenty-first," said Marek Vale.

Nobody had been looking at the back of the room. Fourteen people along two side walls looked at the back of the room, and a table of five looked at the back of the room, and Hesper Rann, standing four feet away with his hands at his sides, turned his head.

"On the twenty-first of the rains, at the fifth bell of the afternoon, in the supervisor's room, with the supervisor at his desk and a third-grade clerk at the counter and the form in front of me, I took out my own book and wrote nine words in my own hand, in the cause box, in the levy office's formula for a cause. I did not file it. I did not show it to anybody. I have had it since the twenty-first and I could not put it in a room until this afternoon, and I want to say three things about it and then I will stop.

"The first is that the nine words are the nine words of that page. I did not invent them. I copied them off a form in a room I was in, and I have not been able to say so in a room for six weeks.

"The second is that I cannot produce them. Not today, not to this board, not ever. My book has four things in it and one of them is a sheet copied out of a page that no clerk may copy, and if I produce the nine words I produce that sheet with them, and the sheet is a second document, and a second document cannot be produced in any room in this city. That is not an excuse. It is a wall, and I have been standing behind it since the twelfth of the rains, and I have not been able to say that in a room until now.

"The third is that I am not confessing to anything, because I did not write the cause. I wrote a copy of a cause, in my own book, at the fifth bell of the afternoon of the twenty-first, in a room I was not a party to and should not have been in. I did not file it. I did not report it. I did not withdraw it. I have watched it go into a drawer and out of a drawer and into a room on the twelfth of this month, and I was on a bench at the back of that room, and my name is not on the page and has never been on the page, and I have had six weeks to be able to say that and this is the first room I have been in that has a record in it."

He stopped, and Harrow wrote down that a person at the back of the room had said those things and had no standing to say them and had not been stopped, and then read the last sentence back, which was *my name is not on the page and has never been on the page*, and Renata Vole said, "That is correct, and it has been the finding of this office since the fifteenth of the rains, and it is the reason a boy's name is in a cause and his is not, and I am not going to pretend to a room that I enjoy the operation of my own office."

---

"I have one more thing," said Marek Vale, "and it is in my coat, and I have carried it for six weeks and I have not been able to give it to anybody including myself."

He came the four steps into the light of the window, the way he had on the previous morning, and took a folded sheet out of an inside pocket and put it on the table face down and then turned it round. It was a one-page certificate with a party query at the top and one word written on it in his own hand.

"Clerk's certificate of posting, the eleventh of the rains, countersigned by me. The query above the answer says *clerk of the day is entered as a party to the matter. Does clerk certify*, and the answer is *Yes*, in my hand, and it is the ground of my suspension, and the Chamber has held it over me since the second, and I have had it in my coat ever since.

"It is not a signature on the false-witnessed entry. It is a separate one-page paper and it says nothing whatever about a reserve and it certifies that a settlement was lawfully posted. It is the only clean thing I have ever signed in five weeks and I am giving it up in a room because a man who keeps the only clean thing he has and then asks a room for anything is a man who kept it for that, and I would rather somebody in this room had it than me.

"I am a suspended clerk. There is no form on which I can give it and none on which anybody here can take it, and the Chair is going to have to rule whether a room can hold a thing a man brings to it. I have thought about that for four days and I have brought it anyway, because I have learned one thing in six weeks and it is that a document you cannot produce is worth nothing, and I have four in a book and none of them worth anything, and this is the fifth, and it is the only one that was ever worth anything, and I am sick of having it."

Bel Harrow looked at Renata Vole, and Renata Vole looked at the sheet and then said, "A person may put a paper before a board. A board may record that it was put before it. That is not a surrender to an office and it is not a surrender to me, and if the finding says otherwise I shall object to the finding and I shall be right to."

"Record it," said Bel Harrow. "A paper was produced at the request of the person who made it and signed it, and it is kept with the finding, and the person who produced it had no standing to produce it and was not asked to, and the Chair has noted that the instrument is the ground of a suspension entered by the Chamber on the second of this month and that the Chamber may take it up with the Chamber."

"And I will tell you what the Chamber will do with it," said Sedge. "Nothing. It will go in a file. That is what a file is."

"I know," said Marek Vale. "That is why I have given it to a room and not to a man."

---

The last thing the board did that day was take the only thing Hesper Rann had that he had not put in a drawer.

"Mr Rann," said Bel Harrow, "the Chair is going to rule that, from this moment, the supervisor of the settlement desk certifies nothing further in this proceeding. That is not a suspension. I have no power to suspend you and I am not suspending you, and nothing is suspended, and the word is in your record from the second of this month and I am not touching it. It is a Chair declining to accept an instrument. There is no clerk in this city who will serve a body it is a party to, and the Chair is therefore the only person in this room who can decide what goes into a record, and I have decided that nothing you put in front of this board after the seventh bell of this afternoon goes into it.

"I want to give you the reason first, because then you are going to tell me that the reason is yours, and I have thought about whether it is."

Hesper Rann stood in the middle of a room built for thirty and said, "It is mine."

"Then say the rest of it, and I will write it down, and it will be the only sentence in the finding that anybody in this room is glad to read, and that is what I am frightened of."

"You are right to be." Hesper Rann did not move. "I want it recorded that I have taken that power over twenty-two years by signing nine forms that said nobody may copy that page, and that I have watched four men in this building be unable to tell you what is on it, and that every one of the four was decent and two of them were senior to me, and that is why the page has not come up this hill.

"You have taken the only thing I have in this room that I did not put in a drawer, and the reason you were able to take it is that the thing it rests on is in a sealed vault and neither of us may look at it. If I could produce the page you could not have taken it, and I have wanted to produce that page every day for six weeks, and I have not, and if you want to know why then it is because I am not willing to be a man who brought it up the hill, and you are entitled to that and so is the boy.

Nobody asked him. Marek Vale noticed that, and wrote it down afterwards in four lines at the back of his own book, because it was the one moment in two days in which Hesper Rann had the floor and did not spend it.

"You may stand at the back," said Bel Harrow. "There is room. Mr Rann, one more thing, and then the Chair is going to let this room run for another hour, because there is a woman on the right-hand wall who has to be asked a question about a kitchen and about a daughter's seat, and she is not going to be asked it at the seventh bell of the afternoon of the day a man lost his authority. She will be asked it at the sixth bell tomorrow, in a yard, in front of the people it is about, which is the only place it is worth anything, and I would like this board's finding to be made in that yard and not in this room, and I would like that to be the Chair's last contribution to this proceeding."
