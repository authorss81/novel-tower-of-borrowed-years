# Chapter 20 — A Recount of the Totals

"A recount is a lawful step," said Hesper Rann, "and I want that said before anybody starts. It is the fourth clause of the settlement desk's own standing practice. Any supervisor may require a re-addition of a certified total. It requires a third-grade clerk present, and it is entered in the desk's book with the hour, and the clerk's name, and the reason."

"Reason?"

"The cause line," said Hesper. "The cause line is why the recount was done, and it goes on the form before the arithmetic is done, because the arithmetic is either going to agree or it is not, and if it is not you will want it in your hand first, and if it is you will have written a sentence explaining why you did a lawful thing at all. Which is what a cause line is for. I have done them that way for twenty-two years. Every one of them. It is not a trick and it is not clever, and if it looks clever to you then you have not read a desk's book."

He had the Tallow entry open on his desk, and the desk's form beside it, and the second draft of the cause line already pencilled in the top box in Hesper's own square hand, which meant he had written it in the room and alone and probably last night, and Marek understood that he was not going to be told what was in it.

"Who is the third-grade clerk?"

"Skar. He was the one on the counter on the day of the ward entry, and he is the only man in this hall who can be got for a recount without a notice, because he is frightened of the notice." Hesper made a small sound. "He's a good boy. He came up to the Fund's room and asked a question about a form that has been on that wall for nineteen years and nobody has ever asked it. Sit down. Not the chair with the leg."

Marek sat on the stool, and Emil Skar came in with a fresh desk's form and a pen and the very slightly glazed expression of a young man who has been summoned and does not know why and has decided that the safest posture is to be competent.

Hesper put the form down, squared it, and wrote the cause line in ink, and Marek watched him do it.

*Cause: the totals of this entry were added at the counter-desk from a defective schedule.*

Nine words, in a square hand, in a box at the top of a form that was not about totals and had no field for causes at all except that box.

Then Hesper sat back and said, kindly, in the middle of it, without any change of tone: "How's the arm."

"Which arm."

"Your arm, Marek, you are a man with one arm that matters more than the other and it was open on the fourteenth and you have been hiding the sleeve since." He looked at Marek's sleeve and then at his face. "It is a bad cut for a man who has to write."

"It is closed. It is fine. Thank you for asking."

"You are welcome." And then, in the same breath and the same voice: "The ward has its water, by the way. I had Petross's report this morning. The screen is holding, the tank is full, the third day of clear weather is forecast, and the low kitchens have a fortnight in front of them without anything moving on them, and the whole of that happened with a chalk mark and a man's word and no authority whatever, and I do not intend ever to be thanked for it, and I would like it noted that I am saying that now, while you are about to do something extremely stupid."

"Mr Rann—"

"Read it back to me," said Hesper, "before Skar does the arithmetic. Because he is going to write it in the book and I want it in two hands."

Marek read it back.

*Cause: the totals of this entry were added at the counter-desk from a defective schedule.*

"Off what?" he said.

Hesper did not look up.

"Off what?"

"Off what was the total added from, Mr Rann."

"It was added from the entry as filed." Hesper said it perfectly level, and to a question he had no reason to be careful about, because a man who has told the truth about a document has never once in twenty-two years been punished for it. "The entry carries four clauses. The fourth cites a labour schedule. That is the schedule the counter-desk had in front of it and that is the schedule the totals were added from. If you want the words, they are the words."

"And the schedule is page three."

"The schedule is page three, and page three has eleven sureties in it with no witness on any of them, and that has been on the record since the fifteenth and a Deputy Steward set every one of those lines aside in a room with the ward in it, and I did not ask him to, and I have not been sorry for one moment of it, and neither should you." He put the pen down. "That is what happened. I do not need you to agree. I need you to hear the sentence and then let the man do his arithmetic."

Emil Skar had the entry open and a pencil out and had been standing at the counter with his eyes on the column since the word *cause*, and Marek turned and looked at him and saw, with a clarity that arrived from about eleven inches away, exactly what Hesper had built, and exactly what it was going to do.

A defective schedule. At the counter-desk. In the hand of a junior of the third week.

It did not need Kelb to be careless. It needed only Kelb to have been at the desk, which was the strongest fact about him, and which was the one thing in the whole business that nobody could get past, and it would be on a form, in a desk's book, under a seal, in a supervisor's hand, in the correct order, before the arithmetic was checked.

It was the first lie in the volume that had a person's name in it and had been put there on purpose by a man who was not frightened of a ward.

Hesper Rann had not done it to save himself. He had already decided, somewhere in the six days since the ninth, that the ward was going to be paid, and what was left in the file was a question of arithmetic about *who* paid, and he had done the arithmetic in his own head and he had found that a boy of twenty at a counter-desk was the answer that a room full of people would accept, and he was going to hand that answer to Renata Vole wrapped in a form and a seal and a lawful step, and he was going to be kind to Marek in the same paragraph, and he was going to be kind to Kelb too in about a week, in a way that would cost him nothing, because the cost had already been taken out of the hand on the night of the fourteenth by a remedy in a margin.

That was the difference between Hesper Rann on the ninth of the rains and Hesper Rann on the twenty-first, and it was not a revelation. There was no moment in that room where a man became a monster. There was a man doing the only arithmetic he had ever done, better, with better paper, in front of two witnesses, and asking after a cut arm in the middle of it, and meaning it.

Marek did not sign, because there was nothing to sign, and he did not object, because an objection made by a clerk in a room with a third-grade clerk in it about a cause line on a supervisor's desk form would be a document, and he could not produce documents.

He did not leave.

He watched Skar add up a column with a pencil, and watched the column agree with itself, which it did, because it was correct, which was the whole of what Hesper had promised him in a room on the seventeenth and had been promising him in every room since, and which had been true since the ninth.

And then, at the fifth bell, when Skar had signed and gone, and Hesper had entered the hour in the desk's book and the clerk's name and the cause, and the recount was a completed, lawful, properly executed act by a supervisor with a third-grade clerk present, Marek got his own book out of his coat.

The back page had a footnote and a margin in it, in his own hand, made in a cold vault on the twelfth of the rains in breach of a standing order of the Yearspire Trust, and under that a labour schedule he had made three times over — once off the original in that same vault, once off his own head on a stair at four in the morning on the night of the fourteenth, and once off Kelb's copy on the night of the sixteenth. He turned to the last half of the page and pulled the ink stopper out of his own pen and wrote a third document.

Nine words. In the levy office's hand. In the box at the top of a form that was not the form and had no box.

*Cause: the totals of this entry were added at the counter-desk from a defective schedule.*

It took him under a minute, which was the fastest he had ever written anything that mattered. And the wet pain came up in the inside of his right elbow the whole way through it, the way it had come up on the sixteenth at the fourth bell of the watch, and he did not stop, and afterwards the hand was not right for the rest of the evening and the tremor was back and he had a second entry in a fortnight in the account of what his own body cost, and the account was the only one in the city that anybody was keeping honestly.

He did not file it. He wrote the date and the hour on the facing page and nothing else, and he shut the book.

Hesper watched him do it. He did not ask what he had written, and Marek understood, standing there, that a man who has spent twenty-two years not asking a clerk what is in his own book has reasons, and that one of them was that asking would put a sentence in Hesper Rann's mouth on a desk's book that could be read out in a room.

"Vale."

"Mr Rann."

"Two things and then I have a train of appointments." Hesper had the form in a drawer and shut it. "The first is that I have known for four days that you would not take the schedule and I have let it stand in that room until this morning because I am a man who offers a thing twice. I do not think you are a coward and I do not think you are a fool and I have watched you for six days and I would have told you to take it, and I am telling you again now, and it is still on the paper."

Marek said nothing.

"The second is not about the file." Hesper took his coat off the back of the chair. "The Fund will get that boy a hand-book. There is a woman on Ropewalk who makes them for the low beds and she is the best in the city and she charges by the finger, and the Fund will take whoever is on the list and the list always has three names on it and the first one is always wrong. I have told Zorka Vendl to say his name by name and not to take the first."

That was the whole of Hesper Rann's kindness, and it was delivered in a supervisor's room on the twenty-first of the rains with a recount in a drawer, and Marek stood in the doorway of it and did not know what to do with it, and did not ask, and would not ask, for a very long time.

---

He was still in the building at the last of the light with the copy room empty and the lamps along the north wall going about their business, and he took out the four sheets and laid them on the desk side by side, because there are four hands in the city tonight with a figure of Tallow Ward's water in each of them, and none of them agree, and the reason they do not agree is that each of them is right.

The file's, under a Deputy Settlement Steward's seal: the junction delivered six hundred and forty hundreds of pints, and the ward's lawful requirement was one hundred and sixty, and the excess was a lawful settlement standing.

The ward's, in four sets of yard books kept by men the water office does not pay: four hundred and forty.

The copy that the Fund's board now carried in a case of its own, off the foot of the roll, in the levy office's hand: two hundred and seventy-nine hundreds and forty pints of excess.

The examiner's, on the back of a complaint sheet, in a hand that had been counting people and not litres since the fourteenth: four hundred and forty-one, because she counted the Ropewalk standpipe by the hour and the yards counted it by the day, and a hundred more over a fortnight of flood is a thing that has a name, and the yard men would say it is her arithmetic and she would say it is theirs, and neither of them is lying, and there is no document in the tower that will ever close it.

Four hands. Six hundred and forty, four hundred and forty, two hundred and seventy-nine and forty, four hundred and forty-one — and then one hundred and sixty, which is not a hand's at all and which only the Trust is allowed to know about.

None of them agreed. Every one of them had been made by somebody who was doing their best, and the best available in Morrowfen on the twenty-first of the rains was a set of numbers that did not close, and a hold, and a boy in a low bed, and a man in a stair with a piece of paper he could not produce and would not be able to produce for the rest of his life.

He put the four sheets away and took out his own book, and opened it at the back page, where a footnote and a margin and a third copy of a page that no clerk may copy lay under a wrapper, and where in the last half of the bottom margin there was now a third document in the levy office's hand with nine words in it and no name.

Under that, in pencil, in his own hand, he ruled a short line and crossed it with four small notches at even intervals, and wrote beside it: *The fourteenth. Six bells. No form. No instrument. There is no form.*

He looked at it for a while.

It was not the shape of anything that happens to a man by accident, and it was at the same four intervals and in the same place as a mark he had been carrying since he was seven years old, and he did not know who had put that one there, and he had asked his mother about it twice and had been answered in a way that was a refusal, and he was not going to ask her a third time until he had a number he could not get another way.

Then he shut the book, and left it open on the desk of the copy room with the lamps burning along the north wall, because there was nobody in the building who would come and close it, and because a clerk who has found something on himself writes it down, and a thing that is written down is a thing somebody will find in a hundred years.

He went home through the Standing Market in the rain, past the shuttered chandler's shop, past a chandler who did not know that the entire moral weight of the seventeenth of the rains had been set down in pencil in a book eight feet above his head, and up the hill to his own room above the lockkeeper's shop, where there was no soup and no wood and no light, and he slept for nine hours, which was the longest sleep he had had since the ninth day of the rains, and he did not dream about the fourteenth, and he woke on the twenty-second having slept badly toward morning, which was new, and did not remember what he had been doing at four.
