## What the volume was about

The **Elmsworth Seed Share** — sixty-one Rainmarket smallholders who pledged their own labour and a third of their own seed in the bad autumn of MR 411 to keep a seed house open — is one short of a harvest it cannot lose. The Trust's credit window for the season is shut. The Sable Compact's Morrowfen house will sell the Share's instrument together with its members' district-rate arrears as **one bundled line at 300**, and the whole of that paper is lawful, and every figure in it balances to the penny, and nobody who signed anything wrote a lie.

**The volume's question was never *who wrote a lie*, because there was not one.** It became *what is standing behind a name, and who is not in the room when the name is written* — and then, in its last movement, *what is a room for, and who is it for, and what does it cost*.

The instrument's own fourth clause recites a **counterweight named as a district** — the Rainmarket low ground, which is the ground the seed goes into, and which is correct. So does the Trust's own printed seasonal advance form, in the fourth box, in type, for nineteen years. **A district cannot be asked anything, and a class cannot be asked anything either, and there has never been a form on which either of them consents.** When the low ground's rate is charged, the ratepayers in arrears **assign** their collection rights to the Compact at a discount, the Compact sells the paper, and the chain walks **nine links** out of Rainmarket and ends on one household's arrears in Tallow Ward in the ninth month — the same month the ward's own reserve carry comes due.

## The volume's institutional change, in one line

**A bundle may not be entered as one line.** It goes in as its parts, with the sum, the date, the discount and the receiving party on each line, and the counterweight named to the people who will bear it and the number of people who will bear it, in the words those people used.

**And the whole of what it cost, in three objects:** a levy of one hundred and forty a season whose cap produces a member who has not been asked; a fifth line for the hours that is empty because the hours are nobody's paper and a sixth line that is empty for ever because there is no box in the kingdom for the days; and a column with nothing at the head of it that nineteen hands declined to name and a yard voted, in four minutes, **not to fill and not to rule out**, because a promise not to name one is a heading too and a room can be held to it in a year by somebody who was not there on the day.

## What was paid for, and by whom

- **Tallow Ward:** nine hundred and forty pounds refused in the seed month, a lamp out until the third month, a rope-maker's days of selling, an independent-witness box that stays empty on a worse reason than the old one, and a document that cannot be produced and cannot be entered. **The yard is not grateful, not thanked, not forgiven and not a party, and it is not going to be.**
- **A woman of fifty-one on the second terrace of the Red Uplands:** a day and a half of walking, eleven switchbacks, four lines onto a wall, and nowhere to write the day down.
- **A man of fifty-eight in the levy office:** nineteen years, four cut orders, a condition he set himself in a yard in the rains, and a finding that ends the only investigation he ever cared about — including the part of it he cared about most, which is now unprovable and says so.
- **A schoolteacher:** a school named twice, at the bottom of a list both times, and a declining she cannot make in a form because a school is not a party.
- **A man of fifty-eight in the third shed of the carriers' row:** a rate arrears with a house at the end of it that he cannot pay, because you cannot pay your own debt, and a written offer in a book that no form permits him to be given.
- **A cooperative of sixty-one:** a lamp, a germination bench, and a rule that turns a lot that does not come up into a charge on a household's ground, for which there is no form in this kingdom.
- **Ovid Merrow:** the right to be the only person in the basin who explains a piece of paper, which is worth about a hundred and sixty pounds a year, and which he had already stopped paying in the first week the Gate shed was open.
- **And nobody pays anything to be told what they owe, anywhere, which is the first thing the room is good at and the only one anybody has ever been able to pay for.**

## The last line of the volume

A trestle in a yard with a box on it and a slate with no total on it, a shed eleven feet by nine with a form in it and a fifth line for the hours that is empty and a sixth line that is empty for ever, a board on the wall with a column at the right-hand end of it and nothing at the head of it, nineteen hands and a yard's vote, a slate with a sixteen-year-old's name on it lying on a trestle where she left it, forty-three and some months still on a plate on a shed door with a house at the end of it, a frame on a wall in Bell Stair with one name under its last line and no officer in the building, a district account held by nobody, a lamp out in Rainmarket, and in a lane outside a shed a piece of paper with a fresh signature on it, and it was not a bundle, and the rule that beats bundles had nothing to say about it, and the man at the counter watched him do it and wrote nothing down.

**A register is not a seed. It is a completed act and it is not a cliffhanger.**
