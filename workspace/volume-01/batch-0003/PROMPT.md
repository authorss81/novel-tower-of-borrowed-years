## Where the story is

MR 412, the **twenty-first of the rains into the twenty-ninth.** The flood is easing. Petross's screen is holding. The ward has water and a bill it cannot pay and a school it cannot keep open, and the city is beginning to talk about who caused a flood that is ending.

- **Renata Vole certified the Tallow settlement lawful on the fifteenth** and the Trust's investigation into the peak is about to open. Her office has already produced a second, kinder false cause: an operator's failure by **Hask and Rundle**, the district's irrigation contractor, upon **Crown Terrace account 4-Crown**. It is entirely true and it fences off the invented standing reserve.
- **Marek's own year scar** is on his forearm: a ruled line with four notches, the same four at the same intervals in the same place as the mark Lio asked about in Chapter 5. He has dated it the fourteenth himself and written it into his own book in pencil. **Chapter 34 owns the explanation. Not before.**
- **Hesper's aimed recount is drafted and sitting in a drawer in the supervisor's room.** It will be filed in Chapter 39. **Nothing in Chapters 21–30 may file it, withdraw it, report it to a third party, or resolve it.**
- **Ilya Senn has been holding the Third Sluice open since the second day of the rains against a sealed Crown Terrace order dated the seventh.** He is owed by the ward and distrusted by the Trust and the ward owes him a debt it has not agreed to name. He has four letters from a Crown Terrace water-clerk in his coat where anybody can get at them.
- **The ward's bill is now 280 hundreds of pints of excess at the ordinary wage across eleven households, four of them payable this season**, and the ward's lawful peak requirement of 160 hundreds of pints is on a permanent Trust record against a drawn 440.
- **The Trust's investigation opens** and the ward is going to have to be in a room about Ilya before the ward has decided what it thinks about him.

## Locked instruments — never blur these

- **The ward settlement entry** is the false-witnessed instrument. Hesper signed it the ninth day; **Ondra Kelb countersigned it in pencil the same afternoon.** It carries the invented standing-reserve recital and the omission of page three from the ward's notice. The collection lands here and only here.
- **The clerk's certificate of posting** is a separate one-page paper. **Marek signed it on the eleventh day** and wrote *Yes* on the party query. It is not the chain, it is not the false term, and Marek's name is never where the collection lands. It becomes the Chamber's instrument against him in **Chapter 32** and the paper he surrenders in **Chapter 44**.
- **The chain follows the instrument, not the folder.** This was said once, in procedure, by the Trust's own hearing officer in Chapter 13. Do not make it a puzzle again and do not re-explain it at length; a clerk may now refer to it in half a sentence.
- **The recount of the twenty-first** is a separate desk's form with a cause line: *the totals of this entry were added at the counter-desk from a defective schedule.* Drafted, not filed. A third private copy of the cause line is in the back margin of Marek's own book.
- **Page three** is the labour-pledge schedule: eleven lines, four columns (household, days, surety, witness), eleven sureties named, **all eleven witness cells blank**, never given to the ward, whose copy runs one, two, four, five. Both copies are on the permanent Chapter 13 record with the difference **noted and not adjudicated**.
- **The levy roll:** eleven named households, the posting names the first four, **the Vale household is seventh of the eleven**. Marek's family pays later rather than sooner. Tomas's vigor loan is a **separate lawful, witnessed, informed contract**; it is not on the roll, it is not the fraud, and it must never be blurred with it.
- The founding standing hold, the clerk of record, and the Trust's no-copy standing order are canon and live in `bible/power-system.md`, `bible/terminology.md` and `state/continuity.md`. **Do not restate them as new.**

## Power rules for this batch

- **Ledger Sight: at most one panel in the whole batch, and zero is the target.** It needs physical contact and a stressed hand, it reads visible entries, the witness chain and one immediate contradiction, and it **cannot produce a missing entry, a name nobody wrote, or an intention.** Its costs are real: headache, tunnel vision, and a small recent ordinary memory gone. Marek has now used it five times in ten days, **declined it once on purpose in Chapter 16, and started budgeting the fee** — that is its own kind of horror and it is the version of the power this batch should use if it uses one at all. He is running an account of what his own hand costs: a cut forearm, a wet elbow, and a book with three private documents in it.
- **No second hold.** The founding standing hold is used once in the series, in Chapter 8, and it is spent. Marek cannot take it again and must not try, and **nobody may suggest a second one is available or desirable.** Nothing above Boundary Hold.
- The delay is his and stays as a year scar. **The collection was never his to direct.**
- **Do not let the Sight solve anything.** It points; Marek still has to stand in the mud, count, ask difficult people, and choose.

## What this batch must do

- **The midpoint.** Chapters 25 and 26 turn the volume over. Marek enters this batch believing he is exposing a man who manufactured a shortage. The turn has two parts and both must land: **Hesper did not manufacture the emergency** — the ward really is short, the river really is rising, and the lie bought speed rather than safety, existing so the ward would not appear to pay for a gate loan that had to be sealed inside a thirty-day notice that expired on the twelfth. And **the man who broke the rules saved the ward.** Ilya's open sluice kept the low kitchens supplied through the peak, and the same sluice put water into the Underbank and cost Aldis Kern a storehouse and a relative's leg. The Trust wants one villain. The volume's question stops being *who lied* and becomes **who pays when everyone needed something hidden.**
- **Split the reader's sympathy on purpose.** Chapter 23 is the chapter where the man who fed the ward names what feeding it cost, and it must be as strong as Chapter 12's speech about the valve. Chapter 28 is the chapter where Ilya refuses to be the villain of somebody else's story and the ward has to decide what to say about a man it owes.
- **Keep Hesper human until Chapter 25 and monstrous at 25 without a mask dropping.** He is a tired man who has kept clinics open for twenty-two years, who found four dead people in nine minutes, and who believes he is the only person in the building who does arithmetic. His second certification in Chapter 39 is where the public mask comes off.
- **Keep Ilya a rule-breaker the ward is fed by and cannot yet thank.** He is not a hero and he is not a martyr. His competence has taught him that he should make the terrible decision before anybody else can, and that is the thing that should make the ward uncomfortable about him and not only grateful.
- **Marda Senn is the volume's clock.** Her pump counts have been ruled out of the record twice and she has spent a fortnight following a figure into a building. Chapter 22 is her trail into the Hollow Galleries. **Her name leaves the warden's roll in Chapter 22 and does not come back in this volume.** She is the person who is most nearly right about the water and the building cannot hold her name.
- **Let the ward do the work of a community.** The individual-consent work in Chapter 35 is the volume's institutional turn arriving from below: households asked one at a time whether they will take a public levy, and some of them say no. The Chapter 19 scar-examination method is the seed of it and should be visible in the procedure without being cited by name as a method the clerk invented.
- **Do not advance the clerk of record.** Marek may describe himself as making a promise; nobody appoints him.
- **Do not let the romance advance past professional respect.** They have had one afternoon of shared work and one argument about a column heading that neither of them won. That is the level. No physical affection, no instant trust, no warmth past what Chapter 19 already had, and no cooling so total that it becomes a sulk.

## Chapter map

Movement III is **What the Valve Held**, and it is the volume's midpoint: the pressure stops being *who lied* and becomes *who pays*. The order is deliberate — the investigation opens, the trail goes cold, the captain gives his account, the fence comes down, Marek confronts Hesper, the ward divides, Ilya is put in a room, Ilya refuses, the reserve is found in the Trust's own district account, and the aimed certification is filed at the end. **The batch's own climax is Chapter 30, when the chain gets a direction and Marek is told he has days.**

**21 — The investigation opens.** Renata Vole comes back with a second way out, and it is not a mercy. She will have the certification struck and leave his record clean if Marek signs a statement that the Tallow file was never assigned to him — which is a second false statement in his own hand on top of the first. *Play Vole as an administrator solving a scheduling problem, not a villain.* The ward's petition is refused because a ward is not a person. The Trust's own procedure is unimpeachable and it buries the thing.

**22 — Marda Senn's trail.** She has followed the junction figure into the tower and the tower has no name for where it went; the difference column on her slate has been ruled out of use twice. She goes into the Hollow Galleries after the figure, and her name comes off the warden's roll inside this chapter, and it does not come back. *This is the volume's clock and its cost. Nobody should explain the Galleries.* The Hollow Galleries preserve records removed from official histories; Marek must not go in and must not understand.

**23 — Aldis Kern's account.** What the open sluice cost his yard and his family: a storehouse, and Ferun's boy's leg. Kern has a rule that the Underbank damage is named before the sluice in any account of that night, and he wants it said that way permanently, and he is right to. He is not grateful to anybody, including the clerks who just saved his ward. *Make this chapter as strong as Chapter 12. It is the other half of the batch's spine.*

**24 — The over-read, traced.** It is a maintenance failure on a Crown Terrace irrigation account, exploited and not caused. Hask and Rundle's fence comes down, and the Crown Terrace connection becomes visible without any accusation being made yet: public discovery, no accusation. *The reader should learn that Crown Terrace's water is paid for with Tallow's future, in this chapter, in a figure, from a clerk, without a speech.*

**25 — Marek confronts Hesper with the arithmetic.** The midpoint turns. Hesper names the four people who would have died and does not apologise, and Marek discovers he cannot win the argument because the gate is real. *The chapter where the villain turns out to have been right about one thing.* Hesper is human throughout. Nothing in this chapter should read as a reveal.

**26 — A second night of high water; the ward divides.** The ward has to be in a room tomorrow and it has two stories about the same night that cannot both be spoken by the same mouth. Kern gives evidence **against Ilya** in order to protect the ward's water, and it is not a betrayal he enjoys. *Keep the volume's institutions — the Wardens, the clerk, the captain — all of them partly right.*

**27 — Ilya's hearing.** Sabotage by the book, rescue by the ward, in the same paragraph and in that order, because that is the order the book uses. The Trust wants a confession and offers terms. *Do not make the hearing a trial of a man's character. Make it an examination of a maintenance account with a man attached.*

**28 — Ilya refuses to be the villain.** He will not say the sentence that would make the Trust's file tidy, and the ward must then decide what to say about the man who fed it for twelve days. The chapter where the ward is not grateful and is not ungrateful. *Marek's Chapter 12 speech is what the ward is arguing about, and it is not a settled thing.*

**29 — The invented reserve is traced into the Trust's own district account.** The blank line is not only a line somebody can lean on; it is a line a district is already paying. Public discovery, no accusation yet. *Crown Terrace's water is paid with Tallow's future, and the figure must close against the locked numbers: 640, 440, 160, 279 hundreds and 40 pints.*

**30 — The aimed certification is filed.** The chain has a direction. Marek has days. *End the batch on the completed act and the clock: the document is filed, it names a junior clerk as the cause of a ward's shortage, and there are days between the filing and the board.*

## Required character changes

- **Marek:** from a man who has paid to a man who has been paid for, to a man who has been asked to sign a second false statement and has to decide what he will do with a pen. He is not a different man. He acquires a second decision to make and no more certainty than the first time. He must be tempted by Vole's clean note, and he must be shown to himself doing the arithmetic on the temptation.
- **Ilya Senn:** from a name on a work order to a man in a room, refusing the sentence that would end the inquiry, and the ward's opinion of him changes in a way he does not want.
- **Aldis Kern:** from a man who will not thank anyone to a man who has given evidence against the man who fed his ward and has to live in the same four hundred people afterwards.
- **Marda Senn:** from a clerk keeping her own pump counts to a name off the warden's roll, and the volume loses the person who was most nearly right.
- **Hesper Rann:** from a man who has been astonished that his arithmetic has a witness to a man who has been asked for a second false statement and cannot answer it, because this time the request is not his to refuse.
- **Neris Kade:** still hostile, still competent, still not warm. She has a ward sheet of her own by now and she uses it in front of people, which is new and is the beginning of her Volume 1 arc.

## Required world reveals, all of them small and all of them institutional

- The Trust's investigation procedure, and the fact that a Trust clerk can be offered a clean record in exchange for a statement and will often take it, because the record is the only thing a clerk has.
- What the **Hollow Galleries** are and are not: a place where records removed from official histories are kept. Not a dungeon, not explained.
- The **Crown Terrace irrigation account 4-Crown** as a live account with gardeners on it, and what a district's water costs when it is bought from another district's future.
- The difference between a **maintenance failure** and a **reading failure**, and the fact that a Trust can certify a wrong number for months without anybody noticing because the wrong number is in a file and not in a body.
- **A ward is not a person.** A ward cannot petition, cannot appeal, and cannot be cross-examined. This one line carries Chapters 21, 26, 28 and 35.
