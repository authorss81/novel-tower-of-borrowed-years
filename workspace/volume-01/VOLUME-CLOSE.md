## What the volume was about

Tallow Ward — about four hundred people, kitchens, tanneries, fuel yards and the city's manual labour — was short of water at the flood peak. The only way to keep the low kitchens supplied before the peak was to seal a ward settlement whose recovery was drawn from **eleven named households** against a **labour-pledge schedule the ward was never served with**, and whose offsetting **standing reserve** resolved, through one footnote, to a blank first line in the oldest register in the city.

**The volume's question was never *who lied*.** It became *who pays when everybody needed something hidden*, and its last movement became a third question: **what does a room do with four hundred people who cannot be a party to anything?**

### The one divergence, and why it is the right one

The outline and the batch brief both say Marek **refuses the order in writing**. **He does not write a refusal in writing**, because there is no form on which a clerk with no office can refuse an instruction addressed to the officer of the record, and **the office of the clerk of record has been vacant for three centuries** — the order cannot be carried out at all, and the person it is addressed to does not exist.

He refuses it **by act**: at the last of the light on the eighteenth of next month he copies the first page of the oldest reference volume into the public register by hand, **leaves the blank line blank rather than inventing a value**, and **writes the signature out in full beneath it, letter by letter, as a clerk is taught to do when the signature is not his** — with the words *written in full*, the date, and *M. Vale, clerk, no office*, which he reads and does not change.

**The mechanism the volume needed was not a refusal. It was the reason the order cannot be executed, which is that an empty page is not a blank line with a signature on it.** An unsigned empty page in a public register is a page anybody at all may write in, and in nine months somebody will, for a good reason, with no idea whatever. That is the recited reserve's own trick, pointed the other way, and it is the volume's last argument.

`outline/volume-01.md` is not edited. This is the record of the difference.

## The volume's institutional change, in one line

**A cut order may not be entered at the close of a month; it must be read, in the words of the office, to every party it names, before it is entered; it must carry the day and not the month; and it must carry the name of the officer who enters it.** The water office is still permitted one such order and still permitted no copy. Grale has signed the first one that has ever carried his name, with a hand that went bad on the last letter.

**And the cost, which the yard worked out in the same finding:** the school is named as a party and that is the whole of the protection; the north gardens' crew is ninth and the school is last; and a yard which knows a line is coming will spend the quarter before it lands.

## Power state at the close of the volume

**Ledger Sight: five batches, fifty chapters, zero panels, and no refusal staged on page in this volume.** Marek has used it five times in five days in the tenth to the fourteenth of the rains and has spent the rest of the volume budgeting the fee, because five uses cost him a parcel he ate and cannot remember, a quarter of an hour, an hour with a man's voice in it, and a mark on his forearm.

**The founding standing hold was used once in the series, in Chapter 8, for six bells, without authority, and it hurt a clerk who did not agree to it.** It is spent. **No second hold exists, is possible, or is desirable, and nobody in the batch so much as wished for it.** Nothing above Boundary Hold.

**The delay is his and stays a year scar. The collection was never his to direct, and in Chapter 39 he watched it land while sitting on a bench, and in Chapter 45 he understood that he had seen two men sent down a stair at the fourth bell and had not known what it was — the same shape, and there is still no form in the city for a claim by a man against a thing he saw and could not read.**

**The clerk of record has not been held for three centuries. He is not the clerk of record. Nobody appointed him, he has no office, and the sentence is in nobody's record because there is no form in which he could file one.**

## The last line of the volume

Lamps are lit along the Tallow Gate, the four yards are running on the public levy, the public register is open on a table by a window, and the Trust's notice is still nailed to the door outside, unsigned by anybody who will admit to reading it. A man with no office has written a blank line and left it blank, and beneath it he has written somebody's signature in full because the seal is not his to break, and he has told a nine-year-old on a bench that somebody has to write down what the tower owes, that he does not know yet, and that the line is not going to be closed while he is the clerk of record.

**It is a completed act and it is not a cliffhanger.**
