## Where the story is

**The fifth day of the second month, MR 414, and the ninth month of MR 414 is the season's clock for this volume.** Ch 101–108 were the tenth month of MR 413; Chs 109 and 110 crossed the year. **Chapters 111–120 should run from the sixth or seventh of the second month of MR 414 to somewhere in the growth month, with the season works made in the second month of MR 414 as the batch's spine.** **The growth month is twenty days long and its last ten days are the eleventh to the twentieth; no event may be placed after the twentieth of the growth month, and the harvest month follows it. Do not state the length of any other month. Do not count days backwards across a month boundary. Do not give a figure for the distance to the ninth month that contradicts anything a previous chapter has said.**

**The register is live in a shed in Tallow Ward and it is now about four months old.** It is a practice, not a law, and it is not a remedy. **Its one rule is a bundle may not be entered as one line, and the first half of that rule, which most people forget, is that a person may enter a line about their own paper and nobody may enter a line about anybody else's** — the first question at that counter every morning is *is that your line, is that the person behind it, is it your words*, and **a paper whose person behind it is not in the room is not entered at all.** That is why a counterweight named as a class or a district is refused, and it was refused in Chapter 108 **without anybody making a ruling**, and **no chapter of this volume may extend that rule, make it larger by a man with no office, or turn it into a remedy.** The fifth line is the hours and is empty because the hours are nobody's paper, and the yard has now refused twice to fill it and the second refusal was its own rule about a round that repeats. The sixth line is the days and is empty for ever. The column on the wall list has nothing at the head of it. A rota on the seed house wall in Rainmarket has days on it and nothing at the head of it. **No heading may be proposed for any of them, in any mouth, as a solution.**

**And the batch inherits the volume's question, which is now asked out loud and answered at the level of the calendar.** In Chapter 106 a Saltglass harbor notary built a mark out of four objects on his table and said that a mark that names a **day** can be checked against that day by anybody with a calendar, and a mark that names a **month** can only be checked against a day by somebody with a **tide table**, and there is one in this basin and it is in two rooms above a chandler's shop on the low-ground road. In Chapter 110 a woman on a river stair asked who says the person with the seal is the right person and got the volume's answer: **nobody, and a signature does not say whether a thing is true, a signature says where a thing can go.** **That is where the batch starts and that is the batch's discovery, and the batch's answer is a form, not a man: a standing authority that certifies a projection has nothing to certify except a number, and a number can be carried forward from one season to the next in a hand that is nobody's.**

**And the batch inherits Marek's decision to sit on it.** On the twenty-fourth he watched Sabin Rell sign a correct instrument and was asked, as a courtesy, whether he intended to do anything, and said **no**, and meant it. In Chapter 110 he had not written to the harbour. **He is a clerk with no office, no post, no pay, no form and no room, and the sum of what has been given to him by people with no authority to give it is a duty to read out loud, four hours a day, in a shed, in the words the person behind a piece of paper used.** He cannot enter one word of it. **In this batch he must not acquire an office, a post, a pay, a vote, a right of entry, or a way of producing the yard's own refusal in any room.** The flat board with four ruled sheets is on a trestle under the register's form and is not evidence and may never be produced in any room.

## Engines, and the one to avoid

**Batch 0001 recorded ten engines and no long table, and the review noted that seven of its ten chapters run the same dramatic shape — a competent man explains a mechanism at length, in the first person, to Marek, who declines to act.** **That is the batch's hardest craft problem and it is not fixed by changing the furniture. Batch 0002 must have at least four chapters in which the load-bearing event is a physical action, a disagreement between two people who are both right, or a thing discovered in a document that is not read out to a room.**

Suggested engines: **111** is a tide table, a lease and a man's hands; **112** is a desk, a ledger, a type case and a man who has to type a number by the second bell; **113** is a list of nine made by four terraces in a shed over a seed room, and then a drain; **114** is a set-off worked out on the back of an envelope in a lane because there is no desk; **115** is a school exercise book read aloud at a fourth bell in a yard, and a woman refusing to be the story; **116** is a clerk with a list of names that does not exist and a column with nothing at the head of it; **117** is two documents and a sheet of paper and a comparison nobody in the room is allowed to make; **118** is a depot at night and a column filled in in advance; **119** is a hearing room built for thirty with a Chair writing in longhand and a question that cannot be answered; **120** is a man on a stair, or a room, saying the true half and the wrong half of his own sentence in the same breath.

**No chapter's load-bearing event may be a document read out to a room. Chapters 115, 116 and 119 are traps** — a tally read aloud, a list of names, and a board sitting again are all set-ups for exactly that — **and each must be carried by the people in it and by what they do with their hands while it happens.** Do not use a copy room at a long table. Do not use a corridor. Do not open a chapter with a date and a feeling.
