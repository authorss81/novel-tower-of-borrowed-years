## What the volume was about

A form called **the lifelong attestation** is one sheet, four ruled lines, no boxes, and it does not name a paper. It names **a line** — a ditch, a roof, a stall, a boundary, a bin — and its printed third line says *I will speak to any paper touching this line while I live*, and its date **is a month and not a day**. A record loan sealed at a regional office in the third month of MR 415 carried its price in the last of five boxes as **fourteen attestations**, and carried it forward when those fourteen were all of record as produced, **as a class: the willingness of the people of this ground.**

**The volume's question was *why does a record loan make a coerced signature look valid*, and its answer is institutional and complete, and the answer is that there is no box.** Not: a signature is not a fact about a moment. That is true and it is the half everybody expected. The other half is the finding: **there is no box on that form, in this printing or in any printing anybody in this kingdom has ever had, on which a person is asked whether they were free, and there never has been one, and it is not going to be one — because a box for it would have to be printed for the first time, and a new form is a form a man in a good coat can produce at anybody in about four years.** The coercion was a queue with a man at it. A queue is not on any form.

**Nobody forged anything, and there is no villain in this volume.** Every one of the instruments was signed by the person whose name is on it, in type, at a counter, in person, about something else, and there is not one mark on any of them a man could point at and say he forged it. **The only criminal available in fifty chapters is a form with one line missing.**

## The volume's institutional change, in one line

**There is no form in this kingdom in which a person may be entered as saying they did not agree to anything, and there never has been one, and the volume restores the one line of type that used to narrow a witness from every paper on a line to one paper — and that line cannot be filled in, and filling it in costs every witness in the kingdom the last thing they have got.**

## The calendar, and the one thing a writer must not get wrong

**A reckoning year is ten months long.** It turns on the last night of the ninth month and the tenth month is the first month of the new year, so a year runs **tenth, first, second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth**, and the seed month is the first month. **Only Chapter 160 in this volume turned a year.** Bells are first to sixth, the sixth when the copy room shuts, the seventh in the evening, the eighth struck in the morning, **and there is no ninth bell.** **No month is ever dated for its length and no days are ever counted backwards across a month boundary.** **The span that goes stale is any month-count computed from a date, and this repository has paid for that mistake four times in four volumes.** What does not go stale is an anchored date.
